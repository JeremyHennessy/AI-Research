# Pass 19 — DiSalvo 2015: transmission of a capacity to carry partners

Susanne DiSalvo and colleagues (2015), **Burkholderia bacteria infectiously induce the proto-farming symbiosis of Dictyostelium amoebae and food bacteria**, PNAS 112:E5029–E5037. [Complete primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4568666/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1511878112).

## Published evidence

Acquiring a symbiont gives previously noncarrying hosts a food-carriage phenotype; removing the symbiont eliminates detectable carriage in tested hosts. After initial exposure, carriage persists through four additional social cycles without renewed symbiont addition. Food is replenished between transfers. Reproductive costs in food-rich conditions can become benefits under food scarcity. Some single-spore-derived lines lose carriage, and microscopy detects symbionts inside some spores and outside them.

The authors explicitly contrast their removal result with the earlier study: treatment and food organisms differed, and the discrepancy was not fully resolved. Their evidence supports a causal symbiont contribution rather than an exclusively host-genetic explanation. It does not show that the host contributes nothing.

## What was actually transmitted?

The maintained object is a pooled association with a capacity to acquire and carry secondary partners. Renewed food means serial transfer does not demonstrate uninterrupted ancestry of each original food bacterium. Intracellular localization supports a vertical route, while extracellular bacteria and colonization of new hosts permit horizontal transmission. Population-level persistence cannot estimate their relative contributions without further lineage evidence.

This is a stronger result than continued coexistence in the original vessel: development and transfer recur. It is narrower than reliable transmission through every isolated offspring. The two claims must not be collapsed. Loss in single-spore-derived lines matters precisely because a large pooled propagule can mask unreliable component-level inheritance.

## Design and inference audit

The initial carriage comparison uses ten farmer and ten nonfarmer clones; serial transfer uses six of each. Clone identity, assay repetitions, sampled sori and repeated transfers are different units. Existing stock collections connect this work to earlier farming studies; neither assay replication nor two phylogenetic clades establishes two experimentally witnessed origins of farming.

Growth conditions, initial partner exposure, transfers and renewed resources are supplied by investigators. Later serial transfers do not repeatedly add the focal symbiont or sort individual positive spores, which makes them informative about maintenance after initial exposure. They still supply demographic structure and a suitable destination.

Removal plus acquisition strengthens attribution but does not isolate every host physiological effect of treatment. The paper proposes mechanisms and possible coevolution; it does not identify a complete molecular reconstruction or experimentally observe the evolutionary origin of the association. Better performance of old hosts could reflect coadaptation or pre-existing host differences.

## Research consequence

For the digital research path, distinguish inheritance of particular components from inheritance of the capacity to recruit useful components. Reacquisition is a potentially legitimate biological process, but it must be visible in the ancestry record. A future claim that every daughter inherits a complete partnership would require different evidence from a claim about pooled-population maintenance. No software implementation follows from this analogy.
