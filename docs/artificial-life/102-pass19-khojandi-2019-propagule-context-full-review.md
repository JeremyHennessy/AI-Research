## Published evidence

All experiments use one host clone. Food bacteria are predominantly outside spores in the reproductive structures, despite intracellular symbionts. Transfer to new conditions produces differing reproductive outcomes by symbiont identity. Locally deposited propagules yield a benefit for some associations that is less evident with widely distributed spores; one symbiont remains largely detrimental. Presence of bacteria therefore does not guarantee successful host reproduction.

Figures report both the fraction of bacteria-positive transfers producing fruiting bodies and fruiting-body counts among positive outcomes. These are conditional denominators. They must not be substituted for unconditional success across all starting propagules.

## What the paper adds to the transmission question

The relevant unit may include spores and surrounding material. Demanding that every functional partner be intracellular would miss a documented extracellular route. Conversely, labelling the whole transferred material a reproducing individual would assume the organizational boundary that needs testing.

The experiment supplies evidence of function after relocation: food carriage can support subsequent development. It is not merely detection of a fluorescent passenger. Yet researchers initiate pairings, move material and supply suitable medium. They do not demonstrate the endogenous origin of dispersal, an autonomously evolving transmission interface, or indefinitely reliable reproduction.

## Replication and causal boundaries

Host-genotype replication is one, regardless of the number of bacterial combinations, images, spores or assay repetitions. Additional symbiont isolates test whether patterns recur across bacterial strains; they do not expand host genetic coverage. This work extends the same farming research system as DiSalvo 2015, rather than independently rediscovering it.

Localization plus bacterial recovery is stronger than imaging alone, but total recoverable bacteria can include both internal and external fractions. The authors leave open whether extracellular carriage results from extracellular retention, release from cells, or other processes. Predominant location at measurement does not identify the whole transmission history. Suggested molecular mechanisms remain hypotheses.

Changing dispersal geometry also changes local density and access to carried food. It is evidence that the assay's spatial context matters, not isolation of one molecular mechanism. Report the initial number of transfers, bacterial presence, subsequent development and conditional productivity separately. This review does not reconstruct absent unconditional rates or harmonize all assay timing descriptions.

## Research consequence

A transmission test should declare its unit: individual spore, whole reproductive structure, or mixed population. It should track both host descendants and carried environmental material. Benefits must be tested in descendants, not inferred from partner presence, and failed propagules must remain in the denominator. These measurement requirements refine the existing future research plan; no organism implementation is authorized or claimed.
