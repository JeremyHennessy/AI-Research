# Pass 19 — Inheritance can preserve recruitment rather than a fixed partnership

**Research interpretation, 2026-10-08. Unimplemented and not independently reproduced.** Builds on [Pass 18's distinctions](98-pass18-relational-continuity-and-reconstruction.md); preserves all seven EERC manifests and existing canonical hypotheses.

## What changed

The two full primary readings supply a qualified positive answer to the last pass: useful associations can persist across host development and transfer, and carried material can support reproduction at a destination. The association need not pass as an unchanged, wholly intracellular pair. However, pooled transmission, fresh food, mixed acquisition routes and investigator-controlled dispersal limit what this establishes.

The [2015 study](101-pass19-disalvo-2015-transmitted-carriage-full-review.md) separates host genotype from symbiont-dependent carriage and follows repeated development after initial exposure. The [2019 study](102-pass19-khojandi-2019-propagule-context-full-review.md) locates much food carriage outside spores and tests subsequent development after relocation. The [2011 origin paper](100-pass19-brock-2011-source-boundary.md) remains E1 because complete methods were inaccessible.

## Three routes that must stay distinct

| Route | What persists | What a measurement must establish |
|---|---|---|
| Direct component inheritance | Particular partner lineages accompany host descendants | Partner ancestry and transmission through a declared propagule |
| Ecological inheritance | Carried surrounding material supports descendant function | Provenance of the material and a causal descendant benefit |
| Recurrent recruitment | A retained host–symbiont state enables acquisition of new partners | Recruitment capacity, source of replacements and function after acquisition |

These routes can coexist. None alone proves a new unit of selection, consciousness or open-ended evolution. An inherited capacity to recruit partners is also not evidence of learned planning or foresight.

## Update to the proposed research path

The next causal question is more precise than “do both partners survive?” Ask whether descendants inherit enough organization to regenerate a useful relation, and identify which parts arrive through ancestry, environmental carryover or fresh recruitment. This is an interpretation of EERC's existing inheritance question, not a new implemented architecture or a newly demonstrated general principle.

A digital analogue must not hide recruitment in an external pairing service or copy an entire working group and count that as evolved reproduction. Ordinary resources and lawful environmental encounter are compatible with reconstruction; investigator repair, sorting or partner assignment must be recorded separately. These distinctions should be reported rather than imposed as a reward that forces cooperation.

## Positive and negative evidence are not a head-to-head comparison

| Evidence | Supported conclusion | Missing conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| DiSalvo: repeated carriage after initial symbiont exposure | Association maintenance across pooled developmental transfers | Reliable transmission by every isolated offspring or retention of original food-partner ancestry |
| Khojandi: development after relocation of carried material | Descendant function depends on partner identity and dispersal context | An autonomously evolved dispersal mechanism or universal benefit |
| [Giger 2024, previously reviewed](49-pass11-induced-endosymbiosis-2024-full-review.md): association declines after sorting stops | Improved inheritance under selection can remain dependent on continued selection | That every induced association must fail, or that amoeba and fungal systems have matched histories |

The amoeba studies use a naturally established system with laboratory initiation and manipulation. The fungal study starts with imposed novel pairing and directed enrichment. Differences in ancestry, development and environment prevent attributing their contrasting outcomes to one factor. Giger is a previously reviewed negative comparator, not a new E2 count this pass.

## Future-only discriminating measurements

- Declare the propagule before classifying success: isolated offspring, whole reproductive structure or pooled population.
- Separate transmitted partners from freshly recruited ones and carried material. Record the ancestry of each, not just species labels.
- Report all starting propagules, viable descendants, maintained associations and functional descendants. Conditional productivity among successful cases cannot replace overall success.
- Compare descendant function across dispersal contexts while tracking density and ordinary resource availability. Co-presence and benefit are separate endpoints.
- Keep failed associations and autonomy escapes. Measure host benefit as well as partner retention; greater carriage can accompany worse reproduction.

These are refinements to AL11's future interpretation, not runnable experiments. No organisms or scientific simulations were executed.

## Next priority

Seek a primary study that quantitatively separates vertical transmission from environmental reacquisition with individual descendant tracking. Prefer an independent host system over another descriptive paper from the same amoeba program. The discriminating result would show whether functional continuity persists when the source of recruited partners is tracked and externally supplied re-pairing is absent. Also retain Pass 18's unresolved denominator and figure-legend checks as a separate archive-audit task; this pass did not resolve them.
