# Pass 20 — Identify the route before claiming inherited organization

**Original research interpretation, 2026-10-08; unimplemented.** Refines the existing AL11 and EERC inheritance questions. All archived EERC versions and canonical hypotheses remain unchanged.

## What the independent systems add

The [insect study](105-pass20-kikuchi-2007-environmental-acquisition-full-review.md) experimentally separates environmental acquisition from tested parental routes and distinguishes acquisition from measured growth. The [clam study](106-pass20-russell-2018-egg-and-environment-full-review.md) supports egg association but relies on indirect evidence to infer the ecological frequency of horizontal exchange. They provide different strengths of evidence, rather than a matched contest between transmission strategies.

Together with [Pass 19](103-pass19-inheritance-through-recruitment-and-dispersal.md), these results make the next requirement precise: functional continuity needs a provenance record. Similar endpoints can arise through different routes.

## Separate five claims

| Claim | Evidence needed | Common overstatement |
|---|---|---|
| Partner material is present | Identity-aware detection and contamination controls | DNA or an identifier means a viable partner |
| A partner was acquired from a specified source | Source tracking plus exclusion or controlled exposure | Environmental presence establishes acquisition |
| A partner passed from a parent | Parent–offspring linkage with alternative sources evaluated | High population prevalence establishes heredity |
| The association benefits descendants | A causal functional comparison with declared endpoints | Larger body size means greater lifetime reproduction |
| The organization reproduces reliably | Repeated functional descendants, all starting units counted, assistance recorded | A successful association establishes a reproducing collective |

The route can be direct inheritance, environmental carryover or new recruitment. Preserve these categories even if all produce the same visible community.

## Why prevalence alone cannot resolve the route

Consider an illustrative bookkeeping model, not a fitted biological result. Let v be the chance of direct inheritance, and h the chance of subsequent acquisition among offspring lacking an inherited partner. If no partners are lost and detection is perfect, the fraction positive before selection is:

`p = v + (1 - v) * h`

A high p can therefore result from high v, high h, or both. One observed fraction cannot identify both unknowns. If positive and negative hosts have different survival, sampling only survivors further changes the observed fraction. Imperfect detection and later loss add more ambiguity. This argument specifies why endpoint prevalence cannot substitute for route-specific observations; it does not infer rates for either paper.

## Research direction

The proposed digital path should allow reconstruction through ordinary environmental encounter, while distinguishing it from copied components and investigator assignment. A recruitment capacity may be inherited even when a particular partner is not. However, neither source witnesses that capacity originating through evolution in a new substrate. Natural host recognition and developmental machinery are starting conditions, not newly emerged achievements.

For a future evaluation, track component ancestry and environmental sources, time of acquisition, persistence of activity, host/partner costs, and descendant function. Do not score only survivors or treat unavailable partners as a defect to repair automatically. Partner presence, host benefit and collective reproduction remain separate outcomes. These principles refine measurement; they do not authorize an organism implementation or establish intelligence or subjective experience.

## What remains unresolved

- The clam study does not directly measure the rate of successful horizontal acquisition or follow functional descendants through a full lineage.
- The insect study supports acquisition and growth-related benefit, not a complete lifetime reproductive comparison or the evolutionary origin of selective recruitment.
- Pass 18's denominator/legend discrepancies and Pass 19's full-text access gap remain open; this pass did not resolve them.

## Next discriminating priority

Review primary evidence of **selective partner recruitment**: how a host differentiates compatible partners from alternatives, when that capacity develops, and whether it can be changed or lost. Prefer causal comparisons that distinguish host filtering from differential partner growth and environmental abundance. This is the missing link between recurrent encounter and reliable reassembly; merely documenting another persistent association would not resolve it.
