# Pass 21 — Ohbayashi et al. (2015): the host's passage gate has bounded selectivity

**Primary study:** Tsubasa Ohbayashi, Kazutaka Takeshita, Wataru Kitagawa et al., “Insect’s intestinal organ for symbiont sorting,” *PNAS* 112:E5179–E5188. [Original publisher HTML](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1511454112) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1511454112).

**E2 review, 2026-10-08:** Read the complete publicly accessible original main paper, Results/Discussion, Materials and Methods, tables and principal figure captions. Did not reanalyze original images, supplementary PDF, raw data or laboratory code. No experimental replication, organism execution or redistribution of original source text.

## What the primary experiments demonstrate

The bean bug *Riptortus pedestris* has a narrow constricted midgut passage separating the nutrient-processing anterior gut from the symbiont compartment. Fluorescent native *Burkholderia* passed through, while orally administered food dyes did not. In direct comparisons, tagged *E. coli* had zero posterior infections in **five** inspected insects; two other tested nonsymbiont species also had **0/5** recorded colonizations each. These are finite, small comparator groups, not evidence of absolute exclusion of all environmental candidates.

Of nine nonmotile bacterial insertion-mutant lines, **eight** failed to establish posterior infection, while exception NM6 colonized **4/10**. A *fliC* mutant with vector control had **0/11** infection and a complemented version **9/10**, while a targeted *fliF* deletion gave **0/12**. Motor/flagellar function is important, but it is not sufficient: motile nonsymbionts were still blocked. Some altered-motility mutants remained capable of colonization. Related observations across ten other stinkbug species support a broadly distributed anatomical feature without tracking its evolutionary origin.

## Study units, causal limits and counterfactuals

These are several interventions within **one pre-existing host–symbiont system**. Mutant isolates are engineered genetic perturbations, not independent evolutionary innovations; individual tested insects are nested assay units, not independently emerged host species. The study identifies a bottleneck but leaves exact host recognition and mucus or immune mechanisms open. A negative posterior PCR/CFU readout need not isolate the passage step from later survival. The experiments **do not observe evolution of the gate**, assess every potentially beneficial partner, show independent collective offspring reproduction or imply subjective experience.

**Crucial correction:** the later broader [Itoh 2019 experiments](110-pass21-itoh-2019-competition-full-review.md) show that some beneficial nonnative bacteria can pass, directly restricting any reading of this work as perfect native-partner choice.
