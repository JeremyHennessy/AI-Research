# Pass 21 — Itoh et al. (2019): within-host competition supplements a leaky gate

**Primary study:** Hideomi Itoh, Seonghan Jang, Kazutaka Takeshita et al., “Host–symbiont specificity determined by microbe–microbe competition in an insect gut,” *PNAS* 116:22673–22682. [Public full original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6842582/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912397116).

**E2 review, 2026-10-08:** Read full original main Results/Discussion, Materials and Methods and principal figures/captions. The separately linked appendix, primary raw sequence/data files and analysis code were not independently inspected or executed. No source PDF copied or scientific reproduction performed.

## Negative evidence for a perfect host filter

The researchers compared **34 Burkholderia species/strains and 18 other species**, rather than limiting themselves to the earlier few excluded bacteria. Several nonnative plant-associated *Burkholderia* species and even *Pandoraea* crossed the passage and established gut occupancy. Among **12** plant-associated candidates, **five** showed reported infection rates of 90–100%, **four** 40–70%, and **three** 0–10%; the two tested *Pandoraea* species achieved 100% in the described conditions. These are candidate species within one host model, not 52 independent evolutionary histories. Nonnative colonizers were **sometimes beneficial** for host emergence, developmental time and adult size relative to uncolonized controls—none of which directly measures lifetime offspring output.

## The second selection stage

Paired mixed-infection comparisons found native stingbug-associated bacteria did not dominate the competing strains in the reported *in vitro* growth comparison, yet usually prevailed within the host symbiotic organ. Fluorescence over days showed initially mixed entry followed by increasing native occupancy. Thus, endpoint specificity need not be entirely due to selective **admission**; a post-entry **ecological competition/accommodation** phase contributes.

**Qualifier essential for falsification:** *Pandoraea* was competitive at the high **5,000-cell** challenge and was significantly displaced by native bacteria at a **250-cell** comparison. Strong words such as “always outcompetes” in the article must not be extended to arbitrary doses, strains, environments or host ages. The results cannot identify a single mechanistic cause: host nutrient supply, localized growth, adhesion and immune tolerance remain alternatives. The authors discuss negative type-VI-secretion tests in a supplement which was not independently audited here.

## Independence and evolutionary origin

These studies share the *Riptortus–Burkholderia* research program and overlapping investigators with [Ohbayashi 2015](109-pass21-ohbayashi-2015-filtering-full-review.md). They supply different controlled contrasts but **not independent origins of evolved selectivity**. The article expressly leaves open whether native symbiont competitive advantages evolved through selection inside the insect or in external environments and were later co-opted. Its cross-species comparison does not reconstruct that causal history.
