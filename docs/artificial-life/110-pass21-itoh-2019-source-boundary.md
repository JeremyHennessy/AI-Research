# Pass 21 — Itoh 2019: source boundary and competition lead

**Primary source:** Itoh et al., [Host–symbiont specificity determined by microbe–microbe competition in an insect gut](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912397116). **E1 remains appropriate.**

The abstract and early Results were accessible through [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6842582/). Later retrieval returned a browser check, publisher HTML a cookie page, and the public publisher PDF an error. The complete competition Results and Methods were not read. No method-audit record is added.

The abstract reports that some nonnative bacteria can colonize and benefit hosts alone, but lose to the native symbiont under tested coinoculation. This is a useful counterexample to equating selective association with exclusive entry. The competitive mechanism and generality remain pending a full reading.

## Questions reserved for a complete reading

- Were exposure, arrival time and viable abundance comparable between alternatives?
- Did both candidates enter before one disappeared, and what distinguishes exclusion at entry from loss afterward?
- Do comparisons outside the host isolate general growth differences, or only one culture condition?
- Is host benefit measured independently of competitive dominance?
- Which conclusions depend on one representative strain versus a broader panel?

The paper overlaps the 2015 study in host system and authors. It is a mechanistic follow-up, not an independent evolutionary origin. “Always” in an abstract must be restricted to the tested comparisons, not projected onto every arrival order. A later complete reading should promote this existing record, preserve C237 at E1, and avoid creating a duplicate source.
