# Pass 21 — Chen 2024: priority without a resolved exclusion mechanism

**Primary source:** Chen et al., [A strong priority effect in the assembly of a specialized insect-microbe symbiosis](https://doi.org/10.1128/aem.00818-24). **E2: complete published main article**, Methods, Results, Discussion, captions and availability statement read through ASM HTML. Separate supplements and deposited data/code were not audited or executed.

## Evidence receipt

Reciprocal arrival order and isogenic comparisons support priority effects. Resident depletion was incomplete; failed replacement therefore does not isolate host control. Complete anatomical closure occurred too late to explain initial exclusion. Immediate mechanism remains unresolved.

## Original causal assessment

Changing arrival order is stronger evidence for historical contingency than merely observing different resident communities. A result with genetically matched candidates also limits explanations requiring a fixed superiority of one genotype. Neither comparison proves that competition disappears: otherwise identical populations can differ in abundance, location and physiological state because of their histories.

The depletion comparison needs special care. Reduced signal is not absence, and low remaining abundance need not imply a negligible ecological effect. A resistant challenger and previously uncolonized controls help address direct drug suppression, but they cannot establish that the depleted host has returned to its original state. A failed rescue therefore narrows explanations without assigning the residual effect uniquely to the host or microbe.

Temporal precedence is a separate test. A structural change that finishes after exclusion begins cannot be the sole necessary initiating event. This does not rule out earlier changes in secretions, local chemistry, feeding or tissue physiology that the anatomical measurement misses. A nonsignificant diameter comparison is also not a formal demonstration of equivalence.

## Sampling and statistical reading

Main Methods report individual-host imaging and colony-based composition, rank tests, a binomial replacement model, and a one-sided test for lumen change. The replacement analysis omits two of 81 individuals that advanced developmental stage; the microscopy depletion subset was selected for weak fluorescence. These distinctions belong in interpretation of denominators and representativeness. Exact plot-level denominators and deposited analyses were not independently reconstructed.

The Results call the lumen test signed-rank, while Methods name a one-sided Wilcoxon test. The text alone does not settle pairing or implementation; keep this as an unresolved reporting detail pending the deposited analysis, without treating it as evidence that the central result is false.

## Consequence for EERC

Stable association can reflect history-dependent access. It is not automatically selection for superior benefit or reliable reconstruction in descendants. The next conceptual requirement is to keep composition, function and reproduction as distinct outcomes. These are research interpretations; no biological manipulation, digital organism or new EERC version is proposed here.
