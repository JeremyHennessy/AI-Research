# Pass 21 — Admission, competition and evolved partner choice are distinct hypotheses

**Original, untested, research-only synthesis dated 2026-10-08.** [2015 controlled entry](109-pass21-ohbayashi-2015-filtering-full-review.md), [2019 broad negative and competition evidence](110-pass21-itoh-2019-competition-full-review.md), [Pass 20 transmission provenance](107-pass20-transmission-provenance-and-evidence.md).

The key challenge is *identifiability*: an adult with mostly one bacterial partner could result from (a) an initial host gate, (b) microbial motility, (c) biased environmental dose or first arrival, (d) later competitive replacement or (e) survival conditioning. A final identity alone cannot determine which stage produced the observation. The broader 2019 panel directly refutes a perfect native-specific entry explanation. Neither paper demonstrates the evolutionary **origin** of the pre-existing host gate or of within-host competitive fitness.

| Proposed observational stage | Distinct question | Main null / confound |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental encounter | What active partners were available to every host? | Supply and dose determine apparent preference |
| Immediate passage | Which partners cross conditional on exposure, before replication? | A narrow passage but no partner-specific discrimination |
| Niche establishment | Which admitted partners survive and occupy sites? | Simple delayed killing or assay detectability |
| Competition and host function | Which remain after co-infection, at what mutual cost? | Within-host growth advantages without improved host descendants |
| Multi-generation origin | Does a heritable new admission/competition trait arise and transmit functional descendants? | Preinstalled gate, curator-provided partners, ecological carryover, artificial copy service |

**H21-A (entry hypothesis, future-only):** relative entry probability differs by partner **after** controlling source abundance and before post-entry growth. A null would be equal passage with divergent later counts.

**H21-B (competition hypothesis, future-only):** within-host partner ratio diverges after verified comparable initial entry, beyond a matched host-free competitor comparison. A null would be entirely dose/order-driven divergence.

**H21-C (evolutionary emergence, future-only):** a genuinely *new heritable trait* confers costed, measured offspring reproductive benefit across independent lineages without author-driven partner placement, forced group copying or changed primitive rules. Refutations include ancestral trait already present, lack of reproductive benefit, zero independent daughters, or survival only under curated supply. Only this stronger question bears directly on emergent organizational innovation.

The current primary studies do not provide an origin test. Shared investigators, one host model and a panel of strains cannot be treated as independent ancestral transition replicates. Natural reproductive endpoints, ecological availability and partner-lineage ancestry must remain separate. If direct multigenerational controlled evolutionary evidence does not exist, **preserve that as a knowledge gap**, not an implicit positive result. Do not infer general intelligence, phenomenal consciousness, or ethical status from adaptive microbial sorting.

**Highest-information Pass 22 target:** find an original benign experimental-evolution paper comparing ancestor, newly evolved and reverted preferences against independent host-versus-environment selection controls, including unsuccessful lineages, offspring counts and costs. Research-only; do not run an organism, simulation or world without separate approval.
