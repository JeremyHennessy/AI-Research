# Pass 21 — selective recruitment, persistence and arrival history

**Status:** original evidence synthesis; no implementation or scientific reproduction. Continues [Pass 20](107-pass20-transmission-provenance-and-evidence.md). EERC v1–v7 remain unchanged.

The evidentiary target is a mechanism that reconstructs a useful relationship. A dominant partner at one endpoint is insufficient to identify that mechanism. We must distinguish the following stages and causal alternatives.

| Question | Evidence that would help discriminate it | Remaining alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Was the candidate available? | Source-specific viable abundance and exposure history | An absent partner may never have been encountered |
| Did it enter? | Early localization on both sides of the relevant boundary | Rapid loss or insufficient detection can mimic blocked entry |
| Did it persist? | Repeated absolute abundance with known initial establishment | Relative dominance may reflect loss of another candidate |
| Did arrival history matter? | Reciprocal order with comparable candidate exposure | Host development and unequal realized ingestion can covary with order |
| Did the resident improve function? | Functional outcomes distinct from partner abundance | A good colonizer need not be a good contributor |
| Was the relationship reconstructed? | Descendant lineage and functional outcomes across reproduction | Within-host retention is not intergenerational continuity |
| Did the capacity evolve? | Ancestral comparison and heritable change in the relevant capacity | A supplied or pre-existing interface may explain assembly |

These are future evidentiary requirements, not an experimental protocol or a claim that the controls have already been performed.

## Original interpretation: compatibility is not quality control

A selection process can be specific at one stage and permissive at another. Even a repeatable resident identity does not reveal whether the host assessed benefit, whether the candidate grew faster after entry, or whether earlier arrival prevented later establishment. “Partner choice” should therefore always name the measured stage and outcome.

The relation between dominance and usefulness has no guaranteed direction. An entry gate might retain an adequate partner while excluding a better later candidate; alternatively it might protect a functioning relationship against disruption. These are rival possibilities, not claims demonstrated by the present sources. Their distinction requires direct functional comparisons and environmental context.

For EERC, a hypothetical internally maintained boundary deserves credit for what it demonstrably causes. It does not receive additional credit for the historical origin of that boundary or for descendant reconstruction unless those outcomes are measured. Similarly, equalized external abundance does not guarantee equal internal exposure, and a failed recolonization after depletion does not prove host memory in isolation.
