## Three original studies and contrasts

1. **Regus et al. 2017**, *American Journal of Botany* 104:1299–1312, DOI [10.3732/ajb.1700165](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.1700165). Previous Pass 22 reading receipt reports host-cell-localized histological responses to ineffective rhizobia in mixed nodules in two host species. The cells were nested in relatively few nodules and plants, not hundreds of independent evolutionary lineages. In-vitro equivalence of near-isogenic strains does not guarantee equal within-host growth; naturally different strains introduce confounding. Neither daughter-host reproduction nor the evolutionary origin of discrimination was shown. The original reading receipt says primary main text was read, not raw data or supplements. **Status: E2 reported in the external handoff, not independently replicated.**

2. **Underwood et al. 2026**, *Journal of Experimental Botany* 77:4761–4772, DOI [10.1093/jxb/erag191](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erag191). Earlier review accessed an abstract and selected original passages, but not a contiguous full Methods and figures. Reported pea response occurred at whole-nodule scale, without a detected single-cell sanction effect in mixed nodules. Species, timing, readouts and statistical power differ from Regus: **not a direct replication or refutation**. **Status: E1, incomplete source access.**

3. **Westhoek et al. 2017**, *Scientific Reports* 7:1419, [full primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-01634-2). Original article's Methods, Results and Discussion publicly available. The team compared fixing and nonfixing near-isogenic pea rhizobia, with marker swaps. At entry, the fixing strain's fraction of nodules followed its fraction in the inoculum (reported slope 0.99, 95% CI 0.95–1.02); the plants did not reject the nonfixer before nodule formation. Nonfixing nodules were substantially smaller, supporting post-entry sanctioning. Importantly, stained sector area in mixed nodules was strongly marker-dependent (approximately 31% vs 75% apparent fixing-strain area depending on label); authors judged the staining unsuitable for measuring relative microbial fitness in mixed nodules. They explicitly distinguished reduced nodule size from demonstrated long-term suppression of bacterial reproduction. This is a negative control for inferring partner recognition from occupancy or partner fitness from microscopy. **Status: primary main-text reading; no supplements, data reanalysis, reproduction or independent evolutionary lineages assessed.**

The Regus/Underwood contrast concerns spatial resolution; Westhoek addresses timing and a measurement artifact. These studies are not three independent demonstrations that a host control mechanism evolved.

## Falsifiable hypotheses (unimplemented)

- **H22-A — resolution trade-off:** whole-compartment sanctions may suppress beneficial co-residents while cell-local control incurs regulatory costs. Reject if lineage-level descendant function is unchanged under matched host, partner availability, exposure and resource accounting despite independently varying control scale.
- **H22-B — recognition null:** partner abundance and mixed-nodule occupancy can reflect entry probability, growth, marker bias and arrival order without quality sensing. Reject only with direct evidence isolating sensing from these alternatives.
- **H22-C — ecological reversal:** the cost/benefit of sanctioning can reverse with environmental resource supply. Reject if sanctions retain an independently measured reproductive advantage under controlled resource reversal and across descendant host lineages.

No study here demonstrates ancestral emergence of control followed by independently viable daughter-organization inheritance, adaptive general intelligence, or consciousness. No organisms, worlds, simulations or original scientific experiments were created or executed.
