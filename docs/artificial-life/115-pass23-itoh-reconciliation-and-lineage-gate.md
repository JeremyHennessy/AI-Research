## Recoverable scientific distinctions (reported by the separate branch, pending independent primary check)

- Of the reported 34 *Burkholderia* species/strains and 18 other bacterial species, some nonnative microbes passed the host's entry structure and occupied the gut. Therefore a perfect exclusive entry filter is contradicted **in those tested conditions**, not universally.
- Within-host mixed-infection time courses reportedly show initial coexistence followed by increased native symbiont occupancy, even though a simple *in vitro* comparison did not give the same advantage. This suggests post-entry competition or host accommodation; it does **not** identify a unique causal molecular mechanism.
- The branch records an explicit dose counterexample: *Pandoraea* persisted at a 5,000-cell challenge but was displaced in the reported 250-cell comparison. Thus neither 'native always wins' nor 'competition independent of dose' is defensible.
- Host developmental and body-size benefits are not the same outcome as lifetime reproductive fitness. Bacterial competitive dominance is not proof of host benefit; successful entry or occupancy is not independent lineage reproduction.

The same *Riptortus–Burkholderia* research program underlies Itoh 2019 and Ohbayashi 2015. Treat them as mechanistic follow-ups with partially shared provenance, **not independent evolutionary origins**. The contrasting Chen 2024 study is another system but cannot establish the evolutionary emergence of a partner filter by itself.

## Next genuinely unanswered causal question

**Can an initially weakly selective host–partner system evolve a new partner-control capability, retain it in descendants, and improve independent lineage reproductive output under ecological costs?** Current observations of host-specific occupancy, sanctions, or post-entry competition do not answer this. Separate the following stages in future *documentary* evidence: (1) known ancestor state; (2) genetic or physiological change during selection; (3) direct control of entry versus post-entry persistence; (4) host and symbiont lifetime reproduction; (5) serial descendant passage without experimental rescue; (6) independent replicate lineages and failure cases; (7) reversal when environmental costs change.

### Falsifiable, unimplemented hypotheses

- **P23-H1 (two-stage null):** apparent specificity can arise from broadly permissive entry plus ecological sorting. Reject for a given system only when direct entry exclusion is shown after controlling exposure, viable inoculum, arrival order, and within-host growth.
- **P23-H2 (reproductive decoupling):** higher symbiont occupancy need not increase host lifetime offspring. Reject only with independently replicated paired measurements of occupancy and lifetime offspring under controlled conditions.
- **P23-H3 (evolutionary origin):** repeated host passage can change partner control without improving independent descendant viability. Reject only with documented ancestral contrast, independent evolved lineages, functional inheritance and a counterfactual cost/benefit assay.
