## Experimental question and design

Does an early-stage artificially mutualistic bacterium already exhibit narrow host range, and how does that compare with a natural cultivable symbiont? The authors studied eight stinkbug species, including the original *Plautia stali* host and seven other species. Eggs were surface sterilized to remove resident symbionts; newborns were orally exposed to one of three bacteria: a laboratory-derived `E. coli` ΔcyaA mutant, a ΔintS *E. coli* control, or natural *Pantoea* Sym C. Uninoculated symbiont-free and native-symbiont controls supplied contrasts. Outcomes included PCR infection at second instar and adulthood, daily survival, adult emergence and adult body size. The host species were long-maintained inbred laboratory stocks, not independent evolutionary trajectories.

**Crucial intervention distinction:** ΔcyaA was deliberately taken from the Keio knockout library on a wild-type background because original evolved *E. coli* came from hypermutating ΔmutS lines. Thus this paper **tests the consequence of a reconstructed single mutation discovered by earlier laboratory evolution**; it does not itself track independent de novo emergence of ΔcyaA in multiple host lineages. The natural Sym C strain was collected from *P. stali* in 2009; its broader host range does not establish when or by which mutations it evolved.

## Main results and negative controls

- In original host *P. stali*, native symbiont yielded **87.7%** adult emergence; symbiont-free controls **0%**; ΔcyaA **54.9%**; natural Sym C **28.6%**; and ΔintS *E. coli* **12.1%**. The wild-type-like control's nonzero adult emergence is important: strict claims of zero ancestral compatibility would be incorrect.
- Across the **seven non-original hosts**, ΔcyaA failed to establish a persistent, developmentally supportive symbiosis by the paper's composite outcome criteria. Sym C supported successful or partial symbiosis in *Glaucias subpunctatus*, *Lampromicra miyakonus* and *Poecilocoris lewisi*, but not all other hosts.
- **Strong negative examples:** *Halyomorpha halys* retained some survival under experimental conditions but none of the replacement groups reached adulthood; *Eucorysses grandis* likewise did not establish replacement infection or adult emergence. In *Riptortus pedestris*, replacement treatments had numerically higher survival/adult emergence but **no established infection** and differences were **not statistically significant**. Do not mistake short-lived nutritional rescue for persistent symbiosis.
- Authors explicitly suggest that trace nutrients carried in inocula could explain survival improvements without infection. This is a competing mechanism, not a demonstrated one.
- PCR positivity at second instar and PCR at adulthood are measured on different sampled insects, and adults are survivor-conditioned. These are not longitudinal measures of a single individual's persistent infection. Egg sterilization, imposed inoculation and controlled rearing are substantial researcher scaffolding.
- Species are distinct host backgrounds, **not eight independent experimental-evolution origins**. The study's comparison does not establish autonomous vertical transmission of newly formed associations or multi-generation viability of the artificial symbiosis without repeated human inoculation.

## Causal interpretation

The contrast supports **early functional host specificity** of a reconstructed ΔcyaA mutant in the *P. stali* system and broader functional host range of Sym C. It does **not** demonstrate that the *host* evolved a new filter, that the mutant independently evolved in this 2024 assay, that broad host range arose later than narrow host range, or that a combined host–symbiont lineage independently reproduced. Possible alternatives include pre-existing host anatomical compatibility, bacterial phylogenetic distance, dose and inoculation conditions, host-local adaptation, transient nutritional rescue, and differential growth after entry. The authors themselves call the reason for *P. stali*–*E. coli* compatibility unresolved.

The key 2022 precursor is Koga et al., *Single mutation makes Escherichia coli an insect mutualist*, *Nature Microbiology* 7:1141–1150 (2022), referenced as #26 in the 2024 article. It shares investigators and the *P. stali* experimental system. **Count this as one dependent research program, not independent confirmation** of the evolutionary origin. A future primary review must separately audit ancestral genotypes, selection lineages, passage intervention, fecundity and independent reproduction.

## Falsifiable future-only tests (no authorization to execute)

1. **Compatibility-before-evolution null:** pre-existing host physiology and bacterial survival differences explain the host-range pattern; reject only with matched ancestor/mutant exposure and direct within-host measurements across multiple independent host backgrounds.
2. **Functional rescue vs persistent partnership:** developmental improvement can occur without sustained infection or descendant transmission; reject only with time-resolved colonization and independently reproduced descendant function, while removing repeated researcher inoculation.
3. **Host-range trade-off:** gain of mutualistic function in one host may coincide with reduced ability to colonize others; reject only with matched ancestral and independently evolved descendant lines across hosts and lifetime reproductive fitness measurements, not adult emergence alone.
