## What actually evolved?

The team replaced the essential gut symbiont *Pantoea* in laboratory-reared, inbred *Plautia stali* with initially non-mutualistic *E. coli*. Ordinary *E. coli* produced only approximately 5–10% adult emergence, compared with over 70% for native *Pantoea* and no adult emergence in aposymbiotic controls. Some rare adults carried *E. coli* in the host's existing specialized gut crypts, deposited bacteria on egg surfaces, and could transmit bacteria to offspring, but infection density and transmission were unstable. **Thus initial association and limited vertical transmission predated the improved evolved mutants.**

The evolution experiment used a deliberately hypermutating ΔmutS strain and a ΔintS non-hypermutating control. Authors report **12 hypermutator colour-selection lines, 7 hypermutator growth-selection lines**, and **11 + 7 corresponding control lines**. A host from a maintained inbred population was supplied each generation, rather than tracking coevolution of independent host lineages. Selection was imposed by investigators: choose either the earliest adult female or the greenest adult female; dissect her gut symbiotic organ; homogenize bacteria; orally inoculate a new group of surface-sterilized hatchlings; freeze stocks. This **experimenter-mediated passage is not autonomous transmission**.

Improvement was heterogeneous. The colour-selected line CmL05 improved abruptly around generation 7; growth-selected GmL07 around generation 2. Other lines did not improve; authors report frequent stochastic extinctions and restarts from frozen bacterial stocks, with lines terminated when recovery failed twice. Later generations focused on selected lines. The result therefore supports multiple bacterial evolutionary trajectories under imposed selection, **not survival of every independent replicate**.

The successful lines had slower culture growth, smaller cells, reduced flagellar motility and extracellular matrix, and altered global transcription. Genomic comparison implicated independent changes in `cyaA` and `crp` affecting carbon-catabolite repression (CCR). Targeted reconstruction of a ΔcyaA knockout or the crp substitution in the control genetic background recapitulated improved adult emergence and green body colour; a crp revertant returned toward poor-control phenotype. These are meaningful causal perturbations **of the host-beneficial phenotype**. Reconstructed mutants did not necessarily match the evolved lines' within-host infection titres, so CCR disruption does not explain all traits.

## Critical distinctions and negative cases

1. **Independent bacterial lines ≠ independent host–symbiont organizations.** The 19 hypermutator lines are laboratory selection replicates within one host background; they are not 19 independent species-level origins or continuously self-reproducing combined lineages.
2. **Limited natural egg-surface transfer ≠ autonomous serial passage of the evolved lines.** Some original E. coli was observed on eggs and transmitted, but the serial experimental evolution used dissected maternal organs and deliberate researcher inoculation each generation. No sustained, unassisted lineage-reproduction assay of the improved mutants was established by that selection procedure.
3. **Adult emergence and colour ≠ full fitness.** Rare initial insects could mate and lay a few eggs, and some bacterial transfer was observed; the principal selection and confirmatory readouts are adult emergence, time, body colour and bacterial titres. Neither full host lifetime fecundity nor durable joint-lineage growth rate across unassisted generations is established by those measurements.
4. **Causal gene reconstruction ≠ spontaneous emergence in a natural setting.** ΔmutS artificially elevated mutation rate and investigators selected phenotypes; independently arising `cyaA`/`crp` changes demonstrate evolutionary accessibility in this engineered setting, not an unassisted natural evolutionary rate.
5. **Alternative mechanism:** CCR loss may avoid costly expression of unnecessary metabolic pathways in nutrient-limited gut crypts, but the original *Pantoea* symbiont retains CCR genes; the authors label their broader evolutionary explanation speculative. Other paths and host-specific physiological compatibility remain plausible.
6. **Study independence:** [Sugiyama et al. 2024](116-pass24-sugiyama-2024-host-range-full-review.md) uses a reconstructed ΔcyaA mutant and overlaps investigators, host and scientific program. It is a downstream host-range comparison, **not an independent replication of the 2022 evolutionary origin**.

## Falsifiable, future-only lineage hypotheses

- **P25-H1, scaffold-dependence:** improved bacterial mutualism under manual passage does not guarantee a viable combined lineage without manual inoculation. Reject only if multiple independently derived evolved bacterial lines support sustained offspring infection, development and reproduction across generations **without manual transfer, rescue or restarts**, relative to matched ancestral/control lines.
- **P25-H2, CCR trade-off:** CCR disruption may improve within-host function while reducing survival/competitiveness outside the host. Reject only with independent, controlled host/external-environment fitness measures across reconstructed mutants, revertants and evolved backgrounds; simple slower growth in rich broth is not a complete ecological cost assay.
- **P25-H3, fitness decoupling:** host adult emergence and colour can improve while lifetime host reproduction or symbiont transmission remains poor. Reject only with longitudinal lifetime fecundity, daughter acquisition and persistence measured together and nested at the independent-lineage level.

## Research conclusion and next step

**Supported:** Experimentally evolved bacterial mutualism under strong laboratory scaffolding, convergent disruptions in a global regulator, and targeted causal reconstruction of host-beneficial phenotypes. **Not demonstrated:** independently reproducing new host–symbiont organization, open-ended adaptive intelligence, consciousness, or autonomous survival of evolved joint lineages after withdrawing experimenter interventions. The positive results are real and valuable, but should not be conflated with a fully new level of individuality.

Next high-information documentary question: among primary published systems, has a *newly evolved* symbiotic relationship been tracked through **unassisted multigenerational host reproduction** with measured descendant acquisition, functional benefit, and independent failed/successful lines? Prioritize studies with intervention-withdrawal controls and avoid treating Koga 2022 and Sugiyama 2024 as independent demonstrations. No organisms, simulators, experimental worlds, AI keys, or other repositories were used or changed.
