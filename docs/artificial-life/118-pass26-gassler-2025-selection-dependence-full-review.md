# Pass 26 — Gassler et al. (2025): inherited intracellular association under maintained sorting is not independent lineage autonomy

**Research-only full-main-primary review (E2 reading depth, not E3 reproduction), 2026-10-08.** Gassler, Giger, Sintsova et al., *Induced endosymbiosis between a fungus and bacterium reveals a shift from antagonism to commensalism*, *Nature Communications* **16**, 10717 (2025), published **28 November 2025**. [Primary article and full Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-65741-9) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-65741-9). Main Results, Methods, discussion, figures and reported data availability were read via original publisher HTML, including the independent-injection and FACS passage methods. The separate supplementary files, deposited Zenodo FACS/images, ENA RNA reads, source code, images and peer-review files were **not downloaded, executed or independently reanalysed**. The article is **CC BY 4.0**, subject to third-party credit exceptions; linked/paraphrased only, not mirrored.

## Why this fills the Pass 25 gap

The outstanding question after the Koga 2022 (pending [PR #5](https://github.com/JeremyHennessy/AI-Research/pull/5)) evolutionary study is whether a newly functional partnership **reconstructs itself in descendants when externally selecting its reproductive propagules stops**. This study provides a stringent adjacent test: a *free-living* bacterium was placed inside a formerly bacteria-free fungus, transmits into a minority of spores, and adapts under sustained host–partner co-propagation. Critically, it does **not** demonstrate unassisted reproduction once selection is removed.

The authors introduced fluorescently labeled *Ralstonia pickettii* K-288 into the cytoplasm of *Rhizopus microsporus* NH. Ordinary extracellular exposure and a reinfection assay did not establish the new bacterial infection; physical injection **bypassed the existing entry barrier**. Fungus naturally produces spores, so the paper tests whether its **pre-existing** spore system can carry bacterial descendants, not the evolutionary birth of an original reproductive apparatus.

## Reproductive evidence, denominators and negative results

- **Independent entry events:** three independent injections yielded only **0.009%, 0.9% and 1.2%** fluorescent bacteria-positive spores. Bacteria were seen in sorted spores and observed growing after spore germination. Positive spore counts are not proof of partner benefit or independent reproductive closure.
- **Early host costs:** bacteria-positive spore germination following the initial injection was **3.8%, 11.1% and 2.8%** (three founders), versus **52 ± 6.5%** for the non-injected fungus. Initial association was mostly deleterious.
- **Continuous selected propagation:** three independent injection founders each produced two separated bacterial-load selection arms, B+ and B++; **six selected lines are not six independent origins**. All six arms were followed through **five rounds**, but only the **two arms of the third injection** continued to **20 rounds**. Researchers sorted and plated **50,000–100,000 bacteria-positive spores per round**, using FACS to enrich a relatively rare state. The study did **not** report a multigeneration, unsorted selection-withdrawal lineage comparison analogous to Giger 2024.
- **Function and trade-offs:** positive-spore germination improved under selection. However, the B+ **lower-load** arm performed better than B++ (higher load), and fungal growth slowed in late selected samples. This favors an emerging **tolerance of bacterial burden**, not automatically reciprocal mutualistic benefit. The paper's "fitness index" is the product of positive-spore fraction and germination success; it is not observed lifetime daughter-lineage growth.
- **Short starvation is not lineage reproduction:** a separate round-2/round-20 assay stored single spores in nutrient-free buffer for up to **28 days**, after which **fresh medium** was added to test germination. At day 28, only **1/288** late R20 B+ spores germinated, compared with **93/288** late R20 bacteria-negative spores (figure caption denominators). The authors report bacteria visible in **over 65% of the late-line germlings** across sampled starvation times, but this is **conditional on the few spores that germinate**, not 65% population-level reproductive survival. No new generation passed autonomously in buffer.
- **Defensive expression:** late samples had attenuated cell-wall/ROS response relative to earlier selected samples; however, only **one injection founder** supplied R10 and R20 expression comparisons (five pooled biological replicates per condition from ten germlings each). These nested germlings are not independent origins. No mutation/revertant reconstruction established a particular genetic mechanism; selection history, host tolerance, altered bacterial burden and physiological carryover remain alternatives.
- **Unresolved reporting check:** The Results describe "**384 individually tracked spores, 114 started to germinate**", yet Fig. 3c's stated counts for the four conditions are **138/151, 19/49, 74/92 and 80/92** (total 311/384). These ostensibly matching denominators yield incompatible totals; perhaps distinct inclusion/exclusion stages are unstated. **Leave discrepancy unresolved pending original deposited image/analysis data**, rather than silently correcting or conflating the two reports.
- **Controls for origin:** bacteria-negative spores can also suffer reduced germination after an infected parent, a potential noninfectious physiological carryover. Retention can reflect selection of rare pre-existing fungal tolerance, bacterial adaptation, or survival conditioning; the longitudinal expression signature does not resolve these mechanisms by itself.

## Scientific independence and history preservation

This work uses a different initial free-living bacterium than [Giger et al. 2024](49-pass11-induced-endosymbiosis-2024-full-review.md), but **overlapping authors, the same fungal host platform and FluidFM/FACS selection program**. The 2024 study documented a complementary **negative**: after experimentally withdrawing FACS-positive sorting, even evolved associations lost bacterial-positive propagation within four to five rounds. The 2025 study did *not* repeat that key washout for its new pairing. Together these represent connected methodological development, **not independent laboratory replication of autonomous endosymbiosis**.

An independent *other-host* comparison is Sørensen et al. (2021), *Rapid compensatory evolution can rescue low fitness symbioses following partner switching*, [DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2021.06.034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.06.034). From the publisher abstract/PubMed only (**E1 access in this pass**), a partner-switched *Paramecium–Chlorella* association adapted in fewer than 50 host generations via alternative host- versus symbiont-sided compensation. This begins with **already symbiotic partners**; its abstract does not establish unassisted reproduction of a de novo origin. An author's accepted manuscript is openly deposited under **CC BY-NC-ND**; it was not copied or modified.

## Falsifiable, future-only hypotheses (not authorized to implement)

- **P26-H1 — sorting dependence:** an increased positive-spore fraction under FACS does not predict stable unselected daughter-lineage co-transmission. Falsify *for a given system* only with independently initiated founder lines maintained for multiple new host generations **without selecting, implanting, rescuing or dosing partner-positive propagules**, including all extinctions and population denominators.
- **P26-H2 — tolerance without reciprocity:** higher bacterial load tolerance can evolve without a net host reproductive benefit. Falsify with paired ancestor/evolved **lifetime descendant reproduction** assays and fitness of the bacterium with and without the fungus; do not equate germination or partner persistence with mutualism.
- **P26-H3 — heritable control mechanism:** altered host transcriptomes need not imply a novel genetic or inherited control function. Falsify non-genetic/carryover alternatives only by independent host/bacterial lineages, genotype tracing and causal reconstruction or equivalent stringent evidence.
- **P26-H4 — survival-conditioning artifact:** conditional detection in survivors may conceal extinction at the whole-population level. Falsify with joint unconditional probabilities of germination **and** bacterial-positive functional descendants under matched environmental constraints; nested spore/plate units must not be counted as independent evolutionary replicates.

## Verdict and next question

**Supported by primary main text:** investigator-induced intracellular colonization, occasional vertical spore carriage, improved tolerance under **continual** FACS selection, persistent growth costs and transcriptional changes; independent injection replicate outcomes were described for early rounds. **Not shown:** natural entry, selection-withdrawn multigeneration lineage continuity for *R. pickettii*, endogenously maintained novel reproductive machinery, demonstrated reciprocal lifetime fitness, independent second-lab replication, open-ended intelligence or consciousness.

**Next genuinely unanswered primary question:** Does a system with *newly evolved* cross-partner functionality achieve **multiple unassisted host generations with quantified combined reproduction** under intervention withdrawal? Prioritize studies with explicit washout, independent founder-origin provenance, loss cases and resource reversals, not more selected-passage positive images. The older 2024 washout and the new 2025 starvation assay test **different properties**.
