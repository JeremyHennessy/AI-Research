# Pass 27 — Historical symbiont acquisitions versus autonomous reproduction

**Research-only literature review, 2026-10-08.** Evidence about repeated phylogenetic acquisitions does not directly demonstrate independent founder-to-descendant reproduction. No experiments or simulations were performed.

## Evidence and counterevidence

**Boyd 2024:** Initial data included 61 *Columbicola* samples and seven from other feather-louse genera. Gene-inventory comparisons covered 36 Sodalis-allied symbionts, excluding two for a 34-lineage comparison. These are **not 34 directly observed acquisition events**. Analyses identified 298 universally intact genes and 887 variably retained genes; B-vitamin pathway retention with many amino-acid pathway losses supports a nutritional-complementarity interpretation. Three independently assembled metagenomes provided a limited cross-check of mapping-based gene calls. No measured vitamin transfer, host fitness assay across all lineages, or unassisted new partnership reproduction was demonstrated by these comparative data. Public data/code identifiers: PRJNA296666; Figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.21830067; Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.10946531. Not downloaded or reproduced.

**Soto-Patiño 2025:** Authors describe 1,020 feather-louse genomes across 140 genera and 228 qualifying Sodalis detections (22.35%) across 57 genera; 14 additional 16S-positive candidates failed quality thresholds. These are genomic detections, **not** independent observed founders, natural establishment rates or serial reproductive lineages. Tree incongruence supports historical replacement/acquisition but does not identify all transmission routes.

**Topchyan 2026:** The indexed abstract describes a non-Sodalis *Enterobacter* louse symbiont with a reduced genome and predicted retained B-vitamin synthesis. Taxonomic divergence and predicted metabolic convergence do not establish a new host trait, direct metabolite transfer or autonomous reproductive closure.

**Negative and alternative interpretations:** Extant survivors bias sampling against failed associations; failed-founder denominators are unknown. Discordant host/symbiont trees may reflect replacement or reacquisition. Reference mapping may miss divergent genes. Predicted metabolic pathways are not measured host benefits. Boyd and Soto-Patiño share Johnson, Bush, Clayton and Dale; Topchyan overlaps the same research community. Full sample-accession overlap is unverified: do not sum cohorts or call them wholly independent experiments.

## Falsifiable future-only hypotheses

- **P27-H1 founder/descendant gap:** initial host benefit does not ensure unassisted descendant transmission. A counterexample requires multiple documented independent founders, complete failures, daughter acquisition and lifetime reproduction over predeclared generations without manual passage, selection or rescue.
- **P27-H2 predicted genes versus function:** retained vitamin pathway genes need not confer measured host benefits. Counterevidence requires direct metabolite transfer and controlled host fitness/rescue.
- **P27-H3 contingency versus robustness:** divergent retained respiratory/DNA-repair modules could cause different perturbation robustness despite similar predicted nutritional function; evaluate with phylogenetic controls, blinded independent lines and all failures reported.
