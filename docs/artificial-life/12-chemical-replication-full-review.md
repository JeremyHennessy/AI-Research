## What the model assumes before it can show replication
1. **Well mixed soup:** molecules interact without spatial compartments or endogenous boundary formation.
2. **Mass-only identities:** species are integers representing molecular mass; isomers and different chemical substances of the same mass are collapsed.
3. **Allowed reaction templates:** `i + j ↔ (i+j)`; this enforces stoichiometric mass conservation but admits transformations that may be chemically impossible in the real world.
4. **Thermodynamic ranking:** each species has assigned standard Gibbs energy of formation `G°_i`, and each reaction pair receives an activation-barrier parameter; physically impossible collections of mutually spontaneous cycles are screened out.
5. **Simplified kinetics:** ideal gas, constant pressure and temperature, elementary mass-action reactions.
6. **Externally replenished substrate:** a chosen resource molecule is kept at fixed population `Q` by adding/removing it against an **unlimited reservoir**. The resource-maintenance mechanism is **imposed by the investigator**, not generated by an organism.
7. **Numerics:** Gillespie stochastic reaction simulation and corresponding mean-field ODE trajectories.

**Important result:** the authors do not require pre-existing enzymes/catalysts as primitives, but select low/high reaction barriers and resource supply. Therefore "spontaneously arises" applies **within a specified artificial-chemistry universe**, not an unconstrained physical origin-of-life history.

### Rate-law engineering detail (restated from the public paper, not executable code)
For synthesis `i + j → i+j`, the propensity scales with
`β·exp(−κ ΔG‡_+ij)·N_i N_j/(S+N)`.
For decomposition `i+j → i + j`, it scales with
`β·exp(−κ ΔG‡_-ij)·N_{i+j}`.
Here `S` is solvent population, `N` the non-solvent population, and `β, κ` arise from transition-state assumptions. The authors use (in a worked example) **T=298.15 K**, pressure **100 kPa**, **β≈6.21×10¹² s⁻¹**, **κ≈0.403 mol/kJ**; a resource count `Q=1000` is held constant by the reservoir in an example. These are illustrative *reported experimental settings*, not universal chemical constants or a validated digital-life recipe.

## Three categories that should never be conflated
- **Self-driven reaction set:** every low-barrier reaction uses at least one reactant produced by other low-barrier reactions. This network-topological property is **not** sufficient for sustainable catalysis or replication.
- **Collectively catalytic:** the network recycles intermediates while continuously converting external feedstock. Authors give a sufficient criterion of *balanced cancelling* of intermediate stoichiometry across low-barrier reactions, with self-driven condition.
- **Self-replicating:** at least one intermediate species is net amplified; sufficient conditions include self-driven, *overproduction* of at least one intermediate, and *no-overintake* across intermediates. The criteria are sufficient but **not necessary**. Multiplication of molecule count is still not proof of a bounded autonomous organism.

## Results and denominators
For **maximum integer molecule size L=6**, authors enumerate **16,825 physically possible artificial chemistries**, of which **6,886 (≈41%)** contain a *self-driven* reaction system. The table finds **21** with sufficient collectively catalytic subsets and **74** satisfying sufficient self-replicating criteria—a **lower bound of 74/16,825 ≈ 0.44%** for such reaction-network patterns.

| Measure, L=6 | Paper-reported count | Correct interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Physically possible artificial chemistries | 16,825 | Enumeration after authors' thermodynamic consistency rules |
| Containing self-driven systems | 6,886 (~41%) | **Cannot infer self-replication** |
| Minimum containing collectively catalytic systems | 21 (0.12%) | Sufficient test gives **lower bound**, not actual prevalence |
| Minimum containing self-replicating systems | 74 (0.44%) | **Lower bound** for this finite model family, not empirical abiogenesis frequency |

As L increases from 4 to 7 the absolute lower-bound counts rise, while proportions may decrease; the true prevalence cannot be inferred without checking dynamic systems that fail the sufficient structural test. **Do not misquote the 41% self-driven fraction as 41% replicators.**

### Side reactions expose a meaningful failure mechanism
The authors' formose-like toy cycle creates additional intermediate from a constant feedstock; another competing side reaction can consume the required intermediate. With its rate parameter `η` relative to reference cycle rate `ω`, **η < 1** delays but permits replication in the studied case, while **η ≥ 1** inhibits it. This threshold is **for the authors' particular reaction network**, not a universal criterion for all chemistries.

### Multi-stage innovations are not proven open-ended
A high-barrier event creates a seed intermediate; a first autocatalytic cycle amplifies it; later low-probability reactions can trigger another cycle and increase molecular mass. The paper's worked construction demonstrates **staged molecular-complexity transitions in selected networks**, not indefinitely increasing functional complexity, heritable organism individuality, adaptation, or general intelligence.

## Where this evidence is strongest—and where it breaks
**Good evidence (within the specified toy chemistry):** dynamically emergent reaction-network self-replication, competing side-reaction sensitivities, role of energy barriers, stoichiometric criteria, and dependency on feedstock.

**Not demonstrated:** spontaneous living compartments, local boundary repair, Darwinian inheritance of novel metabolic *organization* between daughter entities, formation of sensors or memory, unlimited evolutionary innovation, real Earth abiogenesis likelihood, or digital subjective experience.

**Crucial design confounds:** infinite externally replenished resource supply, ideal gas, uniform mixing, only mass-labelled species, arbitrarily chosen barrier distributions, absence of spatial biochemistry, finite `L`, no experimental organism identity. The paper itself notes absence of real isomer constraints, chemical transformations and spatial organization.

**Licensing:** paper marked CC BY on PMC. The GitHub repository contains a MATLAB ZIP with no repository-root LICENSE evident on inspection; do **not** infer permitted redistribution or training rights for the code from paper's license.

## Falsifiable next-stage R1A experiment (planning only)
**Question:** does a reaction system *actively preserve its own organizational structure* with **finite** resource input and decay, or merely amplify components while feedstock is externally constant?

**Independent variables:**
1. Infinite fixed-concentration reservoir vs **finite-resource/flow with independently measured inflow/outflow**.
2. Intact catalytic/repair feedback vs knocked-out critical reaction.
3. Well mixed vs spatial compartment whose boundary constituents must be produced by its own chemistry.

**Controls:** nonreplicating self-driven systems (important!), inert equilibrium, network with same stoichiometric resources but broken catalytic loop, resource-only externally reset control.

**End points:** component repair after damage, resource balance, viable daughter network transfer, intergenerational functional fidelity and per-world extinction. Use preregistered independent random chemistries to prevent hand-selecting a success; report failed attempts and severe sensitivity.

**Falsifier:** apparent maintenance disappears after removing externally imposed reservoir/compartment reset, or descendants lack heritable organization. **No organism is implemented by writing this plan.**
