# Pass 28 — Reproductive transmission, host benefit, and packaging are separate gates

**Research-only primary-text review, 2026-10-08.** No organisms, simulations, model training, or independent reproduction. Two 2026 experimental studies give complementary *negative* controls to the inference that colonization or short-term transmission creates a self-maintaining beneficial lineage.

## Primary source A: experimentally introduced symbiont transmits but harms its host

Krüsemer, Carvalho, Keller et al. (2026), *Experimental Sodalis infection eliminates ancient insect symbiont*, *Nature Communications* 17:3153, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71143-2), [full primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71143-2).

**Review scope:** public main article abstract, Results, Discussion, Methods, figure captions, data availability and rights examined. E2 **main-text reading**, not independent experimental reproduction; supplementary PDFs, source-data spreadsheet, code and raw transcriptomics not downloaded/reanalysed. Main article CC BY 4.0 with third-party exceptions.

Investigators introduced *Sodalis praecaptivus* into female *Oryzaephilus surinamensis*, which already carries the tyrosine-precursor symbiont *Shikimatogenerans silvanidophilus*. The novel bacterium colonized host tissues and ovaries. Reported transovarial maternal transmission to F1 larvae was **93.75%**; infected beetles were maintained by mating through the **third offspring generation**, when the remaining individuals were sampled. No paternal transmission was observed in the stated mating controls. **Founder acquisition was experimenter-initiated by injection; subsequent offspring transfer was not a repeated injection.** Do not conflate this with a de novo natural origin.

The host-fitness sign was adverse: over an 80-day observation period, control survival was **80%** while nearly all Sodalis-infected beetles died; more than half the infected beetles had died by day 50. Infected females produced significantly fewer larvae. F1 adult melanization was lower, although measured cuticle thickness did not significantly differ. The native symbiont was absent in **all six infected third-generation specimens examined** (five adults, one pupa), while controls retained it. This is a small sampled endpoint, not proof that every descendant or future lineage must lose the native symbiont.

**Interpretation:** The study experimentally supports initial colonization, efficient short-horizon maternal transmission and displacement of an existing beneficial symbiont, while directly refuting *net host benefit* in the tested conditions. The authors explicitly describe meeting **three of four** prerequisites for functional replacement, not the fourth. Short-term persistence is not proof of indefinite reproductive autonomy or a beneficial new combined individual.

**Alternative explanations and limits:** Injection/wounding and host immune response may contribute to costs; saline-injected controls address handling but do not isolate every microbial mechanism. High transmission conditional on surviving mothers does not establish positive joint-lineage growth or successful long-run replacement. Shared experimental founders/offspring are nested, not independently evolved lineages. No prospective selection-withdrawal experiment showing virulence attenuation or acquired nutritional function was performed.

## Primary source B: host benefit and adult colonization do not guarantee daughter acquisition

Pons, García-Lozano, Emmerich et al. (2026), *Fidelity in co-diversified symbiosis*, *Nature Communications* 17:1644, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69366-4), [full primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69366-4).

**Review scope:** public main article abstract, Results/discussion, Methods, figure captions, data/rights statements examined. E2 **main-text reading**; supplements, microscopy source data, transcriptomics and raw data not independently reanalysed. Publisher article open access; confirm reuse terms separately for each supplemental/third-party item before redistribution.

The authors exchanged egg-associated *Stammera capleta* spheres between tortoise beetle species using experimental caplet removal and reapplication. Non-native symbionts could colonize embryos, larvae, adult foregut symbiotic organs and even maternal ovary-associated glands. Host developmental rescue depended on the genetic distance between native and exchanged symbionts. Yet the non-native *S. capleta* did **not** embed in the egg-associated spheres; diagnostic PCR found no symbiont in **eight sampled non-native caplets**, and tested larvae emerging from these eggs were aposymbiotic. In the reported comparison, re-infected native controls retained successful daughter transmission (Fig. 6G: n=48 larvae native versus n=30 non-native; these are larvae, not independent founder lineages).

**Interpretation:** Functional colonization, persistence through metamorphosis, maternal-gland entry and even partial developmental rescue can coexist with a **complete observed failure at the reproductive packaging boundary**. The underlying transmission mechanism is not yet established: gland morphological remodeling and mismatch in extracellular matrix properties are possible explanations, not proven causes. Experimental exchange between already evolved species is not a spontaneous new origin or a long-term evolving new partnership.

**Study independence:** This is a separate beetle system and author team from Krüsemer et al. 2026, but is not an independent replication of that study's intervention. Pons 2026 and García-Lozano et al. 2026 (Yellow protein protective matrix) overlap researchers and the same ancient *Stammera* program; count the latter as a mechanistic follow-up, not an independent new-origin experiment.

## Cross-study causal distinctions and future-only falsifiers

| Gate | Krüsemer 2026 | Pons 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduced partner colonizes | Yes after researcher injection | Yes after researcher caplet exchange |
| Benefit to host | Negative in measured conditions | Context-dependent developmental rescue |
| Maternal transmission | High over observed generations | Non-native fails despite adult gland colonization |
| Existing symbiont displaced | Observed in sampled F3 infected beetles | Native replaced experimentally at embryo stage |
| Independently evolved, indefinitely self-maintaining beneficial joint lineage | **Not demonstrated** | **Not demonstrated** |

- **P28-H1 transmission–fitness decoupling:** transmission efficiency and combined-lineage population growth can vary independently. Reject a strict decoupling prediction only with preregistered longitudinal estimates of transmission, maternal survival, fecundity and descendant lineage growth across independent founder lines and explicit failures.
- **P28-H2 packaging bottleneck:** adult colonization and maternal-gland occupancy do not guarantee offspring infection; predict that native extracellular matrix embedding/viability is an independently necessary stage in the *Stammera* system. Discriminate material incompatibility from gland architecture or host recognition with separately approved, controlled assays.
- **P28-H3 replacement-without-function:** an invader can eliminate a beneficial incumbent before evolving compensatory function. Distinguish displacement from beneficial replacement using host development, reproductive success and directly measured transferred metabolites across descendants after all researcher transfer/rescue ends.

**Evidence boundaries:** These are *author-reported biological experiments*, not artificial-life experiments performed here. Neither paper establishes general intelligence, consciousness, open-ended evolution or independent digital reproduction. Their experimental protocols, species, control cohorts and measurements differ; never pool survival/transmission rates as a common sample. The next documentary priority is to find intervention-withdrawal studies that jointly measure daughter transmission, lifetime fecundity and beneficial function, including failed founders, over multiple independent generations.
