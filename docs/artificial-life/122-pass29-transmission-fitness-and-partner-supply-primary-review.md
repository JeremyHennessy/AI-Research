# Pass 29 — Descendant transmission, host fitness and external partner supply

**2026-10-09 | Research-only primary-source critical review.** The [unified Passes 1–28 roadmap](UNIFIED-RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS.md) identified a missing joint demonstration: newly established beneficial partnerships must transmit through independently viable descendants without manual reassembly or repeated partner supply. This pass checks two independent biological systems and one explicitly partial comparator. **No organism, experiment, simulation, code from the papers, or independent reproduction was performed.**

## Source A: Russell & Moran (2005) — successful multigeneration transmission can be costly

**Original:** Russell, J. A. & Moran, N. A. *Horizontal Transfer of Bacterial Symbionts: Heritability and Fitness Effects in a Novel Aphid Host*, *Applied and Environmental Microbiology* 71:7987–7994. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.71.12.7987-7994.2005) · [publisher primary full text](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/aem.71.12.7987-7994.2005). **E2 main-text review:** original Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Tables 1–2 and captions examined; deposited raw data, code and independent statistical reproduction **not** inspected. Publisher rights/2005 article license for redistribution **not established**; original analytical paraphrase only, no paper/images/data copied.

**Design and nesting.** Researchers used hemolymph microinjection to move preexisting symbionts from other aphid species into *Acyrthosiphon pisum*. Multiple donor and recipient clones were examined for transmission, but the principal fitness comparison used a single isogenic recipient background (clone 5A), with infected lines descended from selected founders. Repeated offspring, mothers, and generations are **nested**, not separately evolved symbiont origins. Infection was assessed by PCR, not direct metabolite flux or whole-lineage fitness. Fitness experiments began at least 10 generations after injection to reduce direct injection-wound effects.

**Key positive and negative outcomes.** Native *Regiella* and novel *Arsenophonus* showed efficient F2/F3 transmission among selected successfully transmitting lines; the authors report maintaining individual infections for more than 20 generations. Among five *Arsenophonus* lineages from four injected founders, two showed incomplete transmission between F2 and F3. By contrast, transferred *Regiella* from *Macrosiphum euphorbiae* was almost absent after F1: among 39 F2–F5 aphids from lineages descended from 23 injected females, only one F3 aphid tested positive; no positives beyond F2 in additional pooled samples. This is **negative evidence** for reliable cross-host establishment, not proof that no possible host or inoculum could work. The authors explicitly raise low inoculum titer as a competing explanation.

**Fitness is not transmission.** The novel *Arsenophonus* infection reduced aphid mean relative growth rate by **9–13%** versus comparison lines, while adult survival was similar in the tested condition. A transferred *Hamiltonella defensa* from *Aphis craccivora* did not significantly alter the measured growth-rate endpoint but had a survival advantage at 25°C (54/56 adults versus 45/55 uninfected and 44/53 native-*Hamiltonella* controls). Those comparisons were conducted in a laboratory host background, not a multi-founder field population. **Transmission across generations is demonstrated for some artificial introductions, but a universally net-beneficial, naturally originated joint lineage is not.**

**Critical boundary:** No repeated injection was required for the observed descendant infections, which is a stronger positive transmission result than the manually serially inoculated Koga 2022 selection experiment. Yet the bacteria already possessed heritable infection machinery; the researchers did not observe its origin, evolution of a new reproductive interface, or an independent population-level benefit after ecological competition. The original authors did not measure every founder's lifetime net reproductive growth.

## Source B: Hoang, Gerardo & Morran (2021) — host fitness evolves under recurring external partner supply

**Original:** Hoang, K. L., Gerardo, N. M. & Morran, L. T. *Association with a novel protective microbe facilitates host adaptation to a stressful environment*, *Evolution Letters* 5:118–129. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/evl3.223) · [publisher full original](https://academic.oup.com/evlett/article/5/2/118/6697547). **E2 main-text review:** Methods, Results, Discussion, figures and licensing examined. Original article **CC BY 4.0**; supporting information and raw data not independently audited or reproduced.

**Design and independent units.** Researchers started with **one pooled, mutagenized, genetically variable *C. elegans* ancestor** and five replicate populations per treatment (four treatment combinations, 20 populations). Worms were evolved for 20 *selection* generations, corresponding to **40 total nematode generations** because heat shock occurred every other generation. Bacterial food was repeatedly supplied on plates: ancestral, nonevolving *Bacillus subtilis* 168 versus *E. coli* OP50; hosts recovered on GFP-labeled *E. coli*. Host populations were frozen/thawed midcourse. **These are 20 host evolution populations, not 20 independent origins of host–microbe transmission or coevolution.**

**Evidence:** After 20 selection generations, heat-stressed hosts evolved alongside protective *B. subtilis* had greater fecundity under heat shock when assayed on *B. subtilis* than comparison evolved treatments (reported B+H+ vs B+H– P=0.002; vs B–H+ P=0.0006). When assayed on *E. coli*, hosts evolved under heat stress showed gains but no additional *B. subtilis*-specific evolution effect (reported interaction P=0.90). Without heat shock, evolved treatments did **not** show a significant reproductive advantage from their different evolutionary histories; on average, worms produced more offspring on *E. coli*. Higher within-host *B. subtilis* CFU and colonization frequency accompanied the heat-stress benefit (population-level fecundity–CFU association reported R²=0.34), but this correlation does not uniquely identify an evolved host recognition mechanism.

**Dispersal ≠ lineage inheritance.** After introducing stressed worms onto unseeded plates, bacterial growth spread; three of five plates had several hundred larvae after about two host generations. This is a finite **dispersal and local ecological persistence** observation. It is not a demonstrated transovarial/vertical partner lineage or a multi-generation independent host–partner reproduction experiment. The authors themselves note ambiguity between bacteria acting as food and a distinct protective partner. The proposed host molecular mechanism is not directly established.

## Comparator C: Guan et al. (2013) — intracellular infection is an accessible trait, not complete closure

Guan, Gris, Cruveiller et al., *Experimental evolution of nodule intracellular infection in legume symbionts*, *ISME Journal* 7:1367–1377. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/ismej.2013.24). **E1 publisher abstract only in this pass:** the publisher exposed abstract and figure placeholders but blocked full methods/results. The authors report three derived lineages from an already engineered nodulating *Ralstonia* ancestor, each gaining intracellular nodule infection after investigator-mediated serial legume–bacterium coculture. Mutations affected virulence regulators. This supports an **author-reported transition in intracellular accommodation under imposed selection**, not proof of spontaneous new symbiosis, independent reproduction of a combined plant–bacterium lineage or nitrogen-fixation fitness under withdrawal. The 2010 engineering precursor and 2013 follow-up share a scientific program; no independence inflation. Publisher permissions and supplementary rights unverified. **Do not upgrade this E1 comparison to E2** without accessible complete text.

### Future-only falsifiable research hypotheses

- **P29-H1 — partner inheritance and benefit are independently limiting:** after new host transfer, high maternal transmission does not predict positive net host-lineage growth. Reject if multiple independent, fully enumerated founders show positive lifetime reproductive fitness consistently tracked with daughter infection and matched native/uninfected controls.
- **P29-H2 — environmental resupply can mimic lineage autonomy:** host adaptation under repeated microbial feeding may disappear when external partner supply is withheld. Reject if independently founded daughter populations preserve partner-dependent function and reproduction through prespecified generations **without** environmental re-inoculation, sorting or rescue.
- **P29-H3 — colonization versus reproductive packaging:** acquisition of within-host occupancy need not generate a heritable transmission interface. Reject only with lineage-resolved emergence of a parent-produced transfer mechanism, causal ablation/rescue and independently viable offspring.
