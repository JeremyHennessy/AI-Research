# Pass 30 — Cooperation and productivity do not establish reproductive closure

**2026-10-09 · Research-only critical literature review.** Starting main: `d6e68cffaf79067329220e86891bb44d59bac9fc`. This pass asks whether experimentally evolved interspecies cooperation and community productivity establish a self-maintaining *reproducing organization*, rather than an interaction maintained by investigator-defined medium, spatial conditions and transfers. **No organisms, experiments, simulations, model training or independent reproductions were performed.**

## Source A — Harcombe (2010): cooperation evolves but depends on reciprocity and structure

**Primary:** William Harcombe, *Novel Cooperation Experimentally Evolved Between Species*, *Evolution* 64:2166–2172, DOI [10.1111/j.1558-5646.2010.00959.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.2010.00959.x). [Full publisher text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1558-5646.2010.00959.x). **E2 primary main-text** Methods, Results and Discussion reviewed; no raw-data/statistical replication, supplementary provenance or permissions audit. Publisher lists free access, not an independently established open redistribution license; paraphrase only.

**Designed starting conditions.** The study used methionine-auxotrophic *E. coli* that metabolized lactose and supplied byproducts to *Salmonella*. *Salmonella* needed to evolve costly methionine excretion to support *E. coli*. Researchers first selected ethionine-resistant *Salmonella*; this precursor alone did **not** excrete sufficient methionine. Investigators then passaged mixed colonies on lactose plates. The reciprocal byproduct channel and agar spatial structure were provided by the experimental setup, not newly evolved organism boundaries.

**Positive evidence and independent units.** High methionine excretion emerged in **2 of 10** indirect-selection replicates from ethionine-resistant precursors; multiple colonies within one replicate were conservatively counted as a single origin. A separate 10 wild-type-start replicates yielded no comparable high-excretion origin. In one assayed mutant, measured methionine excretion increased from about 0.005 ± 0.002 mM to 0.08 ± 0.02 mM; the second origin had functional cross-feeding but no independent HPLC measurement. In three spatial-structure competitions, cooperators starting at 1% rose above 80% after four transfers (roughly 20 generations), with increased community density. These competition replicates are **not** additional de novo origins.

**Negative intervention contrasts.** On acetate plates, where *Salmonella* no longer depended on *E. coli* waste, cooperators fell from 98% to below 5% over four transfers. In well-mixed lactose flasks, wild-type nonexcreters rose toward fixation over 20 passages and *E. coli* fell below detection. Methionine excretion was intrinsically costly without feedback (reported selection coefficient versus wild type −0.43 ± 0.05 in acetate). The study demonstrates evolved cooperation conditional on a preexisting reciprocal pathway and structured ecology, not a self-packaging joint lineage or automatic co-transmission of both partners.

**Alternative explanations and limits.** Ethionine pretreatment facilitated mutational accessibility; controlled plate ecology selected local feedback. The study does not disentangle all genetic mechanisms or follow complete naturally dispersed descendant collectives without human passage. Strong within-system mechanistic contrasts are not proof of transfer to unrelated species or unconstrained habitats.

## Source B — Hillesland & Stahl (2010): productive syntrophy remains serially maintained

**Primary:** Kristina L. Hillesland and David A. Stahl, *Rapid evolution of stability and productivity at the origin of a microbial mutualism*, *PNAS* 107:2124–2129, DOI [10.1073/pnas.0908456107](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0908456107). [Full primary Methods/Results/Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836651/); [author manuscript](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5tq5g1g7). **E2 primary main-text review**; SI recipes, raw time series and independent statistical reproduction not audited. PMC manuscript copyright/third-party rights not assumed reusable; original analytical summary only.

**Designed starting conditions and independent units.** Researchers paired *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* with *Methanococcus maripaludis* in **24 independently propagated cocultures**, 12 shaken and 12 unshaken, based on **four ancestral genotype combinations**. Cultures were **manually transferred at 1% inoculum** into fresh sulfate-free medium approximately weekly subject to density criteria; static tubes were vigorously mixed before transfer. These are 24 evolving communities, not 24 separately invented mechanisms of collective inheritance, and descendants were not left to autonomously seed new habitats.

**Positive and negative outcomes.** After 45 transfers (~300 community doublings), surviving communities showed faster growth and higher yields: up to 80% faster growth and up to 30% more biomass productivity than ancestors. Early dynamics were unstable; **2/24 cultures went extinct**. Growth assays covered **20/24** original cultures: two extinct and two not ready by assay time. **19/20 assayed** improved significantly in growth rate. Recombining evolved and ancestral partner populations suggested adaptation in both species, but contributions were not simply additive, consistent with partner-genotype interactions. The growth/yield assay includes technical replicate cocultures; these are not new independent evolutionary founders.

**What is not shown.** Neither productivity nor repeated serial propagation establishes autonomous group reproduction, evolved shared packaging, partner co-dispersal or a multi-generation withdrawal of manual transfer. The two species retained separate cellular reproduction; no new joint germline or self-assembled dispersal unit was observed.

## Dependent follow-up — Hillesland et al. (2014): specialization may reduce partner independence

**Primary follow-up:** *Erosion of functional independence early in the evolution of a microbial mutualism*, *PNAS*, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1407986111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1407986111); [PMC original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4205623/). **E1+ partial primary results/abstract review**, not asserted as a new complete E2 methods audit. Following extended evolution of the **same research program**, the authors reported significant sulfate-respiration loss-of-function populations in **13 of 21 independently evolved cocultures** by 1,000 generations. They explicitly flagged a likely U9/U10 cross-contamination resemblance and **excluded U10 as an independent evolutionary event**. Preserve that qualification; do not add the 2014 lineages to 2010 as independent origins. Sulfate-respiration loss can improve adaptation to syntrophy while eroding solo metabolic options, but does not itself establish jointly inherited organism-level autonomy.

## Causal synthesis: three separate gates

| Gate | Harcombe 2010 | Hillesland & Stahl 2010 | Hillesland et al. 2014 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evolved new reciprocal performance | Methionine excretion evolved under structured selection | Coculture productivity and stability improved | Some evolved populations lost solo sulfate-respiration capacity |
| Evolved autonomous partner co-inheritance | **Not tested** | **Not tested** | **Not established** |
| Reproducing joint lineage after all transfers/selection withdrawn | **Not demonstrated** | **Not demonstrated** | **Not demonstrated** |

The two 2010 systems are distinct experimental designs and laboratories, **not independent replications of one claim of reproductive closure**. The 2014 follow-up is not independent of Hillesland 2010. In particular, community productivity can rise while individual metabolic independence falls, and cooperation can vanish when external spatial reciprocity changes. These are **negative boundary findings**, not evidence that autonomous joint reproduction is impossible.

### Future-only, falsifiable hypotheses (not performed)

- **P30-H1 (coupling vs output):** Increased coculture yield without a heritable co-dispersal mechanism will fail to predict successful joint descendant establishment in unassisted dispersal. Reject if independently founded, enumerated descendants repeatedly establish both partners and preserve a causal functional benefit without manual mixing or reseeding.
- **P30-H2 (ecological subsidy):** Apparent cooperation under externally enforced spatial structure may disappear when that structure is removed. Reject if evolved partner-directed reciprocity remains selectively stable across prespecified unstructured generations with matched noncooperator controls and no selection/sorting.
- **P30-H3 (specialization paradox):** Loss of solo metabolic functions may increase obligate dependence without increasing collective reproductive autonomy. Reject if mechanistic ablation/rescue demonstrates that the same evolved change generates a parent-produced joint transmission unit with viable beneficial descendants.

**Highest-information next audit:** independently founded experimental lines where the **same daughter collectives** are measured for partner retention, offspring reproductive output, partner-derived function and persistence after withdrawal of transfers, external partner supply and sorting. Record extinct and contaminated lines and founder nesting. If no source jointly meets those conditions, report the evidence gap instead of manufacturing proof.
