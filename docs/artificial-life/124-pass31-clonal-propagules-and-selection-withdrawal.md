# Pass 31 — Self-generated clonal propagules versus ecological autonomy

**9 October 2026 · research-only primary-paper review.** Starting main `a92686234f890f15e1fe1a56a31d2e7adb8e4276`. Tests a narrower question than full autonomous life: do selected multicellular yeast make daughter clusters, persist after settling selection is withdrawn, and evolve higher-level properties? Sources are overlapping research lineage, **not independent biological replication**. No organisms, experiments, simulation, training or statistical reanalysis performed.

## Ratcliff et al. (2012) — transition to reproducing clonal clusters

**Primary:** Ratcliff, Denison, Borrello & Travisano, *Experimental evolution of multicellularity*, PNAS 109:1595–1600, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1115323109](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1115323109), [full primary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3277146/). **E2 main-text Methods/Results/Discussion reading**; SI and deposited raw data not independently reanalyzed. Freely accessible PNAS open-access paper, but reuse rights for third-party material not separately checked; paraphrase only.

**Design:** Researchers performed daily gravity-settling selection on initially unicellular yeast, serially transferring selected sediment to fresh medium. In the first experiment, snowflake phenotypes appeared within 60 transfers in 10/10 selection lines (consistent with the original report); separately starting genotypes and reused clones must not be treated as repeated independent discoveries of every later phenotype. Daughter multicellular clusters arose when parent clusters fragmented, making a physically inherited clonal life cycle distinct from transient single-cell adhesion. Cell death near fracture points was associated with propagule formation but alone does not prove a genetically programmed developmental mechanism.

**Critical withdrawal contrast:** Three lines selected from *one* day-30 source population underwent **35 further manual transfers without gravitational selection**. No invasion by unicellular types was detected. This is evidence of short-run phenotypic stability **without settling selection**, but *not* 35 unattended generations, cessation of manual culturing, long-term survival in open ecology, independent founder repetitions, or proof of sustained group advantage without all researcher support. A separate 35-transfer manipulation varied 5/15/25-minute settling-selection strength and found altered group reproductive size, demonstrating response to selection **while the protocol remained imposed**. Do not conflate these two 35-transfer designs.

## Bozdag et al. (2023) — clonal propagules and long-term size selection

**Primary:** Bozdag et al., *De novo evolution of macroscopic multicellularity*, Nature 617:747–754, DOI [10.1038/s41586-023-06052-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06052-1), [public main text and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10425966/). **E2 main-text and accessible methods review**, not independent analysis of supplements, images, genome sequencing or data/code. Journal/editorial rights and manuscript deposit distinct; paper figures, code and data not redistributed.

**Design and nesting:** Fifteen snowflake-yeast populations (five each anaerobic, obligately aerobic and mixotrophic) underwent **600 daily researcher-managed settling-selection rounds**, approximately 3,000 generations. These lineages began with **already multicellular snowflake yeast**, so the title's “de novo” refers to macroscopic multicellularity rather than a second independent invention of multicellular parent–offspring organization. The study shares a research group/model lineage with Ratcliff 2012; it is a later mechanistic extension, not independent lab replication.

**Results and negative controls:** All five anaerobic lines evolved macroscopic clusters while the other ten remained microscopic under their tested regimes. Anaerobic cluster size increased markedly (~20,000-fold volume according to the paper's principal estimate), with ~10,000-fold cluster toughness and cellular elongation/branch entanglement. The large structure retained **a clonal multicellular life cycle and small propagules** under continuing selection. The anaerobic versus aerobic contrast supports metabolic-ecological constraints, not proof that oxygen alone dictates every evolutionary trajectory. Evolutionary success here is within a tailored daily transferred laboratory setting.

**Limit:** Group-level selected traits and parent-produced propagules are stronger observations than merely measuring aggregation. Yet no prospective intervention-withdrawal study demonstrates that these selected macroclusters colonize an unsupervised environment over many independent complete daughter lineages, without researcher transfers, size sorting, selection or prepared habitat. Nor are the five anaerobic lines independent origins of clonal multicellularity.

**Counterhypotheses:** The 2012 withdrawal result may be driven by the stability of a simple clonal genotype under laboratory propagation, rather than collective ecological self-maintenance. The 2023 size gains may depend on the externally enforced sedimentation advantage; failure of other metabolic treatments is a meaningful boundary, not general impossibility. Neither source tests competing natural colonizers or full lifetime dispersal and establishment.

### Falsifiable future-only hypotheses

- **P31-H1 — propagules versus environmental establishment.** A cluster that makes viable clonal offspring under researcher transfers may still fail to establish stable daughter populations when no transfers/sorting occur. Reject if prospectively enumerated founders repeatedly generate and establish independent beneficial multi-generation descendants in an unsorted, non-reseeded setting.
- **P31-H2 — selection withdrawal and stability.** Persistence over manual no-settling transfers does not necessarily predict open-environment lineage stability. Reject if independently originated genotypes retain population-level fitness benefit and clonal reproduction across predefined, unassisted descendant generations.
- **P31-H3 — scale versus reproductive closure.** A larger/tougher cluster does not imply superior independent lineage establishment. Reject if controlled, independently founded cohorts show a direct positive causal effect of increased toughness on parent-to-daughter establishment with matched small-cluster controls.
