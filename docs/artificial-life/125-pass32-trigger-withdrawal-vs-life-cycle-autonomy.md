# Pass 32 — Trigger withdrawal versus reproductive autonomy: distinct experimental gates

**2026-10-09 · research-only, source-critical primary review.** Starting main: `e14bc9981b5b6baa6598a9abb7549eec3e9373c7`. This pass investigates **what survives when an initial environmental induction is withdrawn** and separately **what remains when a researcher-supplied intergenerational life-cycle scaffold is removed**. Those two interventions are not equivalent. No organisms, experiments, simulations, models, source code from papers, or independent scientific replications were executed.

### Operational distinctions

1. **Phenotype induction:** an external stimulus triggers cluster formation. Its withdrawal asks whether clustering has become genetically stable.
2. **Individuality and reproductive mechanism:** a lineage makes propagules or reliably transitions between growth and dispersal states. Measure descent and fitness at that level, not just cell counts or cluster size.
3. **Ecological/reproductive scaffold:** manual transfer, cluster picking, fresh habitat, sorting, fixed phase timing and lineage replacement. Ending an original inducer or changing a genotype **does not remove this scaffold**.
4. **Unassisted closure:** multiple independent founders produce viable daughter collectives that repeatedly establish and maintain the relevant organization with no investigator reconstitution, rescue, periodic re-seeding or replacement. Neither paper prospectively demonstrates this full conjunction.

## A. Chavhan, Dey & Lind (2023) — induction can be genetically assimilated

**Original article:** *Bacteria evolve macroscopic multicellularity by the genetic assimilation of phenotypically plastic cell clustering*, *Nature Communications* 14:3555, [DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-39320-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39320-9), [public primary Methods/Results/Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272148/). **E2 main-text** (including experimental selection and assay methods, results, captions and rights); supplemental films, raw sequences and independent reanalysis not performed. Main paper **CC BY 4.0**, with third-party exceptions; only original paraphrase, no copied figures or source datasets.

**Initial, induced state.** `E. coli` and `S. aureus` grew centimetre-scale clusters in high salt (6% NaCl) but were planktonic in ordinary salinity (0.5% NaCl). The authors also observed plastic clustering in `Citrobacter freundii` and `Pseudomonas aeruginosa`, but **not** in the tested `Serratia marcescens`: this is a negative species comparator. The induced clusters involved both cells remaining together after division and adhesion/aggregation. Demonstrating this *ancestral* inducible capacity is different from independently evolving a new group reproductive life cycle.

**Selection and independent experimental units.** Ten `E. coli` populations, all clonally derived from **one MG1655 ancestor**, evolved in two environments: five shaken (S) and five resting (R). Over **50 daily researcher-managed passages**, the authors gradually reduced salt from 6% to 3%, using an active **cluster-picking/washing selection** method. They physically removed a fragment of each previous day's macroscopic cluster, washed it four times to dilute free cells (~10^8-fold), and transferred it to new medium. The experiment did **not** withdraw selection for clustering during evolution. Ten populations are replicate selection lines, not ten unrelated ancestral origins or ten independent tests of future unassisted dispersal.

**Positive endpoint and failures.** At normal salinity, endpoint isolates from **4/5 S** (S1, S2, S4, S5) and **4/5 R** (R1, R2, R3, R5) selection lines formed macroscopic clusters without the original high-salt *induction*; S3 and R4 did **not** meet that normal-salinity endpoint. All ten maintained high-salt clustering. The eight successes therefore show **stable expression under inducer withdrawal**, not 8/10 autonomous multi-generational reproductive closure.

**Mechanistic qualification.** Cell-wall-related changes, prominently repeated `mraY` variants in seven of ten sequenced endpoints, implicated genetic assimilation and cell shape/adhesion changes. Under normal salinity, proteinase K inhibited clustering in 7/8 assimilated isolates; cellulase also reduced clusters. The authors distinguish extracellular bonding chemistry in induced and uninduced conditions. These results give a plausible mechanistic basis for the *phenotype* but do not directly demonstrate that a new self-generated group-to-group propagule interface evolved, that each whole cluster is genetically pure, or that selection remains beneficial without a prepared laboratory niche. Alternative effects of cell-wall change, adhesion and culture conditions remain possible.

**Critical boundary:** Daily selection, washing, colony isolation and later assay inoculations remain external operations. Individual `E. coli` cells retain cellular reproduction; no independent series of parent-to-daughter **collectives** is enumerated after all transfers cease. The authors' use of “obligate” multicellular growth is operational (constitutive macroscopic clustering in their tested laboratory media), not proof of an obligate autonomous organism-level germline.

## B. Doulcier, Remigi, Rexin & Rainey (2025) — switching benefits lab-scaffolded collective survival

**Original article:** *Evolutionary dynamics of nascent multicellular lineages*, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 292:20241195, [DOI 10.1098/rspb.2024.1195](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2024.1195), [author-hosted 12-page primary PDF](https://www.normalesup.org/~doulcier/articles/doulcier_evolutionary_2025.pdf), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12040459/). **E2 main article**: introduction, experimental protocol, fitness measurement, Results, and figure captions inspected; figures 1–2 and selection/genealogy method visually checked. Electronic supplementary material, complete colgen model specification, deposited raw genomes and independent analysis **not** audited or executed. Original main article **CC BY 4.0**, subject to specific third-party exceptions.

**Parent study, founder independence and intervention.** The studied `Pseudomonas fluorescens` SBW25 systems descend from **one previously evolved successful “line 17”** of an earlier experimental program, not 192 independently invented multicellular systems. Researchers compared its switching genotype (`lcs+`, including a defective `mutS` mismatch-repair system linked to WspR phase switching) with an isogenic `lcs−` version in which the `mutS` defect had been reverted. These are paired derivative variants, not de novo unrelated ancestors. Two vessel sizes (standard S; larger L) yield **four treatments**; the report describes **48 microcosm lineages per treatment**, blocked into **six groups of eight**, over **10 experimentally managed collective life-cycle generations**. Genotypes, vessels, blocks, time points and technical sequencing observations must not be pooled as independent origin events.

**Exactly what the experimenter still does.** A wrinkly spreader (`WS`) forms an air–liquid cellulose mat (“soma-like” stage). Non-mat (`SM`) cells arise, and **researchers transfer** these cells into fresh microcosms; they then collect `WS` cells and deliberately choose a single `WS` genotype for the next passage. Mat persistence is evaluated at six days and re-emergence at three days. When a lineage fails, a *living competitor is chosen and transferred* to occupy its culture position; entire blocks lost are replaced from other blocks. The experiment deliberately constructs lineage-level extinction/replacement and competition. This is an excellent **ecological-scaffolding test**, but not observation of unattended dispersal and settlement.

**Results and negative observations.** `lcs+` generally suffered fewer extinction events than the switched-off control. Larger microcosms were more difficult: the authors report **40/96 versus 3/96 first-cycle mat-failure events** in large versus standard conditions pooled over the two genotype treatments, not 40 independent new organisms. `lcs−` lineages initially fared poorly but improved in parts of the experiment; in large `lcs−`, surviving endpoint lineages descended from **one ancestral microcosm**, not dozens of independent successful origins. A novel `mutL` change enabled switching in an `lcs−` treatment, restoring a **previously available functional role**, rather than originating the whole lifecycle without scaffolding. The authors' counterfactual conclusion that otherwise all the large `lcs−` lineages would have been extinct by the third generation is based on their lineage reconstruction; it must not be treated as a separately performed prospective withdrawal experiment.

**Important opposing outcome.** `lcs+` lines in **standard** microcosms showed an overall **decline in collective fitness** compared with the ancestral genotype, despite the initially favorable switch; this differs from results in larger vessels. These outcomes defeat an unconditional “more switching always means higher fitness” claim. The authors measure fitness chiefly as avoiding extinction over the *laboratory-defined* lifecycle, not unassisted net reproduction of established collectives in an open environment. The colgen software infers ancestral changes through a Bayesian model; inferred adaptive breakpoints do not count as independently manipulated mutations until experimentally checked.

**Why stronger than simple clustering but short of closure.** Here both group-level selection and explicit two-stage lineage failure are measurable; a causal genetic switch contrast is an important advance over simple photos of aggregates. However, the parent/offspring relation is partly supplied by **microcosm scheduling and investigator transfers**. What evolved is a more reliable *biological component* of the scaffolded life cycle, not verified replacement of the full scaffold by an autonomous collective reproductive interface.

## Cross-study inference without false pooling

| Question | Chavhan 2023: `E. coli` | Doulcier 2025: `P. fluorescens` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original inducer withdrawn during test? | **Yes**: high salinity not required for 8/10 endpoint clones at 0.5% | Not analogous; instead an inherited switching mechanism was genetically disabled |
| Positive evolved outcome | Constitutive clustering and altered bonding | Context-dependent viability of soma–germ transitions |
| Negative/control results | S3/R4 not constitutively clustered; tested `Serratia` did not show inducible clustering | Switch-off lineages often fail; standard `lcs+` fitness declines; mat failure in large vessels |
| Researcher-run transfer/reconstitution | **Yes**: daily cluster picking, washing and prepared inoculations | **Yes**: transfer each stage, select a founder, replace failed lineages |
| Population-level daughter collective establishment without researcher action | **Not measured** | **Not measured** |
| Independent origins of full reproductive autonomy | **Not shown** | **Not shown** |

The papers are distinct bacterial taxa, experimental groups, geographic programs and intervention designs; they are **not two independent replications of the same closure test**. The 2025 study explicitly continues the Rainey/Hammerschmidt laboratory line; it must not be counted as independent confirmation of its earlier founder's evolutionary origin. These two studies establish **real partial transitions**: (a) phenotype stability after withdrawal of an inducer; (b) selection on a genetic switching mechanism during a designed collective life cycle. Neither proves a completely independent reproducing entity, and the absence of proof is not proof that no such entity can evolve.

### Prespecified future-only hypotheses — untested

- **P32-H1 — inducer removal is an insufficient closure gate:** genetically assimilated clusters can still fail to establish daughter collective lineages when manual cluster selection/transfer is withdrawn. Reject if multiple independently derived, fully counted lines repeatedly establish matched multi-generation offspring collectives without selection, researcher reassembly or rescue.
- **P32-H2 — endogenous switching may not replace exogenous scheduling:** a heritable soma/germ transition can confer a fitness benefit under protocol-controlled transfers without being able to organize timed dispersal and recolonization in an unattended environment. Reject if independent founders produce, disperse and establish daughters under matched unsorted conditions after both transfer and replacement interventions end.
- **P32-H3 — fitness depends on ecological context:** the advantage of a switch may reverse between vessel/competition conditions, so a single assay fitness increase does not predict population maintenance. Reject if replicated causal comparisons show invariant and beneficial lifetime daughter output over a prespecified ecological gradient, with explicit counts of extinction and failed propagules.

**Highest-information next source-critical audit:** search for prospective non-reseeded observations of entire parent–offspring collectives where each generation contains documented origin, new function, growth, propagule release, establishment, viability and all intervention withdrawals; record failed founders, transfer subsidies, asymmetric fitness and source independence. If absent, state the verified missing joint evidence rather than implying life/agency/consciousness is established.
