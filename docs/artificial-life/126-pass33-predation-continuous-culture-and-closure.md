# Pass 33 — Predator withdrawal, continuous culture, and independently established multicellular descendants

**9 October 2026 | Research only | Starting main:** `79e27cc312e34fe4198746a8dc8887883c9a3603`.

**High-information question.** How closely do evolved algal life cycles approach reproduction without manual passage, and what evidence distinguishes inherited multicellular organization from reversible predator-induced clumping? Compare a continuously fed chemostat with sustained parental colony production; independently evolved algal lineages maintained without predators; and a negative phenotype-withdrawal control. **Do not conflate endogenous budding, genetically stable colonial morphology, continuous population maintenance, and autonomous ecological daughter establishment.** No organisms, experimental evolution, simulations, agents, models, source code, or independent reanalysis were created or executed by this review.

## A. Boraas, Seale & Boxhorn (1998) — a continuously maintained colonial lineage

**Original:** *Phagotrophy by a flagellate selects for colonial prey: A possible origin of multicellularity*, *Evolutionary Ecology* 12:153–164; [DOI 10.1023/A:1006527528063](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1006527528063); [publisher landing page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1006527528063); [author-uploaded full original text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263614820_Phagotrophy_by_flagellate_selects_for_colonial_prey_A_possible_origin_of_multicellularity). **E2-depth main-text reading:** original Culture system, Sampling/observation, Results and Discussion inspected in author-uploaded text; printed figures, underlying raw time series, exact replication count and independent reproduction were **not** audited. The author upload carries an explicit copyright warning, and neither publisher nor third-party redistribution rights were established. No text, figures, media or dataset from the paper was copied into this repository.

**Intervention and background.** `Chlorella vulgaris` UTEX #26 grew in a **500 mL** illuminated, temperature-controlled, nitrogen-limited laboratory chemostat with **ongoing pump-controlled fresh nutrient flow**. Investigators introduced predatory `Ochromonas vallescia` into an established algal steady state. The predator ate unicellular `Chlorella`, initially depressing prey density. Several generations later, algal colonies appeared and became dominant under the same continuous culture architecture. This setup crucially avoids the *discrete manual selection/transfer at every generation* that underlies the 2012–2025 yeast, `Chlamydomonas` and bacterial selection programs, but it remains dependent on built infrastructure, continuous nutrient/energy input, controlled light, temperature, and a defined predator environment.

**Observation and measured unit.** Colonies initially contained from a few to hundreds of cells; the population gradually shifted toward predominantly **eight-celled colonies**. The authors' time series describe a sustained mixed predator–prey steady state and note colony morphology in cultures aged hundreds of days. They directly observed **colony budding**: daughter cells grew within maternal structures and separated as small colonies by opening the mother wall. Fully sized colonies were resistant to ingestion, while individual algal cells and some small nascent colonies could be eaten. Thus, within an already functioning culture, the biological population was generating **offspring multicellular colonies itself**. This is stronger positive life-cycle evidence than a single microscopy image of a cluster or an externally assembled community.

**Founder and failure accounting.** The authors report that repeat predator challenges yielded multicellular algae in **about 70%** of trials, but do **not give a complete independently enumerated experiment count** in the paper passage analyzed. This percentage is not a denominator-qualified prospective success rate and must not be reported as `7/10` or proof of general cross-genotype reproducibility. Cultures share laboratory stock and equipment. Individual budding events are nested within a founder culture and are not new independent origins of multicellularity.

**Environmental counterfactual and limitation.** Authors observed heritable colony morphology after predator removal in **monospecific** chemostats for several months and on agar. A two-stage chemostat contrast found colonies washed out when the **second stage was darkened**; this supports a dependence on local photosynthesis/resource and ecological conditions, not the claim that the colonial genotype requires a predator to remain colonial. The authors argued against a purely predator-induced, instantly reversible phenotype, but a complete randomized predator-withdrawal and fitness-controlled multi-founder study was not supplied. Continuous culture without repeated **manual organism transfers** is not no-scaffold autonomous ecological colonization: flow, prepared medium and containment still come from the investigator, and there is no recorded independent migration/establishment into a fresh habitat by daughter groups.

## B. Herron et al. (2019) — inherited predator-resistance and multicellular propagules, but continued transfers

**Original:** *De novo origins of multicellularity in response to predation*, *Scientific Reports* 9:2328; [DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-39558-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-39558-8); [publisher primary full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-39558-8). **E2-depth primary Methods, Results and Discussion** reviewed in this pass (previous repository entries cite only E1 excerpts; those **historical metadata remain unchanged** until separate explicit reconciliation). Supplemental movies, deposited images, analysis macro, and raw time series not rerun. Journal main article **CC BY 4.0** with third-party exceptions; rights to supplementary material inspected only at the linked-label level, no redistribution.

**Independent experiment units.** Investigators crossed two `Chlamydomonas reinhardtii` mating types, creating an outcrossed shared F1 starting pool. They separately propagated **five predator-exposed populations** (B1–B5, `Paramecium tetraurelia`) and **three predator-free controls** (K1–K3), **weekly for 50 transfers** (~750 asexual generations), with fixed-medium replacement and investigator pipette mixing/inoculation. **2/5 experimental populations (B2 and B5)** had evolved stable multicellular traits by the endpoint; no analogous new trait appeared in the **3/3 controls**. Eight colonies were isolated and replated from each of B2, B5 and K1; seven chosen evolved focal strains are **nested descendants of just two successful population histories**, not seven independent origins. As previously indexed, this study overlaps with the scientific program of Ratcliff/Herron and Chen et al. 2025 and must not be double-counted as independent author-group replication.

**Observed offspring behavior.** Evolved strains differed substantially. Some retained motile single-cell propagules and produced mixed aggregates with extracellular matrix trapping unrelated free swimmers. Three B2 strains (`B2-11`, `B2-03`, `B2-04`) formed **strictly clonal groups** within maternal cell walls and predominantly produced multicellular propagules; controls and nonconverted isolates retained ancestral single-cell propagule behavior. Time-lapse observations ran **72 hours** over structured imaging assays with five technical replicates per strain, not an unassisted ecological trial. Larger multicellular strains showed lower biomass loss in an independent predator-resistance assay. Those data provide observable daughter release and defensive function, with biological differences among evolved lineages rather than one uniform endpoint.

**Longer persistence without predators.** Evolved laboratory strains continued showing their characteristic multicellular phenotypes as **unfrozen, in-use laboratory stocks for four years without predators**. This is much stronger evidence for inherited phenotype stability *after selection pressure withdrawal* than one endpoint isolate grown once in common garden. However, “in-use” indicates maintained stocks, not four continuous unattended years, a predetermined lineage tracing experiment, or hundreds of independent unassisted daughter populations. Initial evolution and subsequent stock maintenance still involved researcher-provided medium, handling, isolation, or culture passage. Predation-related phenotype benefits were measured under specified lab assays, **not directly as autonomous establishment success** in an unprepared habitat.

**Important counterhypotheses.** Their ancestral alga already possessed facultative palmelloid organization and transient multicellular mitotic stages. Mutations may constitutively express existing modules, rather than inventing all components of a new organism from zero. Cohort-level positive results coexist with **3/5 negative predator populations**, and a successful lineage can produce single-celled or nonclonal propagules. Do not pool focal-strain, microscopy-well, and new-origin counts. This work is a strong positive **reproductive-developmental and heritable morphology** test, but not a complete ecological closure assay.

## C. Fisher, Bell & West (2016; online 2015) — a contrasting, reversible predator response

**Original:** *Multicellular group formation in response to predators in the alga Chlorella vulgaris*, *Journal of Evolutionary Biology* 29:551–559; [DOI 10.1111/jeb.12804](https://doi.org/10.1111/jeb.12804); [publisher original](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jeb.12804); [data repository DOI](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.c5902). **E2 main-text Methods, Results and Discussion** reviewed; published **free-access** main text, but a CC redistribution license was not confirmed. Underlying Dryad data not downloaded, recomputed or copied. Distinct study group from Boraas 1998, but not a strict replication because it used **another predator and different culture architecture**.

**Design and primary controls.** Using `C. vulgaris` and ciliate predator `Tetrahymena thermophila`, experiments compared live predator exposure, predator-conditioned filtered supernatants, density gradients and matched nutrition controls. Independent 1 mL microcosms were measured **destructively** at daily intervals, **15 independent microcosms per condition across the five-day scheme** (three samples per treatment per day); those are replicated *assays of a response*, not independent long-run evolved origins. Nutrient-matched controls address an alternative explanation that predator stock media simply increased algal growth. Blind image-based cell/colony counts measured the **proportion of cells in colonies**, not just raw colony counts confounded by population size.

**Observed positive and negative results.** Live predators and predator-conditioned medium increased the fraction of algal cells forming clusters; dose response was positive, but average colony size did not significantly increase with predator level. In the extension testing reversibility, cultures received **no new predator after initial exposure**, yet were **transferred to fresh medium at day 10**. By day 20, as mobile predator counts approached zero, predator-exposed treatments **did not show a pairwise excess** of colonial cells over the no-predator control. The article's overall day-20 F test (`F(3,8)=6.19, p=0.02`) is not numerically identical to these **nonsignificant individual contrast tests**; preserve both rather than claiming the treatments were mathematically identical.

**Interpretation limit.** The rapid reversibility and predator exoproduct induction favor **facultative phenotypic plasticity**, but the authors explicitly acknowledge that short-window selection for, then against, colony-associated genotypes is **not experimentally excluded**. This is a different predator (`Tetrahymena`, not `Ochromonas`), different volume (1 mL versus 500 mL), and 20-day assay versus multi-month chemostat. It does **not falsify** Boraas's stable colonial lineage, and it establishes that a superficial “predator makes colonies” observation is insufficient to infer an inherited multicellular individual.

## D. Previously archived genotype comparator (no duplicated review)

The existing [Pass 10 Chen et al. 2025 full-primary audit](46-pass10-chlamydomonas-ancestry-2025-full-review.md) is **already E2** in canonical metadata (DOI [10.1093/gbe/evaf090](https://doi.org/10.1093/gbe/evaf090)). From 24 experimental evolution populations drawn from one shared 10-genotype pool, **4/12 predator-selected** and **0/12 settling-selected** produced stable heritable multicellularity after ~600 generations; only **two founder genotype backgrounds** were inferred among sequenced multicellular outcomes. This is a distinct prospective cohort but overlapping Herron/Chen investigator network and methods—not an independent lab replication or 4+2 origins per one sample set. It makes **founder genetic predisposition and selection regime** important counterhypotheses to generic universality of predator-induced multicellularity. Its counts, source metadata, and audit text are not edited here.

## Comparison — what exactly persists?

| Evidence gate | Boraas 1998 | Herron 2019 | Fisher 2016 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Predation drives a colony phenotype | Yes, selection in continuous culture | Yes, 2/5 selected population histories | Yes, often induced |
| Endogenous production of colonial daughter forms | Direct budding in continuous culture | Direct time-lapse for some multicellular lineages | Not a demonstrated inherited collective lineage |
| Colonial phenotype without live predator | Months in laboratory monospecific cultures | Four years in maintained laboratory stocks | Predominantly reversed by day 20 |
| Periodic *manual* transfer required in observed main ecology | No serial manual colony-selection passage; flow reactor still externally run | Yes, 50 weekly selections; stock handling thereafter | Yes, day-10 fresh-medium transfer |
| Independent offspring groups repeatedly establishing in **unprepared, unassisted** habitats | **Not established** | **Not established** | **Not established** |
| Fully measured founder failures and ecological controls | Replicate denominator unreported for ~70% claim | 2/5 successes and three negative predator lines | Predation and nutrition controls, but not an evolution-origins cohort |

**Evidence-supported conclusion:** Some studies go considerably beyond aggregation: parent-produced multicellular propagules, heritably stable form, persistent chemostat populations and functional protection are real biological capacities in their recorded regimes. **No reviewed source simultaneously measures natural daughter dispersal/colonization, multi-generation net benefit, all extinct founders, and withdrawal of every ecological or intergenerational reproduction scaffold.** That joint gap is **not** proof that open-world multicellularity is impossible. Continuous nutrient/environmental energy supply does not *by itself* disqualify life—living systems require resources; the central experimental deficit is *investigator-selected reseeding, lineage rescue, and absence of independently measured daughter establishment*.

## Future-only hypotheses: falsification requirements

- **P33-H1 — continuous reproduction can still depend on a supplied niche.** A stable, budding colonial lineage in a chemostat need not repeatedly establish daughter lineages after uninstructed dispersal into novel habitats. Reject if multiple independently founded and fully enumerated chemostat-evolved populations spontaneously colonize and maintain viable daughter-collective growth in untouched receiving habitats with naturally available resources, and retain their new organizational trait without manual sorting or rescue.
- **P33-H2 — inherited phenotype is different from autonomous reproductive establishment.** Four-year laboratory trait persistence may fail to predict successful independent group recruitment. Reject if predeclared lineage-tracing cohorts show repeated unassisted daughter colony settlement and reproduction with partner/propagule identity, negative founders, and matched ancestral controls.
- **P33-H3 — predator-cue plasticity and selected genetic change can be confused.** A single predator exposure that yields colonies need not change genotype-level heritable life cycles. Reject if independent multi-generation common-garden offspring, replicated phenotype re-expression, reciprocal predator additions/removals, and causal genetic reconstruction demonstrate a stable inherited change without contamination or rapid genotype sorting.

**Next highest-information scientific audit:** actual multigeneration predator–prey ecological microcosms with *passive* daughter dispersal into new habitat patches (no experimenter-selected offspring, rescues or imposed reproducer identity) and separately counted successful/failed new settlements. Trace photosynthetic nutrient/resource flows and bottlenecks; distinguish ecological niche supply from deliberate inheritance. Do not infer cognition, consciousness, or open-ended evolution from a stable colony.
