# Pass 34 — Dispersal advantage, life-cycle regulation and ecological scaffold endogenisation

**2026-10-09 · critical source review · research only.** Starting `main` `b7f4acac8851b8225f80075e1319e6904a8a47aa`. Question inherited from [Pass 33](126-pass33-predation-continuous-culture-and-closure.md): **can a daughter collective or propagule establish a new population because of an evolved endogenous reproductive strategy, rather than because a researcher deliberately creates a new habitat, switches phenotypes and transfers founders?** This pass tests the narrower evidence for (a) competing in patchy resources and (b) evolutionary persistence of a collective strategy after ecological conditions revert.

This is an interpretation of **one empirical fitness-manipulation paper, its published figure-label correction, and one independent mathematical/theoretical study**. They do **not** constitute two biological origin experiments or two independent replications of fully autonomous collective reproduction. No scientific experiments, model simulations, code execution or organism creation were performed for this pass.

## A. Barrere, Nanda & Murray (2023) — switching can be advantageous when researchers supply the timing

**Primary:** *Alternating selection for dispersal and multicellularity favors regulated life cycles*, **Current Biology** 33:1809–1817.e3. [DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2023.03.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.03.031); [PMC original manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175205/); [publisher journal article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982223003196). **E2-depth source-critical reading** of publicly indexed original text (Results, figure legends, STAR Methods and Data/Code availability) plus publisher abstract; a direct PMC open intermittently returned a browser verification page, so the independently rendered complete PDF, supplemental protocol and Harvard Dataverse archive were **not** reanalysed. Publisher © 2023 Elsevier all rights reserved; the PMC manuscript is publicly readable, which does not establish re-licensing of publisher figure assets. We paraphrase, do not copy original images, methods, datasets or code.

### Discovery versus engineering must be kept separate

**Wild isolates.** Authors characterized **22 phylogenetically diverse wild `Saccharomyces cerevisiae` isolates** plus the single-celled laboratory reference **S288c**, not 22 independently evolved life cycles in a new experiment. Haploid strains generally formed larger mother–daughter clonal clusters than `MATa/MATα` heterozygous diploids. Genetically manipulated diploids homozygous at the mating-type locus regained more clustering in a five-isolate test. Carbon-source identity and concentration also altered cluster scores, with a notable shift toward clustering in galactose. These are **environmental and genetic response measurements**, not spontaneous origination of a new reproductive mechanism within this paper.

**Engineered test of fitness.** Researchers used laboratory strain **W303** in three constructed states: wild-type constitutively single-celled; `ACE2` deletion constitutively clustered; and an **inducible `ACE2` promoter controlled by externally supplied β-estradiol**. `ACE2` affects daughter-cell separation. Without inducer the engineered strain clustered; with inducer it dispersed as single cells. Thus the crucial ability to alternate was **installed by investigators**, and the switch timing was externally chosen. Neither new switching alleles nor natural regulation evolved during the reported competition protocol.

### What the competitions establish, including negatives

- **Homogeneous low sucrose:** Clustering could be beneficial through the local retention of glucose/fructose products of extracellular sucrose hydrolysis. The corresponding glucose+fructose control **did not show that same cluster-size fitness effect**. The reported outcome also depended strongly on the **dilution regime**; below about 10,000-fold dilution the paper reports no fitness difference between single cells and clusters in a relevant control. The benefit therefore is **conditional**, not an intrinsic universal superiority of collective growth.
- **Patchy glucose emulsion:** Researchers dispersed inocula in droplets seeded on average with **fewer than one yeast cell each**. Under these spatial constraints, smaller/single-cell units had a colonisation/competitive advantage; a larger cluster can cover fewer independent droplets for the same number of cells. Homogeneous glucose competition was a negative control in which no comparable cluster-size-dependent fitness difference appeared.
- **Alternating environment:** Researchers cycled prepared low-sucrose growth with externally emulsified glucose droplets. They added **β-estradiol** to release single cells from the inducible `ACE2` strain before the emulsion stage, then allowed clustering again in the next sucrose stage. The regulated treatment outcompeted both permanent alternatives in their specified competitions. Figure 4D reports **three biological fitness-assay replicates** and **two complete selection cycles**; these are assay replicates and protocol cycles, not independently evolved regulated life cycles or a long-term unassisted new species.
- **Researcher labor:** The protocol included growth and mixing, `1:10,000` experimental bottlenecks, emulsion preparation and subsequent *breaking*, medium exchange, measurement by fluorescent flow-cytometry events, and timing the inducer. A fluorescent **event** could represent an entire cluster rather than a single cell; the authors rely on stable cluster-size distributions to infer relative competition fitness. This should not be silently equated with directly tracked per-founder reproductive output.
- **Disconfirming boundary:** The constitutively clustered state did **worse** in droplets while the constitutively single-cell state did **worse** in the cooperative sucrose regime. Even though combined cycling favored externally switched reproduction, none of the experiments asked whether evolved daughters themselves recognize, reach or create new habitat patches without staff manipulating the switches and transfers.

**Published correction, not a second independent experiment:** Barrere, Nanda & Murray (2024), *Current Biology* 34:1160, [DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2024.02.048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.02.048), [corrigendum full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11253032/). In the original **Figure 3D**, the two treatments were mislabeled: **triangles correspond to 20 → 0 nM β-estradiol; circles correspond to 0 → 20 nM**. The online figure was corrected. Do **not** infer direction of the switching curves using the original labels. This correction is **not independent replication** and does not demonstrate evolved switching; it should accompany all citation of the intervention kinetics.

## B. Doulcier, Takacs, Hammerschmidt & Bourrat (2024) — endogenisation is mathematically possible, but within an authored patch world

**Primary:** *Stability of ecologically scaffolded traits during evolutionary transitions in individuality*, **Nature Communications** 15:6566, [DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-50625-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50625-1); [complete publisher Results, Methods and Discussion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-50625-1); [code-output archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8335843). **E2-depth original mathematical/analytical source audit**; the article presents mathematical derivations and authors' own stochastic simulations, **not a new laboratory evolution cohort**. Its supporting derivations and Zenodo source code were **not reexecuted or independently checked** in this pass. Main article **CC BY 4.0** with third-party material caveats; Zenodo is separately labelled **CC BY**, but repository contents were not downloaded/reused. This authorship network partly overlaps **Doulcier 2025** from [Pass 32](125-pass32-trigger-withdrawal-vs-life-cycle-autonomy.md); theory cannot be counted as a new independent experimental replication of that program.

### Authored lifecycle assumptions, not spontaneous new rules

The model supplies **D fixed resource-bearing patches** connected by a graph. Particles consume resources and either duplicate or leave as **single-particle propagules**, according to probabilities governed by an evolving trait θ. Only empty, adjacent patches accept colonisation; the disperser **dies if no empty patch exists**. When local resources reach zero, all particles there die and the **model instantly restores the patch's resources**. These extinction, birth, migration, patch occupancy and renewal mechanics are **part of the investigator-authored model itself**, not novel functions that emerge from evolution.

The authors relate collective lifespan τ(θ) and the expected propagule number ρ(θ) to fitness. In the simplified fully connected patch limit, collective population growth requires **ρ(θ) > 1**, but this is an **analytical viability condition inside the programmed life cycle**, not an empirical measurement of real daughter settlement or an evolution of ecological patch-creation.

### A decisive internal negative control

With an **intrinsic**, density-independent duplicate-versus-disperse decision `p(θ) = θ`, evolutionary strategies change with the resource-richness parameter R; when R is returned to its ancestral value, the adapted trait returns as well. **No endogenisation**. This is a meaningful negative counterexample against the claim that externally scaffolded collective-level adaptation inevitably becomes stable without its original selection regime.

The authors construct a **density-dependent regulatory trait**, with `p(θ,n)=θ` for a lone particle and `1−θ` for populations of at least two. The strategy `θ=1` duplicates alone but disperses with companions. At **R=100**, the modeled fitness landscape has a lower coordination stable state near **θ≈0.41** and a second fully coordinated state **θ=1**, separated by a fitness valley around **θ≈0.66**. At **R=20**, the valley disappears and `θ=1` is selected. A sufficiently long stay in the small-patch regime allows crossing the valley; restoring R=100 then leaves the coordinated strategy stable, a form of **evolutionary hysteresis**. **Premature restoration produces reversion**, and intermediate durations produce variable success because mutation arrival is stochastic.

**Limits that change interpretation:** At θ=1 the model strongly rewards extremely small collectives (usually one to two particles) and perfect, instantaneous density sensing. The authors explicitly note that relaxing assumptions permits somewhat larger collectives (~four particles) but that the characteristic hysteresis tends to **disappear** as groups become large. The model omits within-patch particle death and prohibits occupancy of already populated new patches, minimizing migration mixing and competition. The model is mechanistically insightful but its evolved θ is not a demonstration of a new biological group boundary, inherited multispecies organization, autonomy or awareness.

### Goldilocks colonisation: modeled, not observed in real world

The authors' spatial extension begins with initially ancestral particles in rich patches and **980 connected patches**, representing 98 resource-richness classes with ten patches per class. Mutants with coordinated dispersal can arise in intermediate poorer “Goldilocks” zones and migrate back toward richer patches, competing successfully with ancestral types. This is a simulation with built-in graph edges, patch renewal and migration success conditions. It shows **conditional theoretical migration/establishment**, not spontaneous formation of the patch system or survival after its removal. There is also a **body/figure-caption inconsistency**: the main-text setup describes a gradient of roughly **R=100 down to R=2**, whereas the Fig. 5 caption describes **R=50 to R=0**; neither is silently substituted for the other without inspecting underlying code and figure data.

**Critical methodological distinction:** Here “scaffold removal” means changing **resource-size parameters back to pre-scaffolding values** while **the externally specified patch network and automatic resource renewal remain**. It does *not* mean removing the entire reproductive scaffold. This precise distinction is central to interpreting claims of “endogenisation” in relation to [Passes 30–33](126-pass33-predation-continuous-culture-and-closure.md).

## Joint interpretation and evidence grades

| Gate | Barrere et al. 2023, empirical | Doulcier et al. 2024, theory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biological group/dispersal mechanism discovered | Clusters observed in wild yeast; experimental regulator built via `ACE2` | Particle coordination `p(θ,n)` **specified before modeling** |
| Multiple patches and new daughter establishment | Single cells outperform groups in human-made emulsion; no naturally established daughter lineages enumerated | Patch colonisation follows programmed transition rules |
| Outcome when conditions change | Favors different states under low sucrose versus dispersed droplets; external β-estradiol timing | Constant-p trait reverts; conditional density-p trait remains after R reset |
| Selection or scaffold truly withdrawn | **No**: manual switching/transfer, droplet creation, ongoing cultures | **No**: patch graph, renewal, death/dispersal rules remain |
| Demonstrated independent, viable, unassisted collective reproductive closure | **Not established** | **Not an empirical test** |
| Author independence | Harvard yeast experimental system | Distinct modeling program; overlaps earlier Pass 32 researcher network |

Both are valuable **partial** demonstrations, but **do not establish the stronger compound endpoint** of self-generated propagules founding independently viable daughter collectives after sorting, phase timing, habitat preparation and rescue end. The studies do not prove this compound endpoint is impossible. In particular, **living systems need resource input**: natural nutrient uptake and environmental energy are *not*, by themselves, experimental manipulations. The unresolved issue is who controls propagule selection, transfer/dispersion, habitat assembly, intervention withdrawal and multi-generation descendant recruitment.

### Falsifiable future-only hypotheses (untested)

- **P34-H1 — eco-regulated switching versus imposed timing:** a strain equipped with a switch may win under externally synchronized β-estradiol cycles but not repeatedly colonize fresh patches when those cycles are removed. **Reject** if prospectively counted descendants autonomously regulate switching and establish daughter populations in an unsorted environment over multiple generations without investigator switching, transferring, or rescue, relative to fixed-state matched controls.
- **P34-H2 — parameter reversal versus actual scaffold elimination:** hysteresis after restoring patch-size parameters will not predict retention of collective reproduction if authored patch segmentation and renewing resource slots are simultaneously removed. **Reject** if independent lineage cohorts retain coordinated daughter production and multi-generation net establishment when experimentally imposed patch boundaries and renewal schedules end, with natural resource availability retained and all failed founders counted.
- **P34-H3 — dispersal advantage can hide reproductive weakness:** single cells may access more droplets even when no clone produces independently persistent multicellular descendants. **Reject** if lineage-resolved per-founder fitness and non-selected daughter re-establishment rates show a causal benefit of the evolved multicellular-plus-dispersal mechanism under an independently varying patch gradient, not merely aggregate fluorescence-event counts.

**Next highest-information primary literature priority:** independently replicated passive patch colonisation where whole parent-to-daughter collective lineages can be traced, endogenous switching is not induced by an operator, the habitat already exists without engineered transfer, total descendant lifetime growth exceeds replacement, and extinct founders are retained. Include a comparison against the 2024 mathematical *density-sensing* mechanism without treating the model as biological proof.
