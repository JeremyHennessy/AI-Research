# Pass 36 — Sequence-dependent compartment growth is not inherited reproductive closure

**9 October 2026 | Research only | Initial main:** `5a3be2398ccc86162b30762d304f20f12badf31e`. Distinct follow-on to [Pass 35](128-pass35-genome-compartment-reproduction-closure.md). This review compares DNA-induced droplet growth, peptide-dependent membrane enlargement, and reported division driven by membrane flip-flop. A full artificial life-cycle would require inherited functional variation, compatible parent-to-daughter compartment formation, autonomous reproductive transmission and net successful descendants. These studies test different partial gates, **not independent replications of one system**.

## A. Minagawa, Yabuta, Su'etsugu & Noji (2025): DNA can enlarge a phase-separated droplet

**Primary:** *Self-growing protocell models in aqueous two-phase system induced by internal DNA replication reaction*, *Nature Communications* 16:1522. [DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-56172-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56172-7); [university explanation](https://www.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/press/pr2025-02-27-001). **E2-depth indexed original main-text/results and publisher abstract**, not independent raw-data or supplement reanalysis: the publisher page was indexed but later direct opening prompted an identity-verification redirect. The available main-text excerpts, publisher abstract and institutional account agree on the key growth findings. Nature Communications states open-access CC BY 4.0 on the indexed publication; no third-party figures were copied.

An aqueous polyethylene glycol/dextran two-phase system forms droplets **without a lipid membrane**. Internal DNA replication is associated with enlarged DEX-rich droplets, consistent with polymer-increased stabilization/phase separation. The authors demonstrated DNA amplification conditions giving >10-fold volume expansion, while a **cell-free transcription/translation–coupled recursive gene-replication condition** caused roughly **1.8-fold expansion over 280 minutes**. This distinction matters: do not describe 10-fold simple growth as the 1.8-fold self-encoded replication protocol. Core machinery and reagents, phase-separation architecture and external maintenance were supplied. Growth of one DNA-containing droplet is not evidence it buds viable daughter droplets carrying the same functional genome.

**Limitations and counterhypotheses:** passive osmotic/phase-equilibrium changes versus a self-sustaining intergenerational organization; external enzymes and nucleotide supply versus endogenous resource recovery; droplet size versus counted daughter founders; no demonstrated mutation–selection across independently traced droplets. Without direct offspring identities or all failed founder counts, "self-growing" should not be read as fully self-reproducing. Not an independent reproduction of Abil et al. 2024 (Pass 35) because the latter uses supplied lipid vesicles, Phi29 self-replication and externally reconstituted cycles.

## B. Baba, Yokoyama, Sato et al. (2026): peptide sequence affects fed fatty-acid vesicle growth

**Primary:** *Growth of fatty acid vesicles coupled with amino acid sequences of peptides toward evolvable protocells*, *Communications Chemistry* 9:234. [DOI 10.1038/s42004-026-02043-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-026-02043-1), [indexed full primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13338293/). **E2 main-text-depth primary Results and Methods/Conclusions**, not supplementary table/raw microscopy independent replication. Main publication CC BY 4.0, subject to third-party exceptions.

Investigators **synthesized and supplied peptides with selected amino-acid sequences**, added decanoate/decanoic-acid molecules to pre-existing vesicles, and measured changes using dynamic light scattering and cryogenic electron microscopy. Specific short peptides promoted vesicle growth; sequence substitutions could have non-additive consequences (**epistasis**), with the same residue change having different effects in different backgrounds. Distinct motif effects persisted in some longer peptides. The physical mechanism involves changes in fatty-acid chemical potential induced by peptide association. It is a **measured physicochemical sequence-to-growth relation**, not sequence reproduction.

**Important negative controls/limitations:** several peptides or residue substitutions exhibited negligible/negative growth rather than a universal gain. Dynamic light scattering size response is a proxy for vesicle growth; it cannot be equated to population-level lifetime fitness or successful compartment division. Peptides were externally prepared and **not replicated or genetically inherited**. The authors explicitly state the system does not yet achieve Darwinian evolution, despite a structured sequence–growth landscape. An apparent "fitness landscape" built from designed peptide variants is not observed evolving lineages. No offspring genetic units or multigenerational ecological closure were measured. Distinct chemistry and group from Minagawa, and not a replication of Abil's genetic selection results.

## C. Lira, Van Tilburg, Marrink & Dekker (2026): membrane division as a separate partial gate

**Primary:** *Flip Flop-Associated Growth and Division of Synthetic Cells*, *ACS Nano* 20(31):21723–21738, published online 29 July 2026. [DOI 10.1021/acsnano.6c03241](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c03241); [publisher record](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acsnano.6c03241). **E1 only**: indexed publisher bibliographic page and accessible high-level summaries; full methods, controls, supplementary figures and raw datasets were not retrieved here (the direct publisher page returned 403). The publisher page states © 2026 authors, but a usable grant of redistribution rights for specific figures/supplemental data was **not independently inspected**. No restricted content reused.

The paper addresses membrane growth/division associated with lipid flip-flop; it is a comparator for whether compartment structural dynamics alone close a reproductive cycle. **Do not assert** that every parent generated viable daughters, that genotype and fitness were inherited, or that the system was autonomous across generations based on the available record. The detailed intervention controls, negative outcomes, exact unit denominators, and active-versus-passive budding mechanism remain **unverified**, so this is not graded E2 or direct empirical confirmation of the other papers.

**Synthesis.** DNA-driven droplet expansion, peptide-dependent vesicle growth and lipid-mediated vesicle division are conceptually complementary. Combining independent claims *on paper* is not experimentally demonstrating the compound system. Existing measured "fitness" may be droplet volume or membrane size rather than a tracked lineage's descendant success; source rights, failure cases, system independence, and unremoved apparatus must remain visible. Environmental resource/energy availability itself is not proof of human-imposed reproductive control; the decisive missing test is **transmission without experimenter-selected replenishment, transfer, or replacement of founders**.

## Falsifiable future-only research hypotheses — no experiments conducted

- **P36-H1:** Genotype-driven droplet swelling can occur without stable parent-to-daughter inherited function. Reject if independently tracked genotype-marked growing droplets generate natural daughter droplets that carry the causal encoded variant and repeat functional growth across prospectively defined generations, including failures, without programmed phase splitting or re-encapsulation.
- **P36-H2:** Externally supplied peptide sequence may predict vesicle size response yet not produce heritable lineage fitness. Reject if distinct **self-generated**, transmitted peptide sequences causally predict net viable descendant vesicle formation over repeated generations with controlled nutrient/chemical potentials, founder counts and competing conformational explanations.
- **P36-H3:** Membrane budding/division can remain uncoupled from genomic inheritance and viability. Reject if prospectively identified daughter compartments inherit function and persist through subsequent reproductive cycles without artificial founder rescue or human-scheduled membrane splitting, with failures quantified and observer-blinded controls.
