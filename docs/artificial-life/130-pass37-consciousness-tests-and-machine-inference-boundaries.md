# Pass 37 — What consciousness theory tests can establish, and why machine inference remains open

**9 October 2026 | Primary-source critical review | Research only.** Starting main: 1a7d572d5f11b064478de1321502a7a7366f8a67. This review publishes the analysis preserved in the previously unpublished Pass 37 research package. It asks a question distinct from the reproductive-closure studies of Passes 29–36: can preregistered neural theories, report-independent ERP signals and information-integration metrics establish robust correlates of *biological* consciousness, and can those correlates legitimately be transferred to artificial systems? These are separate questions. **No experiments, simulations, models, agents or organisms were created, trained or executed for this literature review.**

**Evidence boundary.** Each E2 rating below means the original publicly available Methods, Results and Discussion were critically reviewed. It is **not** independent raw-data reanalysis, independent scientific replication, or E3 validation. Original supplementary figures, analysis code and original raw data were not rerun. The three papers test distinct hypotheses and must not be counted as direct replications of one another. A negative prediction about a proposed brain implementation does not refute every formulation of a philosophical theory, and a brain signal associated with human awareness does not provide a calibrated ground-truth label for subjective experience in machines.

## A. Cogitate Consortium / Ferrante et al. (2025): adversarial IIT and GNWT predictions

**Primary:** *Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness*, Nature 642, 133–142; published 30 April 2025. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1; primary article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1. **E2-depth original main Methods/Results/Discussion.** Original article CC BY 4.0 with credited third-party exceptions; nothing reproduced from original figures, supplements or datasets.

**Protocol and independent units.** A theory-neutral consortium and proponents of integrated information theory (IIT) and global neuronal workspace theory (GNWT) preregistered differing predictions, observation windows and interpretation rules. **256 human individuals** viewed suprathreshold visual stimuli of different durations. There were three distinct *measurement groups*, **120 fMRI, 102 MEG and 34 intracranial EEG (iEEG) participants**. The modality samples are **not three replications of machine consciousness**, and multiple electrodes/recordings within an individual are not independent participant units. An optimization subsample preceded confirmatory testing; not every exploratory local effect can be described as a preregistered final-sample prediction.

**Positive observations.** Cortical representations of category and duration arose across visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal areas. Sustained activity in occipital and lateral temporal cortex reflected stimulus duration; some content-specific synchronization was observed between frontal and early visual areas. These observations support parts of the preregistered predictions. A simple “both theories failed completely” claim would be false.

**Disconfirming observations and alternative explanations.** The specified sustained posterior synchronization was not found as predicted by the evaluated implementation of IIT. GNWT's specifically timed stimulus-offset prefrontal ignition and certain prefrontal content representations were weaker/absent than predicted. Neither theory received an uncomplicated victory. Intracranial sampling reflects clinically determined electrode positions; stimulus category, attention and task demands confound pure phenomenal interpretation. Suprathreshold presentation produces experienced-content contrasts, not a stringent trial-wise experienced-versus-unexperienced split. Rival theory tests are valuable falsification exercises, not independent phenomenal ground truth for artificial agents.

## B. Cohen, Dembski et al. (2024): no-report EEG and the report confound

**Primary:** *Neural signatures of visual awareness independent of postperceptual processing*, Cerebral Cortex 34(11):bhae415; journal publication 13 November 2024. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhae415; full original article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11558846/. **E2 original main text.** CC BY-NC 4.0; noncommercial reproduction is license-bound and commercial redistribution requires separate rights. No OSF data or task code downloaded or reused.

**Methods and denominator.** Masked visual faces were shown with stimulus-to-mask separations of **17, 33, 50, 67 and 83 milliseconds**. In the report condition subjects detected a face; in no-report blocks their task was to detect occasional **green rings** while face information was irrelevant. Of **56 recruited participants**, the first **15** were used to choose ERP windows/electrodes and excluded from the final confirmatory analysis; **eight** suffered technical EEG issues and **eight** were excluded for preregistered criteria, leaving **25 confirmatory participants**. Exploratory analyses can include the original pilot and 25 final participants (**n=40**), but that is not a separately preregistered confirmatory cohort of forty. Final windows/electrodes were preregistered after pilot optimization and before new final-sample acquisition.

**Positive measurement.** A fronto-central **N2 response at approximately 250–290 ms** bifurcated with the non-linear visibility transition in the *no-report* task. Earlier P1 activity reflected sensory strength more linearly; later P3b activity and sustained decoding were more tied to explicit task reporting. This supports N2 as a candidate correlate not wholly explained by a requirement to *report* the face.

**Negative controls, proxy limitations and counterhypotheses.** N170/visual-awareness-negativity did **not** show the corresponding clear non-linear bifurcation under these settings. No-report blocks contain **no trial-by-trial subjective report** of face awareness; visibility is inferred from a separate report condition, and subjects could covertly recognize/categorize faces anyway. Attention differences, mask-ERP subtraction, spontaneous categorization, age range and limited confirmatory sample restrict generality. A candidate correlate cannot be promoted to a necessary, sufficient or substrate-independent consciousness detector. This is not a direct independent replication of the Cogitate suprathreshold protocol.

## C. Luppi, Uhrig, Tasserie et al. (2026): information integration under anaesthesia

**Primary:** *Convergent transcriptomic and connectomic controllers of information integration and its anaesthetic breakdown across mammalian brains*, Nature Human Behaviour 10:777–802; first published **28 January 2026**. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02381-5; original: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02381-5. **E2 primary Results, Methods and Discussion; no independent source-data/supplement or original-model execution.** CC BY 4.0 main article with credited media exceptions.

**Comparative evidence and actual units.** The authors analyzed fMRI time series during waking and anaesthesia across **humans, macaques, marmosets and mice**, measuring information integration with a revised statistical decomposition metric, **Phi_R**, and contrasting it with a previous **Phi_2008** formula. Phi_R is a particular *pairwise time-series information calculation*, not automatically equivalent to the full current axiomatic IIT account or a definition of subjective experience. Human paired observations involved **15 individuals**. Macaque conditions included **11–24 imaging runs from 2–3 animals each**; marmosets contributed **48 runs from four animals per condition**. Imaging runs from the same animal must not be treated as independent biological individuals; other species and anesthetic protocols are heterogeneous. The authors constructed their own connectivity/transcriptomic models, but this review performed no simulation.

**Positive results.** Phi_R tended to decrease under diverse anaesthetic conditions and across biological species, and to return toward waking levels upon recovery. In macaques, thalamic deep-brain stimulation during continued anaesthetic delivery changed responsiveness and network integration, adding a stimulation-linked intervention contrast. Patterns correlated with species-specific PVALB/Pvalb expression maps. Correlation of maps is not causal proof that one particular gene determines experience.

**Important failures and inferential limits.** The older Phi_2008 metric was **unchanged** under some human and macaque anaesthetic comparisons and **increased** under some mouse/marmoset conditions; not every appealing “integration” statistic is a stable state marker. Pairwise whole-brain averaging can miss higher-order interactions. BOLD haemodynamics, drugs, movement, and behavior can jointly affect the physiological proxy. Unresponsiveness can reflect altered motor/sensory access rather than strictly zero experience. Neither the original authors' models nor an anesthetic-state classifier provide an independent AI consciousness label. The authors' multi-species comparisons are important biological evidence but not independent repetitions of Cohen's no-report EEG contrast.

## Three-study comparison and boundary

| Gate | Cogitate 2025 | Cohen 2024 | Luppi 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct human experience report | Visual task/behavioral judgments, not pure direct qualia measurement | Report task, not during individual no-report trials | Behavioral/clinical responsiveness; animals cannot provide human verbal reports |
| Main observable | Cortical timing/localization/synchrony | Masked-visual N2, P1, P3b | State-dependent brain Phi_R and stimulation |
| Disconfirming result | Central implementation predictions for IIT and GNWT challenged | P3b largely report-sensitive; N170 lacked clear bifurcation | Older Phi_2008 inconsistent across anesthesia regimes |
| Independence | One preregistered multimodal consortium | Separate EEG paradigm, not same-method replication | Distinct fMRI/anaesthesia protocols and author-built models |
| Calibrated machine-phenomenology ground truth | **Absent** | **Absent** | **Absent** |

**Conclusion.** Pre-registration, explicit competing forecasts, carefully separated measurement units and serious attention to negative findings improve *testability*. The three studies do **not** establish that neural processing is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, that one specified theory has been decisively proved/disproved, or that any machine is or is not conscious. Simulated neuron-like connectivity, integrated information, self-reports of consciousness or sophisticated output in an LLM/agent are not evidence of subjective experience without an independently justified link between proxy and target. Conversely the results do not prove that artificial consciousness is impossible. The only currently justified generalization to AI research is **methodological discipline**, not transfer of biological thresholds.

## Future-only falsifiable research hypotheses (no experiments performed)

- **P37-H1 — no-report confounds.** The Cohen N2 visibility bifurcation may diminish under independently pre-registered controls minimizing covert categorization, while holding sensory strength and attentional demands constant. Reject if blinded independent cohorts repeatedly demonstrate the same N2 bifurcation with strong within-subject visibility calibration and effective no-report controls. This tests an ERP *interpretation*, not consciousness itself.
- **P37-H2 — robustness of preregistered implementation predictions.** Sustained posterior synchrony and PFC offset-ignition predictions may fail to generalize jointly across independent cohorts, stimuli and measurement modalities when analyses are frozen prospectively. Reject if independent multicenter studies repeatedly obtain both precommitted signatures with adequate sensitivity and matched attention. Such a result supports those predictions, not an entire philosophical ontology.
- **P37-H3 — proxy transfer under distribution shift.** Brain Phi_R or ERP-derived consciousness classifiers may fail across drug states and species when physiology is not explicitly calibrated. Reject if blinded, independently registered multi-cohort cross-state tests remain accurately calibrated after matching movement, sensory/motor access, drugs and signal quality. This is a proxy-validity hypothesis **not** a test of machine experience.

**Next research priority:** seek truly independent tests and contradictory results for no-report neural signatures, biological state indices and preregistered rival-theory predictions. Establish which measurements actually replicate under protocol differences and which merely recapitulate the same researchers' data. Explicitly separate discrimination of responsiveness from inferring subjective experience.
