# Pass 38 — No-report awareness classifiers versus dream-level neural complexity

**9 October 2026 | Critical review | Research only.** Base main `c092807be4bfa040bac0e0ff3a37bc831c5799ec`. Extends [Pass 37](130-pass37-consciousness-tests-and-machine-inference-boundaries.md) without repeating its three studies. This pass tests the **independence and transportability of consciousness proxies**: whether a no-report visual-neural signature generalizes to other paradigms, and whether within-sedation neural complexity can discriminate a participant's later report of subjective experience. These are *different scientific constructs*, not attempted literal replications. No experiment, model, simulation, agent, animal, artificial organism or other repository was executed or altered by this review.

## A. Kronemer, Aksen et al. (2022): no-report visual recognition depends on a covert behavioral classifier

**Primary:** *Human visual consciousness involves large scale cortical and subcortical networks independent of task report and eye movement activity*, Nature Communications 13:7342, published 29 November 2022. [DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-35117-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-35117-4); [publisher full original](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35117-4); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9707162/). **E2 primary original main Methods/Results/Discussion and figure legends**, not an independent reproduction of source data, supplemental eye-classifier details, or original analyses. Main article **CC BY 4.0**, third-party imagery exceptions; this document includes no publisher figures, copyrighted source text, raw recordings, or original software.

**Protocol and measurement units.** The experiment exposed participants to physically identical **50 ms face images calibrated near individual 50% perception threshold**, then distinguished consciously perceived/not perceived trials using either overt report or a **covert classifier inferred from pupil dilation, blink rate and microsaccade rate**. Participants performed an alternating/report-plus-no-report paradigm in which some otherwise similar face stimuli were irrelevant to the instructed report. The broader study reports **144 healthy recruited adults** across several datasets, **seven patients with clinical depth electrodes**, and detailed overlapping modality cohorts. Separate no-report EEG and fMRI datasets each show **N=65**, but these are protocol/modality counts, *not 130 independently validated conscious-individual labels* nor repetitions of a wholly independent study. A figure describes **N=68** for a specific eye-signature example while EEG final analysis lists **N=65**; avoid silently treating all numbers as the same denominator. Clinical depth-electrode participants were tested in a **report** paradigm, not a representative no-report generalization cohort.

**Positive result.** A visual awareness negativity (**VAN**) distinguished covertly classified perceived versus not perceived threshold faces under both report and no-report conditions. P2/N2 and later P3/late potential contrasts were stronger when subjects overtly reported the stimulus. Report-free fMRI detected an extensive cortical/subcortical network spanning visual, thalamic, arousal/salience, frontoparietal and default-mode changes. Sorting/contrasting by pupil, blink and microsaccade responses reduced some eye-movement-associated effects; much of the distributed response remained. Thus the authors did more than observe an uncorrected pupil-linked correlation.

**Disconfirming and inferential boundaries.** “No overt report” does **not** mean the study objectively knows each stimulus's phenomenal status. The *perceived/unperceived label itself* is estimated from physiological signs trained/validated against overt judgments. Pupillary and oculomotor events can covary with arousal, attention, salience and task preparation, creating a shared-method confound between inferred awareness and neural measurements. The authors also acknowledge covert voluntary report/postperceptual processing is not excluded. fMRI hemodynamic and EEG temporal resolutions differ, clinical thalamic recordings sample selected patients, and group-level images do not establish that every listed cortical structure is necessary for subjective experience. Positive results resist **specific** ocular controls; they do not rule out all systemic physiological or attention explanations.

**Pass 37 connection, not a duplicate paper review.** Cohen et al. 2024 measured an N2 around 250–290 ms in a separate *masked-face* no-report task, whereas Kronemer measured a more report-independent earlier VAN and comparatively report-sensitive P2/N2 and later responses. These results are **not a contradiction at a fixed neural generator or a preregistered replication**: stimulus contrast, visibility calibration, timing, attention instructions, trial labels and ERP subtraction methods differ. Crucially, **Michael Pitts is a coauthor of both 2022 Kronemer and 2024 Cohen**, and Hal Blumenfeld/Michael Pitts appear in the 2025 Cogitate author list: overlapping scientific networks should not be represented as completely independent research teams even though the experiments are separate.

## B. Bajwa, Nilsen, Skukies et al. (2025): negative dream-proxy discrimination within sedation

**Primary:** *A repeated awakening study exploring the capacity of complexity measures to capture dreaming during propofol sedation*, Scientific Reports 15:32746, published **24 September 2025**. [DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-12695-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-12695-z); [publisher primary Methods/Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-12695-z); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12460656/). **E2 complete main Methods/Results/Discussion**, but supplemental protocols and raw EEG/TMS signals/code were not downloaded or rerun, and there is no independent E3 scientific reproduction. Main article **CC BY 4.0**, credited third-party materials separately restricted.

**Protocol and independent units.** **Twenty healthy participants** underwent individually titrated propofol sedation; the researchers recorded spontaneous EEG and TMS-evoked EEG, and repeatedly attempted to awaken each participant to obtain *immediate retrospective reports*. Among **52 attempted awakenings**, 24 were eventually classed as evidence for experience/dreaming, **five** as no experience, and **23** as insufficient information (unresponsive or unclear). These are **52 correlated episodes within 20 people**, not 52 independent people. Some subjects were awakened only once/twice; three did not complete TMS recordings because of motor-threshold, artifact or stimulus-target limitations. The original article separately accounts for 92 spontaneous EEG recordings (40 waking, 52 sedated) and a smaller usable TMS subset. Neither missing reports nor poor TMS quality are equivalent to scientifically verified absence of consciousness.

**Predeclared positive and negative contrasts.** Both perturbational complexity index of state transitions (**PCIst**) and spontaneous EEG single-channel Lempel–Ziv complexity (**LZc**) were lower overall during propofol sedation than ordinary waking, matching expected *state-level* differences. Yet, **inside the sedated state**, experience-report classification was **not a significant predictor** of PCIst (**F=0.536, p=0.590**) or LZc (**F=0.727, p=0.489**) in the authors' mixed-effects analyses. These negative findings are not a declaration that dreaming and non-dreaming are biologically identical: five negative reports, 23 uninformative awakenings, limited within-person sampling and noise constrain sensitivity. EEG effects may be driven by anaesthetic depth, arousal or ability to recall/report rather than experience itself. The authors explicitly separate *state* from *content/experience* classification.

**Methodological counterhypotheses.** A “no-dream” answer may reflect memory failure and propofol's amnesic effects, not proof of absent experience. Deliberate awakening perturbs the system being measured; timing between EEG windows, stimulation and recollection matters. Small and imbalanced labels, nonrandom missingness, movement/muscle artifacts, drug dose variation, imperfect sedation stability and TMS quality exclusions can reduce ability to detect a true effect. Conversely, aggregate wake-versus-sedation classification may appear strong merely because drug physiology is distinct. Sensitivity of a measure to drug-induced brain state is **not sufficient evidence** that it tracks subjective content conditional on the same state.

**Independence and prior work.** This Oslo/Kongsberg author group and propofol protocol are distinct from Kronemer's Yale threshold-visual network and the Cogitate/Cohen report studies. Authors discuss earlier related sleep/dream analyses by **Aamodt/Nilsen/Juel**, some with author overlap; those should not be pooled as multiple wholly independent confirmations. A negative difference under their *specific* sedation study does not refute all cortical-complexity theories. It directly challenges promoting wakefulness/disconnection proxies into universal experience classifiers.

## C. Cross-system tests: what actually transfers?

| Gate | Kronemer 2022 (threshold vision) | Bajwa 2025 (deep propofol) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biological unit | Healthy cohorts including N=65 no-report EEG and N=65 no-report fMRI, overlapping recruitment; special clinical depth cohort | 20 humans; 52 correlated awakening episodes, 24 experience/5 no-experience/23 indeterminate |
| Observable | Pupil/blink/microsaccade-classified visibility, VAN and distributed fMRI | PCIst, LZc and immediate post-awakening dream reports |
| Positive | Earlier VAN and broad network under report-irrelevant stimuli and some ocular controls | Both complexity proxies distinguish waking versus sedated physiology |
| Negative/limit | Later P2/N2 and P3 report-sensitive; physiologically inferred labels may confound neural response | Neither PCIst nor LZc distinguishes dream-report categories in sedated state at tested power |
| Strict independent replication of same consciousness construct | **No** | **No** |
| Validated proof of phenomenal AI consciousness | **No** | **No** |

**Evidence-supported synthesis.** The studies teach a sharp operational lesson: removing overt report can replace one confound with a *covert classifier* (eyes, attention, task salience), while an apparently effective state classifier can fail to distinguish reports of experience *within* the same state. This is a challenge to simple one-number/binary consciousness tests, not proof no neural correlate exists. Differences in timing, condition, physiology, authors and proxy label mean they are scientifically complementary, not one positive and one negative replication of the same assay.

**Relevance to AI/artificial life:** No physiological EEG/fMRI proxy here has independently established construct validity for arbitrary nonbiological systems. Large recurrent computation, a global workspace, high entropy, self-reported “experience,” responsive behavior or an artificial agent's silence cannot alone serve as a verified subjectivity label. Absence of evidence is not a demonstration of impossibility. The work's transferable result is rigorous separation of observed functional indicators, confounds, denominators, independence and phenomenal interpretation.

## Future-only falsifiable hypotheses (NOT experiments performed)

- **P38-H1 — classifier dependence:** No-report neural visibility effects trained on pupil/blink/microsaccade labels may partly track covert attention and arousal. **Reject** if independent blinded cohorts retain a precalibrated awareness-specific neural effect after simultaneously controlling attentional load, pupil dynamics and sensory strength with independently verified trial-wise subjective labels collected without contaminating the primary no-report condition. This tests a proxy, not consciousness.
- **P38-H2 — state versus experience discrimination:** PCIst/LZc will generalize well to wake-versus-propofol comparisons while showing weak calibrated discrimination of different experience reports *within one matched sedation state*. **Reject** if independent high-powered, within-person awakening cohorts with balanced credible reports, matched drug dose/arousal and preregistered analysis distinguish experiences beyond chance and maintain external calibration. This does not prove that absent recall equals absent experience.
- **P38-H3 — false pooled replication:** Classifiers/ERP latencies reported as no-report “awareness” across different paradigms will not be transportable under fixed thresholds without task-specific recalibration. **Reject** if blinded multisite validation with frozen models and verified independent participant/author programs reproduces the relevant localization, latency, negative controls and classification using common ground-truth rules. A positive result would improve biological proxy reliability, not independently determine machine phenomenology.

**Next priority:** critically audit further genuinely independent *within-state* dream reports, emerging 2026 experimental comparisons of general anaesthesia and consciousness, and the pre-registration/analysis selection governing VAN versus N2. Prefer negative and mixed outcomes over extra paper counts; distinguish participant from episode, state from content and phenotype from subjective experience. No experiment or system implementation authorized here.
