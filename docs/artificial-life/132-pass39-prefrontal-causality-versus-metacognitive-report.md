# Pass 39 — Prefrontal intervention and lesion evidence: metacognitive calibration is not phenomenal necessity

**9 October 2026 | Research-only source-critical review.** Prepared against live `main` `ce044ee372356a7dfd21b7b1edbf1898565ab2f5`, after verified Pass 38 main-push workflow 37964655625. This is a distinct question from Passes 37–38's neural decoding and no-report correlates: do causal perturbation and focal lesion findings show that prefrontal tissue is *necessary* for experience, or do they more narrowly change the relation between objective performance and a person's confidence in that performance? No participant recruited; no clinical intervention, experiment, model, simulator, organism, agent, scientific code or source data run, reproduced or changed.

## Research question and measurement gates

Separate five inferential levels: **objective discrimination (type-I performance)**, **confidence/visibility response bias**, **metacognitive sensitivity** (ability to distinguish one's correct versus incorrect judgments, often expressed as meta-d'), **phenomenal experience** (not observed directly), and **causal dependence** (requiring a reliable selective intervention or lesion contrast plus adequate controls). Changes in metacognitive confidence can occur while sensory discrimination remains stable; that would show a relation to self-evaluation but **not uniquely prove** that subjective experience was removed. Likewise, a temporary TMS null does not prove that prefrontal cortex is never needed.

## A. Fleming, Ryu, Golfinos & Blackmon (2014): focal anterior PFC lesions and domain-specific metacognition

**Primary:** *Domain-specific impairment in metacognitive accuracy following anterior prefrontal lesions*, *Brain* 137(10), 2811–2822. Published online 6 August 2014; DOI `10.1093/brain/awu221`; original main article: https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/137/10/2811/2847667. **E2 original main Methods/Results/Discussion.** Oxford University Press article copyright © Author 2014; exact permitted republication rights not established, therefore only brief DOI metadata and original critical paraphrase here.

**Participants and causal exposure.** This observational neuropsychology comparison involved **seven** patients with anterior PFC lesions, **eleven** patients with temporal-lobe lesions, and **nineteen** neurologically intact controls. Lesions followed surgical procedures for tumours and/or epilepsy, assessed in a *chronic* period at least **six months after surgery**. Structural MRI maps and neuropsychological screening helped characterize groups, but the location, extent, clinical history, presurgical deficits, chronic compensation and patient recruitment were not experimentally randomized. The brain lesion target is *anterior PFC*, not necessarily the same dorsolateral prefrontal TMS coordinates studied in Rounis/Bor.

**Positive contrast.** Accuracy-matched perceptual and memory tasks measured subjective confidence in correct versus incorrect choices, summarized by **meta-d'/d'**, a signal-detection-theoretic estimate intended to adjust for task-discrimination differences. The anterior PFC lesion group had impaired **perceptual** metacognitive efficiency (95% CI **0.28–0.64**) but not correspondingly impaired **memory** metacognitive efficiency (95% CI **0.78–1.29**). Temporal-lobe comparison patients were not impaired in either domain. The dissociation supports a domain-specific contribution of anterior PFC to evaluating perceptual decisions, rather than a uniform loss of sensory accuracy or every kind of introspection.

**Counterhypotheses and limits.** **n=7** anterior-lesion patients is a sparse biological denominator; lesions differed in size/topography and were not induced under random assignment. Chronic reorganization or broader connected-tract damage could influence compensation. Confidence calibration is not direct measurement of phenomenal contents: participants with impaired meta-d' still made visual discriminations. The study supports a causal-anatomical association with *perceptual metacognition*, **not** proof of PFC sufficiency or necessity for all consciousness. No individual lesion image, original data or metadata was reanalyzed.

## B. Rounis, Maniscalco, Rothwell, Passingham & Lau (2010): positive bilateral DLPFC cTBS result with original-methods access limits

**Primary:** *Theta-burst transcranial magnetic stimulation to the prefrontal cortex impairs metacognitive visual awareness*, *Cognitive Neuroscience* 1(3), 165–175, online 23 March 2010. DOI `10.1080/17588921003632529`; original publisher: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17588921003632529. **E1 original abstract/publisher metadata plus E2 Bor 2017 methodological comparison**, **not** an independent E2 review of full Rounis manuscript (publisher direct full text not available in this run). Do not promote the prior study beyond inspected access. Publisher rights are not assumed permissive; original paraphrase only.

**Claim:** Bilateral dorsolateral PFC continuous theta-burst transcranial magnetic stimulation (**cTBS**) versus sham produced lower visibility/confidence sensitivity for correct masked visual judgments while leaving objective stimulus-discrimination performance stable, interpreted as a selective reduction in metacognitive sensitivity. The original cohort had **20 participants** according to Bor et al.'s explicit comparison; this is **not** a new cohort collected by this review. The original authors used subjective clarity labels; this is not the same operational outcome as a universal machine-consciousness test.

**Critical dependency:** Bor et al. received original-study summary data and found **13/20** original participants had at least one experimental block with a signal-detection hit or false-alarm rate outside **0.05–0.95**. Under their predeclared validity criteria, only **7/20** would have qualified for their analysis. This is a **methodological reanalysis critique by another author group**, not an independently performed rerun here or a proof that Rounis's original result is mathematically false; classification and exclusion choices may also affect conclusions. The apparent effect is **disputed**, not unilaterally erased.

## C. Bor, Schwartzman, Barrett & Seth (2017): attempted replication with active controls and two null experiments

**Primary:** *Theta-burst transcranial magnetic stimulation to the prefrontal or parietal cortex does not impair metacognitive visual awareness*, *PLOS ONE* 12(2), e0171793; published 13 February 2017. DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0171793`; full original: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0171793. **E2 complete publicly accessible main Methods/Results/Discussion**, publisher **CC BY 4.0**; critical paraphrase only, no figures or original data republished. Original dataset openly linked at figshare by authors but **not** downloaded/analyzed here.

**Experiment 1: denominator and controls.** **90 volunteers recruited** into five **between-person** groups: bilateral DLPFC, bilateral posterior parietal cortex, left/right mixed PFC/PPC, and an **active vertex control**. The authors tried to replicate the masked binary perception task with stimulus contrast staircased near **75% objective accuracy**, pre/post **cTBS** and metacognitive estimates from confidence–accuracy correspondence (meta-d', type-II d', accuracy-confidence correlation). Their comparison differed meaningfully from Rounis: confidence versus relative clarity labels, *active vertex control* versus sham, altered training/contrast stabilization, and **exclusion of extreme SDT hit or false-alarm rates** (<0.05 or >0.95). The 90 recruits are not 90 valid participants in every endpoint: **27/90** had extreme SDT values and other exclusions applied. On the reported interaction, the final degrees of freedom were F(4,48), consistent with approximately **53 analyzable participants across five arms**; the exact group denominators must be read from their Table 1 rather than inferred as 90 independent endpoints.

**Negative result:** no evidence for a site × pre/post interaction in meta-d'−d' (F(4,48)=**0.06**, p>0.1), type-II d', or accuracy-confidence association; control comparisons and Bayesian checks were broadly compatible with no effect. This is an independent **nonreplication of a specified cTBS metacognition effect**, not a proof of “no prefrontal role in consciousness.” Within-arm sample sizes of roughly 11 mean some nulls are weakly informative, and exact matching of stimulation delivery was not independently physiologically established.

**Experiment 2: not a fully crossed randomized repeated design.** An additional **27 recruits** received bilateral DLPFC cTBS first, with later **vertex and return sessions contingent on the magnitude of the previous day's change**. Ten were excluded for unstable SDT measures, leaving **17 evaluable first sessions**; ten of these 17 had no large metacognitive change and were never retested. Of seven possible responders, **three worsened and four improved**, and most showed vertex changes too. This **response-triggered conditional follow-up** is more focused on individual reproducibility, but it is not an ordinary balanced randomized within-person crossover. No one produced the predicted selective reproducible DLPFC impairment absent with vertex across valid return sessions. Mean meta-d'−d' change was **+0.07** (the original Rounis result was about **−0.35** by their comparison). Combining relevant DLPFC first sessions with experiment 1 gave **t(31)=0.14, one-tailed p=0.44**, Bayes factor **0.34**, near—but not deeply beyond—their threshold for evidence for null.

**Critique and boundaries.** The 2017 team showed that adding *unstable* estimates generated a spurious significant difference, notably in a control condition. However, strict exclusions can also select unusual participants; different confidence labels and calibration procedures mean their replication is **not exact**, and cTBS 5 cm anterior to a motor hotspot is not a fully MRI-neuronavigated verified DLPFC perturbation. A null effect from this protocol cannot exclude other intervention timings, targets or metacognitive domains. Authors overlap neither the Rounis experimenters nor the Fleming lesion investigators, but stimulation and chronic lesions probe different structures, timescales and operational readouts.

## D. Evidence reconciliation and relevance to adaptive AI

| Gate | Fleming 2014 | Rounis 2010 | Bor 2017 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Study type | Chronic post-surgical anatomy with observational patient groups | Acute bilateral DLPFC cTBS vs sham | Two attempts at cTBS effect, active vertex controls |
| Evidence depth in this review | E2 original main | **E1 original abstract**, original n from Bor methodological report | **E2 original full main** |
| Denominator | n=7 anterior PFC; 11 temporal-lobe; 19 controls | n=20, 13/20 deemed SDT-unstable by Bor rule | Exp1 n=90 recruited / about 53 evaluable; Exp2 n=27 / **17 evaluable** |
| Positive evidence | Selective perceptual metacognitive deficit (memory spared) | Reported reduction of metacognitive sensitivity, objective discrimination stable | Active-control and data-quality checks improve causal interpretation |
| Negative or disputed | Small, heterogeneous chronic lesion group; no phenomenal measure | SDT instability, sham control and different confidence labels | No replicable selective metacognitive deficit across protocols; cTBS suppression not independently proven |
| What it **does not** show | PFC necessary for all experience | Consciousness eliminated by perturbation | PFC never relevant to metacognition or experience |

**Scientific conclusion:** A chronic anterior-frontal lesion may impair a person's estimate of their own *visual-decision correctness* while leaving memory-domain metacognition intact. A landmark acute cTBS claim of selective impaired metacognitive sensitivity is methodologically contested and was not reproduced under a modified active-control protocol. Both lines test **metacognitive report accuracy**, not a validated ground-truth “conscious/not conscious” sensor. Neither demonstrates or refutes the possibility of conscious experience in a non-neural AI. For artificial systems, confidence calibration, model self-report, explainability and state perturbations may be useful *functional diagnostics*; none should be mislabeled proof of machine phenomenology without a separate warrant for the mapping and independent calibration.

## Future-only, falsifiable proposals (NO original experiments conducted)

- **P39-H1 — robustness of acute metacognitive necessity.** When TMS targeting and physiological effect are prospectively verified, adequate blinded independent cohorts may show no robust DLPFC-specific reduction in confidence–accuracy metacognitive sensitivity relative to active control. Refuted if a large, pre-registered target-verified human study replicates a selective deficit while sensory discriminability, bias and SDT validity are matched. This would support a *functional* DLPFC role, not automatically prove a phenomenal content change.
- **P39-H2 — regional and domain selectivity.** Chronic *anterior PFC* lesions may predict visual metacognition impairment more strongly than memory metacognition when lesion volume, connectivity damage, surgery etiology and objective discrimination are prospectively accounted for. Refuted if larger independent lesion cohorts show similar memory and vision changes or neither conditional association. Not an experimental lesion proposal.
- **P39-H3 — measurement artifact sensitivity.** Estimates of apparent cTBS effect may vary substantially with signal-detection exclusion thresholds, boundary corrections and confidence wording. Refuted if preregistered multi-estimator sensitivity analyses produce stable direction and interval estimates across valid choices, independently from control responses. This is future research design only, not an instruction to run existing TMS data in this repository.
