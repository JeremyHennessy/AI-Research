## Precise research question

Do eleven distinct primary studies—including function-encoding DNA selection inside liposomes, evolved physical linkage of cooperative RNA genes, chemical compartment processes, DNA-bearing granddaughter vesicles and engineered beneficial-variant selection in SpudCell—establish a lineage sustaining complete synthetic-cell reproduction without investigator-controlled division or reconstruction? **That stronger combined result is not established by the sources inspected here.** The Tokyo 2011/2015 work demonstrates DNA-associated division and experimentally assisted grandchildren; Gaut et al. (bioRxiv, 2 July 2026) additionally reports beneficial *engineered* promoter selection over mechanically divided generations and a **separate** genetically associated division assay. These positive results must not be erased by the remaining autonomy gap. The eleven studies include **ten peer-reviewed journal publications plus one rights-restricted preprint**, spanning **seven research programs**: overlapping Boekhoven (four), overlapping Kurihara/Sugawara Tokyo vesicle studies (two), Oxford/Fletcher, Otto, Minnesota/Adamala, Danelon/Toulouse and the Ichihashi/Mizuuchi cooperative-RNA program (one each). Unique studies and distinct laboratories are not independent replications of the same complete system.

**Six noninterchangeable evidence gates:** (1) autocatalytic molecular amplification; (2) demonstrably active function in the relevant assembled/offspring phase, rather than mere encapsulation; (3) compartment maintenance or formation; (4) observed parent-to-daughter transfer of functional information; (5) heritable, causal per-lineage advantage including failures; (6) sustained descendant contribution after researcher-selected enrichment/reconstitution ceases, under *otherwise permissive* material/energy conditions. Do **not** demand that living systems survive without food, energy, light or other environmental inputs; the challenge is dependency on manually recreating identity or choosing winners at each cycle.

## A. Soria-Carrera, Kauling & Boekhoven: reciprocal chemical coupling with no daughter-pedigree closure

**Primary:** *Primitive genotype-phenotype coupling in fuel-dependent synthetic cells with an autocatalyst*, *Chem*, DOI [`10.1016/j.chempr.2025.102816`](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chempr.2025.102816). Published online **5 November 2025**; assigned to volume 12, issue 3 (**12 March 2026**). **Access:** publicly indexed original publisher full-main-text excerpts containing experimental results, comparisons, Methods-related chemistry and discussion; direct DOI opening failed in this audit, and supplements/raw data were not independently accessed. **Grade: E2-depth indexed original excerpts, NOT a verified cover-to-cover full-method read or E3 rerun.** The publisher identifies the article as **CC BY 4.0**, subject to independently checking rights for any third-party embedded items; this critical manuscript copies no figures, article text or data files.

Researchers combined externally fueled coacervate droplets with autocatalytic click chemistry. The same droplets are short-lived without sustained chemical activation. **Observed benefit:** arginine-rich KR-containing replicator formed *in situ* increased bulk droplet turbidity/lifetime from roughly **7 minutes to 25 minutes**; poorly partitioning anionic or neutral variants did not generate the same effect. The reverse direction—compartment favoring a specific replicator—is supported by a calculated compositional 'error rate' of **59.2 ± 0.8%** without droplets versus **29.7 ± 3.1%** with droplets, or **21.6 ± 0.8%** under a seeded variant of the assay. Those percentages reflect a particular product-composition metric, **not** measured genome mutation frequencies across newborn daughter cells. Partition coefficients for KR-N3 and C-N3 were **32.2 ± 1.9** and **1.7 ± 0.2**, consistent with electrostatic selection by the compartment; the publisher describes statistical testing with ANOVA/Tukey and checks of variance assumptions.

**Mechanistic controls and meaningful negatives:** high ionic strength suppresses droplet assembly and removes the reaction bias; competing similarly charged azides are not preferentially sorted. Making the autocatalyst *before* droplet formation fails to achieve the in-situ longevity advantage; microscopy places the in-situ material toward the interface, compared with more even distribution when added ex-situ. These interventions weaken a simple claim that any chemical replication boosts survival and support **context-dependent spatial partitioning** as a live alternative to heritable cell-level instructions. Several fuel–starvation rounds preserve aspects of the interaction, but each round requires investigator-provided chemical fuel; the authors identify **autonomous division with replicator inheritance** and competitive mutant selection as future work. There is no measured count of descendants from traced parent droplets or founder failures across unassisted generations.

**Non-overclaim:** a helpful autocatalyst inside a droplet is exciting reciprocal chemistry, not yet a demonstrated genetically heritable, selectable *offspring-producing cell lineage*. The apparent lifespan advantage is a physical ensemble proxy, not proven lifetime reproductive fitness. Tests do not separate passive stability due to partitioning from a mutationally inherited reproductive adaptation.

## B. Zhang, Howlett, Silvester, Kukura & Fletcher: cyclic vesicle dynamics without genetic descent

**Primary:** *A Chemical Reaction Network Drives Complex Population Dynamics in Oscillating Self-Reproducing Vesicles*, *Journal of the American Chemical Society* **146**, 18262–18269 (2024), DOI [`10.1021/jacs.4c00860`](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c00860); published **25 June 2024**. **Access:** original ACS indexed Results, figure explanatory text and conclusions; original article archived as [PMC11240260](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11240260/), but a fresh direct full-text request received an access challenge or HTTP 403. The publisher separately lists experimental, microscopy and calibration Supporting Information; these files and raw traces were not fully audited. **Grade: E2-depth selected original main-text/results and indexed methods; not a verified cover-to-cover Methods/Supporting Information audit or raw-data E3.** ACS identifies this specific publication as **CC BY 4.0**; do not assume supplementary data or third-party figures are distributable without separate checks.

**Positive result:** a chemically fueled autocatalytic reaction network generates repeated phases of vesicle appearance/growth/collapse/dormancy. The authors tracked aggregate population number/size/mass over time using **interferometric scattering microscopy (iSCAT)** and **dynamic light scattering (DLS)** as well as molecular measurements. The rhythm is genuinely interesting **non-equilibrium organization**: components respond collectively as fuel chemistry changes.

**Control and intervention audit (2026-10-10):** Original ACS Figure 2(c) reports that omitting nucleophilic catalyst **DABCO** led to gradual surfactant formation but **no oscillation within the measured window**; this is a conditional negative result, not proof that DABCO is uniquely indispensable in all possible kinetic regimes. A continuously supplied hydrogen-peroxide stream, mixing, and tuned reaction kinetics sustain the observed oscillator. External energy and feed supply are compatible with living systems in principle, but this is **not a test of continued organization after withdrawal of experimenter-specified selection/assembly interventions**. Indexed Figure 3 discussion reports oscillating particle number/mass, considers aggregate merging and nucleation mechanisms tentative, and notes that late-pulse aggregate-size trends are **not consistent across all repeats**. Review of source Supplementary Figure S4 and full independent replicate denominators remains outstanding; do not transform cycle count into biological cohort count. The directly measured units are chemical concentrations and aggregate populations, *not barcoded parent-daughter pedigrees*.

**Central interpretive limitation:** repeating vesicle population peaks do not, by themselves, establish a genealogy of individually identified parents, daughters, inherited variants and fitness. Vesicles can form de novo by reaction chemistry. Whether any daughter carries a *parent-specific beneficial function* is a different measurement from whether the population oscillates. External reactants and engineered reaction conditions remain required. This Oxford study is **independent of the TUM study as a researcher group and experimental mechanism**, but not an experimental replication of the TUM genotype/phenotype coupling. No comparable 'mutation rate', controlled daughter-success denominator, lineage-level selection coefficient or unsorted transgenerational inheritance is established from the inspected record.

**Counterhypothesis:** a phase-transition chemical oscillator can cycle many times without biological reproductive ancestry. A lineage-neutral, composition-matched physicochemical oscillator is thus an essential conceptual null, not a claim that the reported oscillations are artifacts.

**Source/provenance correction (2026-10-10):** The ChemRxiv record identifies the **13 August 2024 version 2**, DOI `10.26434/chemrxiv-2024-x4fdr-v2`, as a **CC BY-NC 4.0 non-peer-reviewed preprint** by the same group. The preprint and published article are **versions of the same study**, and the code archive is a modeled interpretation rather than an independent wet-lab replication. Neither downloadable source data nor model code were downloaded, run, or audited. The published final article remains under its own journal rights regime; do not transfer the preprint license to it or redistribute original figures.

**Access-dependent unknowns:** exact independent replicate denominators, founder accounting, flow residence times, variant carryover, predeclared versus post-hoc variant selection, and negative controls require a full peer-reviewed Methods/Results audit. No inferences about sustained mutant success after stopping flow enrichment or within newborn compartments are warranted from the accessible abstract.

## D. Wenisch and colleagues: verified offspring-like fragmentation and fueled second generation

**Version-of-record:** *Toward synthetic life—Emergence, growth, creation of offspring, decay, and rescue of fuel-dependent synthetic cells*, *Chem* 11(9), 102578 (2025); DOI [10.1016/j.chempr.2025.102578](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chempr.2025.102578). TUM institutional record lists September 11, 2025 issue date; an Elsevier linked-articles index gives May 9, 2025 online date. Publication dates should not be silently conflated. **Same-study preprint:** Wenisch et al. *Towards Synthetic Life—Molecular Design of Complex Coacervates that Self-Divide*, ChemRxiv posted August 6, 2024, DOI [10.26434/chemrxiv-2024-035v4](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2024-035v4). The original authors and distinct titles reflect manuscript revision, *not independent experiments*.

**Positive observation (E1 original abstract/preprint abstract, not full final Methods):** Coacervate droplets with mixed-length polyanions develop dense inclusions. As larger droplets decay following fuel depletion, separated speckle-derived fragments persist as offspring-like droplets. Further externally administered fueling can rescue these offspring-like structures into a second generation. The authors report tuning offspring production/survival and retention of partitioned molecular cargo through separation. These observations support physical descent more strongly than mere cyclic de novo nucleation in an untracked bulk sample.

**Discriminating limits and negative interpretation:** The inspected accessible original abstracts do *not* establish heritable variation of catalytic instructions, productive propagation of a specifically beneficial variant, parent-by-parent offspring reproductive denominators or survival without deliberately timed fuel rescue. Retained molecular cargo is not automatically a copied genotype; a second fuel-supported droplet generation is not the same outcome as multiple unassisted generations. The final paper’s figure-level original controls, exact N, raw images, software provenance and SI are **not audited** here. The preprint declares CC BY 4.0 for that version only; TUM catalog indicates copyright vested in the authors for the final publication but a precise final reuse license has **not** been independently confirmed. No paper figures, text, movies or raw data redistributed.

**Counterhypothesis:** Shrinkage and phase sorting of initially manufactured parent droplets passively bud persistent polyanion-rich fragments, which can regrow after supplied fuel without copying or selecting an inherited functional replicator. This is a chemically real division-like process, but the inferred molecular ancestry/function gate remains untested.

**Primary access receipts:** [TUM institutional journal record](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/toward-synthetic-lifeemergence-growth-creation-of-offspring-decay/) and [ChemRxiv original preprint abstract/rights](https://www.cambridge.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/66afd279c9c6a5c07a0101fd). Source metadata accessed 2026-10-10. E1 pending a full final-original Methods/SI audit.

## E. Holtmannspötter and colleagues: longer-lived RNA-containing daughter fragments

**Primary:** *Design of Fuel-Dependent, Complex-Coacervate-Based Synthetic Cells*, *Chemistry—A European Journal* 32(31), e71099 (2026), first published **May 6, 2026**; DOI [10.1002/chem.71099](https://doi.org/10.1002/chem.71099). **Access grade:** E2-depth **original public full HTML article, figures/captions and main Results/Discussion** independently reviewed; the 3.6 MB supplementary methods PDF, microscopy videos, complete raw traces and author-held data were *not* downloaded or analyzed. The publisher explicitly identifies this original article as **CC BY 4.0**; third-party figures and supplementary assets were not separately rights-audited. (Rights check: original publisher version of record, 2026-10-10.) Critique below consists of original paraphrase only.

**Positive original main-text evidence:** The lab systematically varied hexapeptide aromatic residues and their positions. A tryptophan-containing W5 design bound polyanions more effectively and resisted waste relative to its earlier design. With polyuracil (pU) and chemical fuel, droplets produced fragments that persisted for **up to several minutes**, materially longer than earlier fragments reported to persist for **only tens of seconds**. tRNA and a long high-G-content in-vitro-transcribed RNA supported droplet formation under some conditions, but the **offspring-specific** imaging was performed for pU, not demonstrated for every functional or sequence-diverse RNA. This is an advance in physically realizable offspring and compatibility, not a demonstration of *inherited RNA-encoded benefit*.

**Specific negative findings and mechanisms:** The authors report waste interference even after one or two fueling rounds in earlier systems. In the updated W5/pU regime, fuel accumulation beyond approximately **150 mM** at 20 mM peptide can cause kinetic trapping rather than clean fuel-cycle dissolution; successive 25 mM injections can accumulate to this regime. A proposed additional transient activated chemical species and its causal effect on prolonged lifetime are **tentative**, not unambiguously identified in the main-text reading. The same group notes that previously observed daughter fragments usually lasted only tens of seconds. Its proposed rescuing of daughter fragments with precisely timed extra fueling, and inheritance of useful RNA within daughters, are **future directions**, explicitly not outcomes established in this experiment.

**Evidence/unit limitation:** The article describes microscopy, HPLC and optical-density outcomes but, without the complete supporting raw observations, does not give an independently verified tally of founder attempts, daughter viability across uncontrolled successive generations, variant-dependent heritability or a measured lineage selection coefficient. pU provides a polyanionic compartment scaffold, not by itself an experimentally proven functional informational genotype. The publisher says additional supporting data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request; not equivalent to an openly deposited complete raw dataset.

**Counterhypothesis:** Increased electrostatic/aromatic affinity and altered activated-peptide hydrolysis stabilize a physically separating chemical compartment. Long-lived fragments can arise as phase-sorting products with no sequence-dependent inherited advantage. This alternative does not deny fragment formation; it localizes what remains undemonstrated.

**Primary access receipt:** [Original publisher's public full HTML Results/Discussion, data availability and linked SI inventory](https://chemistry-europe.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/chem.71099), accessed 2026-10-10.

## F. Holtmannspötter et al. (2024): a direct negative test of assuming that encapsulated catalysts remain functional

**Primary:** *Regulating Nucleic Acid Catalysis Using Active Droplets*, *Angewandte Chemie International Edition* 63, e202412534 (first published 9 August 2024), DOI [`10.1002/anie.202412534`](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202412534). The [original full HTML main article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/anie.202412534) was rechecked against the numerical and component controls on 10 October 2026. **Evidence:** original full HTML main results and controls, plus **selected original publisher Supporting Information methods paragraphs** examined separately; the full 30-page SI, all SI figures/traces and author-held raw data were **not** audited and no E3 reproduction was performed. Publisher labels the **2024 article** Open Access and TUM records author copyright, but no article-specific Creative Commons license text was independently established in the checked HTML/institutional record; the precise **2024 article/SI reuse rights remain UNCONFIRMED**. This must not be confused with the distinct 2026 Holtmannspötter article, for which CC BY 4.0 was separately verified. No source materials reproduced. **Independence:** shares Job Boekhoven and other authors with this study family; the sixth paper within the original six-study comparison and one of eight papers across **four** research programs after adding the Tokyo lineage. It is not an independent whole-lineage replication.

Researchers supplied the **pre-made engineered 17E DNAzyme**, including an A10-arm version favoring partitioning into chemically fueled peptide/polyU droplets. The modified catalyst had a reported droplet partition coefficient **8,600 ± 500** and only **1.5%** remained in supernatant just after droplet formation. Both modified and unmodified DNAzyme cleaved about **94%** of substrate in **three hours without droplets**; with sustained droplets the modified DNAzyme achieved only **14%** conversion after three hours. Critically, the article's **component controls** report that fuel alone did not reduce activity, whereas polyU and peptide independently reduced the A10-modified catalyst's one-hour activity by around **45%** and **70%** respectively. The observed loss is not equivalent to genotype loss or failure of a daughter lineage: it is engineered catalytic suppression associated with partitioning, peptide interactions and DNAzyme unfolding. Selected biochemical activity panels reported **three assay preparations**; the DNAzyme supernatant-fraction assay used **six**, while circular-dichroism readouts used **two or three** depending on condition. Confocal partitioning sampled five imaging positions and selected ten droplets per image before pooling image averages across triplicate preparations; individual image regions are *not* independent preparations or parent-to-daughter lineages. The selected SI additionally documents pre-folding of the DNAzyme, externally initiated droplet formation, and additional fuel during some one- and three-hour assays to maintain the intended compartment conditions. Thus measured inhibition is under controlled external intervention, not spontaneous inheritance or unassisted offspring viability. See the primary [source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/anie.202412534) for the component and condition boundaries.

After fuel depletion the catalyst could regain activity as droplets dissolved, but recovery was **incomplete** under some conditions: a gel assay reported approximately **67%** modified DNAzyme released by 30 minutes with no further release at 60 minutes. Zinc cofactor changed the dissolution behavior; without ZnCl2 the turbidity returned toward baseline after around **22 minutes**, while ZnCl2 permitted residual turbidity. The original authors suggest reciprocal DNAzyme effects on droplet properties as **future work**. This study shows externally programmable regulation, **not** the evolved selection of beneficial catalytic function, nor inheritance, physical daughter production or net descendant advantage.

**Revised interpretation:** functional-information transfer, *expression of that function within the daughter compartment*, and positive lineage-level descendant fitness are three distinct missing tests. A catalytic cargo may be present but suppressed by the compartment environment. A future case claiming successful heritable function must establish active catalysis (or a beneficial controlled state) in relevant offspring conditions, not merely verify fluorescent colocalization or composition. Plausible alternatives include component-induced unfolding, zinc-sensitive phase kinetics and residual-waste effects. **Selected** supporting-methods assay denominators and handling conditions were reviewed, but full supporting figures, source-data traces, all repeats, author-held raw data and asset reuse rights remain unaudited. The Wiley main article states that underlying data can be requested from corresponding authors; public access to an article does not itself mean raw data are available for redistribution.

## G. Kurihara et al. (2011): DNA copying coupled to parent–daughter vesicle division

**Primary original:** *Self-reproduction of supramolecular giant vesicles combined with the amplification of encapsulated DNA*, *Nature Chemistry* 3, 775–781 (2011), DOI [`10.1038/nchem.1127`](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.1127), published 4 September 2011. [Publisher original](https://www.nature.com/articles/nchem.1127). **Depth E1:** original publisher abstract and figure headings, not complete full-text methods, independent SI review or founder-level raw observations. Reprint/permission navigation is not evidence of a general reuse license; original article and SI redistribution rights **UNCONFIRMED**. No source figure, text or movie copied.

**Positive evidence:** polymerase-amplified DNA was contained inside giant cationic vesicles; externally supplied membrane precursor drove vesicle enlargement/division while DNA material was distributed to daughters. The article reports that DNA amplification increases division frequency. Unlike generic coacervate-fragment analogies, this is **a documented coupling between molecular copying and physical daughter production**. It is not evidence of beneficial **sequence-specific heritable variant selection**: the reviewed material does not establish distinct informational genotypes with sustained per-descendant fitness differences.

**Causal alternatives and missing data:** electrostatic interactions between DNA and cationic membrane components may increase division independent of any information-coded function; ongoing copying and division require laboratory-supplied biochemical machinery and membrane precursor. From accessible E1 material, the counts of unsuccessfully produced daughter lines, independent founders, individual sequence inheritance and intervention-withdrawal outcomes are **not independently established**. Missing details must not be converted into categorical claims that the uninspected supporting information contains no such observations.

## H. Kurihara et al. (2015): DNA-bearing daughter-to-granddaughter production under externally triggered stages

**Primary original:** *A recursive vesicle-based model protocell with a primitive model cell cycle*, *Nature Communications* 6, 8352 (2015), DOI [`10.1038/ncomms9352`](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms9352), published 29 September 2015. [Publisher open original](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9352). **Depth E2 main-text methodology**, NOT SI/raw-data reanalysis or E3; seven supplementary figures and individual failure counts remain unaudited. **Rights: original article explicitly CC BY 4.0** on the publisher page, with standard exclusions for separately credited third-party assets. Original synthesis and citation only; no article figures/data republished.

**Major corrective observation:** researcher-created DNA-bearing daughter giant vesicles were replenished through pH-triggered fusion with conveyor vesicles containing fresh substrates, then underwent thermally directed DNA amplification and further division after supplied membrane precursor; the study reports **granddaughter vesicles** (a third modeled generation). Membrane/DNA labels and population-level cytometry support this staged progression, rather than merely an oscillating droplet count. This demonstrably exceeds the earlier six-study family's limited second-generation or population-oscillation comparators.

**Precisely bounded conclusion:** each modeled cell-cycle phase was activated by a **particular external stimulus**: pH-dependent fusion/replenishment, thermal PCR, and addition of membrane material. Necessary food/energy provision is *not* itself disqualifying; the still-unresolved dependency is investigator-controlled phase coordination and cargo restoration. The paper considered one DNA sequence and does not show selected advantageous variants retained across independent generations. DNA abundance and physical lipid interaction plausibly influence division, but are not a demonstrated sequence-specific inherited function. Independent lineage failure denominators, genetically distinct competing descendants and an otherwise resource-permissive no-staging series were **not established by the sources inspected**. The authors describe fully spontaneous evolution-like behavior as a future direction.

**Independence:** 2015 follows the same Kurihara/Sugawara/Toyota/Suzuki 2011 program. These papers are two unique studies but **one research line**, separate from the three six-study research programs; they do not replicate an autonomous evolutionary result independently.

## I. Gaut et al. (2026) — SpudCell: engineered genetic selection during mechanically divided generations

**Primary original:** Gaut, Deich, Cash, Hoog, Engelhart and Adamala, *A Chemically Defined Synthetic Cell Capable Of Growth And Replication*, **bioRxiv version 1**, posted **2 July 2026**, DOI [`10.64898/2026.07.01.735724`](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.01.735724). [Original preprint and licensing record](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.01.735724v1). **Status: non-peer-reviewed preprint, E2-partial indexed original main-text/results/figure descriptions; full SI/raw data, peer review and independent reproduction unverified.** A provisional patent application is disclosed. **Rights: all rights reserved; bioRxiv's display license does not authorize copying article figures, tables or supplements.** This review is original short critical paraphrase only.

**Substantive positive result:** the authors describe liposomal synthetic cells containing a chemically defined protein-expression system and an approximately 90-kbp genome distributed across multiple DNA components. They report DNA copying, expression, feeder-liposome-mediated growth, and **five investigator-managed cycles**. In their competition experiments a **deliberately engineered promoter variant** enhanced production of a feeding-associated membrane protein, changing reproductive output under experimental selection. This is **reported inherited engineered fitness variation**, and rules out the overbroad claim that useful heritable selection has never been shown in a synthetic compartment. Figure 3 describes **three independently prepared liposome samples for the five-generation assays**; nested microscopy cells and per-plasmid qPCR measurements must not be counted as three-generation-independent replications.

**Crucial separation of experimental arms:** in the experiments demonstrating repeated cycles and genetic selection, researchers **mechanically divided/extruded cells after incubation** and supplied new feeder liposomes. **Genetically encoded division was assessed separately** through a membrane-associated expressed factor together with added recognition/linker components. The inspected source does **not** establish five generations of the *same pedigree* sustaining both gene-associated division and variant-selective benefit without human stage coordination. External food/energy supply by itself is not disqualifying; **manually imposed fission and replenishment of already constructed molecular expression machinery** are distinct, unresolved dependence boundaries.

**Controls, units and negatives:** ±Phi29 and ±feeder comparisons support particular copying and feeding dependencies; gene-associated division assays include component omission comparisons. The preprint reports that approximately **30% of the analyzed single-cell samples after five mechanically imposed generations** retained all **seven plasmids assayed**. This is neither a verified fraction of *all initial founders* yielding viable complete offspring nor the rate of complete-genome inheritance following genetically encoded fission. The preprint's descriptions of **seven, eight or nine DNA components** across its assays and related descriptions are not independently reconciled here; assay inclusion and version differences remain open, not silently standardized. Pooled daughter-fraction amplification cannot prove that all DNA units coexist in every identifiable daughter. Supplementary Tables S6 and S9 and individual founder denominators were not directly reanalyzed.

**Causal alternatives and independence:** imposed splitting could mediate selection differently from spontaneous gene-associated division; component segregation may break otherwise useful inheritance; engineered promoters prove selection of available variants, **not origin of de novo beneficial novelty**; linker conditions may alter measured fission yield. The work is one distinct Minnesota research program, **not an independent reproduction** of the earlier eight papers' entire system. These are unresolved counterhypotheses, not demonstrated failures of the positive reported results. A further independently verified lineage-complete, failure-inclusive study would be needed to resolve them.

## J. Sierra Heras & Danelon (2026): encoded useful molecular function can win under compartment-level selection

**Peer-reviewed primary original:** Laura Sierra Heras and Christophe Danelon, “Autocatalytic selection of gene functions in synthetic cells,” *Communications Biology* **9**, article 1124, **27 May 2026**, DOI [`10.1038/s42003-026-10372-z`](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10372-z); [publisher's original full main article, Results, Methods, statistical statements and rights](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-026-10372-z). **Evidence E2 original full main-text/methods/results critical reading, not E3 independent replication**. Complete supplementary figures, raw replicates, sequencing files and code were not separately reprocessed. Publisher labels the original article **CC BY 4.0** with normal third-party-material exceptions; this review is original paraphrase with no paper figures, datasets or method text copied.

**Positive finding and unit:** a DNA template containing φ29 replication components `p2` and `p3` plus an encoded useful gene underwent protein-dependent *molecular* amplification in researchers-prepared giant liposomes containing a supplied PURE expression system, purified accessories and nucleotides. With externally supplied T7 polymerase limited 30-fold, T7-encoding DNA amplified **2.7×** versus **1.6×** for a shorter non-encoding DNA (relative advantage 1.7×), whereas enzyme-sharing in bulk reversed the outcome (shorter noncoding DNA **47.8×** versus T7-coding DNA **6.1×**). This is substantive compartment-mediated genotype–function coupling, not merely a chemical activity indicator. Figure 3 competition reports **three independent experimental preparations**, not three generations of cell reproduction.

**Useful nonreplication-function selection:** researchers engineered LacI/lactose feedback so that **functional `lacZ`-encoded β-galactosidase** supported DNA amplification. In one lower-starting-frequency comparison, the functional genotype's DNA fraction rose from **31% to 51%**; matched nonselective conditions did not show the same enrichment. A **researcher-designed 32-codon NNS library** at residue E538 enriched the *already functional* glutamate allele under imposed selection. This is real selection for existing beneficial biochemical function—not the spontaneous creation of new functional novelty nor a daughter-cell fitness assay. Figure 5 uses generally **three independent preparations**; sequencing reads and individual liposomes are not independent generational pedigrees.

**Disconfirming controls and leakage:** encoding guanylate kinase supported dGTP regeneration for DNA amplification from supplied dGMP; under low-isolation conditions diffusion rescued non-encoding templates from nearby producers. Incorporating **30% cholesterol** reduced that cross-rescue and increased the reported relative selection contrast to **6.0× versus 2.7×** (selected Fig. 6 assay panels show **three to four** biological preparations). This is direct evidence that the genotype–phenotype link can fail through environmental sharing. Co-encapsulation of different templates and initially irreproducible PURE compositions are further author-reported method limitations, not independently reconstructed failures here.

**What it does and does not establish:** experimentally constructed selection conditions causally favor useful **gene-encoding DNA**, and bulk-versus-compartment competition changes direction. **Liposome parent-to-daughter division, transmission of a complete expressive genome through identifiable offspring, survival of failed founders, multigeneration cell reproduction, and persistence following withdrawal of investigator-selected conditions were not demonstrated.** Essential PURE translational machinery and other components remain exogenous. This is a distinct Danelon/Toulouse program relative to the nine V7 sources, but **not** independent replication of their full lineage-level outcomes. The Pass 35 Abil and Katla studies remain previously published work and are not double-counted here.

## K. Ueda, Mizuuchi & Ichihashi (2023): evolution of a linked multi-function RNA under external dilution and droplet renewal

**Primary peer-reviewed source:** Kensuke Ueda, Ryo Mizuuchi & Norikazu Ichihashi, “Emergence of linkage between cooperative RNA replicators encoding replication and metabolic enzymes through experimental evolution,” *PLOS Genetics* 19(8):e1010471, **4 August 2023**, DOI [`10.1371/journal.pgen.1010471`](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1010471); [full original main text, results, discussion and methods](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1010471). **E2 original complete publicly accessible main-text critical reading; not E3 scientific reproduction.** The PLOS version of record is **CC BY**; the review paraphrases without redistributing supplementary spreadsheets, original images or sequence files. Published supplementary legends were consulted but the underlying files were **not independently reanalyzed**. The associated 2018 work (DOI `10.1038/s41559-018-0650-z`) is **same-program E1 context only**, not an eleventh-plus independent experiment. Related 2020 Furubayashi RNA-ecology study (DOI `10.7554/eLife.56038`) is already catalogued in this repository; do not double-count it as the present primary review. This program is separate from Kurihara/Sugawara's Tokyo vesicle work, notwithstanding institutional geography.

**Positive: emergent physical multi-gene linkage and functional replication.** The group began with an unlinked Rep RNA (2,041 bases, encoding the Qβ replicase catalytic subunit) and NDK RNA (752 bases, encoding nucleotide diphosphate kinase). After **46 rounds** in a first serial experiment, a chosen mutated pair seeded a distinct **79-round** experiment. DNA sequencing of **47 final-round putative linked clones** found both intact coding regions on one `rep–ndk` RNA molecule; around **45%** of these clones were directly end-to-end joined, and **26%** carried one inserted nucleotide at the junction. A selected linked clone produced both proteins and supported full-length plus/minus-strand RNA copying in a *supplied cell-free translation system*. The 2023 paper therefore provides substantive experimental evidence for **assembly of previously separate cooperative genetic functions into a molecular genome**, not merely coexistence of fragments. These **125 serial rounds** are two connected laboratory experiment histories, **not 125 autonomous daughter-cell generations**.

**Quantitative and genealogical qualifications.** Putative linkage was PCR-detected in **15 of 32 sampled rounds** of the second experiment. The round-79 linked population was estimated below **1 nM**, less than **10%** of either unlinked component's abundance. At **10 nM** the isolated linked clone copied at **<10%** of the amount attained by unlinked Rep and NDK molecules; linked-protein expression was also approximately halved. When all three were assayed together at **1 nM** rather than **10 nM**, replication of unlinked Rep and NDK declined **8.7-fold and 13-fold**, respectively, while the linked clone declined only **1.3-fold** and outperformed Rep RNA under the lower-density condition. This supports **density-conditioned assortment resilience**, not a demonstrated general speed advantage, stable population takeover or complete founder-to-offspring lineage census. Linked and unlinked sequences had different mutation patterns (especially in Rep), consistent with some inherited molecular continuity; weaker NDK-region resolution and end-point sampled clones leave recurring-recombination and selective-survivor alternatives incompletely resolved.

**Intervention-withdrawal counterevidence.** Every replication round was deliberately **diluted typically fivefold, occasionally 100-fold**, provided with **fresh already-assembled translation machinery**, and mixed vigorously to fuse and redivide water-in-oil droplets. The authors explicitly state that compartment membership is transient, with content redistribution among droplets. In a relevant control without raising the dilution rate at high RNA concentrations, **parasitic RNA expanded and the two cooperating populations declined below detection**. This is strong negative evidence against reading operator-managed molecular copying as autonomously protected cooperation; it does **not** isolate whether parasite escape is due specifically to removal of a population bottleneck, altered concentrations, altered resource renewal or their combination. Maintaining ordinary food and energy is not the same intervention as imposing adaptive dilution or manually reshuffling compartments.

**What the source does NOT establish:** biological or synthetic cells creating their own heritable boundary, endogenously regenerating a complete protein-translation system, independent daughter-compartment establishment, unassisted beneficial reproductive closure, spontaneous emergence of a permanently selectable higher-level organism, or independently replicated E3 outcomes. The 2023 result supports a partial genetic-integration gate and a **measured counterexample to robustness after withdrawal of a specific intervention**; neither the negative control nor the positive selection can be extended universally. See the separate [original source](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1010471) and unchanged local Ueda methods-audit predecessor for exact published observations.

**Do not conflate these distinct autonomy concepts:** (a) living things need continuous energy, substrates and benign environments, so eliminating *all* external inputs is invalid; (b) generic environmental cycles may constitute passive provision; (c) Tokyo requires separate stage commands for fusion/PCR/membrane supply; (d) SpudCell uses externally imposed mechanical splitting for its clearest multigeneration selection evidence while showing genetically associated division in another arm; (e) researcher-designed beneficial variants can undergo **real selection** without demonstrating spontaneous origin of advantageous variants; (f) DNA-level enrichment inside a prepared liposome (Danelon) is not a descendant synthetic-cell reproductive advantage; and (g) Ueda linked-RNA selection can occur in a periodically reconstituted RNA/droplet population without self-renewing compartment progeny. A physical granddaughter and a selected beneficial engineered allele are real, differently bounded observations—not demonstrations of whole-lineage independence.

**Strongest remaining counterhypotheses:** division advantage may arise from electrostatic DNA–lipid interactions rather than informational coding; cyclic peaks may reflect de novo assembly instead of genealogies; passive cargo partition and catalyst sequestration may inhibit expression; Tokyo externally staged PCR/fusion could be essential to observed grandchildren; SpudCell mechanical segmentation or full-genome plasmid loss could constrain its observed selection under gene-associated fission; selected surviving microscopy fields may obscure founder failures. Ueda also shows parasite invasion when high-dilution interventions cease; because dilution alters density, encounter rate and resource renewal together, it does not uniquely identify one causal process. None of these explanations is proven universally. All are future falsifiability questions only, not authorization for experiments.

## Falsifiable future-only hypotheses: not authorized or executed

**P40-H1: beneficial molecular catalysis can become inherited *daughter-compartment* benefit.** Include phase-resolved catalytic expression, not just co-localized cargo, as a necessary intermediate endpoint. In a separately approved future study, first establish individual parent–offspring compartment identities and track all failures; compare catalytically active, composition-matched inactive and *ex-situ* assembled controls. Under equal nonsorted resource conditions, function-bearing descendants should retain **higher net offspring success** attributable to the inherited active variant, not merely increased droplet lifetime. **Disconfirmation:** effect vanishes after phenotype is reset, no traceable daughters occur, or success follows bulk phase partitioning regardless of parent lineage.

**P40-H2: oscillatory 'reproduction' can be distinguished from de novo chemical reassembly.** A later preregistered lineage-tracing analysis should distinguish parent-provided material/information from independent nucleation in matched oscillating solutions; evaluate all attempted founders over multiple cycles. **Disconfirmation:** population peaks recur without parental contribution or inherited function despite repeated aggregate formation.
