# Pass 41 — Genome-encoded membrane abscission versus autonomous reproductive closure

**Research-only source-critical review, 10 October 2026.** The question is whether expressing a membrane-cutting protein from DNA inside a prepared artificial vesicle establishes only a functional *abscission module*, or whether it already establishes self-sustaining, beneficially heritable reproduction. The studies below show important improvements in division machinery without demonstrating the latter. No original experiment, simulation, model, organism or production change was performed for this review.

## Three distinct evidence levels

1. **Biophysical scission** means that a neck loses lipid continuity and sealed lobes may separate. This is a measurable event, not necessarily birth of functioning descendants.
2. **Genetically encoded scission machinery** means a supplied nucleic-acid template produces a functional division protein in a preassembled transcription/translation environment. This is stronger than reconstituting an externally purified protein, but it does not mean that a cell builds its own translation machinery, copies an entire genome, or transmits functioning division capacity into offspring.
3. **Reproductive closure** requires descendants, not just membrane fragments: a tracked parent produces daughter compartments with inherited functional instructions, daughters regenerate the required capacity and themselves contribute descendants, and benefits persist after operators stop directing each reproductive transition (ordinary food, energy and permissive habitat may remain supplied). The studies below do not report this complete sequence.

## A — Sharma et al. 2026: DNA-directed DynA scission in pre-deformed vesicles

**Original study:** Charu Sharma, Nikki Nafar, Jacob Kerssemakers, José Ángel Fernández, Gijsje H. Koenderink and Cees Dekker, *Division of Synthetic Cells Using a Genomically Encoded One-Protein Divisome*, bioRxiv version 1, posted **22 July 2026**, DOI [10.64898/2026.07.21.739846](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739846); [original main article](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.21.739846v1.full). **Evidence grade: E2-depth, indexed original main Methods/Results/Discussion**, with limits: direct article opening intermittently returned HTTP 403; the version-1 full-main original was accessible in indexed excerpts, but all supplementary raw traces, movie events and independent founder counts were **not** reanalyzed. The publication is a **non-peer-reviewed preprint**, not an independently replicated result. Its rights statement is **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**; this original critical paraphrase reproduces no source figure, table, source code or substantial copyrighted text.

**Experimental unit and preparation:** Investigators constructed giant unilamellar vesicles (GUVs) containing a supplied DNA template encoding 137-kDa bacterial Dynamin A (DynA) fused to the fluorescent protein mVenus, as well as a prepared cell-free PURE expression system. Osmotic sugar asymmetry preformed dumbbell-shaped vesicles; near-isotonic conditions yielded about **41%** dumbbells in the vesicle preparations. The shape and the gene-expression components were supplied by the investigators. This is not inheritance of an endogenously renewed translation apparatus.

**Protein expression and membrane dependence:** Full-length DynA-mVenus was detected; suitable expression depended on membrane conditions. Raising negatively charged DOPG from 8% to 20% and then 30% reduced aggregation, while the 30% DOPG / 100-mM-sucrose condition gave approximately **55%** of classified dumbbells with DynA localization at necks. The authors categorized **550–800 vesicles per membrane/sugar condition**; those are observed vesicles within preparations, not independent genetic lineages. Neck enrichment is not itself proof of full scission.

**Scission rather than mere pinching:** The key functional readout was fluorescence recovery after photobleaching (FRAP) of one lobe, measuring whether labeled membrane lipids could diffuse across the intervening neck. In **26 analyzed necks across three independent preparations**, the preprint reports **27% full scission**, **15% hemi-scission**, and the remaining **58% still open**, despite the prepared membrane geometry. Full scission and hemi-scission must not be combined into a 42% *complete division* rate. The paper reports that vesicles whose membrane neck had undergone scission could still adhere physically and required a **small external separating force (~1.4 nN)**. Loss of membrane continuity and separation of physically stuck products are different observations. The figure-caption shorthand describing full scission as no recovery has an apparent inconsistency with its displayed normalization value in the accessible text (main criteria define no recovery as near zero); numerical source plots and the full figures warrant separate verification before stronger quantitative claims.

**Smaller protein comparator:** The study reports that the isolated **71.2-kDa DynA D1 domain** retained membrane-remodeling activity. Of **24 assayed necks from two independent preparations**, **21% showed full scission**, **17% hemi-scission**, and the rest remained open. D1 is more compact but this is not evidence that daughter cells express or inherit D1 or DynA over successive complete cycles.

**Negative/intervention controls:** High-GTP cell-free expression conditions may inhibit purified DynA: in one contextual comparison, reconstituted purified DynA produced ~**76%** abscission under optimized conditions but only **25%** with the prepared PURE reaction components. Adding GTP also reduced the short-term activity of purified DynA, with recovery reported after incubation, so neither an uncontrolled scaffold nor a generic protein-presence proxy establishes active division. The DOPG and PURE dependence matters: these outcomes are conditional on engineered membrane chemistry, provided transcription/translation machinery and osmotic pre-deformation. The paper does **not** provide full inherited genome counts, independently traced newborns that re-express division machinery, multigeneration reproduction or competitive descendant fitness. Genomic **encoding of one protein** is not equivalent to organism-level genome reproduction.

**Interpretive alternative:** Strong DOPG-dependent protein folding, preferred high-curvature neck binding and vesicle pre-deformation could explain the observed scission as a biochemical submodule that does not transfer functional reproductive capacity. This alternative accepts genuine scission events without equating them with independent life.

## B — De Franceschi et al. 2024: purified DynA, same lab, not independent replication

**Original:** Nicola De Franceschi, Roman Barth, Sabrina Meindlhumer, Alessio Fragasso and Cees Dekker, *Dynamin A as a one-component division machinery for synthetic cells*, *Nature Nanotechnology* 19:70–76 (2024), published online **5 October 2023**, DOI [10.1038/s41565-023-01510-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-023-01510-3). **Grade: E2 original publisher abstract, figure descriptions and reported selected controls, with full article/supplementary audit not completed.** Reuse rights for the original paper are not independently verified beyond the publisher's author-under-exclusive-license statement; no figures/data copied.

Purified DynA was reconstituted in pre-deformed dumbbell liposomes and monitored with FRAP and fluorescence microscopy. The authors reported neck localization, hemi-scission and full-scission events. This demonstrates physical sufficiency of the purified protein under prepared conditions, but it does not demonstrate DNA-directed synthesis in descendants or newborn genetic inheritance. The later Sharma 2026 preprint develops **a new expression step within the same Dekker research program**. The two must **not** be counted as independent laboratory replications of whole-cell self-reproduction. Assay denominators and exact all-preparation scission success distributions remain an outstanding source-depth question.

## C — Panevska et al. 2026: independently developed near-scission constriction

**Original:** Anastasija Panevska, Aleksandra Šakanović, Gianfranco Paccione, Germán Rivas and Petra Schwille, *Optimizing spatial organization of FtsZ rings for large-scale constriction in synthetic cells*, *Nature Communications* 17:2320, published **6 March 2026**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-026-70091-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70091-1). [Original accessible full main](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70091-1). **Grade: E2 main article Results/Methods/Discussion reviewed; supplementary source spreadsheet and individual raw movies were not reproduced or audited.** The original publisher reports **CC BY 4.0**, subject to embedded third-party restrictions; no illustrations/data reused.

This independent group reconstituted the membrane-binding FtsZ-366-mts variant with the cytoplasmic FtsN tail (cytoFtsN) in giant vesicles. **In the absence of cytoFtsN**, FtsZ predominantly formed small ~0.4-µm rings that did not cause comparable global deformation. With a large molar excess of cytoFtsN, the researchers observed large **~10.9-µm** closed rings and progressive vesicle constriction; they used **GTP plus GMPCPP** to stabilize dynamics. With GTP alone the bundles could aggregate within an hour. The figures report surveys of **more than 90 vesicles** and a tracked diameter subgroup of **14 vesicles**; these are physical vesicle observations, not multigeneration offspring counts. The authors explicitly state that their studied rings progressively constricted membranes **without full abscission**. Experimental purified-protein reconstitution also does not establish descendant expression of these proteins. This work is scientifically independent of the Dekker DynA research program, but **tests a different mechanism and endpoint**, not a direct independent replication of the Sharma preprint.

**Counterhypothesis:** CytoFtsN can shift filament organization and residence time enough for nearly complete constriction, without resolving the final high-curvature membrane-topology change. The absence of scission here is a measured boundary of the studied setup, not proof that FtsZ-based approaches cannot achieve scission under any configuration.

## Cross-study conclusions and evidence ranking

- **Supported by observations:** protein-mediated scission of some prepared liposomes, DNA-directed expression of one membrane-remodeling protein in supplied PURE components, and large-scale constriction with a distinct FtsZ-based apparatus. A portion of measured starting vesicles fail the relevant scission readout; these failures matter to interpretation.
- **Unverified across these studies:** one exact individually tracked parent-to-daughter-to-granddaughter pedigree with correct segregation of *complete* functional instructions and autonomous re-expression of division proteins; full descendant fitness including failures; continued copying and division with no stage-by-stage operator-specified neck formation or reconstruction. Evidence of module activity is not evidence of integrated autonomous heredity.
- **Research independence:** A and B share the Dekker program; C is an independent laboratory and a different membrane-division mechanism. They are **not** three independent validations of a single whole-cell lineage result. The 2026 Sharma preprint has not been shown to have completed peer review or an independent reproduction.
- **Rights and transparency:** Original references and paraphrases only; no copied figures, raw data, software or restricted article reproduction; do not upgrade source confidence from indexed access to a claim of executed replication.

## Five prospective falsifiable questions — future only, not experiments

- **P41-H1 / functional abscission versus neck-localization proxy:** If DynA enrichment is a causal and sufficient predictor of scission under controlled membrane conditions, increased neck localization should correspond to fewer FRAP-open necks after accounting for DOPG, tension and expression variability. **Null:** protein recruitment correlates with curvature but remains a poor predictor of membrane connectivity loss.
- **P41-H2 / inheritance of active division capacity:** Genomically encoded scission should yield self-propagating capacity only if complete newborns inherit, express and deploy the relevant template and translation machinery. **Null:** observed initial abscission occurs but daughters cannot regenerate the module without reconstitution by operators.
- **P41-H3 / preparation dependence:** A genuinely autonomous division module should remain functional without investigator pre-selecting all dumbbell necks, given otherwise permissive materials/energy and appropriate geometry. **Null:** activity collapses when prescribed pre-deformation is absent, even though purified-protein scission can still occur in preshaped samples.
- **P41-H4 / denominator and timing sensitivity:** Headline success fractions derived from assayed necks may overestimate end-to-end success from all starting vesicles, including aggregation, no dumbbell formation, open necks and post-scission adhesion. **Null:** a complete preparation-to-descendant accounting substantially reduces net functional division yield.
- **P41-H5 / constriction distinct from separation:** Different mechanisms may have different bottlenecks: a coherent large FtsZ ring yields near-complete constriction without full scission, whereas DynA can cut preformed necks but not generate them or establish inherited cycles. **Null:** no single tested module alone bridges both stages while retaining full offspring genome and regenerative machinery.
