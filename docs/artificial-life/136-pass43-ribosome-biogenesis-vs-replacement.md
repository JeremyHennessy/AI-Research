# Pass 43 — Functional ribosome biogenesis is not yet autonomous translation-machinery replacement

**Research-only critical primary-literature review — 10 October 2026.** This is a distinct continuation of [Pass 42: partial biochemical constructor regeneration](135-pass42-self-regenerating-constructors-vs-closure.md), not a second publication of its three studies. No new organism, world, scientific experiment, simulation, trained model, production deployment, laboratory procedure, purchased service or other-repository edit is authorized or performed.

## A. Question, evidence levels and decision

**Question:** After a cell-free system makes rRNA, ribosomal proteins or proteins encoded on its own ribosomal RNA, does it have enough *new, demonstrably functional ribosomal machinery* to replace the active ribosomes with which it began and subsequently support another generation without fresh preassembled ribosomes and researcher-directed rescue?

**Finding:** Ribosomal RNA transcription plus functional ribosome assembly (2013), coordinated transcription/translation/assembly of nascent ribosomal subunits (2025), and in-cis protein translation from a chimeric ribosomal RNA (2026) are distinct demonstrated functions. The 2025 study is especially substantial: it reports new functional ribosomal subunits assembled from newly expressed rRNAs and ribosomal proteins. It also explicitly reports **9.8 nM small and 3 nM large new-subunit functional equivalents from 80 nM starting native ribosomes**. The large-subunit ratio is approximately 3.8%; authors estimate **at least 26-fold improvement** for continuous ribosomal replacement. Its reaction still needs native starting ribosomes, prepared DNA and an externally derived E. coli soluble-proteome extract. None of these papers demonstrates continuous complete translation-machinery self-reproduction, tRNA and genome propagation, resource-balanced constructor renewal, or functioning daughter-cell lineages. These are *absent demonstrations in reviewed sources*, not proof of impossibility.

**Evidence grades:** S1 and S2 are E2 original accessible main text (Methods, Results/negative controls, Discussion, rights; original supplements and raw data not independently reanalyzed). S3 is **E1 original publisher abstract/figure descriptions and bibliographic record only** because the full original Nature Methods/results were not reliably accessible for this review. A publication is not an E3 independent reproduction. No EERC hypothesis is empirically validated here.

## B. Source 1 — Jewett et al. (2013): integrated rRNA synthesis, assembly and active protein production

**Original:** Michael C. Jewett, Brian R. Fritz, Laura E. Timmerman and George M. Church. *In vitro integration of ribosomal RNA synthesis, ribosome assembly, and translation.* Molecular Systems Biology 9:678, **25 June 2013**, DOI [10.1038/msb.2013.31](https://doi.org/10.1038/msb.2013.31). [Accessible publisher original](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/msb.2013.31); [author/reuse provenance at PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3964315/). **E2** whole original main Methods/Results/Discussion inspected; no independent reanalysis or supplement inspection. Original open article identified as **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0** by the PMC version; avoid treating downstream reposts describing other licenses as authority. Original paraphrase only.

**Observed:** The integrated synthesis, assembly and translation (iSAT) system co-transcribes rRNA genes and assembles their product with **pre-purified total native ribosomal proteins**, while S150-derived extract supports a coupled luciferase translation readout. 50S subunits assembled from in-vitro-transcribed 23S rRNA supported roughly **0.63 ± 0.10 nmol/l** firefly luciferase after four hours, versus roughly **3.2 nmol/l** using mature ribosomal RNA under comparison conditions; across those conditions functional output was about 20% of the mature-RNA assembly reference. Earlier reconstitution controls included no-functional-subunit outcomes when necessary rRNA/protein inputs were omitted. The report states at least four independent reactions in the integrated Fig. 2 datasets; those are assay replicates, not independent evolving populations.

**Discriminating controls:** No 23S-rRNA-gene control produced no detectable luciferase in the tested reaction. Author controls also investigated contamination by pre-existing rRNA in protein fractions. Substitution of separately transcribed/purified 23S RNA reduced downstream luciferase approximately 200-fold, indicating that *cotranscriptional assembly conditions* mattered. Mutant rRNA producing altered clindamycin resistance supplied a functional identity discriminator. Some buffer/temperature optimization did **not** improve the integrated reaction, an important negative result.

**Limits and counterinterpretation:** This is synthesis/assembly of RNA coupled to proteins *supplied from previous living ribosomes*. The r-proteins are not themselves all generated by this reaction, the S150 reaction contains externally derived enzymes and translation factors, and another native subunit is supplied in key assays. Reporter protein output is evidence of some functional assembled particles, not proof that every newly generated component can replace all initial ones at a renewal-positive rate. The 2013 paper should not be retitled or upgraded into whole-ribosome autonomy.

## C. Source 2 — Kosaka et al. (2025): both nascent ribosomal subunits made from expressed genes

**Original:** Yuishin Kosaka, Yumi Miyawaki, Megumi Mori and colleagues, *Autonomous ribosome biogenesis in vitro.* Nature Communications **16:514, 8 January 2025**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-025-55853-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-55853-7). [Original accessible publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-55853-7); [PMC manifestation of same paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711502/). **E2** original main full access inspected; supplementary files and source data not downloaded, recalculated or reprocessed. Original publisher identifies **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**; do not adapt/distribute licensed figures or raw supplements without a separate rights basis.

**Observed:** Authors used a prepared E. coli **S150 soluble-proteome extract** with accessory proteins, added natural-ribosome seed, and externally constructed DNA templates encoding three rRNAs and **54 ribosomal proteins**. After iterative optimization they detected nascent 30S small (21 protein genes) and 50S large (33 protein genes) subunits, then simultaneously produced both in one first-stage reaction. Function was tested using highly sensitive LacZ reporters distinguished from native background by an orthogonal small-subunit decoding signal and separate antibiotic-resistance characteristics. A purified nascent small-plus-large subunit combination was functionally active under a double-antibiotic condition; equivalent negative-control preparations without the artificial tagged rRNA operon were not. Authors showed incorporation evidence using expressed mutant **uS12 K43T** and resistance assays. Isotope labeling linked many nascent proteins to introduced genes, with bL35/bL36 requiring a distinct fluorescent-labeling method because mass spectrometry could not resolve them.

**Negative results and control boundary:** Initial small-subunit trials gave **no nascent reporter signal**, including an initial droplet-assay condition. Lowering rather than raising DNA template concentrations was critical, plausibly reflecting competition or energy depletion (mechanism unproven). Physiological potassium/glutamate salts supported the reaction where ammonium/chloride controls did not. An unexpectedly sensitive droplet assay initially exposed **native ribosome fluorescence background**; the authors used orthogonal pairing and antibiotic controls to separate signals. Thus a simple fluorescence count would overstate certainty. Main figures report three biological replicates for functional assays, and three independent experiments for purified-subunit tests; droplets and serial assay events are not independent reproductive lineages.

**Quantified closure failure:** Reported reporter-calibrated functional equivalents are **9.8 nM small subunits and 3 nM large subunits**, from **80 nM initial natural ribosomes**, corresponding to about 12% and 3.8% per respective subunit and a stated lower bound of **26-fold** improvement before one-for-one full ribosome replacement. This is an author-side throughput shortfall, not a general physical impossibility theorem. The first-stage native ribosome seed, accessory enzymes/proteins in the bacterial S150 extract, optimized operator-prepared templates, energy/nutrients and second-stage reporter reagents are material dependencies. The resulting parts were not shown to be repeatedly self-made and selected into freely reproducing daughter systems over unassisted cycles.

**Interpretation:** The phrase "autonomous ribosome biogenesis" refers to simultaneous component expression, processing and assembly in one preparation. It does not describe an autonomous cell, complete ribosome-reproducing lineage, or independence from a naturally produced proteome.

## D. Source 3 — Alizadeh et al. (2026): self-encoding ribosomal RNA, not ribosome reproduction

**Original:** Kasra Alizadeh, Dorota Klepacki, Nora Vázquez-Laslop and Alexander S. Mankin, *Rewiring the ribosome to translate proteins encoded in its own RNA.* Nature, published **2 September 2026**, DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10962-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10962-1); [original abstract and figures list](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10962-1), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42686901/). **E1 original abstract/figure-list and publication metadata only**: attempts to open complete publisher article resulted in an authentication gate. Original full Methods, numerical assay replication, exclusions and supplementary results have **not** been audited. Do not assign E2 or claim reproducible numeric effect sizes. The full article's redistribution rights remain unverified; named inventors disclose patent application **PCT/US2025/047444**. All interpretations below are limited to the publisher abstract.

**Reported advance:** Researchers engineered a hybrid messenger–16S-ribosomal RNA (mrRNA) and showed that a small subunit bearing that RNA joined with a large subunit into Ribo-M, which translated a protein-coding region carried by the very RNA that was part of the ribosome. The abstract says disabling mutations and antibiotic controls abolished this cis-linked output. A tethered configuration, Ribo-TM, united encoding, decoding and peptide synthesis on a single scaffold.

**Why it matters and why it is not the same claim:** This is a tighter **information-to-function linkage**: a ribosome can translate a protein encoded on its own rRNA scaffold, rather than only an unassociated message. It does **not** demonstrate that the encoded product is a complete new ribosome, that the original ribosome makes another copy of its own RNA and all its proteins, or that it generates daughters. The paper is not an independent replication of Kosaka's de novo component-biogenesis endpoint and is not evidence of multigeneration synthetic-cell heredity. Patent and source-access caveats constrain the claims.

Three institutions/research programs **do not equal three independent replications** of the same self-replication claim. The historic iSAT publication is an intellectual predecessor and is cited by Kosaka; compare mechanisms rather than treating them as separate votes for complete closure. Technical wells, assay rounds, subunit measurements and individual droplets do not furnish a biological founder or descendant denominator.

## F. Counterhypotheses, missing measurements and source-rights controls

- **Carryover versus de novo function:** Natural ribosomes, pre-purified proteins and bacterial soluble enzymes could explain part of the observed function unless discriminating controls distinguish old and newly synthesized particles. Mutant rRNA/protein/antibiotic controls and isotope measurements narrow this alternative; they do not prove full natural machinery has been withdrawn.
- **Fluorescence versus useful output:** Reporter function verifies a molecular capacity under tested conditions, not full constructor stoichiometry, output energy budget, waste handling or an offspring's independent ability to regenerate the translator.
- **Throughput:** Subunit ratios remain below one-for-one renewal. Any true extension must count correctly assembled **active** subunits after losses, not only molecular bands, fluorescence spots, or isolated functional ribosomal proteins.
- **Selection and optimization:** S150 accessory factors, natural seed, external genes and tuned DNA levels are supplied or selected by investigators. A selected biochemical operating point is not a spontaneously constructed system and absence of a full-closure demonstration is not proof closure could never exist.
- **Controls and failure inclusion:** No single study here reports a prospectively sampled, failed-founder-inclusive *whole constructor lineage* with ordinary energy feed but no researcher-supplied active translator and no new genome templates. One reaction with several droplets is not a population of self-producing descendants.
- **Rights:** The E2 sources have distinct restricted Creative Commons licenses (2013 BY-NC-SA; 2025 BY-NC-ND). The 2026 main article's reuse grant was not established and patent interests are disclosed. Only independent critical paraphrase and bibliographic links are added; no figures, paper tables, sequences, supplements, raw data or copied source code are republished.

## G. Six falsifiable **future-only hypotheses** (not experiments)

**P43-H1 — Seed-dependent functionality.** Apparent newly assembled ribosome output may decline disproportionately after accounting for the finite turnover of active native seed. **Falsifier:** independently traced ribosome biogenesis maintains equal or increasing active translation after original native ribosomes become nonfunctional, with measured loss-inclusive stoichiometry and no other preassembled ribosome additions.

**P43-H2 — Functional bottleneck dominance.** With present S150-supported recipes, the large ribosomal subunit remains the limiting contributor to whole-translator renewal even when the small subunit is more productive. **Falsifier:** quantitative full-70S activity accounting demonstrates that improving the current small-subunit reaction alone suffices to reach sustained complete renewal in an otherwise unchanged constrained system.

**P43-H3 — Ancillary machinery dependence.** De novo rRNA and ribosomal proteins will not maintain full functionality after externally supplied accessory factors and maturation enzymes cease being replenished. **Falsifier:** under a fixed resource input and matched assay, all functionally essential accessory activity is itself maintained by the same lineage over successive generations without prepared rescue.

**P43-H4 — Template continuity.** Reporter-positive nascent ribosomes will not remain heritably productive when experimentally supplied DNA/rRNA templates are not replenished. **Falsifier:** fully source-authenticated templates reproduce and support function in descendants under matched material/energy availability without recurrent template injection.

**P43-H5 — Cis-coding linkage versus usable self-production.** Having a ribosome translate its attached RNA can improve linkage of message to translator but need not improve construction of a second complete active ribosome. **Falsifier:** a reproducible, independent assay finds increased functional second-generation ribosome production uniquely attributable to cis-encoding relative to a resource-matched trans-encoding comparator.

**P43-H6 — Assay-to-closure transfer.** Technical positives for single-reaction protein output will not predict net replacement capacity when measured on whole-constructor inventories including assembly losses and waste. **Falsifier:** a preregistered full input-output inventory with independent blinded failure accounting shows durable active ribosomal units and inherited templates replacing all essential starting machinery without operator-induced step changes.

These are conceptual future research questions. No simulation, protocol implementation, experimental run, organism or self-updating world was created by writing them.

## H. Decision and handoff

**Decision:** Preserve Kosaka's strong positive new-subunit result and the explicit **minimum ~26-fold large-subunit throughput shortfall**, rather than either denying a major biological synthesis accomplishment or claiming fully autonomous life. The 2013 iSAT precedent remains a distinct limited-protein-input comparator; the 2026 Ribo-M paper remains an E1 self-encoded-function comparator until complete methods are verified. This is a new **ribosome-specific dependency gate**, not a revision of Pass 42 and not an endorsement of EERC.

**Next high-information literature-only question:** find a genuinely independent, full-methods source connecting newly made active ribosomes, tRNA supply, gene-template inheritance and repeated ribosome renewal **after removal of preassembled native machinery**. Positive and failed examples count; a missing study should be recorded as an evidence gap. Do not merely add another paper from the same lab for a higher pass count.
