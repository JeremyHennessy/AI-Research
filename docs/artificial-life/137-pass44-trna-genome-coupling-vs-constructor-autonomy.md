# Pass 44 — Functional tRNA regeneration and evolvable DNA do not yet establish complete self-renewing translation machinery

**Research-only critical primary literature review — 10 October 2026.** A distinct question following [Pass 42 on non-ribosomal constructor factors](135-pass42-self-regenerating-constructors-vs-closure.md) and [Pass 43 on nascent ribosomes](136-pass43-ribosome-biogenesis-vs-replacement.md). No experiment, model, simulation, organism, training, purchase, runtime, other-repository change or human/agent activation was performed or authorized.

## 1. Specific question and qualified finding

**Question:** Has any reviewed original study assembled three necessary pieces into *one* sustained system: newly formed functional ribosomes, functional tRNAs transcribed from preserved/inherited genes, and self-encoded genomic DNA reproduction **without refreshing the original active translation machinery or manually rescuing failed DNA transfer rounds**?

**Answer from these sources:** **No demonstration of this full conjunction was found in the three original studies evaluated here.** That negative scope is a review boundary, *not a scientific proof that no such study exists*. Two independent tRNA-expression programs demonstrate successful production of 21 functional tRNAs in defined translation reactions; an older Tokyo program demonstrates DNA replication/evolution in operator-managed droplets over 60 transfers. The earlier [Pass 43](136-pass43-ribosome-biogenesis-vs-replacement.md) independently documents functional new ribosomal subunits, with incomplete replacement throughput. No study in this pass puts all of those successes together in one founder-to-descendant autonomous lineage.

**Distinguish inputs carefully:** A natural energy/molecule feed is not equivalent to adding **already active ribosomes, translation factors or genome-recovery PCR products** each round. Nor is externally programmed droplet fusion/division proof that daughters produced those processes. Conversely, positive tRNA translation and DNA evolution results must **not** be dismissed merely because they fall short of whole-organism closure.

**Review grades:** Three sources below receive **E2 original full main-text methodology/results/Discussion review**, *not* E3 independently repeated experiments. Full publisher supplements/raw source-data files were not reprocessed; none of these studies was run or reproduced. Rights and program overlap are described explicitly.

## 2. Li, Baranwal & Maerkl 2025 — all 21 tRNAs transcribed while translation continues, but ribosomes are refreshed

**Primary paper:** Fanjun Li, Amogh Kumar Baranwal & Sebastian J. Maerkl, *Continuous in situ synthesis of a complete set of tRNAs sustains steady-state translation in a recombinant cell-free system*, Nature Communications **16:6212**, published **5 July 2025**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-025-61671-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61671-8). [Original publisher text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61671-8), [PMC manifestation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12228740/). **E2**: original accessible publisher main methods, measured results, negative trials, Discussion, rights reviewed; the supplemental PDF, chemostat trace spreadsheet and quantitative raw samples were not independently reanalysed. **Rights:** original publisher CC BY 4.0 subject to credited third-party material; independent critical paraphrase only.

**Positive result:** Researchers synthesized 21 minimal tRNAs from optimized individual linear DNA templates or from a nicked circular plasmid encoding all 21. In the PURE cell-free translation system the resulting tRNAs support protein expression. With a microfluidic chemostat, steady-state fluorescence-associated sfGFP synthesis persisted for up to **20 hours**. At **0.4 nM sfGFP reporter DNA**, feed of **100/150 nM linear tRNA templates** produced approximate steady-state sfGFP concentrations of **10.5/17 micrograms per mL**. The maximum reported steady state from a nicked plasmid was **13.6 micrograms per mL** at **2.86 nM**. The authors compare these outputs with externally supplied native *E. coli* tRNAs; the translation capacity remains lower, not equivalent to natural-cell or full PURE reconstruction. tRNA processing steps (including plasmid nicking) have explicit operator-supplied machinery.

**Failure and negative controls:** A first continuous-condition attempt with **4 nM reporter template** yielded an initial sfGFP peak but lost translation activity, despite external feeds. Higher NTP concentrations (2-fold) caused the reaction to die rather than rescue it; 1.25-fold NTP gave only minor improvement without steady state. Reduced reporter-template load was chosen *after those negative findings* to mitigate mRNA versus tRNA resource competition. At **0.71 nM nicked plasmid**, the chemostat did **not** show stable expression. Un-nicked plasmid-transcribed tRNAs supported substantially weaker batch reporter output than corresponding nicked plasmid products; the authors reported precursor/off-target RNA species. No-tRNA controls and commercial tRNA controls define assay dependence. These failures are part of the inference rather than hidden by the positive optimized condition.

**Crucial closure boundary:** The apparatus replaces approximately **20% of reactor volume every 20 minutes**, supplying an operator-prepared **protein/ribosome mixture** and **DNA/energy mixture** at a 3:2 feed ratio. The article's phrase continuous in situ tRNA synthesis does *not* mean continuous endogenous manufacture of the active ribosomes/translation factors in that feed, nor endogenous replication/transmission of the 21-tRNA plasmid. It is **steady-state replenished biochemical translation**, not demonstrated closed constructor renewal. Reactor duplicates (**n=2 chemostat rings** in key continuous readouts) are not independently reproducing artificial-cell lineages; a 20-hour sustained trace is one bounded continuous system, not biological generations.

**Remaining alternatives:** Conserved background tRNA could contribute to some reporter signals unless source controls exclude it; initial protein synthesis still depends on supplied native ribosomes. A high-sfGFP reporter output may not ensure stoichiometrically correct synthesis of the complete missing tRNA/translation-protein inventory under finite load, and some reduced output may arise from designed input concentration rather than intrinsic tRNA damage. Full withdrawal of prepared ribosomes versus ordinary nutrient replenishment was not evaluated.

## 3. Miyachi, Masuda, Shimizu & Ichihashi 2025 — single polycistronic tRNA-array DNA, with fidelity penalties

**Primary paper:** Ryota Miyachi, Keiko Masuda, Yoshihiro Shimizu & Norikazu Ichihashi, *Simultaneous in vitro expression of minimal 21 transfer RNAs by tRNA array method*, Nature Communications **16:7418**, published **26 August 2025**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-025-62588-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62588-y), [original publisher](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62588-y), [PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12381190/). **E2 original full Results/Methods/Discussion and rights**, figures described but supplemental files, sequencing reads and raw mass-spectrometry evidence not independently reprocessed. **Rights:** original **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, not generic unrestricted Creative Commons; this review reproduces no figures, sequences or datasets.

**Positive result:** In a prepared tRNA-depleted PURE system the authors expressed and processed **all 21 essential tRNAs from a single DNA template**. Their tRNA-array architecture combined a self-cleaving ribozyme with externally provided RNA-processing reactions (RNase P and T4 PNK) to mature linked transcripts, and supported translated luciferase, sfGFP and GUS reporters. Corrected tRNA sets were also used in an engineered genetic-code reassignment demonstration, with identity support from product peptide mass spectrometry. This is a meaningful genetic-packaging and functional-expression achievement, distinct from EPFL's continuous chemostat.

**Discriminating controls and failures:** The team explicitly discovered that **native ribosomes and EF-Tu contain residual tRNA**, requiring additional purification of both to make the no-tRNA background more informative. A 5'-G variant of tRNA for proline lacked activity; certain asparagine variants yielded low RNA and reporter production, and the fMet background persisted in omission controls. Various tRNA-array ordering experiments reduced function, and a **single long 21-tRNA operon** proved much worse than the effective multiarray organization encoded together on one DNA. Although the authors' main active configuration supported about **3.7 micrograms per mL** protein compared with **56 micrograms per mL** using native *E. coli* tRNA in an illustrative comparison, reporter and protein-specific effects differed. In tests of product-specific activity, luciferase made with the 21-tRNA array showed about **46%** of activity-per-molecule obtained with native tRNA; sfGFP and GUS activity also decreased. Main-assay bars typically derive from **three independent reactions**, not three ancestral lineages.

**Critical interpretation:** Functional translation with new tRNA is **not proof of full translation fidelity** under all downstream gene sets. Template positional bias, processing fidelity and missing natural post-transcriptional modifications matter. Reporter specific activity must be distinguished from protein concentration: the system may synthesize proteins without reproducing their ordinary function at the same efficiency. The paper does not continuously replicate the entire 21-tRNA DNA template in vivo-like cycles, replace native ribosomes or manufacture essential RNase P/T4 PNK itself. The single-template arrangement is favorable to *possible future linkage*, but no heritable whole-cell transmission/repair advantage was measured.

## 4. Okauchi & Ichihashi 2021 — DNA-encoded replication and evolution through transfers, with manual rescues

**Primary paper:** Hiroki Okauchi & Norikazu Ichihashi, *Continuous Cell-Free Replication and Evolution of Artificial Genomic DNA in a Compartmentalized Gene Expression System*, ACS Synthetic Biology **10:3507–3517**, first online **16 November 2021** (issue date 17 December), DOI [10.1021/acssynbio.1c00430](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssynbio.1c00430), [open ACS original](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acssynbio.1c00430). **E2 accessible original full main methods/results/negative and author limitations**, not a reproduction or raw sequencing-file audit. **Rights:** ACS original **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**; no paper tables, DNA sequences, figures or raw data reproduced.

**Positive and measured evidence:** Circular artificial DNA encoded **phi29 DNA polymerase and Cre recombinase**, both produced by a prepared cell-free protein-expression mixture, allowing rolling-circle amplification and sequence rejoining. The researchers compartmentalized template DNA and expression machinery in water-in-oil droplets, then repeated **60 operator-controlled serial dilution cycles in two independently run series from the same initial DNA population**. Circular-DNA descendants sampled at round 30 had acquired mutations and showed improved DNA-replication ability in subsequent isolated-clone comparisons. The improved DNA replication is a genuine **genotype-linked adaptive evolution result within the defined synthetic reaction**, not a complete living artificial cell or ribosomal-lineage replacement.

**Essential dependency and negative observations:** Every round mixed an aliquot of old droplets with **fresh preassembled protein-synthesis reaction components** and physically agitated droplets to induce fusion/division, rather than daughters making their own translation machinery, tRNAs or membranes. Critically, when circular-DNA levels approached the detection floor, the authors used **PCR to amplify the full DNA sequence, re-circularized the product, and reintroduced it for the next round** (rounds **9 and 18** in experiment 1, **15** in experiment 2). Later rounds sustained levels without that explicit PCR intervention, which should be preserved as a positive late-stage result, but the complete 60-round record is **not fully unassisted**.

The authors subsequently could amplify a complete DNA sequence at round 30 but **failed to recover a full-length sequence at round 60**, proposing shortened/parasitic templates as one possibility without establishing the mechanism. In a *separate* **44-round repetitive-linear-DNA alternative**, DNA concentrations declined and repeated PCR rescues were performed (rounds **6, 15, 21, 27**); sampled clones showed few shared substitutions and authors inferred adaptive evolution was rare in that mode. These negative comparisons constrain claims of robust autonomous inheritance across replication mechanisms.

**Observation unit and counterinterpretation:** "About 140 generations" in the source is an author-calculated **molecular replication equivalence** across dilution/amplification, not 140 tracked, independently reproducing whole cell descendants. The **two serial experiments share an initial genome repertoire** and are not independent laboratories or separately evolved biochemical factories. PCR interventions may introduce mutations and select which genome family continues, complicating attribution of every subsequently observed evolutionary event to only the cell-free encoded polymerase. None of the original translation machinery or all 21 tRNAs were encoded/reconstituted; fresh reaction mixture and droplet handling are explicit dependencies.

**Research-program independence:** EPFL/Maerkl's tRNA and prior Pass 42 PURE-reconstitution papers share authors/laboratory, so they do **not** constitute independent confirmations of one complete autopoietic system. Tokyo/RIKEN/Miyachi and Tokyo/Ichihashi/Okauchi share Norikazu Ichihashi and knowledge lineage; Miyachi 2025 also shares Yoshihiro Shimizu with Pass 42 Hagino 2025. These are **three original articles from two major overlapping research networks**, not three independent replications and not multiple complete living organisms. All three published authors report meaningful achievements at *different* module boundaries.

**What would falsify the present limitation?** A separate original full-methods study would need to demonstrate, in **the same system**, functional newly generated and replaced ribosomes, newly transcribed functional tRNAs, copied heritable templates encoding those operations, daughter-by-daughter provenance and continued production after **operator-specific** rescue/prebuilt machinery replenishment stops. Ordinary feed of simple material and usable energy would remain permitted. Pre-specified success/failure denominators and loss-inclusive machinery/energy accounting would prevent equating short-term throughput with durable closure. This defines a **future evidence criterion, not an implementation request**.

## 6. Seven falsifiable *future-only* competing hypotheses

**P44-H1: endogenous tRNA output versus external ribosomes.** A 20-hour reporter steady state with in situ tRNA synthesis may still collapse if newly supplied mature ribosomes and external translation factors cease being supplied. **Falsifier:** function and complete ribosome activity remain stable across many turnover opportunities with verified source tracing and with no fresh preassembled translation machinery, while natural small-molecule resources continue.

**P44-H2: transcription resource-competition ceiling.** Increasing tRNA gene load within one reaction will eventually reduce useful protein synthesis due to competition with messenger transcription, independent of whether tRNA is functional. **Falsifier:** an independent source-calibrated complete transcript/output ledger finds no adverse effect across controlled, comparable template loading when available energy, RNA supply and reporter demand are correctly accounted for.

**P44-H3: tRNA expression versus translation fidelity.** Single-template 21-tRNA production can produce detectable proteins but lower sequence-correct, active output across diverse genes due to tRNA balance and maturation differences. **Falsifier:** independently verified quantitative amino-acid/protein identity and specific-activity tests show matched fidelity and function across unrelated essential translation proteins at fair material/input budgets.

**P44-H4: molecular DNA fitness is not cell fitness.** Genomes adapting to preferential replication in an operator-supplied translation droplet system may fail to improve functional whole-constructor reproduction, even where DNA copying improves. **Falsifier:** same-founder variant traces show a precommitted genome-specific fitness benefit expressed in independently viable descendants under the complete constructor-renewal endpoint rather than only DNA qPCR enrichment.

**P44-H5: PCR-rescue and lineage-selection bias.** Rescued genotype histories may overstate unassisted persistence when early dilution trajectories fall below detection and operator PCR re-creates amplifiable templates. **Falsifier:** a source-authenticated prospective lineage exhibits the same sustained functional genome transmission and full-length fitness advantage without PCR/ligation recovery or investigator-directed preferential transfer, including failed histories.

**P44-H6: genome compression and parasitism.** Selection for faster-replicating DNA can favor short or defective genomes that lose required constructor genes, breaking the link between inherited sequence and complete function. **Falsifier:** full-length verified genome and essential-function continuity remain coupled across long descendant sequences, with failures and background/helper genome fractions included rather than censored.

**P44-H7: module-composition versus regenerative closure.** Separately demonstrated ribosome/tRNA/genome achievements may fail when joined in one bounded resource environment because they compete for transcription, cofactors, assembly and essential protein throughput. **Falsifier:** a single independently replicated, complete-cost assay sustains all three self-production modules and function-bearing daughters without specialized rescues; all startup/maintenance losses and ordinary feed are conserved and reported.

These are conceptual, falsifiable *research hypotheses only*. There were **zero** real or simulated experiments, no new models/organisms/worlds or external actions in this pass.

## 7. Decision, source rights and preservation

**Decision:** Accept demonstrated 21-tRNA expression and up-to-20-hour chemostat translation, as well as demonstrated adaptive replication of a two-gene DNA replicator across the documented laboratory protocol. **Do not** claim these establish a single fully self-reproducing biochemical constructor: the chemostat receives fresh ribosome/protein machinery; Tokyo's translation systems start with purified active components; and the DNA evolution protocol included several investigator PCR rescues and operator-managed droplet transfer. This is a coherent new **interface/continuity audit**, not a second review of Passes 40–43 and not support for declaring EERC proven.
