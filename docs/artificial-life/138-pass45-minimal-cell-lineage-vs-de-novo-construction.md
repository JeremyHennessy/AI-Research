# Pass 45 — Minimal-cell lineages versus de novo construction from nonliving components

**Research-only critical review | 2026-10-10 | two original full-main-text assessments (E2), one original abstract-level assessment (E1).** This is the next distinct test of the joint constructor/lineage requirement after [Pass 42](135-pass42-self-regenerating-constructors-vs-closure.md), [Pass 43](136-pass43-ribosome-biogenesis-vs-replacement.md) and [Pass 44](137-pass44-trna-genome-coupling-vs-constructor-autonomy.md). No experiment, synthetic organism, simulation, model training, production change, cost, new schedule or other-repository edit was conducted.

## 1. Question and answer

**Question:** Do published synthetic-genome and minimal-cell studies show (i) a cellular genome taking control of a living recipient, (ii) an already living streamlined lineage maintaining and evolving itself, or (iii) a wholly assembled cell arising from nonliving ingredients with reproductive and constructive closure? These are **different achievements**, and one does not imply the others.

**Finding from three primary studies reviewed:** (i) and (ii) have meaningful positive demonstrations. Gibson et al. 2010 transplanted a chemically assembled bacterial genome into **preexisting living recipient cytoplasm** and obtained self-replicating descendants. Pelletier et al. 2021 report that restoration of normal morphology in a division-impaired minimal genome requires a *combination* of seven added genes (based on the public original abstract). Moger-Reischer et al. 2023 experimentally evolved an **already living** minimal Mycoplasma lineage over approximately 2,000 researcher-managed transfer generations and recovered much of the fitness cost of genome reduction. **None of these three original studies demonstrates a newly assembled cell booting and reproducing from only nonliving precursor materials.** This is a bounded literature finding, not a claim that bottom-up life is impossible or that no paper elsewhere has demonstrated it.

**Crucial nuance:** Once transplanted, the synthetic-genome cells *did propagate and renew their material constituents* across descendant divisions. The initial living-recipient dependency must not be misstated as a requirement for researchers to transplant DNA at every generation. Routine externally supplied nutrients/energy also differ from externally supplied **already active** ribosomes, membranes, cellular infrastructure, or genome-transplantation procedures.

### What counts as evidence, and what does not

| Gate | Positive observation in reviewed source | Unmeasured or non-demonstrated proposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genome sequence controls phenotype | Genome-transplanted colony had donor-type properties and synthetic watermarks (2010) | A nonliving cell-free assembly spontaneously made a cell |
| Continuity after founding | Multiple recipient-derived progeny divisions (2010); propagation of minimal Mycoplasma cultures (2021, 2023) | An initial constructor without biological parent or preexisting cytoplasm |
| Division morphology | Seven-gene **joint** restoration of near-parental morphology in minimal-cell background (2021) | Each added gene alone is essential for life; normal shape equals reproductive independence |
| Heritable adaptive evolution | Four evolved minimal-cell populations regained relative fitness in rich medium under daily transfers (2023) | Autonomous establishment after intervention withdrawal or novel ecological function beyond selected growth |
| Physical/energetic autonomy | Real biological cells metabolize and reproduce while fed ordinary growth medium (all) | Independence from externally preassembled founding cellular machinery, or self-managed ecosystem colonization |

## 2. Gibson et al. 2010 — genetic takeover of living cytoplasm, not abiotic cytoplasm creation

**Original:** Gibson DG et al., *Creation of a Bacterial Cell Controlled by a Chemically Synthesized Genome*, Science 329:52–56, DOI [10.1126/science.1190719](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1190719), first online 20 May 2010; [publisher original article](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1190719). **E2:** original publicly visible main text/Abstract/Discussion, reported construction and failure/quality controls and rights were reviewed, but experimental supplemental files and original laboratory evidence were not independently reprocessed. **Rights:** AAAS copyright 2010; accessible to read does not imply a CC redistribution license. Critical paraphrase and original bibliographic links only.

**Positive observation:** A designed and chemically assembled approximately **1.08 Mbp Mycoplasma mycoides genome** was propagated/handled using yeast intermediate assembly, then **transplanted into an existing Mycoplasma capricolum recipient cell**. After recipient genome displacement the derived colonies displayed donor-type phenotypic features with the synthetic genome and engineered watermarks. Reported continuity exceeded **30 cell divisions** after colony outgrowth, corresponding to more than a billionfold dilution of original input cellular content (author argument, not single-molecule tracing). This is powerful evidence for information-bearing genome control and repeated biological propagation.

**Constraints and negative evidence:** Early genomic transplantation attempts failed until researchers addressed donor-DNA methylation/recipient restriction incompatibility (or disrupted the recipient restriction system). A single base deletion affecting the essential **dnaA** gene had stopped genome functionality for weeks despite near-complete construction. Corrected genome transplantation required extensive screening and genome quality assurance. The designed genome was largely a derivation of an already biologically viable species rather than a novel discovered functional design. Crucially, a **living cytoplasm, preexisting membranes, enzymes, metabolites and translation infrastructure** provided the initial bootstrap. The paper itself defines its “synthetic cell” as synthetic-genome-controlled, while acknowledging the recipient cytoplasm was not synthetic.

**Mechanistic discrimination:** Genome replacement can switch genotype/phenotype while using inherited cellular machinery as a *temporary launch platform*. The author's dilution argument supports downstream turnover and genome-directed renewal but is not a direct test of a bottom-up nonliving founder or of completely source-free biochemical apparatus assembly. Reject both an exaggerated “life created from scratch” claim and the opposite exaggeration that no new functional cells arose or ever made their own machinery after takeover.

**Independence and interests:** The authors share the J. Craig Venter Institute program with later minimal-cell studies. The published paper discloses Synthetic Genomics Inc. institutional/author financial ties and patent applications; those disclosures are relevant to interpretation but do not negate reported controls. Independent E3 reproduction was not carried out here.

## 3. Pelletier et al. 2021 — morphogenesis restoration is an informative failure-and-rescue result

**Original:** Pelletier JF et al., *Genetic requirements for cell division in a genomically minimal cell*, Cell 184:2430–2440.e16, DOI [10.1016/j.cell.2021.03.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2021.03.008), 29 April 2021; [original publisher summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867421002932), [MIT author-manuscript record](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/33e96f80-22b2-4315-b3f5-eafbe7d2f88e). **E1:** publisher original summary/abstract, independent institutional original-manuscript *metadata and abstract*, and PubMed abstract reviewed; **complete peer-reviewed Results/Methods were not reliably inspected**, so no E2 label, precise denominator or effect size is assigned here. The MIT manuscript is a submitted version, not evidence that the final publisher text has identical methods.

**Positive observation:** JCVI-syn3.0 had highly irregular cell morphologies. In the research summary, adding **19 genes** omitted from syn3.0 yielded JCVI-syn3A with more normal morphology. A subset of **seven acting together** restored a phenotype resembling the larger JCVI-syn1.0 parent: **ftsZ** and **sepF** (known division-associated genes), one hydrolase of uncertain substrate, and four membrane-associated proteins of unknown function. The authors used a **microfluidic chemostat** to observe time-varying cell propagation and abnormal forms.

**Essential boundary/negative control:** Smallest-genome viability or ability to proliferate in rich medium did **not** guarantee normal single-cell division. The seven are a joint set associated with rescue of normal *morphology* in that genomic context; this is **not proof that all seven are individually required for any cell replication** or that the organism acquired unassisted new division machinery. Investigators restored genes by design, and the microfluidic reactor supplied externally managed conditions. From abstract-level access the magnitude, replicates, statistical uncertainty, all failed intermediate combinations and possible off-target genetic contributions remain **unverified**. No false quantified negatives should be invented.

**Rights and dependence:** Published Cell article states an Elsevier user license; MIT's **submitted-version** repository records a **CC BY-NC-ND** license for that file. Neither makes wholesale redistribution of the final article or its figures permissible. Authors include J. I. Glass and J. Craig Venter Institute collaborators with 2010/2023; separate question and protocol are not a fully independent de novo-cell replication.

## 4. Moger-Reischer et al. 2023 — minimal cells adapt after massive genomic deletion, inside an operated environment

**Original:** Moger-Reischer RZ et al., *Evolution of a minimal cell*, Nature 620:122–127, published 5 July 2023, DOI [10.1038/s41586-023-06288-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06288-x); [original publisher full main](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06288-x), [PubMed original record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37407813/), [publisher correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06454-1). **E2:** accessible original main Methods/Results/Discussion and key figure descriptions/controls/rights reviewed; no source raw files, supplementary tables, deposition or code run. **Rights:** original main CC BY 4.0, third-party exclusions; independent critical paraphrase only.

**Actual strain/founders:** Compared genomically streamlined **JCVI-syn3B (493 genes)** with related **JCVI-syn1.0 (901 genes)**, both *already living*, grown in **SP4 rich medium**. JCVI-syn3B is not identical to earliest syn3.0: the Methods say it has a second rRNA operon, lacks an efflux-encoding gene, restores **19 genes**, and includes genetic-manipulation landing pads. Do not attribute all numerical findings to the 2010 synthetic-genome ancestor or to unmodified syn3.0.

**Design and repeat units:** Four independently propagated experimental-evolution populations per strain were operated for **300 days of daily 1% liquid transfer**, approximately **1,997 generations estimated from dilution** (not individually followed parent/daughter histories). A separate mutation-accumulation procedure comprised **87 non-minimal and 57 minimal single-colony lines**, with 20–36 weekly transfers used to quantify mutational input under reduced selection. Baseline and evolved populations were compared using both **maximum growth rate** and **competitive relative fitness**. The ancestor was a *single clone* per cell type; four technical ancestor measurements are not four independent founding genomes. Four evolved populations per strain are biological replicate evolution lines, not independently synthesized founding cellular systems.

**Positive and negative quantitative findings:** Minimization reduced initial maximum growth rate by **57%**, and ancestor competition-measured fitness by **53%**. Over managed evolution, relative fitness regained essentially the deficit: mean evolved minimal lineage **0.998** versus ancestral non-minimal **1.00**, statistically indistinguishable in that reported test (**P=0.957**, not proof of identity). The authors estimate the minimal strain's rate of *relative-fitness* adaptation was **39% faster** than the non-minimal strain in this experimental context (**P=0.032**, n=4 lineages per type), while **maximum-growth-rate improvement rates were comparable**; these are separate endpoints and should not be conflated. Mutation rates around **3 × 10^-8 per nucleotide per generation** were high in both strains and not changed by genome minimization. Some expected gene-category patterns were unsupported (e.g. membrane transport mutation enrichment **P=0.934**, biosynthesis enrichment marginal **P=0.090**). Minimal-cell diameter did **not** show the large increase seen in non-minimal cells; authors connect this constraint partly to epistatic consequences of **ftsZ** variants.

**Scope limits and alternatives:** Daily human-managed inoculation, nutrient-rich media and stable constant lab conditions distinguish fitness adaptation from a new autonomously established reproductive ecology. The 39% result estimates a finite laboratory competition-fitness difference with n=4 groups per arm, not a universal rate law for reduced genomes. Recovered growth in one rich medium does not establish general resilience under starvation, pathogen attack, fluctuating habitats, or unassisted dispersal. The study used real cells that genuinely divide and evolve; interpreting it as “no self-reproduction” would also be wrong. Experiment selection and growth environment may favour compensatory alterations after harmful deletions, rather than previously inaccessible new ecological functions.

**Correction/independence:** The publisher **26 July 2023 correction** fixes an author affiliation and a duplicated supplementary **Extended Data Table 2** (originally repeated Table 3); the online paper was updated. The collaboration includes JCVI coauthors from the minimal-cell lineage, so these are *distinct experiments*, not independent origin-of-life replications. The paper declares no competing interests; the correction and available code/data links were noted but not reanalysed.

## 5. Cross-program causal account and negative evidence

- **Genome boot versus cell boot:** Gibson establishes synthetic *genome* activation through living recipient machinery. It does not show bottom-up assembly of a metabolically active cell; conversely, descendants can later manufacture replacement components under genome instruction.
- **Viability versus well-regulated division:** Pelletier demonstrates that a cell that can grow with a minimal genome may still divide abnormally; adding appropriate genes can rescue morphology. “Normal form” and “self-generating lineage without a cellular founder” are not the same endpoint.
- **Evolution versus an independently achieved origin:** Moger-Reischer establishes mutation, competition and adaptive fitness change among reproducing cells. It starts after self-reproducing cells already exist; growth selection alone cannot answer whether a nonliving inventory assembles and bootstraps the first living constructor.
- **Program independence:** The 2010, 2021 and 2023 articles have overlapping JCVI / John I. Glass scientific lineage and different designs. Their three positive findings are not three independent E3 reproductions of a complete artificial-life transition.
- **Relevant failures stay visible:** initial genome transplant failures and dnaA repair (2010); malformed division in minimal genome and designed genetic rescue (2021); initial >50% fitness penalty, no appreciable minimal-cell size evolution and nonsignificant enrichment results (2023). A reporting-only review cannot establish absence of unpublished attempts.
- **What is NOT concluded:** No assertion of impossible de novo life; no “dead matter becomes alive” proof; no proof of fully unassisted ecological colonization; no extrapolation to artificial consciousness or digital agents. Existing genuine cellular self-maintenance should not be dismissed because initial materials came from biology.

## 6. Falsifiable future-only hypotheses (none executed)

These are **comparative evidence questions**, not operating procedures, experimental approvals or instructions to create an organism.

1. **P45-H1 — Founder provenance matters:** Otherwise comparable genomic takeover cases with functional recipient cytoplasm will establish descendants more often than attempts to bootstrap from nonliving mixtures without a functional cellular scaffold. **Disconfirm:** repeatable nonliving-start lineage establishment with verified absence of preexisting working cellular assemblies.
2. **P45-H2 — Propagating versus bootstrapping separates over generations:** Once successful genome takeover is established, descendants will renew essential constituents despite dilution of the original recipient material. **Disconfirm:** measured essential activities remain entirely attributable to irreplaceable original recipient structures over generations despite reproductive claims.
3. **P45-H3 — Morphological rescue is conditional rather than universal:** The seven-gene rescue is sufficient for morphology in the evaluated genetic/background conditions but need not ensure normal division or competitive fitness across all environmental contexts. **Disconfirm:** independently reproduced equal rescue across diverse environments/genomic backgrounds with matching quantitative daughter-viability and fitness endpoints.
4. **P45-H4 — Rich-medium fitness recovery has boundaries:** Recovering minimal-cell competitive fitness in constant rich medium will not imply equal descendant persistence in sharply different natural resource regimes. **Disconfirm:** registered, matched multi-regime lineage comparisons establish unchanged ranking and no resilience penalty.
5. **P45-H5 — Endogenous division does not entail ecological establishment:** Laboratory cell proliferation and maintained descendant fidelity can coexist with failure to found an enduring population after externally managed transfers are withdrawn. **Disconfirm:** genuinely self-established descendant populations after withdrawal with verified multigeneration genealogy, resource accounting and no manual rescue.
6. **P45-H6 — Origins cannot be pooled:** Independent evolution lines founded from the same laboratory synthetic-cell chassis will differ in adaptation yet will not independently demonstrate that chassis' original abiotic construction. **Disconfirm:** independent provenance-confirmed cell bootstraps from nonliving components at multiple laboratories with complete exclusion of inherited cellular scaffolds.
7. **P45-H7 — New adaptive function differs from growth compensation:** Fitness restoration may disproportionately re-optimize disrupted preexisting pathways rather than produce a new heritable ecological capability. **Disconfirm:** blinded independently replicated descendant fitness advantage attributable to a previously unavailable function, surviving relevant environmental and causal knock-out controls.

**Minimal future evidence record:** Explicit founder and seed inventory, first-self-maintaining-lineage timing, independent founder count versus technical repeats, genetic and membrane inheritance, energy/feed versus preassembled component inputs, descendant continuity after intervention withdrawal, errors/extinctions, predeclared functional fitness comparison and versioned provenance. *No such study was conducted in this pass.*
