# Pass 46 — Genomically inactivated recipients can be revived, but a functional inherited chassis is not abiotic cell construction

**Original-source critical research review | 10 October 2026 | research only.** Pass 45 separately established genuine genome-governed descendants from *living* recipient cytoplasm. This pass asks whether disabling the recipient's own genome while leaving its molecular machinery operating moves the evidence boundary, and what the result does **not** prove. One previously unreviewed **2026 original preprint** is the new substantive source. Two earlier evaluated lines of work are contextual comparisons only; **do not count them as new independent studies or metadata upgrades**. No experiments, organisms, worlds, models, simulations, training, costs, automation changes, production systems or other repositories.

## 1. Executive inference: a new intermediate state, not unstructured abiogenesis

**Positive result as reported by the investigators:** Seidel et al. achieved donor-genome-directed bacterial outgrowth from `Mycoplasma capricolum` recipient populations after damage to their own genomic DNA rendered almost all recipients unable to grow independently. An **antibiotic-free plating** readout worked even though classical transplantation typically required resistance selection. Original comparisons used a separate plasmid-transformation readout, genotype checks, two classes of genome source and two different chemical DNA crosslinkers.

**What “dead” means in this protocol:** the original recipient genome could no longer sustain replication/colony formation. The authors explicitly sought to **preserve existing transcription, translation, membrane and metabolic functions** long enough for an incoming genome to use them. Thus “dead” is operationally a **genomically disabled but biochemically competent preexisting cell**, not an unassembled mixture of molecules or a chemically inert, depolymerized membrane-less residue. Positive donor-directed descendants are meaningful; the claim of *life built entirely from lifeless unorganized inputs* is not tested.

**Bounded conclusion:** the new result is stronger in founding-chassis provenance than 2010 living-recipient transplantation because it removes a working **recipient genome** before functional restoration. Yet it does not demonstrate bootstrapping a transcription/translation apparatus, membrane, or complete energetic organization from nonliving molecules, nor an unassisted new ecological reproductive interface. We should not portray it as impossible, as already done, or as direct support for artificial consciousness.

### 2.1 A causal intervention: disable hereditary machinery while preserving cellular machinery

The authors chemically damaged the preexisting host genome using DNA crosslinking, then transplanted donor `M. mycoides` genomes into recipient `M. capricolum` cells. This isolates an unusually informative causal difference: the **genetic information of the recipient** is no longer competent to reproduce, but **some preassembled machinery** remains functioning. A successful colony is evidence that donor information can redirect that functioning machine toward renewed propagation.

**Important limits to the manipulation:** the treatment targeted DNA replication; it was explicitly selected *not* to eliminate transcriptional and translational activity at the useful treatment range. Preexisting recipient-origin components, repair/energy machinery, membranes and cellular architecture were not separately manufactured from scratch or shown absent. In addition, the recipient had a researcher-disrupted restriction system suitable for donor-genome compatibility: it is **not** a fully arbitrary bacterial chassis.

### 2.2 Do not confuse two different efficiency denominators

The published main Results report:

| Original comparison | Classical transplantation, untreated recipients | Genome-inactivated recipient transplantation | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Approximate growth-capable recipient denominator | ~`10^10` | ~`7.2 × 10^3` | Treated culture retains a **small nonzero viable-recipient tail** |
| Absolute donor-type colonies per reaction | `68 ± 59` from `N=7` | `25 ± 19` from `N=6` | **Absolute transplants fall** rather than increase |
| Author-defined ratio, transplants / viable recipient | ~`1 / (1.5 × 10^8)` | ~`1 / 288` | Huge denominator-dependent change |
| Reported derived headline | — | ~**500,000-fold** increase in *transplants per residual viable recipient* | **Not a 500,000-fold increase in total transplants** |

The authors also report a treatment-series effect: colony-forming recipient viability dropped about **one millionfold**, whereas transplant yield dropped around **tenfold** in a comparison. At more severe genomic-damage settings **no successful transplant colonies** were observed, consistent with an essential threshold for preservation of functional recipient machinery. The means have large standard deviations relative to the central outcomes, and `N=7` vs `N=6` represent transplantation biological reactions, **not independently created abiotic founders or independent laboratories**.

**Scientific issue:** if a very small number of native recipients survive with unusually high transplant competence, a recipient-viability-normalized metric could overstate certainty that every eventual donor colony came from a genuinely nonviable individual. The treatment-associated absence of ordinary plasmid transformants is a valuable partial discriminator, but does not individually trace the origin of **every** transplanted colony. Present revival of genome-inactivated recipients as the study-supported interpretation, not single-cell lineage proof beyond the original data.

### 2.3 The control that makes the conclusion stronger

The authors compared **whole-genome transplantation** against transformation using a standard independently replicating plasmid in comparably treated recipient cells. The original main Results report that conventional transformation was **undetected** in treated cells under conditions where genome transplantation remained detectable. This supports their argument that recipients had lost normal genome-replication competence rather than simply becoming much better overall at accepting DNA. It is not an identity-proof control: a large genome and a plasmid differ biologically in fate, so rare-survivor or assay-sensitivity counterhypotheses require more direct founder-level evidence.

The authors used **blue versus white colony readouts** plus **multiplex genotype PCR** on **three sampled blue and three sampled white colonies** from a depicted comparison; blue donor-genotype colonies and white recipient-genotype colonies supported the identity assignment. They also found that without treatment, dense recipient growth on non-antibiotic plates obscured donor colonies. **Selection-free** in the title means **without antibiotic-resistance selection**, not “no differential survival, no investigator-prepared medium, no screening or no selection of outcomes.” PCR on six exemplar colonies is a check, not independent sequencing of all recovered complete chromosomes.

### 2.4 Independent chemical manipulation is not independent lineage replication

A second DNA crosslinker, **psoralen with UVA exposure**, could also produce genome-inactivated recipients compatible with whole-genome transplantation in a relatively **narrow condition range**. This is method-level robustness to more than one genome-inactivation route, not evidence that two autonomous organizations evolved independently. More extensive photoactivation reduced successful transplantation; treatment harshness introduces a second causal bottleneck by damaging functional non-genomic machinery needed to express the donor.

**Contextual negative observations:** earlier non-Mycoides or intraspecies transplantation attempts could give false positives through local antibiotic-marker recombination, rather than full chromosome replacement. This paper tests a workaround. It did **not** show successful whole-genome transplantation across distant bacterial phyla or into a de novo assembled cell-free membrane; anticipated generality is a future research claim, not a measured result. The *M. capricolum* platform and compatibility engineering are essential provenance.

### 2.5 The strongest interpretation and two competitive explanations

- **Study-supported interpretation:** a cell that has lost the capacity to reproduce from its own genome can become reproductive when given a functioning donor genome, using its remaining cytoplasmic and membrane components. Some short-lived non-genomic activity is therefore sufficient to bootstrap donor expression.
- **Alternative A — rare recipient survivors:** if even rare still-viable recipients are disproportionately responsible for transplantation, culture-level evidence alone could misclassify the per-cell origin. Negative plasmid-transformation and viability controls make this less straightforward but do not supply a direct complete single-cell genealogy.
- **Alternative B — no nonliving biochemical bootstrap:** even if *every* recovered colony truly came from a recipient that could not form a colony on its own, the positive result might depend entirely on **enzymes and membranes preassembled by earlier living cells**, plus an intact donor genome. This is consistent with the authors' stated preservation of translation and metabolism, and is not a failure of the actual donor rescue finding.

## 4. Operational criteria must not change to fit a positive headline

What the reviewed data **do** strengthen: conditional bootstrap of hereditary information when the *old* genetic program is disabled, with an enzyme/membrane scaffold persisting. What they **do not** establish: an initially absent or inactive entire synthetic-cell proteome becoming constructed from inert building blocks by self-encoded instructions alone, no environmental feed, cross-phyla generality, unattended ecologically founded daughter populations, unassisted newly accessible adaptive ecological function, or machine consciousness.

A useful discrimination is **three independently measured initial-state variables**:

1. `G`: original recipient-genome replication competence (off here for most recipients);
2. `M`: preassembled recipient metabolism/translation/membrane activity (explicitly left on here);
3. `D`: donor-genome functional information (added by the experimenter).

**The study supports an `G-off / M-on / D-on` rescue. It has not tested the much stronger `G-off / M-off / D-on` bottom-up reconstruction from raw abiotic components.** “Dead” is a label for failure of one biological capacity, not an invariant measure of whether all preexisting organization was destroyed.

This three-state evidence map is a future **reporting standard**, not a biological experimental protocol or permission to alter existing EERC definitions. The [unified research synthesis](UNIFIED-RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS.md) and its [EERC v1–v7 evidence](../../data/alife/combined-path-eerc-v7.json) remain unmodified and unvalidated hypotheses.

## 5. Falsifiable future-only research hypotheses (all untested)

These are prospective *evidence-discriminating questions*, not instructions, actual experiments or authorized organism creation.

1. **P46-H1 — Rescue depends on still-functional old machinery.** In independent evidence, donor-genome rescue will correlate with preserved recipient biochemical activity even when recipient-genome activity is disabled. **Counterexample:** rigorous zero-active-machinery starting inventories followed by repeatedly established donor-directed descendants without borrowed active cytoplasm.
2. **P46-H2 — Survival-tail alternative matters.** A culture-level transplant-to-viable-recipient ratio alone cannot unambiguously determine whether donor colonies originated exclusively from genome-inactivated cells. **Counterexample:** independently verified individual-founder records excluding preexisting viable recipients for all analyzed transplant descendants.
3. **P46-H3 — Denominator choice can reverse the headline.** Dramatic viable-recipient-normalized efficiency gains can coexist with reduced **absolute** donor-colony yield. **Counterexample:** matched runs where absolute transplants rise without altered viable-recipient denominator or detection-window conditioning.
4. **P46-H4 — Plasmid and chromosome readouts are not exchangeable.** A loss of plasmid transformation alongside retained whole-genome transplantation supports genetic inactivation but may not provide logically exhaustive exclusion of rare exceptions. **Counterexample:** orthogonal single-founder viability and provenance observations that agree for all qualified descendants independent of construct size/readout.
5. **P46-H5 — Chassis destruction threshold.** Stronger genomic damage may stop donor rescue once preexisting transcription/translation or membrane functions are also lost, even if DNA itself is intact. **Counterexample:** equally robust rescue despite validated total loss of active non-genomic machinery.
6. **P46-H6 — Technique generality has phylogenetic limits.** Positive `Mycoides` recipient rescue does not entail comparable full-genome rescue across distant bacterial groups. **Counterexample:** independently reproduced full-chromosome genotype confirmation across diverse bacterial clades with matched failures and viability accounting.
7. **P46-H7 — Donor program can renew the first borrowed chassis through descendant divisions.** Following verified successful transfer, donor descendants can generate their own replacement molecular components even if the original scaffold was borrowed. **Counterexample:** long-term lineages shown to require persistent irreplaceable founder-origin active infrastructure rather than molecular renewal.
8. **P46-H8 — Genuine new function needs more than restoring viability.** Donor takeover, colony outgrowth and chemical-revival success need not show a *previously unavailable and newly heritable beneficial ecological function*. **Counterexample:** registered causal evidence for an emergent adaptive function beyond donor-source or preexisting-host traits, with appropriate nonlineage and borrowed machinery controls.

**Priority evidence gap:** observer-independent original-founder identity, complete donor-versus-recipient chromosome state, non-genomic machinery inventory, viability-assay sensitivity, descendant continuity and fitness, negative founders, whole-genome sequence identity for sampled offspring, truly independent laboratory methods, and a clear distinction between ordinary food/energy support versus added **already active specialized machinery**.
