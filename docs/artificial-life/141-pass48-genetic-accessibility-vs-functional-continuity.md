# Pass 48 — Genome-wide access to variation is not integrated, viable, self-maintaining new function

**Research-only original-primary critical review | 10 October 2026.** A distinct three-program comparison after [Pass 47's transplant-versus-chromosome-swap controls](140-pass47-genome-compatibility-vs-chromosome-swapping.md). It does **not** repeat the 2010/2016/2026 Mollicutes transplantation studies or represent any new scientific experiment, simulation, world, organism, model, agent, AI training, paid action or production change.

## 1. Question and main finding

**Question:** Once physical access to substantial genetic variation is experimentally demonstrated outside Mollicutes, does that access by itself generate (a) viable, genome-integrated new organization, (b) a genuinely *newly available*, heritable and useful function, or (c) independent daughter lineages that maintain it after specialized researcher intervention ends?

**Bounded answer:** These three original studies show progressively stronger **but distinct** links: a 2022 E. coli allelic-exchange *library* explores previously existing donor phenotypes; a 2023 Bacillus transformation program maps replacement-accessible genomic positions across separate hybrids with important distant-donor failures; a June 2026 synthetic-recoded E. coli effort demonstrates substantial engineered sequence redesign and impressive **operator-guided** recovery from induced fitness collapse. **None joins all these endpoints in one fully self-bootstrapping new artificial-cell founder or proves autonomous origin of a new inherited ecological function.** Genetic accessibility, within-cell viability, beneficial function and reproductive closure are separately testable, not the same measurement.

**Negative source search result:** A bounded current primary-literature search did not verify the *particular* independent non-Mollicutes **complete chromosome transplantation into a deliberately genome-inactivated recipient** sought after Pass 47. It did find genome-wide recombination and major synthetic segment integration into **already living** bacteria. These are independently important results but must not be relabelled direct replication of [Pass 46's revived-genome-disabled-cell preprint](139-pass46-revival-of-genomically-inactivated-cells.md). This is a scoped evidence gap, not a claim about all possible publications or scientific impossibility.

**Evidence depths:** Original main Methods, Results, Discussion, figure text and licensing evaluated for all three (**E2 critical main-text reviews**). For 2022 and 2023, material was available in publisher-indexed/original PMC full-text excerpts, although some direct PMC openings encountered browser challenges; 2026 original Nature Communications full main text opened. Supplemental raw sequencing, complete datasets, computational workflows and independent E3 replications were **not** downloaded, executed or reanalysed. The 2026 journal publication is peer-reviewed, but peer review is not external experimental reproduction.

## 2. Khetrapal et al. (2022): library coverage and transfer of an *already existing* trait

**Original:** Varnica Khetrapal, Liyana Ayub Ow Yong, Sylvester J. M. Lim & Swaine L. Chen, *Development of mass allelic exchange, a technique to enable sexual genetics in Escherichia coli*, **PNAS** 119(45):e2105458119, 2 November 2022 (issue 8 November), DOI [10.1073/pnas.2105458119](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2105458119), [original full open manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9659338/), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322728/). **E2 original main-text critical review**; original publisher **CC BY 4.0**, third-party material separately protected. The review below uses only independent paraphrase; no genetic designs, harmful experimental protocols, source figures or data are reproduced.

**Positive result:** This Singapore-led research program used a researcher-created genetic exchange system to produce a **collection of living E. coli genetic hybrids**. A reported library had donor-origin alleles touching about **96% of recipient chromosome positions in at least one clone**. Whole-genome sequencing showed imported segments ranging approximately **16–1,780 kbp** (median **298 kbp**) across observed clones; **267 of 353 (75.6%)** sampled hybrid genomes carried **one main replacement block** rather than an entirely new donor chromosome. The investigators tied a donor-acquired phenotype to a preexisting genomic locus linked to host-cell interaction, demonstrating genotype-to-phenotype causality *within existing E. coli biology*. This is significant evidence for useful **access to naturally existing functional variants**, not an intrinsically invented new function or nonliving cell creation.

**Crucial denominator:** The statement `96% genome coverage` refers to **union over an arrayed clone library**—not one viable E. coli in which 96% of the chromosome was replaced, 96% of possible DNA was biologically compatible, or 96% of founders forming daughters. Likewise 353 screened strains are *assay products*, not 353 independent abiotic origins. Positive bacterial survival was conditional on laboratory transfer, engineered parents and investigator-designed selection; this did not show new individual cells organizing from inactive components.

**Negative and interpretive limits:** Imports vary in length; many candidate hybrids and gene variants are never observed, and a chromosomal recombination event can be neutral, beneficial, deleterious or undetectable in the assay. The authors note that the approach requires manipulation/transfer competence in parental strains and a selection system; the paper does not test unattended emergence of the exchange machinery itself. A functional donor phenotype was **transferred from a naturally occurring donor**, not observed to evolve de novo in an open-ended new ecology. Future transfer to other bacterial genera remained an expectation, not measured generality in this paper. Author interests: original states **no competing interest**. Reported laboratory infection-related assays are not independent evidence of broader self-maintaining reproductive autonomy or acceptable evidence of beneficial ecological function in other contexts.

## 3. Förster et al. (2023): high collective genomic reach with divergence and survivor exclusions

**Original:** Mona Förster, Isabel Rathmann, Melih Yüksel, Jeffrey J. Power & Berenike Maier, *Genome-wide transformation reveals extensive exchange across closely related Bacillus species*, **Nucleic Acids Research** 51(22):12352–12366, publisher first online **16 November 2023**, DOI [10.1093/nar/gkad1074](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkad1074); [publisher original main text](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/51/22/12352/7424431) and [PMC original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10711437/). **E2 original full-main-depth critical review**, not source-code execution, independent sequencing analysis or E3. **Original rights CC BY-NC 4.0** (not the earlier 2023 bioRxiv preprint's separate CC BY-ND license). No figures, bulk source prose, raw data or genomes are reproduced. The University of Cologne program is independent of the Singapore group and of the Harvard 2026 authors, but tests different endpoints.

**Design and nested unit of evidence:** Living, genetically prepared `B. subtilis` recipients were evaluated with four donor backgrounds: **`B. spizizenii`**, **`B. vallismortis`**, **`B. atrophaeus`** and a cross-genus **`Geobacillus thermoglucosidasius`**. Each series ran **20 investigator-managed transformation cycles**; sequence snapshots at rounds 10 and 20; final sequencing was **8, 8, 7 and 8 lineages** respectively, with seven no-donor controls. The authors also compare wider hybrid sets, including **42 pooled strains** for cross-sample core-gene accessibility. Do not conflate *42 pooled strains*, *8 donor-series replicates*, *20 transfer cycles* and *number of viable individual daughter founders*.

**Positive:** Across the combined experimental set, at least **96% of B. subtilis core genes** were reached by donor-allele replacement from `B. spizizenii` in one or more observed hybrids; **137 genes** remained without observed replacements in the pooled analysis. This shows accessible **sequence positions**, not 96% donor-genome replacement in one bacterium, a jointly compatible donor cell genome, or newly evolved functionality. The original model reports core-genome replacement-rate estimates of approximately **0.210 ± 0.020**, **0.1000 ± 0.005**, and **0.003 ± 0.001 percent per DNA-uptake hour**, respectively for `B. spizizenii`, `B. vallismortis`, and `B. atrophaeus`. These are **laboratory transformation rates** in maintained lineages and not spontaneous reproductive or self-organizing rates.

**Direct negatives and counterevidence:** The cross-genus `Geobacillus` arm yielded **no detected orthologous replacement** in the measured window. Only **5/8 `B. atrophaeus` hybrids** had any detectable replacement; changes clustered in more similar sequence regions. Seven no-donor controls had no detected orthologous imports. In competition assays the **mean hybrid fitness did not differ significantly** from the reference distribution, but *some individual hybrids* became measurably better or worse and distributions widened with time. Importantly, nonviable or overnight-non-colony-forming hybrids were **excluded by assay ascertainment**, and some controls lost accessory elements even without new donor alleles. Consequently, “fitness neutral” means **approximately no measured mean shift among recovered colony-formers under the specific assay**, not unconditional absence of negative effects across all original founders. The author's own 2023 article notes that historic experiments with added selection or DNA-damage treatment produced different genome-transfer dynamics; do not treat same-team predecessors as independent replication.

**Interpretation:** High accessible variation under intentionally permissive conditions does not prove that all possible combinations are viable, adaptive, jointly reconstructible, or new. An unrelated-genera zero in one finite assay is not absolute barrier proof; detection limit, donor genomic divergence, and laboratory competence machinery are conditional explanations. The 2023 paper declares no competing interests. Its raw reads and analytical scripts are deposited, but neither was executed here.

## 4. Nyerges et al. (2026): viable redesign and fitness recovery require extensive externally designed debugging

**Original:** Akos Nyerges, Anush Chiappino-Pepe, Bogdan Budnik et al., *Probing the limits of genetic recoding using multi-omics-guided evolution*, **Nature Communications** 17:5311, published **22 June 2026**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-026-74300-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74300-9); [full original publisher Results/Methods/Discussion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74300-9), [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13287592/). **E2 original complete main text** reviewed; **not E3** and raw omics data/supplements not reanalysed. **Publisher CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, so do **not** call the work unrestricted CC BY or redistribute adapted figures/text. The original discloses relevant genome-construction and directed-evolution patents, industry employees and author equity/founder interests; disclosures are provenance, not independent evidence of invalid results.

**Key construction-status distinction:** The team chemically synthesized components covering a **designed approximately 4-Mbp 57-codon `E. coli` genome** in many DNA segments. **They did not integrate those full sequences into one viable 57-codon bacterium in this paper.** Instead they assembled and characterized **six partly recoded living strains**, with the largest **45.8% of the target synthetic genomic sequence in one strain**. The design aimed to remove seven coding triplets; reports of ~**162,521** intended DNA changes and **62,029** recoded codon instances describe the full synthetic design/combined construction effort, **not** completed simultaneous changes in one viable recipient. A separate completed `Syn57` line with a different design belongs to another cited study; its full construction cannot be credited to this `Ec_Syn57` study.

**Causal multi-omics outcome:** The original main text links numerous seemingly synonymous codon replacements to altered regulatory signals and gene expression, transcription/translation defects, and severe environment-dependent fitness penalties. Strains with **more than 2.2%** synthetic DNA in the relevant intermediate series lost growth on a tested minimal-medium condition. The research campaign reported **89 integration rounds, 113 editing cycles and 680 cumulative days** of adaptive laboratory evolution across its stages, plus an **author-estimated** ~6.1 trillion tested variants. An inferred theoretical variant-search count is not a literal independent founder pedigree and cannot be treated as organism autonomy.

**Recovery is real, but heavily scaffolded:** The authors used measured expression deficits and human-chosen intervention/selection rounds to obtain faster-growing variants in a troublesome partly recoded segment configuration. One comparison improved measured doubling time from **110.6 ± 5.83 min** in the **impaired precursor** to **64.8 ± 3.09 min** in selected variants, with optical density maximum **0.31 ± 0.02** versus **1.01 ± 0.02**. These are measurements under laboratory growth conditions, **not a comparison of fully completed 57-codon cells with natural founders**. The authors report local fitness recovery to ordinary parental performance but are explicitly still working on the **remaining five construction steps** toward the complete target genome. "Fully restores fitness" is a localized repaired-strain conclusion, not completed globally autonomous synthetic life.

**Failure modes and independence:** Initial sequence-recoded loci could be lethal; synthetic DNA carried synthesis errors and unwanted mobile element inserts; conventional methods accumulated parental escape sequences; strongly recoded intermediates lost growth in minimal medium and altered many expression programs; extensive measured phenotype repairs were necessary before combining segments. Fixed human-imposed mutation proposals, lab selection, design correction and reassembly are **not endogenous emerging metabolic repair**. Sampled clones/target libraries do not constitute independent abiotic origins. The Harvard-led team is institutionally separate from the 2022 Singapore and 2023 Cologne work; the studies are *independent programs on different questions*, not three replications of de novo-cell origination or unassisted evolutionary novelty.

## 5. Cross-program controls: “possibility” does not entail biological closure

| Evidence gate | 2022 Singapore E. coli exchange | 2023 Cologne Bacillus accumulation | 2026 Harvard recoding | What cannot be inferred |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Substantial donor/synthetic DNA accessed | **Yes**, *across a library* | **Yes**, *across pooled lineages* | **Yes**, *across 6 partly recoded strains*, maximum 45.8% in one | Any one organism is fully replaced or entirely synthetic |
| Function inherited | An existing donor-linked phenotype appears in a modified recipient | Genome sequence heredity, heterogeneous neutral/deleterious fitness; no broad benefit inferred | Growth fitness can recover after lab-guided debugging | A previously impossible adaptive function arose without investigator design |
| Source of capacity | Already living E. coli and donor trait | Already living B. subtilis, competence and supplied donor DNA | Existing E. coli cellular infrastructure and many selected interventions | Abiotic machinery autonomously built from unstructured reagents |
| Negative endpoints | Not every allele/block generated or tested; technique conditional | Cross-genus 0; distant donor mostly 0; colony-only viability ascertainment | Deadly recoding, minimal-medium failure, incomplete full genome integration | The negative lineages should disappear from the denominator |
| Independent lineage reproduction | Existing laboratory bacterial clones propagate | Maintained bacteria grow and transmit recombinant genes | Maintained partly engineered cells can be propagated | A new organization founded from inactive parts reproduces indefinitely without human stage management |
| Useful ecological novelty beyond original sources | No: donor's trait preexisted | Not demonstrated from genomic reach | Not demonstrated by fitness restoration of a damaged strain | Genotypic change automatically adds a new accessible and beneficial ecological behavior |

A relevant countermodel is **genotype-accessible but developmentally/organizationally inaccessible**: many genome variants can be obtained, while too few protein/regulatory contexts, resources or repair pathways allow them to become functional in the same descendant. Conversely, the existence of severe penalties **does not mean** evolution cannot discover beneficial variants under other conditions. Those are prospective interpretation categories, not findings of a new tested ALife mechanism.

## 6. Eight falsifiable future-only hypotheses — none executed

These hypotheses are **source-prioritization and evidence-discrimination criteria**, not engineering recipes, proposed immediate experiments or permission to create organisms.

1. **P48-H1 — Library union is not individual closure:** Broad pooled-library genome coverage will overestimate the fraction of accessible allele combinations that can coexist in one healthy lineage. **Disconfirm:** independently observed near-total joint compatible donor-genome occupancy in individually traced clones with measured fitness and no external rescue.
2. **P48-H2 — Missing founders bias fitness readouts:** Excluding non-colony-forming or very slow variants will understate the true negative tail of genetic changes compared with fitness estimates for only recovered descendants. **Disconfirm:** prospective accounting for all starting recipient cells yields the same distribution and denominator as colonies-only estimates.
3. **P48-H3 — Divergence changes reachable variation:** Genetic similarity and local genomic context constrain transfer rates and location rather than imposing a universal single cut-off. **Disconfirm:** matched independent studies find transfer rates and spatial maps invariant after controlling for homology, DNA integrity and detection sensitivity.
4. **P48-H4 — An imported useful phenotype is not newly invented:** A phenotype encoded in an original donor can be transferred to recipients without demonstrating de novo innovation. **Disconfirm:** complete donor/recipient provenance plus independent causal records establish a previously absent function arising after the transfer, not present in any starting donor.
5. **P48-H5 — Synonymous instructions have regulatory dependencies:** Large nominally protein-silent recoding may disrupt expression, growth and propagation even when designed protein amino-acid sequences are unchanged. **Disconfirm:** independent large-scale matched backgrounds exhibit no noncoding, transcriptomic, translational or fitness disruption despite comparable sequence edits.
6. **P48-H6 — Operator-selected fitness recovery is not autonomous repair:** Repair following investigator-targeted multi-omics choices is insufficient to attribute repair **strategy formation** to a newly self-maintaining organization. **Disconfirm:** founder-to-descendant records show endogenous repair-policy heritability and survival after specialist interventions end, with resource/fitness matched controls.
7. **P48-H7 — Ecological generality is a separate endpoint:** Restored growth in a rich laboratory environment may be decoupled from fitness or the same new function in untrained, resource-limited environments. **Disconfirm:** independent preregistered assessments show broadly sustained viability and novel functional advantage after environmental transfer without targeted additional changes.
8. **P48-H8 — Genetic viability is not reproductive closure:** A lineage can grow with altered code and still depend on preexisting cell machinery, investigator transfer and continuously supplied specialized active components at founding. **Disconfirm:** multiple independently constructed, traceable descendants start without any inherited functioning cellular constructor and reproduce useful organization over generations without operator-managed stage transitions.

**Priority reporting fields for any later research:** initial and final genome completion fraction in a **single** viable organism; union-over-library versus per-founder denominator; previous donor possession of claimed function; nonviable and slow-growth losses; researcher-supplied inherited active machinery versus food/energy; independently viable daughters; whether selection/readout and repair were imposed; genotype-phenotype causal control; external vs same-program replication; rights and deposited raw evidence. These are documentation criteria, not implementation instructions.
