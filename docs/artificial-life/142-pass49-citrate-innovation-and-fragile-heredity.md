# Pass 49 — A genuinely accessible new metabolic function can evolve, suffer fitness costs, and be lost

**Research-only critical original-primary review | 10 October 2026.** This pass changes the question from genome engineering [Pass 48](141-pass48-genetic-accessibility-vs-functional-continuity.md) to **observed functional innovation, genotype dependence and the limits of heritable persistence**. It assesses three original peer-reviewed articles not previously reviewed as Pass 49 primary sources: one from a separate Idaho laboratory and two related to the same long-term E. coli evolution program. The earlier [Pass 9 contextual discussion](43-pass9-ecological-path-dependence-evidence.md) of the 2008 citrate-origin report and [Pass 18's 2023 ecological opportunity study](97-pass18-turner-2023-constructed-opportunity-full-review.md) remain intact and are **not re-counted** as new primary reviews. No organisms, experiments, simulations, software models, costs, training, agents, other repositories or production work were undertaken.

## 1. Testable question and evidence-calibrated answer

**Question:** Has a previously inaccessible beneficial metabolic capability genuinely arisen through heritable variation, can different research groups reproduce that *type of capability* with different selection schedules, and does its presence guarantee sustained fitness and transmission once the experimental advantage or support changes?

**Positive:** Under aerobic culture conditions, strains of *E. coli* that previously could not gain net growth from citrate evolved the capacity to metabolize it. A separate Idaho group repeatedly obtained the phenotype under intensive direct selection; a later Texas study reconstructed how the same first functional step could be modestly beneficial, neutral or very damaging depending on a lineage's genetic background. A subsequent study evolved already Cit-positive clones further in an expanded citrate-only niche and observed growth refinement in maintained daughter populations.

**Negative:** Transplanting this lesson into a complete artificially living system would be a category error. The novel **whole-cell physiological function** reuses existing transporter genes and preexisting biological machinery; it does **not** imply a new protein family, an entirely new constructed organism, or independent constructor generation. Moreover, the genetic architecture underlying weak citrate use can be unstable: a specific 2018 study generated a Cit-negative revertant by growth without citrate, and a citrate-negative clone was sampled even from a continuously citrate-selected 2020 population. Several reconstructed intermediate genotypes suffered sizable competitive penalties and the 2020 study found substantial clone-specific mortality. **The available sources do not furnish a comprehensive, registered multi-founder selection-withdrawal survival trial.** One isolate's reversion is a genuine negative observation, not a population-level loss rate.

**Functional-novelty definition:** Novel *to the starting aerobic organism and its reachable physiological phenotype in that environment*, rather than new *gene sequence encoding wholly unprecedented enzyme chemistry*. “Novel function” has a contextual, measurable meaning. For general-purpose artificial systems it is the **new accessible beneficial capability and its reproducible transmission**, not its publicity or label, that matters.

## 2. Van Hofwegen, Hovde & Minnich 2016 — independent direct selection can obtain Cit-positive lineages

**Primary:** Dustin J. Van Hofwegen, Carolyn J. Hovde & Scott A. Minnich, *Rapid Evolution of Citrate Utilization by Escherichia coli by Direct Selection Requires citT and dctA*, **Journal of Bacteriology** 198:1022–1034 (online 17 March 2016), DOI [10.1128/JB.00831-15](https://doi.org/10.1128/JB.00831-15), [full original journal Methods/Results/Discussion](https://journals.asm.org/doi/full/10.1128/jb.00831-15). **E2 original full-main critical review**, not E3 external experimental repetition by this custodian. **Rights:** original journal labels ©2016 American Society for Microbiology, *All Rights Reserved*; publicly readable does not establish unrestricted content reuse. This note is an original critique, with no source figures, genetic instructions, source-code assets or bulk article text copied.

**Observed outcome and genuine independent program:** Under author-imposed selective environments, the Idaho group isolated **46 Cit-positive mutants** from multiple *E. coli* strain backgrounds, including ancestral LTEE strain REL606 and wild-type K-12. The original reports positive clones as early as about **12 bacterial generations**, some highly efficient variants before **100 generations**; this contrasts with the single Cit-positive clade arising after roughly 31,500 generations in the original daily-transfer LTEE. **Selection was very different**, however: citrate could be the main/sole usable carbon resource for a long period in these cultures, while other treatments lengthened the selection window before manual transfer. The 46 outcome count is a count of **laboratory-derived phenotypic mutant isolates**, not 46 genome-wide verified *independent de novo genes*, independent lab replications or autonomous artificial organisms.

**Genetic and negative controls:** Authors confirmed utilization using multiple culture readouts and consumed-metabolite measurements; they sequenced **four** representative Cit-positive mutant genomes, not all 46. Amplification and regulatory rearrangement enabled aerobic expression of an **already encoded CitT citrate transporter**, with DctA-associated changes contributing to downstream improvement. `citT` deletion strains **did not yield** the phenotype in reported tested conditions; `dctA` deletion strains were negative in the bounded short experiments **but one eventually yielded a Cit-positive strain after 134 days**. Accordingly, **DctA is important for the common rapid route, not shown absolutely required for every conceivable citrate-adaptive route**.

In the low-citrate weekly-transfer experiments, **15/16 K-12**, **4/12 B**, and **6/12 REL606** flasks acquired Cit-positive cells during the nine-week observation; these are separate *condition-specific* culture denominators, not a pooled global emergence probability. Delayed growth and transient rough, chained cell morphology were observed; a reversible rich-media cell-shape change is not proof of irreversible creation of novel cellular architecture. External maintenance and re-isolation of candidate cultures never ceased for a rigorously monitored autonomous lineage demonstration.

**Alternative causal account:** The new capability can be built by changing when and how much of existing equipment is expressed, rather than by inventing an entirely new catalytic function. Such **regulatory novelty still changes the reachable phenotype**, but is not automatically a previously unreachable *molecular* reaction or a proof of evolved species status. Original authors explicitly argue that direct selection duration explains their rapid rates; a later competing explanation emphasizes lineage-dependent and population-level competition. Different regimes may both influence accessibility; the Idaho work does not *erase* the 2008/2018 history-dependent result.

## 3. Leon, D'Alton, Quandt & Barrick 2018 — changing genetic history makes the same functional step beneficial or harmful

**Primary:** Dacia Leon et al., *Innovation in an E. coli evolution experiment is contingent on maintaining adaptive potential until competition subsides*, **PLOS Genetics** 14:e1007348, 12 April 2018, DOI [10.1371/journal.pgen.1007348](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1007348), [full publisher Results/Methods/control comparisons](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1007348). **E2 original full-main critical review**, not independent E3 replication. **Rights:** original **CC BY 4.0**, independent paraphrase; authors declared no competing interests.

**Causal design:** Researchers compared the effect of a *precisely added* regulatory construct that activates `citT` with matched neutral constructs in **23 different previously evolved clones** taken at distinct times from the **same LTEE Ara−3 ancestral population**. These clones are phylogenetic and time samples, **not 23 independent founding lineages of the innovation**. Careful controls for genetic background, neutral-marker fitness and promoter expression distinguish “this transporter is expressed” from “that expression is beneficial to the organism.”

**Disconfirming outcome and an unresolved source-count discrepancy:** The original reports testing **23 additional** pre-Cit-positive reconstructed backgrounds, followed by claims of **four** statistically beneficial comparisons, **nine** nonsignificant comparisons and **eleven** harmful comparisons; these counts sum to **24 rather than 23** if construed as three disjoint categories. **Do not infer an exclusive partition or silently change an author's reported number.** One-sided versus two-sided testing or inclusion of a separate control could explain the mismatch, but that explanation is **unverified** without a case-by-case original figure/data reconciliation. The independently unambiguous result is that **eleven** backgrounds were reported to be harmed, **five** by more than **20%** competitive fitness; apparent nonsignificance is not proof of exactly zero effect. All are assays on related archived isolates, **not independent origins**. The naturally evolved early weak citrate-positive isolate's measured advantage from the activating duplication was only about **2.2%** in the indicated laboratory comparison.

**The direct selection-withdrawal evidence:** The authors describe generating one **Cit-negative revertant** from an early weak Cit-positive clone by propagation **in medium without citrate**, during which the genomic duplication driving `citT` activation spontaneously collapsed. This is a *real observed loss of a once-accessible inherited function when selection changed*, but it is **one constructed comparison**, not a prospective measure of instability in all extant Cit-positive strains over many independent unassisted generations. Their more stable engineered knock-in was built specifically to compare background fitness without this unstable copy-number confound.

**Interpretation:** When an organism competes with clones adapting more rapidly to glucose, a weak ecological novelty might be **functionally reachable but evolutionarily unsustainable** until other changes allow its lineage to survive. This supports a causal distinction between *accessible novelty* and *selectable transmitted novelty*. It is a descendant of the Lenski/Barrick research family and a reconstruction of their archived LTEE isolates, not a new unrelated laboratory origin event or proof of open-ended autonomous organizational genesis. Engineered knock-ins constitute an investigator-controlled assay, not evolved unassisted invention of the genetic construct.

## 4. Blount et al. 2020 — adaptation to an already opened niche includes strain death and occasional trait loss

**Primary:** Zachary D. Blount, Rohan Maddamsetti, Nkrumah A. Grant et al., *Genomic and phenotypic evolution of Escherichia coli in a novel citrate-only resource environment*, **eLife** 9:e55414, version of record 23 June 2020, DOI [10.7554/eLife.55414](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.55414), [original final article](https://elifesciences.org/articles/55414v2), [original figure descriptions and methodology](https://elifesciences.org/articles/55414/figures), [original peer review discussion](https://elifesciences.org/articles/55414). **Source level: E2-partial** — original indexed article Results, selected Methods, figure captions and author responses were accessible; direct continuous full-page opening returned a client challenge. Whole supplementary spreadsheets, raw sequence reads and original figures were **not reprocessed**. **Original paper rights CC BY 4.0** subject to credited third-party assets; independent critique only.

**Actual population and ancestry:** The authors started from **three Cit-positive clones of one 33,000-generation LTEE Ara−3 population**, alongside their **three marker-reverted derivatives**, not six independent invention events. They founded **24 separately maintained descendants**: **12 in citrate-only DM0** and **12 in the ancestral citrate-plus-glucose DM25**, each evolved approximately **2,500 inferred generations** using **operator-controlled fresh-media transfers**. Studied clones were sampled from these populations and chromosomes sequenced. Evolved lineages were not self-provisioning experimental ecosystems, nor did the experiment independently create Cit-positive from Cit-negative donors in 24 new founding events.

**Positive and negative results:** Populations tended to grow sooner/faster in the citrate-only habitat; DNA sequence changes and copy-number amplifications arose, with recurrent genetic patterns suggesting adaptation. Yet **some measured evolved clones or populations appeared less fit than ancestors in certain competition assays**, and stationary-phase staining on a **subset of five clones** showed much greater “dead”-classified proportions in some Cit-positive lineages. For example, the LTEE ancestral clone was about **10.7%** dead-classified in DM25 versus Cit-positive samples from generations 33,000 and 50,000 at **29.6%** and **39.9%**, respectively, in the same medium; two descendants further evolved for 2,500 generations could be **53.0%** and **51.6%** dead-classified in citrate-only DM0. These measurements are **not 24 independent population-level mortality estimates**. Cell viability based on fluorescence at stationary phase is a context-specific proxy; culture-age/growth-stage confounding and missing population-level mortality assays were raised in peer review. The authors acknowledged limited replication and tempered the interpretation.

**A second, narrower trait-loss observation:** A sampled DM0-evolved clone **ZDBp874** was Cit-negative and lacked the `citT` duplication, whereas a second isolate from the **same DM0−2 population** was Cit-positive. Hundreds of screened clones revealed no additional Cit-negative variant in that population. The authors could not tell whether ZDBp874 was a *fresh transient reversion* or a very rare persistent Cit-negative sublineage. This is **not evidence that the entire DM0 population lost citrate use**, but it is evidence that a loss genotype can occur **even under continuing citrate-only selection**.

**Independence and critical interpretation:** Barrick and Lenski connect 2020 to prior LTEE work; it is a separately designed follow-up within one source lineage, **not** independent external replication of the primary origin of citrate utilization. Long-term refinement in a managed growth medium is valid adaptive evolution but **not** an experiment withdrawing the original ecological resource or the externally imposed stage transfers. A trait may coexist with resource specialization, apparently damaging physiological tradeoffs and occasional reversal. Fitness and **viability of daughter populations** need to be measured separately.

## 5. Cross-study causal separation: four logically different observations

| Endpoint | Source-supported observation | Not established by it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Newly accessible physiology** | Aerobic citrate-fed growth arises in old species using rearranged expression of preexisting genes (Idaho 2016; LTEE context) | A new protein chemistry or nonliving cell constructed itself |
| **Selection-conditioned recurrence** | Independent Idaho selected culture isolates arise rapidly in certain source conditions | Historical contingency impossible; identical rates in the LTEE's original short pulses |
| **Immediate competitive advantage** | Matched activated-CitT step is beneficial in some 2018 genetic backgrounds | Uniform benefit in all backgrounds, even when transporter is functionally active |
| **Intervention withdrawal and genotype retention** | One weak Cit-positive clone lost an unstable duplication during citrate-free passaging; another Cit-negative clone appeared in a separate citrate-selected population | A general population-wide trait-loss rate, or automatically long-term heritable function without selection |
| **Downstream lineage fitness** | 2020 inherited citrate function coexists with growth adaptation, occasional maladaptation and clone-specific death measurements | Whole-organism ecological self-maintenance independent of daily lab transfers, or zero costs after growth refinement |

**Three non-equivalent “withdrawal” concepts:** (a) remove *citrate* (removes the function's selective benefit); (b) stop **artificial selection for Cit-positive clones** while continuing ordinary nutrients and culture (can expose unstable genotype); (c) stop **operator-assisted culture transitions**, leaving a normal ecological food/energy source but no manual rescue (tests a different level of autonomy). The papers address parts of (a)/(b) and report continued reliance on managed culture; they do **not** jointly measure (c). Don't treat a negative (a) reversion as proof that every Cit-positive lineage must collapse under (c).

## 6. Falsifiable future-only hypotheses (P49-H1–H8, none executed)

These are *research interpretation criteria*, not an experimental protocol or permission to grow organisms.

1. **P49-H1 — Novel phenotype by regulatory reassignment:** A strain can acquire a formerly inaccessible ecological capability by changing when existing proteins operate, without needing newly invented coding sequence. **Disconfirm:** a confirmed phenotype emerges with no relevant altered expression, regulation or genomic configuration and depends on unrelated new chemistry.
2. **P49-H2 — Selection window modulates observed origins:** Duration/frequency of direct phenotypic advantage alters emergence and fixation probabilities even with shared possible genetic routes. **Disconfirm:** preregistered independently reproduced matched populations show equal transition distributions across meaningfully different selective schedules once all founders and failures count.
3. **P49-H3 — Intermediate benefit is genotype-history dependent:** The same verified functional module has opposite **competitive fitness** effects in different inherited backgrounds. **Disconfirm:** causal construct and neutral controls show identical effects for prospectively sampled backgrounds under the same environment.
4. **P49-H4 — Loss after withdrawal can be architecture-dependent:** Copy-number-dependent weak novelty may be easier to lose in the absence of stabilizing selection than later genetically consolidated versions. **Disconfirm:** matched longitudinal genetic-provenance data show no difference in persistence between architectures under selection withdrawal, with all founder losses counted.
5. **P49-H5 — Population and clone metrics diverge:** Improved whole-population growth can coexist with less-fit isolated clones, rare trait reversions, or high mortality in specific backgrounds. **Disconfirm:** longitudinal lineage-matched assays show identical individual and population fitness/viability distributions.
6. **P49-H6 — Initial innovation can carry energetic costs:** Novel substrate access may initially worsen metabolic balance and death risk, so net fitness must include survival and cost rather than only final optical density. **Disconfirm:** ancestry-matched viability/mortality controls show no excess death when growth stage, resources and sampling time are matched across independently evolved backgrounds.
7. **P49-H7 — Reversion is not absence of a capability:** A transient Cit-negative descendant under continued citrate selection need not indicate stable loss of the ancestor population's new ecological niche. **Disconfirm:** fully sequenced serial parent–daughter data show the entire population loses the capability and remains Cit-negative across all assayed branches.
8. **P49-H8 — Beneficial heritability and reproductive closure remain separate:** Robust retention of a novel metabolic phenotype in extant living bacteria will not, alone, demonstrate genesis of a constructor or a self-establishing collective lineage from nonliving components. **Disconfirm:** independent matched evidence additionally demonstrates founder construction without preexisting functioning specialized machinery and daughter-lineage maintenance after specialized intervention stops.

**Future source gate:** In independently followed founder cohorts, jointly observe baseline Cit phenotype, genotype and copy number, time/rescue records, fitness *and* offspring viability, no-citrate and alternate-resource retention, full founder-denominator attrition, selective-treatment and passage history, ancestry overlap, and direct support for newly originated rather than borrowed ecological function. No experiment has been performed here.
