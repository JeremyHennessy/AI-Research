# Pass 50 — Kinematic biobots, repair, and the boundary of reproductive closure

**Primary-literature critical review, research only; prepared 2026-10-10.** This is a publication-oriented synthesis of the previously prepared, corrected Pass 50 review; it is not a laboratory replication, model, organism, simulation, experimental protocol, authorization to run an experiment, or Ora Stage 3 result. Earlier Passes 1–49, the canonical Pass 21 interpretation and EERC v1–v7 remain authoritative and unchanged. Source counts refer to research papers, not distinct successful artificial-life founders.

## Main question and verdict

Do motile Xenobots collecting living cells to create offspring, Anthrobots forming motile constructs and bridging tissue scratches, or a separate cardiomyocyte biobot study demonstrate a *new, beneficial, independently inherited and self-maintaining reproductive system* once specialist human interventions stop?

**No.** The positive results are real within their experimental scope, but depend on externally supplied living cells, pre-existing cellular biosynthetic machinery, selected morphology, prepared environments, developmental media or tissue substrates. Observed motility, finite kinematic assembly and localized scratch closure are different outcomes from sustained construction of every essential apparatus role, including a constructor, with independently traced energy/material sources and reproducible descendant function.

### Primary study A — Xenobots

Kriegman S., Blackiston D., Levin M. and Bongard J. (2021), *Kinematic self-replication in reconfigurable organisms*, PNAS 118(49):e2112672118; DOI https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2112672118 (original open article; CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, requiring careful quotation and no redistribution of derivative figures). Source grade: E2, reviewed original main-text methods and reported results; not E3 independent reproduction.

Researchers assembled motile clusters from existing Xenopus embryonic cells. The clusters gathered **separately supplied living embryonic cells** into offspring clusters that themselves became motile. Four of five wild-type trials had one filial round and the fifth had two. Three experiments with AI-designed, manually sculpted semitorus parents reportedly achieved 3 ± 0.8 generations on average, maximum four; their daughters did not inherit the sculpted semitorus form, reverting toward spheres. These are finite rounds under prepared feedstock and experimenter involvement, not indefinite independent construction. The reported electronic-circuit utility was simulated, not a physical inherited useful function.

**Important counterhypotheses:** geometrical collection of loose cells, rather than full renewal of cellular machinery, explains the observed offspring; parent morphology rather than inheritable daughter shape drives performance; exhaustion of available loose cells can stop the chain. A living Xenopus cell is not a newly fabricated cell from nonliving feedstock.

### Primary study B — Anthrobots

Gumuskaya G., Srivastava P. et al. (published online 2023-11-30; *Advanced Science* 2024 issue), *Motile Living Biobots Self-Construct from Adult Human Somatic Progenitor Seed Cells*; DOI https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202303575. Source grade E2 original published full main-text findings, not E3. The authors disclosed relevant research sponsorship/founder interests; assess accordingly without treating disclosure as proof of invalidity. The article is openly available; verify exact article/figure rights before reuse.

Adult lung progenitor cells under cultured conditions self-assembled into spheroidal ciliated motile structures without each structure being hand sculpted. Among 2,281 assessed constructs, 1,127 were motile. Investigators reported cell-sheet scratch bridging by groups of Anthrobots. Ten bridge attempts yielded five stable bridges extending to 72 hours, with local closure evident in those five stable cases. This is not evidence that neuronal electrical function was restored, that a newborn Anthrobot inherited tissue-repair ability, or that the constructs reproduce through resource-closed descendants. In vitro adhesion/mechanical stabilization, signaling and selection of surviving bridges remain competing explanations of the observed repair-like morphology. Passive-material controls rule out only the specific simple effects they actually tested.

The cultured lung progenitor cells already possess biological machinery, and media/surface conditions are externally chosen. The paper is not independent replication of the Xenobot kinematic-reproduction endpoint, despite overlapping Tufts/Levin connections.

### Independent but lower-grade comparator — stem-cell cardiobots

Ho C. et al. (bioRxiv v1, 2025-07-09), *Derivation of cardiomyocyte-propelled motile aggregates from stem cells*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.07.09.663178. **E1 comparator only**, not verified peer-reviewed E2 publication or evidence of self-reproducing descendants. The USC group obtained motile/contractile aggregates from mouse stem cells after externally specified cardiogenic signals, including induction/organizer interventions. Baseline protocols had limited efficiency and revised protocols increased contractile behavior. This demonstrates another route to assembled motility, not an independent confirmation of Xenobot reproductive closure or inherited tissue-repair functions. No unverified preprint reuse/license is asserted.

## Comparison and decisive gaps

| Question | Xenobots | Anthrobots | USC cardiobots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reported outcome | Finite assembly of motile offspring using loose living cells | Self-assembled motility; localized in-vitro scratch bridge | Protocol-dependent contractility/motility |
| Biological constructor ancestry | Supplied embryonic living cells; pre-existing organelles | Supplied human progenitors; cell machinery | Supplied mouse stem cells; cell machinery |
| Externally supplied inputs | Living feedstock, sculpting, culture and selection | Defined culture/extracellular matrix/neural sheet | Induction signals, media, organizers |
| Function inherited through independent generations? | Sculpted shape not faithfully inherited; circuit utility simulated | Daughter reproduction not observed | Daughter reproduction not observed |
| Complete energy/exergy/feed/waste/upkeep ledger? | No | No | No |
| Independent successful beneficial reproductive closure? | Not established | Not established | Not established |

No positive claim about whole-system replacement follows from experiments on already living cells alone. An intervention-withdrawal test must specify exactly which supports vanish versus ordinary finite environmental nutrients remaining. Unlike a theoretical reproduction metric, failure-inclusive independent founder/descendant counts require tracking every founder, including extinct lineages. The viable simpler controls must pay for their real energy, materials, handling and repair too; **unknown costs are not zero**.

## Eight falsifiable future-only hypotheses (not run or authorized)

- **P50-H1 morphology:** artificially selected non-spherical parental shapes will not recur reliably in daughters without externally repeated shaping. *Falsifier:* demonstrably heritable shape across independent no-sculpting descendants.
- **P50-H2 feedstock:** kinematic offspring assembly halts when externally provided intact living cell stocks are absent. *Falsifier:* de novo construction of offspring cells from noncellular finite feedstock with complete ancestry.
- **P50-H3 benefit:** a scratch bridge does not establish neuronal physiological restoration. *Falsifier:* blinded functional electrophysiology and injury-denominator recovery versus appropriate controls.
- **P50-H4 transmission:** the scratch-closure outcome lacks demonstrated independent daughter inheritance. *Falsifier:* multiple tracked descendant generations reproducing the capability after input withdrawal.
- **P50-H5 energy:** a complete finite feed/exergy/waste/handling ledger reduces apparent net advantage over an attainable simpler mechanical control. *Falsifier:* verified positive surplus after all paid inputs.
- **P50-H6 independence:** dependence on shared source cell families overestimates independent laboratory replication. *Falsifier:* distinct independent founders using separately sourced machinery and protocols.
- **P50-H7 negative cases:** full inclusion of nonmotile constructs, failed bridge attempts and unsuccessful offspring chains lowers conditioned success rates. *Falsifier:* high reproducibility under intention-to-treat accounting.
- **P50-H8 closure:** even successful motility cannot replace all apparatus roles, including the constructor, after specialist interventions cease. *Falsifier:* failure-inclusive renewable end-to-end constructor lineage with paid surplus.

**Decision:** retain as strong evidence of context-dependent living-cell organization, finite assisted assembly and a limited tissue-closure observation. Reject as proof of unassisted self-maintaining reproduction.
