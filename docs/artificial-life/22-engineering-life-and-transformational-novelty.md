## A. Stepney's 2025 engineering framework (primary review)
**[Towards origins of virtual artificial life: an overview](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2024.0298)**, Susan Stepney, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 380:20240298, **2 October 2025**, DOI [10.1098/rstb.2024.0298](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2024.0298); [PMC full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12489504/). Open-access **CC BY 4.0**.

The author proposes three **levels of engineering explanation**:
1. **Requirements:** abstract characteristics to be explained.
2. **Design:** organization capable of exhibiting those characteristics.
3. **Implementation:** concrete mechanisms/algorithms/physics.

Her suggested *high-level requirements* include **autopoiesis, agency, and open-ended adaptation**. The review itself notes no existing virtual artificial life has been shown to fulfill all those strong requirements, although partial mechanisms exist. These are **a proposed conceptual synthesis**, not a globally accepted necessary-and-sufficient life test.

**Proposed operational translation for our research:**
- Autopoiesis → internal productive dependencies, material turnover, and self-repair after ablation without engineered reset.
- Agency → action/interaction changes *because* prospective survival conditions differ, not because a programmer assigned a fixed "be curious" reward.
- Open-ended adaptation → independently verified new functions across changing ecological conditions, with neutral controls and finite test horizons.

Do not transform three normative/engineering *requirements* into a universal scalar life score.

## C. 2026 "Transformational Novelty with an Automata Meta-Chemistry"
**Author abstract:** Alyssa M. Adams, Celia Blanco, Valerii Chirkov, Kathrin Korte, Eric Medvet, Eleni Nisioti, Sidney Pontes-Filho, Susan Stepney, Paul Templier, *ALIFE 2026 Late-Breaking Abstracts*, **pp.96–98**. [Author-maintained record](https://www-users.york.ac.uk/~ss44/bib/ss/nonstd/alife26-late.htm).

The authors report **initial studies** of an automata chemistry whose entities can alter **not only code but the computational language/interpreter**. They describe porting a **Physis** system from CPU to GPU and reproducing older results, with some novel observations. This source is a **brief late-breaking abstract**, **not an established demonstration of long-horizon transformational evolution**. The full 3-page late-breaking booklet was not inspected; no source code/weights were retrieved here.

### Interpret the mechanism carefully
- **Code modification:** a program changes its instructions while preserving a fixed instruction semantics (the "language" is unchanged).
- **Interpreter modification:** a program or system can alter the mapping of instruction symbols to operations, typing rules or transition mechanisms.
- **Transformational novelty:** a new kind of functional operator or representational degree of freedom becomes available, rather than a new program composition using the old operators.
- **Useful novelty:** novel interpreter state must remain executable, **causally heritable**, and enable at least one transferable new survival/coordination function under independent tests.

**Danger of false novelty:** changing opcodes or adding random noise may merely break existing executable code. Interpreters redefined by an external update script aren't a genuinely internally generated innovation.

## E. Two new falsifiable questions (research-only)
**AL-H13—Distributed causal individuality:** Can an objective lineage rule identify causally self-reproducing *disconnected* organizations with lower false replication rate than connected-component tracking? **Falsifier:** oscillators and glider-guns are falsely classified or the improvement disappears after hidden rule/seed changes. Source: [2026 Outlier causal review](21-outlier-causal-selfhood-2026.md).

**AL-H14—Evolving interpreters:** Does an ability to modify the instruction **semantics** increase the number of *functional, inherited, viable* behaviors under fixed computation/resource budget compared with a fixed interpreter? **Controls:** equal instruction-set size, random interpreter scrambling, nonheritable semantic changes, same external novelty reward. **Falsifier:** new syntax appears, but functionality/lineage transfer does not improve. Related published leads: 2026 Physis/automata chemistry abstract, 2016 Stringmol and 2020 MetaChem.

These are proposed research hypotheses—not results and **not authorization to build an evolving interpreter**.

## F. Why this matters for our research plan
The original question "can an artificial organism emerge without an LLM?" has two independent bottlenecks: (1) **self-maintaining, causally traceable organizational identity** and (2) **a substrate that admits viable new modes of organization and reproduction**. Stepney's synthesis may provide requirements; Outlier causal tracing tests one property; meta-chemistries explore whether the *space of possible mechanisms* can itself change. None demonstrates all properties together. Keep the three streams separate until measured effects justify an integrated design.
