## I. Central structure: three levels, three requirements
Stepney highlights that candidate definitions mix individual and ecosystem properties, high-level requirements and low-level implementation details. She offers an *engineering definition* with:
1. **Requirements:** abstract properties we would demand, not mechanics. She proposes **autopoiesis, agency, open-ended adaptation**.
2. **Design:** generic organizational processes capable of realizing those properties (repair, regulation, reproduction, embodied interaction, learning, evolution).
3. **Implementation:** substrate and algorithmic choices (automata chemistries, virtual worlds, self-rewriting programs, robotics, self-organizing fields).

The framework does **not** say all virtual worlds today meet the requirements. The author explicitly states virtual ALife satisfying them all has **yet to be exhibited**. Stepney considers systems satisfying subsets as potentially "partially alive" under some definitions; the strength/ontological acceptability of this interpretation remains disputed.

## II. Requirements are not universally settled
**Autopoiesis:** process continuously produces and maintains its own organization and identity despite constituents turning over, depending on resources from outside. A fixed repeating trajectory or engine reset is not equivalent to self-production.

**Agency:** more than a designer-allocated instruction-to-goal controller. The author frames it as autonomously determined goals and purposive actions including self-preservation, potentially at the level of much simpler pre-cognitive "agential material". We cannot infer agency from language, movement, or novelty by appearance alone.

**Open-ended adaptation:** continuous changes responding to other beings and an evolving environment. The author distinguishes:
- **Variation:** new instances **within** a model's set of types/rules;
- **Innovation:** the model changes (new type, behavior, relationship);
- **Transformation** (previously called emergence): *meta-model* changes—a new kind of type/rule/relationship becomes possible.
A merely vast finite state space can sustain variation for a very long time and still lack higher-level conceptual transformation. **This is a model-relative definition**, not a machine-independent theorem.

**Author's explicit assumption:** §3(b) contrasts Rosen's material/categorical claim that virtual life is impossible with virtual-machine/organizational arguments. Stepney **assumes virtual life is possible for purposes of the review**; she does not claim to have logically refuted all substrate-dependent positions.

## III. Crucial distinctions found in the full methods/synthesis
### Closure is simultaneously open and closed
The **living process** is organizationally closed in the sense of reproducing the processes/components defining its identity, but can be **thermodynamically open** to external material/energy inputs and waste output. Hence a simple "self-sufficiency" criterion that forbids all external input misunderstands the claim. Conversely a world replenished by an **unlimited engineer-controlled reservoir** may still not show internal autonomous organization.

Stepney highlights two self-maintaining modes:
- **Homeostasis:** feedback returns internal state variables to a range.
- **Homeorhesis:** feedback returns an evolving trajectory/growth process after perturbation.
For a developmental organism, return to a *trajectory* may be more appropriate than return to an identical static shape.

### Design mechanisms are plural, not synonymous
Processes include self-assembly, autocatalytic network upkeep, growth/repair, sensory interaction, homeostatic control, embodied resource flows, Darwinian inherited adaptation and within-lifetime learning. Each may realize pieces of the top-level requirements. None is asserted universally sufficient.

A virtual chemistry may be represented by primitives + reaction rules + a reaction-mixing policy, and automata chemistries may interpret programs as molecules. Many simplified worlds provide *fixed*, externally defined food renewal, constant energy and automatic repairs: such shortcuts enable mechanistic research but obscure stronger organismal-autonomy claims.

### Change of language is significant but still bounded by implementation physics
Under a fixed instruction interpretation, a genome can access a huge combinatorial space without changing what opcodes *mean*. Self-modifying automata chemistries can change the model and possibly its meta-model *if* a viable internal mechanism exists to preserve and transmit the new interpretation. A working compiler or an external instruction-set version update is not an organism's autonomous transformation.

### Conclusion: isolated mechanisms are scientifically useful but incomplete
The review's §5 calls the capacity to isolate single mechanisms a **strength** for identifying causes and a **weakness** for integrating a coherent fully living virtual system. It proposes interoperable *component models* and documentation of assumptions/interfaces, but also warns that these conventions may block unexpected new interaction modes. There is no underlying cross-substrate total "aliveness score" validated by this review.

## IV. Independent scientific inferences for our project (not claims made by the author)
| Stepney framework | Our proposed measurable check | Explicit false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Autopoiesis | Internal repair-loop knockout + constituent turnover and spatial boundary generation | Built-in engine resuscitation, inert stable glider |
| Homeorhesis | Recovery of *developmental trajectory* after unseen perturbations | Overfitting one target image or exact state |
| Agency | Prospective counterfactual viability under cue-shuffle/neutral reward control | Programmed task-reward seeking only |
| Open-ended adaptation | Independent new inherited function under changing ecology, compare neutral shadow | Visual novelty score; prolonged exploration of fixed library |
| Innovation / transformation | Source-backed semantics/operation revision, causal new function and inheritance | Syntax aliasing, external developer module |
| Organization vs environment | Cross-system resource/constraint ledger and interface mapping | Free external feedstock interpreted as internal metabolism |

These are **original prospective study ideas**; the paper supplies a conceptual framework, not numerical evidence for them.

## V. Limitations, disagreements, and next review
- Stepney's selection of autopoiesis/agency/open-ended adaptation is a **normative engineering proposal** and not a scientific consensus definition.
- Subjective experience, consciousness and legal/ethical status are separate questions.
- Computational abstract "self-construction" and material biochemical self-production need explicit bridging assumptions; both can be informative without being the same phenomenon.
- An observer's taxonomy and model/meta-model choice shape what counts as innovation. An **"unbounded"** verdict cannot be empirically proven by finite model runs.
- This 2025 article is a **review**, with no new dataset generated; do not mark it E3/experimentally replicated.
- The author's 2026 short conference summary is a separate source; a **complete review of this 2025 paper does not automatically promote the 2026 abstract**.
