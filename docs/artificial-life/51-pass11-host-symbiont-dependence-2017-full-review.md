# Pass 11 E2 — Fisher et al. 2017: inherited symbionts, host dependence and the limits of correlation

**2026-10-08 | Complete open primary article main-methods/result/discussion review, E2; no independent reanalysis or new biological replication.** Roberta M. Fisher, Lee M. Henry, Charlie K. Cornwallis, E. Toby Kiers & Stuart A. West. *The evolution of host-symbiont dependence*, *Nature Communications* **8**:15973 (2017). [DOI 10.1038/ncomms15973](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15973) · [publisher full open text](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms15973). Article openly accessible; underlying cross-study effect estimates available from corresponding author on request, **not downloaded/recomputed**.

## Why this source matters

A system can be **functionally obligate** yet still fail to become a new evolutionary individual. Fisher et al. examine whether transmission mode and service delivered by symbionts correlate with host fitness dependence across taxa. It adds a broad comparative context to direct experiments in [Giger 2024](49-pass11-induced-endosymbiosis-2024-full-review.md) and the strict separation of *ecological dependence* from *reproductive cohesion*.

## Actual data and units

- Literature-based meta-comparative analysis of **106 unique host–bacterial symbioses**, involving **58 bacterial symbionts** and **89 host species**. These **are not 106 new experimental evolutionary origins**, and host/symbiont taxa overlap.
- Host dependence operationalized as **percentage loss in measured host fitness after experimental removal of symbiont**, using published experiments with often different proxies (fecundity, size, survival, time and resistance).
- Main predictors: vertical vs horizontal transmission (defined by predominant average mode, not absolute exclusivity), nutritional vs defensive benefits, genome size, intra- vs extracellular location and host/symbiont phylogenetic relatedness.
- Fitted Bayesian phylogenetic mixed models to account for nonindependent evolutionary histories, and ran restricted fitness-proxy and uncertain-transmission sensitivity analyses.

## Headline published associations with important denominators

- Symbiont removal correlated with **52% mean host fitness decrease for vertical** transmission versus **21% for horizontal** transmission (*not* a causal effect of imposing transmission mode). The corresponding plot identifies **N=38 unique symbioses** available for that comparison, not all 106.
- Nutritional symbionts correlated with a host fitness decrease of **43%**, compared with **28%** for defensive symbionts; the figure depicts **N=28 nutritional and N=7 defensive** pairs.
- Vertically transmitted symbionts tended to have reduced genome size, and host dependence was inversely correlated with symbiont genome size **within vertically transmitted** symbionts, not in horizontally transmitted comparisons.
- Intracellular location by itself **did not significantly predict greater host dependence** in the fitted models (paper discusses possible confounds): an important negative against 'physical enclosure is enough'.
- Several results remained in sensitivity analyses adjusting phylogeny, fitness measurement type and ambiguous partially biparental symbiont transmission.

## Limitations flagged by the authors and this review

1. **Correlation, not demonstrated evolutionary causation:** present-day dependence and transmission were measured across established pairings. Study does not evolve those traits in fresh independent laboratory lineages.
2. **Selection/measurement bias:** symbionts that cannot be removed without harming host may be missing. Biased taxonomic sampling includes relatively few marine associations and disproportionately many insect datasets.
3. **Noncomparable host fitness estimates:** removing a defensive symbiont without realistic predator/pathogen pressure artificially understates its functional benefit, despite the regression adjustments.
4. **Host dependence != mutual dependence:** whether the symbiont can live freely may differ from whether host requires it; both directions must be assayed for mutual dependence.
5. **Dependence != cohesion:** parents may need a partner to live but transmit that partner imperfectly to daughters; stable ancestry and reproductive synchrony are independent dimensions.
6. **Dependence != organism:** co-location, genome reduction, trophic interdependence and even perfect vertical transmission are not by themselves proof of emergent higher-level repair, adaptive learning or phenomenal consciousness.

## Combined-path inference (original, unverified)

A *reproductive-interface* hypothesis gains support from correlated vertical transmission and host dependence; however, causal evidence must come from perturbing transmission/functional service separately.

**Prospective orthogonal test:** match host metabolic benefit while comparing vertical/high-fidelity transmission, reassembled horizontal acquisition and random partner reassortment. Define:
- dependence: host survival/offspring after partner removal and partner rescue;
- cohesion: co-transmission frequency to **independently viable daughter units** over time;
- persistence: post-support-withdrawal daughter-line survival **without human-selected carriers**;
- group innovation: novel inherited capability that disappears with interface knockout and returns with causal rescue.

**Null:** vertical transmission correlates with dependence only through ancestral genome size or ecological niche; or has no effect on autonomous higher-level heredity after environment and phylogeny are controlled.
