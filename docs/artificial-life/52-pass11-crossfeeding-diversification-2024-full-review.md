## Exact question and interaction

Can a community member supplying a metabolite preserve new diversity in another evolving species?

- *Acinetobacter johnsonii* metabolizes **benzyl alcohol** and leaks **benzoate**; *Pseudomonas putida* consumes benzoate. This is **unidirectional commensal cross-feeding**, not experimentally evolved bidirectional obligate mutualism.
- Authors report **four independently evolved replicates** of *P. putida* monocultures and **four co-culture lines** with *A. johnsonii*, followed for **30 days / ~200 generations**, plus four producer monocultures in the original experiment.
- **Critical reuse:** the original evolution experiment was published by **Rodríguez-Verdugo & Ackermann** previously. The 2024 paper explores its archived evolved outcomes, selected isolates, genomic associations and **additional** competition/replay assays. Do **not** count a second set of independent 200-generation origins. Invasion assays use morphotypes from **one** replicate; 30 isolates per line are not independent whole-population evolutionary replicates.

## Main author results and negative mechanisms

| Report | What is causally supported | What remains ambiguous |
|---|---|---|
| Two *P. putida* colony morphotypes appeared in monoculture and co-culture; both persisted **~150 generations only in co-culture** | A second species can maintain within-species diversification under the tested resource ecology | Does not mean lineages form a joint inheritable organism |
| High-yield *fleQ* and related flagellar-regulation mutations became fixed in some monocultures but remained polymorphic under co-culture | Motility/biofilm reductions and growth-yield trade-offs correlate with genomic changes | Not every morphotype's genetics fully reconstructed or experimentally rescued |
| Direct swim/biofilm tests identified loss of motility in some *fleQ* mutants | Functional evidence for an individual genotype trade-off | Doesn't establish novel group reproduction |
| Five starting-frequency reciprocal invasion assay, using *one* co-culture replicate, **did not support** the authors' frequency-dependent selection hypothesis | A valuable falsification; early mechanism guess rejected | Other ecological mechanisms remain open |
| Population replay with *A. johnsonii* showed stabilizing effect on polymorphism | Community context matters for coexistence | May arise from changed resource concentration / population size rather than distinct partner-induced evolutionary constraints |

## Crucial missing control from authors' own discussion

They did **not** carry out a matched **exogenously supplied benzoate** evolution arm with equivalent metabolite flux/concentration in otherwise identical *P. putida* monocultures. The monocultures used residual assimilable organic carbon, while cocultures included biologically produced benzoate. Therefore, a population-size/extra-resource explanation cannot be ruled out.

**Most important unanswered counterfactual:** does a *partner-caused* ecological opportunity alter evolutionary diversity beyond what the same material/resource inflow would do if supplied externally?

## Interfacing with individualhood research (original hypothesis, not published result)

There are at least three non-equivalent evolutionary outcomes:
1. **Ecological buffering**: one partner's by-product supports another's density or polymorphism (study supports this qualitatively).
2. **Heritable relational dependence**: lineage fitness or transmitted function depends specifically on the *identity/behavior* of its partner, not only a general nutrient concentration (not demonstrated by the resource comparison).
3. **Collective reproductive unit**: the interacting partnership makes daughters retaining partner coupling, and these daughters themselves reproduce, after externally selected re-pairing is disabled (**not demonstrated**).

A proposed future causal assay would contrast (i) active producer, (ii) same externally supplied metabolite flux, (iii) metabolite-free controls and (iv) partner-depleted plus restored product/partner. Add group-level genealogies and measure function with no designer-generated new niche reward.
