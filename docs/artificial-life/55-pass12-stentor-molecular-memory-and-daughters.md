# Pass 12 — Stentor single-cell habituation, inherited state, and unresolved molecular mechanism

**2026-10-08 | Primary evidence reconnaissance, E1 (selective main-text sections), NOT complete independent full-article E2 review or reproduction.** Scope: publicly indexed original sections, primary publisher highlights, PubMed bibliographic record, several indexed result/method segments. Whole PDF, all supplemental assay denominators, raw data and complete figure sets were **not** independently inspected. No model, cell culture or external code executed.

## Exact primary publication

Deepa H. Rajan, Ashley Albright, Hyeyoon Kim, Ulises Diaz, Yina Hudnall, Niklas Steube, Gautam Dey, Tao Liu and Wallace F. Marshall. **Molecular pathways for learning in the single-cell Stentor coeruleus**, Current Biology 36(9):2367–2381.e9 (published 2026-05-04; online 2026-04-22). DOI [10.1016/j.cub.2026.03.080](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2026.03.080). [Primary PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42025171/); [open primary PMC index with selected full-text segments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13192338/).

## Authors' reported interventions and results

- Stentor contracts to mechanical taps. Repeated fixed-force taps reduce contraction probability while stronger/electrical stimuli can still produce contractions. That specificity helps separate habituation from irreversible motor failure, but is not proof of consciousness.
- Surprisingly, inhibition of new protein translation with **puromycin or cycloheximide** accelerated habituation and prolonged apparent response attenuation. The authors interpret continued protein synthesis as contributing to **recovery of normal responsiveness**, rather than being required to *form* this specific short-timescale habituation.
- Indexed main text describes **40 μg/ml puromycin** short-term training, **27.5 μg/ml** for longer memory tests; approximately **12 hours of training**, test pulses through **90 minutes** after training. Cycloheximide arms used separately selected doses for short vs overnight tests. These are drug-specific laboratory effects, not transferable engineering memory recipes.
- Pharmacological and biochemical evidence points toward **calcium signaling and phosphorylation**. RNAi reducing the EF-hand calcium-binding protein **SteCoe_6763** accelerated habituation; increased extracellular calcium altered habituation; kinase/phosphatase inhibitors impaired it.
- With **KN-93** versus its control **KN-92**, authors report differences consistent with a potential CaMKII contribution, **but both compounds also affect voltage-gated calcium channels**; KN-93 interacts with other kinases and channels. A pharmacological effect does **not** uniquely identify CaMKII or an exact molecular storage molecule.
- Most intriguing observation: the **habituated behavioral state could remain in daughter cells after division**. This is evidence for transfer/persistence of an acquired state over at least some divisions, **not** proof of DNA mutation, specific epigenetic carrier, stable inheritance across long independently reproducing lineages, or new collective individuality.

## Critical counterevidence and research integrity

1. **Detachment exclusion**: translation inhibitors made cells more likely to detach during tapping, which the paper itself flags as a possible quantification confound. Controls with higher-force and electrical contraction help but do not rule out all behavior-classification biases.
2. **Causal attribution**: mechanoreceptor inactivation by phosphorylation is a **working explanation**, not biochemically proven causal storage across daughters. Knockdown of one calcium-binding candidate and broad inhibitors do not reconstruct the complete learning pathway.
3. **Memory persistence vs genetic evolution**: detecting an altered daughter response does not identify a stable hereditary genome adaptation or reproducible ecological fitness gain.
4. **Within-life behavior vs training task**: this is a laboratory mechanical-tap stimulus deliberately chosen by scientists; it demonstrates non-neural habituation, not externally unprompted emergence of broadly useful prediction, language or moral behavior.
5. **Replication/rights**: scientific results are author-reported, no raw figure recomputation; article lists Creative Commons open-access conditions, but raw data and supplemental materials carry independent provenance/rights to check before copying.

## Historical lineage and competing findings

- [Rajan et al. 2022/2023, Single-cell analysis of habituation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2022.11.010) established a **step-like change per cell** whose asynchronous timing yields a gradually changing whole-population curve. This is not a distinct laboratory demonstration of daughter inheritance.
- [Rajan & Marshall 2025, receptor-inactivation model](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.05.071) proposes changing active receptor counts as a hidden state, and reported several challenge predictions, including weak/strong alternating stimuli and memory from continued otherwise asymptotic training. Its physical identity remains hypothetical.
- [Escobedo et al. June 2026 bioRxiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.06.730631) reports a **fractional-order approximately −30 dB/decade response** across temporal frequencies and says earlier models do not, as formulated, explain this. It is **not peer-reviewed independent replication** and comes from a related research community. Do not select the receptor model as final truth.

## Distinguishing future scientific tests — not performed

| Proposed causal question | Matched null/alternative | Falsifying outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Does prior tap history causally determine later response after washout? | Same current stimulation, different blinded histories, untouched/sham groups, matched detachment rates | Attenuation disappears once handling/movement controls match |
| What carries the memory across division? | Mother vs daughters, protein-state perturbation/rescue, unchanged genotype, daughters reared apart | Daughter effect explained by common medium or direct exposure, rather than transferred cell state |
| Does biochemical maintenance encode or erase habituation? | Protein synthesis inhibition vs matched viability/contractility and alternative interventions | Reduced contraction wholly explained by toxicity or fatigue |
| Does the hidden state improve ecological viability? | Perturbation tasks neither used to train nor select cells; independent costs | No benefit after correcting energy, damage and false responding |
| Can a non-neural process predict unfamiliar sequences? | Cue timing inversion, shuffled histories, reflex-only benchmark | Apparent 'learning' is a fixed reflex or response decay with no history-conditioned adaptation |

**Research interpretation:** persistent **chemical state** is one of several candidate forms of low-level information storage. Whether that state can evolve and be maintained as a necessary, transferable organizational relation in a digital ecology remains untested. No claim of an emergent conscious organism.
