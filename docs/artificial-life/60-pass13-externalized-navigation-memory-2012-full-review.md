## Question: does an organism-produced trace have a *functional* ecological use?

The organism is the multinucleate single-cell plasmodium of *Physarum polycephalum*. As its search front migrates, it deposits an extracellular glycoprotein slime. Investigators tested whether slime-avoidance provides a spatial record of prior exploration and improves subsequent foraging around an obstacle. This is an organism-modified **external substrate**, **not a head-internal representation or trained neuronal map**.

### Methods, independent units and pre-installed conditions

**Y-choice assay:** pairs of identical oat-food agar arms. The surface in one arm is pre-coated with extracellular slime deposited by the same laboratory stock; the other is fresh agar. Which arm received slime was alternated. Control arms were **both plain agar** or **both coated**. As soon as a plasmodium touched food, its branch was scored as its selected arm; simultaneous food arrivals classed as splits and excluded.

**U-shaped trap assay:** twenty-four plasmodia per treatment, given a **120-hour** horizon. All received a U-shaped acetate barrier and an attractive glucose well placed outside the obstacle; both received the same obstacle, food and experimental temperature. On plain agar the plasmodium could distinguish its fresh deposited slime trail; on pre-slime-coated agar newly deposited slime did not provide informative contrast. The **already present background coating** is an externally imposed masking intervention, not a memory module added inside the living cell.

**No-trap chemotaxis control:** n=13 plain and n=16 pre-coated agar, with same food cue but without U barrier. This isolates whether slime coating alters *basic* nutrient-gradient sensing/movement rather than history-based avoidance in a complex topology.

**Important statistical unit:** each assayed plasmodium/maze is a physical test replicate, not an independently evolved genotype, biological species, or long-lived reproduced digital organism. The stock genotype(s) and prior laboratory propagation history were not individually lineage-identified in this paper.

### Why this is better evidence than 'it looks smart'

- **Causal intervention on environmental trace availability:** in a fixed visual geometry, adding background slime disrupts distinction between explored and unexplored paths and worsens navigation.
- **Held-out-style ecological problem:** the U barrier is a different problem from merely choosing between two agar arms, but **both conditions are designed and tested by the researchers**. This is not proof the organism learns an entirely new skill family or develops spontaneous, unrestricted goals.
- **Controls:** no difference in baseline speed; no-trap glucose sensing not impaired. The study does not identify every possible material effect of added slime (viscosity, moisture, chemical microgradients) and uses masks rather than selective molecular genetic knockout.
- **Censoring:** failures at 120 hours count as failures; elapsed times only among successes have survivor-selection bias if interpreted as general speed.

### What did not evolve or get reproduced here

- Trail **secretion and avoidance are existing biological traits**, not shown to originate spontaneously through this study's within-life learning or de novo evolutionary selection.
- A slime trace deposited **by a different plasmodium** can influence the tested one. Hence a response to chemical slime does **not** establish a unique, organism-specific autobiographical memory. The environment can serve as a distributed record without proving the individual distinguishes its own authorship.
- The goal was fixed experimenter-installed glucose, on apparatus assembled with an acetate barrier. There is no autonomous long-term niche construction, heritable novel daughter structure, change of physical laws, conscious navigation, moral conscience or subjective experience.
- The paper did not demonstrate inheritance of the deposited surface signal to daughters *as an independently reproducing collective*. Reusable environmental markings are not equivalent to reproductive lineage memory.

## Prospectively falsifiable transfer to EERC (original hypothesis, NOT executed)

**Candidate EERC-T question:** can an internally evolving process write marks into its own ecology and benefit from them on an unfamiliar obstruction **without the investigator installing an external visited-cell map or reward function**?

Suggested separately authorized comparison:
1. **Local environmental mark available** vs mark chemically masked vs **inert material** matched to viscosity/resource costs.
2. **False marks from unrelated process** vs truthful own marks; measure whether the reader can distinguish reliability/usefulness, not merely avoid one marker.
3. **Resource regeneration reversal:** the former 'visited' area now becomes good again; blindly avoiding any mark could **hurt survival**. Test adaptive read/write/ignore, not unconditional avoidance.
4. **Changed geometry and hidden goal positions**, matched chemotaxis cues; measure reaching novel nutrition under resource-budget and latency constraints.
5. **Mother/offspring transfer:** is history retained in producer physiology, the local environment, or daughter processes? Distinguish vertical state, inherited avoidance reflex, and external cue.
6. **Scoring**: independent organism lineages/seeded worlds, all timeouts/extinctions, confidence intervals, resource spending, false-positive false-trail controls; no retrospective novelty scorer.

**Unverified:** the benefits of evolved external memory over a fixed chemical-avoidance rule are *not* shown in the 2012 paper. This is a candidate test architecture, not evidence of a living conscious digital organism.
