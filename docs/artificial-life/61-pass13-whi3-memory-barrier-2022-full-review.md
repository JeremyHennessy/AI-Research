## Real biological mechanism

In budding yeast, prolonged exposure to a mating pheromone without successful mating allows a mother cell to escape pheromone-induced cycle arrest and resume budding. A super-assembly of RNA-binding **Whi3** releases translational repression on **Cln3** and contributes to a refractory *history-dependent* response. Unlike generic prions, Whi3 mnemon is normally **retained in the experienced mother**, associated with the **endoplasmic reticulum** membrane; daughter cells usually recover ability to react to mating pheromone.

The 2022 paper experimentally examined whether **ER lateral diffusion barriers** at the yeast bud neck confine Whi3 aggregates and behavioral response to experienced mothers, and whether loss of that barrier allows a switch from mother-retained to **mitotically transmissible** memory.

### Primary experimental interventions and controls

- Fluorescent **Whi3-3GFP** imaging in normal mothers versus three ER barrier-mutant backgrounds **bud6Δ, sur2Δ and bud1Δ**.
- Main directly measured bud occupancy: wild type **24.00 ± 6.22%** with Whi3 aggregates, versus **62.24 ± 3.85%** (bud6Δ), **64.01 ± 3.65%** (sur2Δ) and **65.26 ± 4.15%** (bud1Δ). Sample sizes WT **122** and mutant **148/180/165** cell observations. These are membrane-localization observations, *not* 122 independent organism origin experiments.
- Daughter pheromone refractory response is concentrated at the **first post-escape division**: authors describe **nearly 50%** first daughters unresponsive, falling to **14.2%, 13.3%, 6.9%, 4.4%** for daughters 2–5. Thus daughter transmission is **rare later**, **not zero**. Individual mother/daughter descendants and meiosis must not be conflated.
- Disrupting barriers increased daughter inheritance (e.g. sur2Δ first daughter 77.9% and second 40.4% refractory), while the mothers often retained their own resistant state.
- **Important causal challenge:** cells were exposed to pheromone **before daughter birth**, pheromone temporarily removed, daughters born without direct pheromone exposure, then pheromone reintroduced. Without prior daughter exposure, refractory daughters occurred in **11.1% ± 9.9% WT** versus **46.5 ± 13.7% sur2Δ, 63.3 ± 23.6% bud6Δ, 55.5 ± 2.3% bud1Δ**. That weakens the explanation that daughters individually underwent the same training.
- **Forced Whi3 partition**: artificial tethering of Whi3-GFP to spindle pole bodies **Spc42-GBP** raised daughter refractory inheritance to **70.3%**, versus **<35%** with either tag alone. This is a deliberate engineer perturbation, not spontaneous selection for memory transmission.
- **Whi3 glutamine-rich domain** deletions reduced inheritance, supporting the role of its assembly state; authors also compared a **different Sup35 prion**, whose localization and inheritance did not depend on the ER barrier in the same way.
- Authors observed 'constitutive escaper' clones after barrier disruption: persistently pheromone resistant even after streaking, with non-Mendelian inheritance in many isolates and signs of Whi3-prion-like propagation. Critically, **Whi3 cannot explain all CE clones**, and Hsp protein cures did not uniformly reverse them.

### Scientific interpretation and limits

**Stronger than correlation:** perturbing cellular membrane partition, Whi3 assembly domains, and forced daughter delivery changes inheritance of a physiological memory-like response. This is relevant causal evidence for the existence of **gating** between within-mother persistence and vertical transmission.

**What is not shown:** the cell **chooses** transmission based on future benefit; the physical gate is a known, genetically encoded yeast organelle architecture, **not evolved de novo in these experiments**. More memory transmission is not necessarily a fitness win. Indeed the authors discuss that passing mating-refractory state to the entire colony could reduce progeny mating opportunities.

**Important missing proof:** a comparison of long-horizon independent lineages' reproduction and viability under environments where either retaining naive daughters or transmitting the refractory state helps more. The paper uses microscope phenotypes/colony resistance, not head-to-head ecological adaptation experiments across new niches.

**Statistical caveats:** daughter cells from one mother do not provide fully independent mother histories; technical imaging fields, genotype strains and repeated cell observations are different units. Mutations bud6, bud1, sur2 have pleiotropic effects on architecture and physiology. Authors' other controls improve inference but do not prove Whi3 is the unique protein in every sustained clone.

### Distinguish inherited memory from hardcoded genetics

- A mother's learned physiological **Whi3 state** can persist while daughters reset most of the time.
- Removing a barrier or forcing molecular inheritance converts what was mostly **individual-retained information** into a **population-propagating state**.
- This does **not** require DNA mutations for each instance of state transfer, but genetically altered diffusion barriers in the experiment are experimenter-installed. The study is not about intelligence autonomously deciding when/what to forget.

## EERC-T consequence (unverified original inference)

The **selective inheritance problem** is a missing complement to the Pass 12 EERC-M learning hypothesis. The ability to copy every state is **not** equivalent to adaptive heredity. A future independently approved mechanism test needs:
1. **Retain-to-mother** vs **give-to-daughter** vs **random partition** vs **externally forced all-state duplication**, with the same resource, material, population and genetic backgrounds where possible.
2. An **ecological switch** where mother's prior adaptive state becomes disadvantageous for daughters, and the opposite condition. Measure offspring mating/viability costs, not just fluorescence.
3. Separate **intact transport mechanics** from **information specificity**, and use independent lineage units rather than individual daughter counts as independent genetic origins.
4. Long enough intergenerational passage to distinguish dilution/temporary maternal carryover from stable transmitted **functional** collective traits.
5. Verify whether selective gating **evolves endogenously** in candidate digital worlds rather than being prewired by an experimenter.
