# Pass 14 — Why the best memory strategy changes with ecology

**2026-10-08 | Selected primary-source E1 reading, NOT experimental reproduction or full-method E2 audit.** Counterexamples to the assumption that longer storage, more memory, or apparent prediction must always increase fitness.

## Engineered switching can outperform persistent memory

[Acar, Mettetal & van Oudenaarden, Nature Genetics 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.110) engineered yeast with random switching between two galactose-use phenotypes. **Fast switching** yielded higher population growth under frequent environmental changes, whereas **slow switching** performed better when favorable conditions persisted. Researchers *designed the genetic circuit and the alternation schedules*: the experiment does not show a yeast cell learning when to change its own switching rate during its lifetime. The paper's source abstract and experimental design are checked (E1), not its whole supplement.

## Memory age and cost can produce counterintuitive simulated outcomes

[Jain, Jolly & George, bioRxiv 2025 v1](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.24.655868) describes *two researcher-imposed phenomenological forgetting rules*: dated (erasing experiences in their age order) and undated (random loss). Their theory suggests that what helps after a single shift may differ from what helps during periodic or stochastic changes. Charging a cost for phenotypic changes can improve modeled benefit by suppressing unnecessary state switches under noisy conditions, but **worsen** adaptation to a sudden, lasting change. **Not peer reviewed, not a new wet-lab experiment, no evolved controller, no code executed here.** Complete appendix/parameter sweep not audited (E1).

## Separate recent evidence from ambitious wording

[Kratz, Wang, Si & Banerjee, PRX Life 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/5zbg-8vll) report single *E. coli* cells tracked in microfluidic nutrient fluctuations and model multi-timescale physiological adaptation from minutes to hours. The article's “emergent learning” label is an **interpretation**, not proof of task-general intelligence or a newly evolved neuronal mechanism. Full main methods, cells-per-group and fitting sensitivity have not been independently reviewed by AI-Research: **E1**.

[Vermeersch and colleagues, Frontiers Microbiology 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2022.1004488) review history-dependent bacterial and fungal physiological responses, including past carbon source, gene expression, epigenetic effects and phenotype heterogeneity. A literature **review** must not be counted as a fresh independent evolution experiment. E1 selected review passages.

[Miao and colleagues, bioRxiv 2026 v1](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.23.740246) report Stentor habituation-associated time-resolved transcriptomic changes in **341 genes**, including a cyclic-nucleotide signaling program and **five PKG paralogs**. **Not peer-reviewed**; pooled RNA association does not causally prove which molecule stores memory, or demonstrate an emerging digital mind. The researchers overlap other 2026 Stentor studies: do not count as new independent species validation. E1 abstract only.

## Memory traps to preserve

| Attractive inference | Alternative explanation | Independent future discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| “Frequent switching shows learned uncertainty” | Fast phenotype transitions **preinstalled** by experimenters | Blind changing frequencies with no engineered policy update |
| “Loss of memory improves response” | Cheaper phenotype switch or passive protein dilution | Same genotype and current cues; carrier-specific knockout/rescue |
| “Information about past means benefit” | Inert history labels and metabolic stores produce correlations | Held-out ecological viability under matched resource costs |
| “Adaptation is intelligence” | Pre-existing methylation **integral feedback** can robustly adapt | Compare fixed feedback, passive filter and endogenous causal rule formation |
| “Candidate genes cause learning” | Transcript changes reflect stress, division or response | Mechanistic intervention plus novel ecological test |
| “Longer memory always helps” | Permanent readout or expression wastes resources after cue ceases | Unexpected permanent regime switch, negative outcomes included |

**Research consequence:** EERC needs an *economy of retention* hypothesis, including environmental reversals and the possibility that forgetting is beneficial. Do not merge all studies into one numeric intelligence score. Neither life nor consciousness is established by their data, and no simulation is authorized in AI-Research.
