# Pass 16 — Beaumont et al. (2009): selectable access is not the same as phenotype access

**Reviewed 2026-10-08 | E2 full primary original Nature four-page text, Figures 1–4, methods summary and limitations read from authors' full-paper version, independently cross-checked to [official publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08504).** Original full-paper text was inspected through [author-public copy](https://www.academia.edu/144912566/Experimental_evolution_of_bet_hedging). Supplementary tables and original genotyping assay data not recalculated; paper under publisher copyright, linked and not mirrored.

**Primary:** Hubertus J. E. Beaumont, Jenna Gallie, Christian Kost, Gayle C. Ferguson & Paul B. Rainey. *Experimental evolution of bet hedging*. Nature **462**:90–93 (2009).

## What happened — and what was supplied by researchers

- The published experiment began with **12 independent bacterial selection lines** of *Pseudomonas fluorescens* SBW25. Experimenters defined alternating habitats **and required choosing a heritably novel visible colony morphology** at every transfer. Each round began from an individual of the selected novel colony, with a **single-colony bottleneck**. This externally installed novelty exclusion rule and bottleneck are not spontaneous ecology.
- **16 alternating-selection rounds** were studied, with **two of 12 original lines** producing stochastic between-colony state switching (1B4, 6B4). The switching lines lasted seven additional rounds before termination. Other ten originals did **not** evolve this strategy in the observation window. Matched stabilizing-colony controls did not show switching.
- One evolved switching lineage (1B4) differed by **nine sequenced mutations** from the ancestor. The final change in central metabolic gene **carB** was necessary for the rapid switching phenotype in this historical genotype: replacing evolved carB with ancestral allele removed switching and installing the evolved allele into the immediate ancestor recreated switching.
- **Crucial nuance:** the evolved carB substitution also produced a switching phenotype when placed in the **distant original ancestor**, but in that background its fitness effect was **deleterious** or not beneficial under the selection assay. The crucial *historical contingency* was therefore **selection accessibility**, not that the phenotype was absolutely physically impossible before earlier changes.
- The authors independently repeated **one round** of the experiment beginning from the potentiated immediate ancestor versus the original ancestor: **3/36** trials showed switching from immediate ancestor, **0/138** from original (Fisher p=0.0083). These are **replay trials** at an already known branch point, not 174 independently evolving full sixteen-round worlds.
- **Negative causal intervention:** when the experimenters repeated selection on 1B4 **without giving colonies created through its rapid switching the special transfer advantage**, non-switching emerging competitors drove 1B4 extinct. Thus its advantage was contingent on the experimental **exclusion/bottleneck mechanism**, not a general intrinsic rapid-growth superiority.
- Two single-cell bottlenecks and clonal colony assays ruled against one major alternative that colonies simply contained stable mixed genotypes. The observed capsule-positive/negative ratios were state-dependent, but the exact endogenous epigenetic molecular switch remained unresolved in 2009.

## Why this revises the earlier EERC idea

**Phenotypically reachable** is weaker than **selectable / fitness-accessible**. A mutation may produce a new dynamical state immediately, yet be weeded out because its earlier genetic background does not support the necessary viability. Earlier mutations can *reweight* an already possible behavior's fitness rather than add a novel primitive behavior to the genome. This is a causal path to historical innovation in ordinary biology, not evidence of invented physics.

The source also falsifies a naive 'more novelty = more life' principle: the **researcher explicitly imposed novelty selection** and a particularly helpful reproductive bottleneck. The authors remove that special advantage and reveal a collapse. Any digital EERC claim must tag the presence of a programmed novelty-reward or founder bottleneck as an *external cause*.

## Statistical and rights limits

- Evolutionary replicate unit: **12 source lines**, with two switching origins. Replay replicate unit: selected source genotype × one additional round, not a fresh complete evolutionary origin. Competitive fitness readout is measured in selected media and strain context.
- This paper and [Gallie 2015](75-pass15-gallie-2015-latent-bistability-and-evolution-full-review.md) analyze overlapping switch ancestry; **do not count independent origins twice**. The 2015 work added molecular mechanism tests.
- Reporter/colony switching does not mean within-individual learning, generalized ecological competence, independently reproducing higher-level individuals, or phenomenal consciousness.
- Full main source E2, no source-code execution or scientific replication in AI-Research. Research only, no wet-lab protocols were performed or provided as a recipe.

## Future-only neutral tests

1. For a candidate digital system, compare identical evolving lineages **with and without a designed novelty-exclusion/bottleneck**, tracking ancestral trait and derived variant separately; avoid privileging organisms by label.
2. Test the candidate genetic/program variant in **both original and immediately preceding backgrounds**, checking separately **trait expression** and **fitness effect**.
3. Pre-register genuine fully independent lineages and replay trials without pooling their denominators.
4. Include negative outcomes where a new phenotype appears but is unstable without assistance. Do not claim life or self-origin based on externally supported survival.
