## Original experiment and what 'novel' means

**Novel phenotype** was defined as a previously unobserved **viability in one of 52 screened environments in which BOTH ancestral tested strains were inviable**. This is *preexisting biochemical viability becoming accessible*, not spontaneous invention of reaction physics or an organism constructing a new nutrient.

Study designers compared two starting *E. coli* backgrounds with **~22-fold different mutation rate**; evolved populations in simpler versus more complex researcher-prepared selection environments and assayed responses to new conditions, with genome sequencing of selected survivors.

The 236 original phenotyping conditions were used to define which viable functions counted as new; this **preset assay taxonomy** is not a self-organism-discovered objective. There were **eight independent evolving populations per selected condition**, but authors subsequently chose **two representative clones from each condition** for in-depth phenotyping/genomics. Two clones are not eight independent lines or all possible survivors.

## Stronger and weaker author evidence

- In five simple environments, elevated-mutator lineages accumulated **3–12× more retained mutations** than lower-mutator lineages, yet did **not** evolve significantly more novel viability traits (Wilcoxon n=5+5, p=0.78 under per-condition comparison).
- More complex environments were associated with **more new viability phenotypes**: across eight selected treatment types, Spearman correlations R~0.87 (lower-mutator) and R~0.82 (higher-mutator), with p=0.004/0.012 respectively.
- **Critical confound:** the complex-environment evolution lasted **~600 generations versus ~100–200 generations** for simple conditions. Researchers calculated partial correlations adjusting generation count and compared subsets matched more closely by retained mutation number, but the most complex group included **only one five-factor environment**. Those controls reduce, not eliminate, inference uncertainty. Do not declare a universal law that selection-complexity always dominates all mutation rates.
- Genomic observations suggested **pleiotropic selection** of broad cellular regulatory and transport pathways, which can alter viability across many conditions not present during original selection. This is an **inferred network explanation**, not demonstration that each new phenotype has been traced to one reconstructed causal allele.
- Initial wild-type and mutator strains already had different **baseline viability profiles** before experimental evolution. Defining novelty only for environments where both ancestors failed is a conservative comparison but also an operational **selection of the novelty universe**.
- A source-generational temporal mismatch and **selected survivors** can bias counts of additional opportunities. Extinct lines and cells incapable of growing under the screening assay remain important biological counterevidence.

## Why this matters for EERC without turning it into a resistance project

The study suggests **structured selection in one environment can indirectly make future functions accessible via pleiotropic changes**. It does not show organisms inventing their own environment or genuinely new primitive chemistry.

Any future *digital nonbiological* test should predefine **resource provenance**, an equal-duration/compute-budget **complexity comparison**, an independent held-out ecological function assay, no imposed target reward, and causal reconstruction of the specific evolved state. The researcher must not modify the substrate's vocabulary or add new functions halfway through and call the new accessibility endogenous.

**Especially important:** More mutational supply can enlarge a library of inert variation while not improving what is **selectably reachable** under the experiment's time limit. Compare actual reachable functions and ancestral alternatives, not just mutation count.
