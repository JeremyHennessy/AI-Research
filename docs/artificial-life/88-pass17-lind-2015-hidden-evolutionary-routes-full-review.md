## Research question and experimental scaffolding

In *Pseudomonas fluorescens* SBW25, the frequently selected 'wrinkly spreader' (WS) colony phenotype is normally attained through activation of cyclic-di-GMP/cellulose production. Previous work found three recurring mutational routes. Are these the **only possible routes**, or simply the easiest mutational transitions?

The authors **engineered an ancestor lacking all three dominant routes**, while retaining the rest of its mature bacterial metabolism/physiology. Their experiment artificially fixed that genotype and selected for the familiar phenotype through researcher-created static liquid growth conditions. The ability to produce cellulose and grow as a bacterium **already existed**; this was not a new biological cell or an autonomous digital organism arising from a simple soup.

## Denominators and what succeeded or failed

- **200 independently initiated static microcosms** were founded with the common-route-disabled ancestor. After six days, the target WS phenotype was recovered from **91/200** microcosms. **109/200** did not produce that detected phenotype within the measurement window; include these negative outcomes.
- Within the 91 recovered variants, authors identified **86 variants with a single mutation** and **five with double/triple mutations**. Across those they characterized **ten single-step mutational paths and three more requiring multiple steps**. The 13 routes are pathway classes/loci, **not** 13 independently evolved species.
- In the original unmodified background, other historical studies generally obtained WS variants faster, and predominantly via the three familiar routes. Previous comparisons are partly historical rather than simultaneous matched entire new 200-population controls.
- The alternative variants usually had **relative fitness close to conventional WS types** in the chosen artificial air–liquid environment. The main difference in route prevalence was consistent with differences in **mutational accessibility**, not an already measured large fitness disadvantage.
- Authors investigated different mutation classes: pre-existing inhibition released, regulatory/promoter changes, gene fusion and more specific activating changes. They propose a qualitative likelihood ordering; it is **not a universal ranked law of evolution** and may depend on target size and network structure.
- Some mutations led to multiple-node effects and occasional complex paths; most mapped to existing c-di-GMP-producing machinery. Their use of sequencing, linkage and promoter/transcription results is causal evidence about mechanisms, **not** origin of new physics.

## Key inference and important null

**Observed:** suppressing easy routes **externally** can reveal alternate routes to a comparable existing outcome; many rare routes are rare despite having similar measurable fitness after they appear.

**Not observed:** a living system itself changes its ecology so the formerly rare routes become accessible, selectable, and independently passed to new *collective* offspring. A path removed by a technician in a designed experiment cannot be called endogenous habitat niche construction.

**Strong falsifier for careless generalization:** the finding does not imply that disabled genetic pathways are permanently blocked by environmental circumstances, nor that any arbitrary physical reaction space harbors abundant high-fitness hidden routes. This bacterial genome includes many pre-existing candidate diguanylate cyclases; a minimal digital chemistry may not.

## Distinguishing evolutionary novelty types for EERC

1. **Physical possibility**: substrate permits a reaction or function.
2. **Genetic route availability**: a given ancestor can acquire it by a specified mutational sequence.
3. **Arrival probability**: a route occurs within finite mutation-time and resource budget.
4. **Selective utility**: resulting phenotype raises measured lineage fitness when the ecology rewards it, with costs.
5. **Independent reproduction**: the new function is transmitted through daughter/granddaughter organizational units without an installed copy primitive.

The paper verifies 2–4 for this mature organism under designed ecology, **not all five for an artificial organism**.

## Next scientific tests (paper only, NOT authorized experiments)

Compare independently produced route-blocking **ecological changes** against experimenter deletions, preserving fixed primitive rules. Track complete denominators, genuine loss of dominant possibilities, how many alternate paths appear under matched mutation supply, and whether any resulting function carries net resource/offspring benefit in an unfamiliar ecology. Include an easy-route-restoration rescue and an inert obstacle control. Do not select the new outcome by a programmed novelty reward.
