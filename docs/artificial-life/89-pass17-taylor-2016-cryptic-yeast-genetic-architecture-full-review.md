# Pass 17 — Taylor et al. (2016): one phenotype, multiple genetically hidden routes

**2026-10-08 | E2 full primary main manuscript, original experimental methods and figures reviewed; no independent statistical or experimental reproduction.** Matthew B. Taylor, Joann Phan, Jonathan T. Lee, Madelyn McCadden and Ian M. Ehrenreich, [Diverse genetic architectures lead to the same cryptic phenotype in a yeast cross](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms11669), *Nature Communications* 7:11669 (2016), [complete original main paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms11669) (CC BY). Supporting files and BioProject PRJNA301897 were **not** downloaded/reanalyzed. No live-cell, genetic, digital-world or AI model experiment was performed by AI-Research.

## Question and causal source design

Yeast crosses derived from two already viable parental backgrounds ordinarily form smooth colonies at normal growth temperatures. Special combinations of existing, previously hidden variants and occasionally new mutations expose a *rough colony phenotype*. The investigators assembled 106 separate parent crosses, screened more than 100,000 haploid progeny and followed the unusual morphology through backcrosses and genetic mapping to identify its different causal genetic configurations.

These are crosses within mature yeast biological machinery, **not independent abiogenesis events** or a general search for new intelligence.

## Main author results and crucial denominator

- **17 biologically independent occurrences** of the rough-colony phenotype were obtained across the separate crosses, using at most one selected occurrence per cross. These are **not** 17 different environmental functions or new evolving species.
- **8/17** required the interaction of one *new* mutation and **one or more pre-existing cryptic variants**; six different mutated gene identities were identified.
- **9/17** emerged **without a new mutation**, due to combinations of existing cryptic variants and higher-order interactions. Some involved recombination inside a known regulatory region, an existing mechanism of genetic reshuffling.
- Mapping identified multiple segregating genomic loci per case; most effects arose through a broadly shared regulatory system. A functional background regulator was necessary in all mapped cases, and different genotype combinations could reach the **same** morphological endpoint.
- The genetic context **changed the causal effect of a mutation**, including how many hidden alleles had to coexist. A visually identical colony is therefore insufficient evidence that the **same causal path** or newly created operation occurred.
- Backcrossing, controls and targeted allele replacement strengthen causal attribution to specific genetic configurations, but some higher-order interactions remain complicated and can be sensitive to growth temperature.
- The authors' comparison of multiple temperatures and variants indicates that **environmental context** affects which hidden genotypes express rough morphology; environmental 'novelty' was not measured as a newly constructed resource or viable daughter collective.

## Misreadings to prevent

**Morphology versus ecological function:** a rough-looking colony is a deliberately selected reporter for epistasis, not independently shown to be a new useful function in an unfamiliar ecosystem.

**Cryptic reservoir versus spontaneous invention:** a variant can exist silently and become visible in a new background. Detecting this does not establish that the substrate has invented a new chemical law, metabolic cycle or intelligence.

**Genetic independence:** the 17 occurrences arose in separate crosses but share parental strains and much of the regulatory network. Backcross progeny, sequencing reads and the >100,000 screened clones are **not** independent evolutionary origins. Do not pool with Lind or Gallie as one common success rate.

**Historical source independence:** this 2016 yeast cross study is physically distinct from SBW25 Pseudomonas switching studies, but the mechanisms/outcomes are different. It cannot be used as a second direct replication of bacterial bet hedging.

## The negative side of the result

Only 17 unusual outcomes were selected out of a very large screened population; the denominator and threshold matter. Some genetic combinations remain invisible under typical conditions. Even successful phenotype expression may not provide any fitness or reproductive advantage when external conditions change.

When evaluating evolving virtual ecologies, pre-register what constitutes **function**, not merely a visually recognizable object/class; preserve negative genotype backgrounds, blocked networks and absent phenotypes.

## EERC prospective diagnostic, not an authorized implementation

Measure separately (i) inherited variant combinations, (ii) direct organization-state shifts, (iii) ecological performance after perturbation, (iv) the number of independently viable descendants and (v) which components were preinstalled by the simulator. In a future isolated, separately approved experiment, demand causal ablation/rescue of specific interactions and counterfactual backgrounds, not a novelty classifier trained on successful patterns.

**Conclusion:** The paper provides experimentally grounded evidence that evolutionary accessibility depends on high-order epistasis and context. It does **not** prove spontaneous origin of useful new ecological opportunity or digital consciousness. Source depth E2 (main text), no E3 reproduction.
