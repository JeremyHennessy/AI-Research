## Interpretation correction to EERC-C v7

The archived [v7 manifest](../../data/alife/combined-path-eerc-v7.json) includes an ecological-cause falsifier stating that externally matched material predicts the same outcomes as a producer. Taken alone, that is too strong. This paragraph supersedes that interpretation; the historical manifest is retained unchanged.

**Original causal reasoning:** if an inhabitant changes its environment by producing a material, replacing that material externally can restore an effect after the producer is removed. Such rescue can support material mediation. It does not, by itself, refute endogenous production in the untreated system. Conversely, rescue does not prove an independently reproducing collective.

Keep three questions separate:

| Question | Informative comparison | Limited conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Does the producer cause the environmental change? | Producer present, producer absent, and removal controls that preserve other relevant conditions | Tests environmental construction; removal may still have side effects |
| Is a specific ecological effect more than extra resources? | Candidate material replacement versus equivalent usable resources without the candidate interaction, including physical side effects | Distinguishes a particular relation from a general resource subsidy |
| Is the relation reconstructed by descendants? | Descendants after all continuing external replacement/re-pairing is withdrawn | Tests continued endogenous reconstruction; initial rescue alone cannot answer this |

Matching only total carbon, energy, or material is insufficient when timing, spatial distribution, accessibility, or other environmental properties differ. Name what is matched and what remains unmatched. A specific producer-mediated effect may still be ordinary niche construction rather than a new organizational individual.

## Observation is not arrival

An endpoint count of successful populations combines opportunity generation, establishment, subsequent competition, and detection. Therefore Lind's 91 positive endpoints out of 200 populations cannot alone identify the mutation-arrival rate. Maintain its 109 negative endpoints, but distinguish an absent mutation from an undetected or lost lineage. Future research should look for time-resolved ancestry and reconstructed backgrounds before attributing a route-frequency change solely to accessibility.
