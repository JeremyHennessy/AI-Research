## What the comparison identifies

The authors start with a selected consortium from earlier coevolution of two complementary E. coli auxotrophs. This is adaptation of an already constructed and selected relationship, not a new spontaneous origin of mutualism. The main evolution contrast compares dependent cocultures without supplied amino acids against supplemented monocultures. Dependence, community context and supplementation therefore change together. It is not a fully crossed isolation of one causal variable. Unsupplemented monocultures cannot sustain the intended comparison, and supplemented cocultures were excluded from the main evolution experiment because they introduce competition and residual interaction.

The figure-level total of **80 samples per treatment/strain combination** comes from **16 established replicate cultures each split into five technical replicates** at the second transfer. Those descendants can subsequently diverge, but they share pre-treatment history. Eighty samples are not eighty independent founder histories. Endpoint clones and subsequent growth assays add another nested level.

## Author-reported findings and contrary cases

Dependent cultures generally show reduced adaptation, collapse, or escape from dependence under the imposed environmental challenges, with treatment- and strain-specific exceptions. Later assays compare coevolved partner combinations with combinations of independently evolved strains. The coevolved combinations perform better when dependence is required, while supplementation reverses the ranking. The result concerns interaction history under particular conditions, rather than a universal benefit or cost of cooperation.

Crucially, the authors explicitly conclude that a simple **weakest-link explanation is unlikely to be the main cause** of their evolutionary patterns. An apparent weakest-partner outcome in one condition does not establish that mechanism across all treatments. Our earlier general motivation must not substitute for their reported negative result.

Phenotypic reversion toward autonomy is asymmetric. No revertants were detected among **540 screened colonies from 60 populations** of one auxotroph; the other generated revertants. Colonies are nested samples, and non-detection is not proof that a lineage or possible genotype is absent. The genetic mechanism of reversion remains unresolved in this paper. Suggested alternative metabolic routes or transfer mechanisms are hypotheses, not reconstructed causes.

## Causal limits

The authors argue against reduced growth/mutation supply as the principal explanation, but this is not a complete genotype-controlled equalization of mutation production, establishment and detection. Survival and endpoint adaptation do not measure mutation arrival alone. The earlier selected consortium limits generalization across arbitrary partner identities. Nutrient supplementation diagnoses conditional dependence but does not test whether a daughter collective independently rebuilds the exchange.

## Consequence for the research direction

Dependence can preserve complementary function under one environment while restricting adaptation under another. Record escape to autonomy as individual adaptation and loss of the original relationship; neither outcome proves collective reproduction. For a future AL11 interpretation, distinguish historical partner compatibility, ecological mediation and inheritance of organization. This review authorizes no biological or digital experiment.
