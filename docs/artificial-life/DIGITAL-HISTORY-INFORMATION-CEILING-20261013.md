## Exact local binary target rule

The **16 newly available home-position packets**, indexed `i=16..31`, become accessible at **global tick 64**. Their target port is

`T(seed,i) = (seed % 2) XOR L(seed//2) XOR E(seed//2,i)`,

where `L` is the parity of the installed `latent15` hash and `E` is one **only if** the first eight hex digits of the installed `noise15` hash yield residue zero modulo 16. `E` is a **deterministic per-packet bit**, not a measured IID Bernoulli event; the modulus threshold does not establish actual exception frequency in held-out contexts.

At site 0, `ACT` selects the lowest accessible remaining packet. For a **nonempty real attempt**, success means `attempted_port == T`, sets personal feedback to 1, removes the packet and yields at most 20 energy before cap loss. Failure sets personal feedback to 0 and **does not remove the packet**. A same-actor immediate retry at the opposite port thus succeeds on that same packet **if it is still present, the actor survives, and no other actor/transition changes the opportunity**. No target expires merely because it was attempted. Every ACT, FEEDBACK, READ, WRITE, branch and control instruction pays its ordinary base instruction charge; program formation and cap losses remain additional costs.

## New deduction 1: the published no-exception pair cannot identify older-history value

**When all 16 released home packets have `E=0`, their target is one constant bit `B=(seed%2) XOR L`.** Suppose the actor has just performed a personally attributable **nonempty** ACT at port `p` with truthful latest feedback `f`. The constant target is already determined:

`B = p XOR (1-f)`.

Therefore, for *predicting the correct port of the next released home packet*, a properly implemented comparator retaining the **latest real attempt's port and feedback** has the complete relevant target information. Older packet observations cannot further distinguish the correct target in this special constant-target case. This is a conditional *information sufficiency* result, **not** a tested paid comparator, zero-memory-cost claim, guarantee of survival or claim that an authored policy achieves the inference for free.

The original Exposure23 ten-history software pair recorded **zero per-packet fresh exceptions**. It demonstrated supplied fixed exploration and actual feedback access, but could not demonstrate an *information advantage specifically requiring older postchange packet history* over a capable current-feedback/current-port reference. Its reactive witness also failed one frozen pre-boundary criterion; retain that refusal exactly. A difference in implementation cost, control flow or death timing between authored programs must not be misrepresented as extra information learned from older observations.

**Falsifier of this conditional claim:** an independently audited situation with all fresh `E=0`, identical real current attempted port `p` and feedback `f`, identical available packet opportunities, but **different true next-packet targets**. Under the present site-0 static target law that should be impossible; corrupted provenance, a nonfresh/remote packet, empty ACT, hidden extra actor or changed resource timing would invalidate the premises instead.

## New deduction 2: limited first-guess opportunity even when exceptions exist

Consider an ideal *information-only* two-port reactive reference that, after any real wrong first try, switches to the opposite port on the **same still-present** packet. Subject to complete opportunity, survival and no intervening consumption, it incurs at most **one failed ACT per packet**. If the first usable personal observation of a fresh released packet comes from the `j`-th released packet actually acquired (`j>=1`), earlier-history information can potentially prevent at most one *first-guess failed ACT* on each of the remaining `16-j` fresh packets:

`avoided_first_guess_failed_ACT <= 16-j <= 15`.

The `15` is an **upper bound on this particular class of later first-guess ACT opportunities**, not on net instruction savings, food gained, energy, lifetime, descendant effects or all available sites. A single ACT saved can change survival and later opportunity; feedback/branch/port-change control overhead also matters. The ideal two-port reference must be replaced by a **fully priced implementable actual-channel comparator** before treating any calculated advantage as experimental evidence. No promise of 15 realized savings or exception exposure follows from the source.

This bound shows why the **whole paid information-processing bill** is decision-critical. The literal `ACT -> FEEDBACK -> WRITE -> intervening ACT -> READ -> later ACT` route alone costs at least six executed instructions, including three explicit information operations, *before* necessary branches, program formation, cold exploration and unused/failed actions. Those are route-specific minima, **not universal ISA lower bounds or charges automatically additional to every comparator**. The closed Capacity60 apparatus already showed how genuine ACT savings can be canceled by feedback/control overhead; do not use its observed costs as a new target threshold.
