## High-information result

Unlike a process in which a lipid membrane is already inherited, the researchers showed a route to lipid **material and bilayer formation** from soluble acetate and cysteine precursors using supplied soluble catalytic enzymes and cofactors. Reported membrane images and lipid assays support formation without inherited membrane lipid template. Original main findings include:

- A combined ACS/ACC/FAS soluble enzyme cascade yielded **42% palmitoyl-CoA at four hours** under its relevant reaction conditions; loss of acetate or a required enzyme was a negative condition.
- Coupled reaction steps produced **43% lipid-product yield after 12 hours** in the in-situ condition and formed bilayers observed by cryo-EM. A separate **80% yield after eight hours** used supplied palmitoyl-CoA and is **not** a competing estimate from the same cascade.
- After washed vesicles received **new, externally replenished isotope-labelled precursor/cofactor stock**, lipid turnover showed **67 mol% new isotope-labelled lipid** in melittin-pore conditions versus **17 mol% without melittin**. The two arms have different permeability, not a controlled comparison of independently self-manufactured pores.
- Excessive melittin (25 mol% condition) damaged membranes. Positive transport and negative membrane-integrity results must both be recorded.
- Technical sample replicates do not constitute independent newly bootstrapped protocell ancestors or independent verification by a second laboratory.

These positive observations support a distinct *boundary-formation* function. They do **not** demonstrate whole-protocell division into living descendants, self-renewing enzymes, synthesis of supplied pore-forming peptides, cost-positive steady-state metabolism or continuous unassisted multi-generation repair.

| Vesicle boundary lipid | Constructed from supplied precursors | Credible partial component formation |

The strongest rival explanation is a supplied-enzyme, fed chemical cascade that makes membranes; it does not yet pay to manufacture and replace the catalysts doing the work. An attainable simpler comparator could make lipids directly from prepared precursors with lower total cost. Do not assign zero cost to missing thermodynamic or labor inputs.

## Decisions the source changes

**Retain:** de novo compartment-boundary production from soluble precursors is a physically demonstrated partial path that does not require a previously inherited membrane scaffold. This expands the list of candidate routes for one apparatus role.

**Reject:** claims that those experiments by themselves demonstrate complete autonomous chemical cell origins, self-construction of enzymes, profitable ongoing apparatus renewal, or offspring who inherit new repair capacity without specialist human inputs.

**Required comparison for future evidence:** isolate independent founder cohorts, account for failed and destroyed compartments, trace original versus renewed catalytic parts, meter every consumable and energy input and waste output, and compare output to the least complex viable positive-surplus control.

## Six falsifiable future-only hypotheses

- **MF-H1, catalyst provenance:** observed membrane renewal depends on supplied intact soluble catalyst. *Falsifier:* chemistry independently makes functional replacement enzymes from finite traced feed.
- **MF-H2, feed withdrawal:** withdrawal of scheduled new ATP/cofactor/precursor feeds halts isotopically traced new lipid production before multi-generation renewal. *Falsifier:* extended material-balanced ongoing construction from permissible finite non-specialized feeds.
- **MF-H3, pore trade-off:** extra melittin improves precursor transport but sufficiently high pore abundance causes leakage or rupture. *Falsifier:* a fully quantified beneficial pore level maintains integrity over failure-inclusive repair cycles.
- **MF-H4, negative founders:** conditioning only on stable recovered vesicles overstates whole-foundation success. *Falsifier:* the same positive performance across all founders including destroyed, inert and lost compartments.
- **MF-H5, competitive surplus:** a simpler attainable positive-cost lipid assembly route equals or exceeds useful lipid output per fully paid cost. *Falsifier:* preregistered complete-cost comparison showing robust superior net value.
- **MF-H6, multirole renewal:** renewal of membrane material alone does not replace every apparatus role, especially the constructor. *Falsifier:* repeatedly observed independent descendants fabricating and repairing all roles with sustained positive surplus after specialized intervention withdrawal.

**Verdict:** useful positive physical formation for **one** role, but not a proof of general self-maintenance or closure.
