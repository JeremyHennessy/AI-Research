A subsequent source-only review identified a cheaper missing reference: because the payload is exactly two bits, two paid reads, XOR, ACT and a loop jump require five instructions, without a generic sentinel-reading loop. The proposed learner needs at least seven instructions per ordinary cached decision. Under equal completed valid-packet opportunities and the stated formation costs, its nominal advantage over that reference is strictly negative. Independent review confirmed this missing reference and closed the current cost justification before source or a cohort. It is not a universal result about differently timed worlds.

The next learning decision is whether a distinct useful question remains after this efficient-reference screen. Any future design also needs explicit payload, cursor, material and lease rules; efficient cue-specific and partial-inspection controls; and a jointly realizable route that survives every paid prefix. A bound on the number of mistakes is insufficient: sixteen fully paid mistakes would cost more than the proposed actor's starting energy without intervening rewards. Nominal return, captured energy, formation costs, deaths and missing opportunities remain separate.
