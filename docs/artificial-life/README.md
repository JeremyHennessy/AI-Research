# Artificial Life, Emergent Intelligence, and Digital Organisms

**New research initiative — 2026-10-08 | Research only.** No organism, agent, simulator, training run or deployment is authorized by this initiative. Do not change Ora, AgentTest, or any other repository.

## Central question
**Can computational processes develop and sustain organizational properties associated with living systems, rather than merely producing lifelike-looking behavior or serving human-defined tasks?**

We are *not* presupposing a Transformer, LLM, biological phenotype, human-like personality or human-assigned mission. Intelligence could be an eventual, contingent outcome rather than the first optimization target.

## Initial evidence position (not a verdict on whether digital life is possible)

| Already demonstrated in research settings | Under active investigation | Not established by this project |
|---|---|---|
| Digital programs can copy, mutate and evolve in Avida/Tierra | Self-maintaining internal organization under perturbations without designer-provided survival shortcuts | That any released simulation is indisputably *alive* |
| Continuous cellular systems exhibit persistent, adaptive-looking patterns | Intrinsic inheritance/evolution and ecological interaction in common worlds | That visual complexity is self-sustained evolution |
| Some artificial chemistries exhibit collective self-replication in models | Coupling boundary repair, metabolism, heredity, and genuine intergenerational evolution | That any digital pattern has metabolism in the biochemical sense |
| Selection can evolve nontrivial computational features | Sustained open-ended adaptation with major transitions in individuality | Infinite novelty, consciousness or emergent general intelligence |

Representative sources: [Avida](https://doi.org/10.1162/106454604773563612), [Lenski et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01568), [Flow-Lenia 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08569), [Taylor's OEE requirements](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07403), [2026 Petri Dish NCA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11248).

## Working vocabulary
- **Life-like**: observed properties analogous to biology. This is a *descriptive* label, not an ontological verdict.
- **Constitutive autonomy**: organizational self-production and self-maintenance through the entity's own interactions; a theoretical term that requires operational translation before measurement.
- **Individual**: causally cohesive, sufficiently persistent process/boundary distinguishable from environment; often nontrivial to identify.
- **Heritable variation**: reproducible lineage differences affecting descendants; not just variation in image appearance.
- **Adaptive novelty**: previously absent, independently measured *functional* capability surviving selection and transfer; not only a new color/shape.
- **Open-ended evolution**: multiple competing definitions exist; use precise *behavioral hallmarks* and long-horizon tests rather than an undefined success badge.
- **Agency**: goal-directed-like regulatory behavior with counterfactual sensitivity to viability conditions; not necessarily intention or subjective experience.
- **Intrinsic selection**: differential persistence/reproduction arising from interactions and constraints of a specified environment; **the environment itself still has designed assumptions**.

## Constraints
1. **Research only**. No new world, digital creature, model weights, simulator, multi-agent deployment, production changes, or Ora/AgentTest modifications.
2. Separate **primary report**, **independent reproduction**, **engineering inference**, **novel hypothesis**, and **philosophical stance**.
3. Human-assigned fitness, update rules, resource accounting, mutations, observation interfaces, and chosen novelty metrics are all design decisions. Write them down and test sensitivity.
4. Do not call a system alive, conscious, autonomous, or generally intelligent because a video appears purposeful.
5. Prioritize falsifying experiments with frozen seeds/controls before larger worlds or beautiful graphics.
6. Preserve the existing research baseline and its evidence levels. Do not equate a metadata search with a full systematic literature review.
