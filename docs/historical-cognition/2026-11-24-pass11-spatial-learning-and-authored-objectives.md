## Strongest result: Darwin X, a positive comparator for acquired representations

Krichmar, Nitz, Gally and Edelman (2005), [Characterizing functional hippocampal pathways in a brain-based device as it solves a spatial memory task](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC548312/), DOI 10.1073/pnas.0409792102. Online January 31; issue February 8. Selected methods, results, discussion and acknowledgments inspected in accessible PMC HTML. The earlier [2002 conditioning paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12122030/) is the same research lineage, not independent replication.

The authors describe nine independently initialized simulated nervous systems operating the same physical device, each with sixteen trials grouped into four blocks. Search times in blocks 3 and 4 were shorter than block 1 under their reported Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p < .01). The device learned spatially selective internal responses while interacting with its environment. The study's 90,000 neuronal units are model populations, not an equivalent count of individual biological neurons. The total nominal trial count is 144, not 144 independent subjects; the same device was used throughout. Raw timing data, seed-level outcomes, full supplement and implementation have not been checked. E1 selected methods evidence, not a full E2 review.

The authors explicitly identify designer-provided behaviors, network organization, reward mechanisms and a sensor detecting the target. Consequently, the paper is valuable **positive** evidence for acquisition of task-relevant internal representations but not for independent acquisition of the objective itself. The authors describe different functional pathways converging on similar behavior; the observational backtracing analysis should not be mistaken for a randomized causal intervention isolating each pathway. This illustrates why a similar behavioral output is insufficient to establish an identical internal mechanism.

The acknowledgments name the Neurosciences Research Foundation, W. M. Keck Foundation, DARPA and ONR. No exact award ID is given in the inspected acknowledgments. Do not infer one from a similar research topic or funding period.
