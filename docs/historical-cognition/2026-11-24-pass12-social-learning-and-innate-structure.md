### CHARISMA and the CIVS project: direct historical prior art for social artificial life

The [official IJCNN 2011 program](https://neural.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/2011%20ijcnn%20program%20book.pdf) contains author abstracts by Matthew Conforth and Yan Meng of Stevens Institute of Technology. Paper 399 was scheduled August 1 (printed p.47); paper 397 August 3 (printed p.127). Their CIVS project (Civilization-Inspired Vying Societies) proposed an artificial-life approach to socially situated agents with autonomous development. CHARISMA was the proposed cognitive architecture, with dynamic knowledge representations and designed motivational machinery. The companion SHYNE abstract reports experiments evaluating a self-reorganizing representation. It does not supply enough methods or quantitative results to establish the scope of the component achievement, much less the success of the broader multi-agent project. Program access is not equivalent to full proceedings access. IEEE redistribution restrictions are noted; only metadata and original analysis retained. DBLP confirms the two proceedings entries and pagination. These are related outputs, not independent validation.

For Ora, the ambitions of bottom-up artificial life, social learning and reorganizing knowledge have explicit published precedent at least as early as 2011. No conclusion about equivalent mechanisms or novelty can be drawn without the full methods and a precise comparison to existing approved evidence. No proposal to implement these mechanisms is made here.

### CHREST: shared learning architecture versus transfer of acquired knowledge

[Gobet 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022022109335186) reports training the CHREST architecture on expert Awalé games, then evaluating recall and play. The model shows approximate agreement with empirical memory findings and imperfect game play. Prior chess applications use the same architecture. That is evidence for reuse of a designed learning mechanism, not proof that one trained agent carries its acquired chess knowledge into Awalé or develops a new representation independently. [Waters and Gobet 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18491491/) reports a comparison to 36 chess players with an explicit serial imagery assumption and poorer recall for off-square positions. The reviewed abstracts do not rule out alternative models.

### Cognitive Symmetry Engine: explicit accounting for innate structure

[Henderson, Joshi and Wang, September 10, 2013, UUCS-13-004](https://www.cs.utah.edu/docs/techreports/2013/pdf/UUCS-13-004.pdf), section 1, states a Domain Theory Hypothesis in which mathematical symmetry theories are provided in advance. Experience informs particular representations built within this formalism. Section 1.1 describes a current architecture, whereas the text identifies integrated affordance learning and lifelong representation as research goals. An author-hosted [2012 precursor](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~tch/publications/pub267.pdf) identifies UUCS-12-005 on its title page; the author's publication list calls it 12-012. Preserve this discrepancy, and do not silently create a second publication.

The 2013 report section 6, printed p.45, explicitly acknowledges NSF grant 1021038. This is a new text-extracted funding/output pointer within the scoped screen. Visual verification failed on the author-hosted copy and the official NSF award-detail page was inaccessible. Award recipient, award dates, amount and final project outcomes therefore remain unknown. The report's author affiliation is not itself evidence of the legal grant recipient.

The conceptual implication is two-sided: a supplied learning formalism does not eliminate the possibility of genuinely acquired knowledge; newly acquired contents do not demonstrate that the system invented the formalism itself. Distinguish acquisition of instances, relationships, representations and mechanisms. No general cognitive outcome is established by these technical reports.
