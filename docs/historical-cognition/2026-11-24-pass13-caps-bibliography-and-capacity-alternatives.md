## What this literature actually establishes

- [1982 READER](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0602_2): author-described word-by-word simulation with parallel production processes designed under constraints from observed human gaze patterns. It is prior art for cognitive modeling without language-model pretraining, not a demonstration that the system learned to read from scratch.
- [1990 Raven model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2381998/): two simulations reportedly approximate distinct groups of college students on a selected nonverbal reasoning test. Model performance on an intelligence test is not sufficient evidence of autonomous acquisition of reasoning machinery or broad transfer.
- [2001 dual-task study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11467915/): authors report joint cortical activation below the sum of separate task activations, despite largely different task-related regions. This supports interaction/constraint among systems under the measured conditions, not identification of a literal conserved resource or a metabolic mechanism.
- [2003 syntactic ambiguity study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14622064/): authors allow more than one computational explanation of the imaging results. Agreement of a model with human measurements does not identify that model uniquely.
- [2006 Tower of Hanoi dissertation](https://ir.vanderbilt.edu/items/85e4e71e-0de7-4768-876a-426eefaebc0f): thesis abstract describes authored planning model and human comparison; identifies learning among subsequent research directions. Do not infer that learning was demonstrated.

### A substantive alternative, not merely a historical citation

[Just and Carpenter (1992)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1546114/) implement a finite activation capacity in a production architecture. [MacDonald and Christiansen (2002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11863041/) argue instead that apparent capacity can arise from architectural and experience-dependent differences without an independently variable capacity primitive. [Just and Varma (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.109.1.55) explicitly defend their hybrid architecture against that account. The opposing commentary is a supplementary context pointer, not another upstream row or an independent reproduction; full methods not audited. Together with the previously recorded 1996 critique, this establishes a live historical alternative to inferring a single resource mechanism from observed performance.

Ora relevance: accounting for an actual substrate constraint is distinct from demonstrating that the constraint explains a cognitive phenomenon. A budgeted system may behave differently because of scheduling, representation, prior experience, or ordinary resource exhaustion. Observed capacity-like behavior alone cannot establish the underlying mechanism. This is a conceptual prior-art constraint, not a proposed implementation, executable test or change to Ora's approved qualification.
