## MDB: a high-priority comparator for Ora

The 2010 paper (IEEE TAMD 2(4), pp.340 onward, published online October 14) reports physical robot experiments using artificial evolution to fit predictive models and select actions. Sections III–IV distinguish world, internal and satisfaction models. In the first hexapod experiment, the authors report reaching a target consistently by 300 iterations. Human resets followed falls or loss of the target; the experiment omitted internal sensors and long-term memory. Its objective used distance to the block. In a separate teacher-guided example, the authors report two robot platforms learning command mappings in about 20 iterations (roughly ten minutes), then using acquired models without the teacher; the teacher had supplied the reward signal. Figure 13 describes reusing saved models when an earlier command mapping returned. Selected indexed text only, not a full-method audit or prospective test; PDF image retrieval failed (HTTP 404). The 2004 full-text route separately returned 403; publisher abstract accessible.

The central distinction is the unit of selection. MDB evolves internal candidate models and behaviors during a robot's lifetime, using designed evaluation criteria. This is meaningful precedent for evolution-based acquisition of useful internal structure. It is not evidence that physically or metabolically bounded organisms reproduce, inherit cognitive machinery or generate their own underlying selection criterion. Conversely, merely adding reproduction to a system does not establish novel cognition. A later research-only crosswalk should compare units of selection, source of variation, objective provenance, cost accounting, memory retention, transfer and inherited effects separately.
