## 1. APEX: procedural control under resource constraints

[Freed and Remington, 1997](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1997/48) report a model of a human air-traffic controller for evaluating interfaces, with reusable plans and resource-aware scheduling. The [1998 NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20020065540) identifies NASA Ames as source and RTOP 548-40-12 as project funding; the government catalog permits public use of that particular work. The [April 2005 reference manual](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20060016440/downloads/20060016440.pdf) documents a software system with predefined procedures and an event-driven execution mechanism; cover checked visually. This is evidence of a documented implementation and author-reported simulation use, not general understanding or a demonstrated capacity to acquire its own procedures. Separate adaptive execution from learning the procedure itself. No claim of independently verified field deployment.

## 2. ARCADIA: selective perception with authored attention rules

The 2015 [object-perception model](https://repositories.cdlib.org/uc/item/0sx081tw) and 2016 [inattentional blindness study](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7v66v149) model selective information processing. In [Briggs et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.13337), the US Naval Research Laboratory researchers report two simulations of quantity perception. Section 4 explicitly describes a rule-triggered switch from prioritizing individual objects to groups, driven by short-term memory contents. Their timing correspondence is approximate, the quantity estimator is a simplified model and they do not claim neural implementation. No exact award number established. Dynamic selection of relevant observations can occur without acquiring a new attentional policy. For Ora, a changed response can reflect selection of different inputs rather than newly learned competence.

## 3. ARDIS: knowledge reuse in a specialized application

[Martín et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0394.2011.00596.x) describe an implemented knowledge-based configuration system for inspecting stainless steel and wood surfaces. The publisher abstract identifies a distinct mode for injecting knowledge. This provides an application example, not a general learning result. The source does not establish how much new knowledge was independently acquired, and quantitative comparisons remain unreviewed. Reconfiguration, knowledge reuse and knowledge creation must not be conflated.

The title page confirms that ARS was renamed SiMA. [Wendt et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAAI.2015.7407100), author-posted manuscript excerpts, describe a simulated agent choosing actions in response to hunger and retrieving a stored episode for navigating an obstacle. The consulted account does not establish that the episode was independently learned. A designed hunger representation and rules connecting it to choices can generate state-dependent subgoals, without demonstrating acquisition of a new underlying motivation, biological self-maintenance or subjective experience.

## 5. ASMO: minimal learning and the limits of self-modification claims

[Novianto, Johnston and Williams, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-02675-6_25) report a companion-robot demonstration of habituation and sensitisation mechanisms. The [2014 UTS dissertation record](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/34414) also reports robot soccer and companion-robot evaluations. The author describes a repertoire of independently programmed processes competing for attention, with learning changing their influence. The thesis is openly accessible but the institutional rights notice reserves reproduction and reuse rights to the author. Only abstract and metadata reviewed; no claim that the system generates new action mechanisms or rewrites its own architecture.

This is directly relevant prior art for minimal learning claims. Reduced response alone is not sufficient to distinguish experience-dependent learning from fatigue, resource depletion or fixed response dynamics. The full paper/thesis, controls and duration of retained effects remain unreviewed. These are interpretive constraints, not authority to design or execute an Ora experiment.

## Scientific synthesis

These five systems help separate four possible explanations of adaptive-looking behavior: changes in selected sensory information, execution of alternative existing procedures, changes in internal motivational state, and experience-dependent changes in response. They can coexist. None automatically establishes the others or proves general cognition. Do not interpret architecture labels such as self-modifying, generative or intelligent as experimental outcomes. The examples constrain interpretation of any later approved Ora evidence but establish no new capability or novelty.
