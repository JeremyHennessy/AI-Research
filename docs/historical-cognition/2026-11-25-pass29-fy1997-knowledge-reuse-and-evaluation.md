## Ora crosswalk: reuse is not acquisition

A system may increase measured performance because engineers repair it between repeated tests, because it reuses knowledge supplied at initialization, or because it acquires new information internally. These are different causal claims. HPKB's evaluation deliberately measures some engineer-mediated modification; repeated test success therefore cannot establish independent learning on unseen tasks. Counting reused vocabulary also does not establish reduced computation, newly acquired meanings, or useful inherited competence. The relevant interpretive boundary for Ora is the provenance of each information-bearing state, the distinction between retention and genuinely new acquisition, and the distinction between measured behavioral improvement and attribution to its internal cause.
