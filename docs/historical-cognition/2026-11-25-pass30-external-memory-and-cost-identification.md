## Method and negative evidence

The within-person experiment crosses two task difficulties with presence of an experimenter-provided carry indicator. Alternating subtraction and text entry creates competing intermediate-state demands; support order counterbalanced. The cue is correct and externally maintained. Participants did not invent, write, maintain or transmit it. The ACT-R model has a supplied one-chunk state resource and fixed restoration cost; its support-use strategy is explicitly implemented, not learned in this paper. Parameters fitted to the unsupported condition are held fixed for the supported condition, but the conditional strategy is added by the investigators. This is a useful condition transfer, not independent replication or autonomous discovery of memory machinery.

In §3.2, the response-time interaction persists despite the cue. Neither task has a significant three-way accuracy interaction; ceiling effects restrict interpretation. In §4, the model fails to predict the observed reduction of the text-entry interaction under support, and the authors deliberately decline a post-hoc adjustment. Eye-tracking results were uninformative and not reported (§2.1); actual cue-reading episodes are therefore not independently identified from eye data. A conditional cue-use explanation fits important observations but is not uniquely established.

## Independent checks (arithmetic/logic, not E3)

1. Sample accounting: 33 recruited minus four low-accuracy, two instruction and three recording exclusions gives 24 retained (27.27% excluded). Nominal 24 x 56 x 20 = 26,880 response opportunities before analytic removal; not independent participants or final sample size. First responses and RT outliers were removed; individual raw records unavailable. Selection may limit generalization.
2. From visually checked Tables 1–2, subtraction interaction drops from 482.90 to 248.15 ms, a 48.61% reduction; text-entry from 282.40 to 160.57 ms, a 43.14% reduction. These are reductions of estimated difficulty-interaction terms, not overall speedups, and neither residual interaction is zero.
3. From the joint model (Table 3), supported interaction is 482.65 - 233.33 = 249.32 ms (subtraction) and 278.26 - 116.69 = 161.57 ms (text entry). The slight differences from separate fits are not arithmetic contradictions. The three-way interaction p-values are .001 and .002 respectively; these do not identify particular individual cue-reading actions.
4. The printed Table 2 text-entry accuracy row for hard subtraction pairs z=.02 with p=.001, which is inconsistent with a conventional two-sided normal Wald calculation (approximately .984). No correction inferred; different testing procedure or printing error unresolved. Do not use this row as affirmative evidence. The key three-way accuracy conclusion in §3.2 remains as reported, not reestimated.
5. An aggregate latency improvement does not uniquely identify a cue policy. In a simplified hypothetical mixture, mean saving D = q*s, where q is probability of cue use and s is conditional saving; for any s >= D there is q = D/s in [0,1]. For example D=100 arbitrary time units can arise from q=1,s=100 or q=0.5,s=200. This is an identifiability counterexample, not a fit to the experiment. The original factorial contrasts are not direct estimates of q or s.
