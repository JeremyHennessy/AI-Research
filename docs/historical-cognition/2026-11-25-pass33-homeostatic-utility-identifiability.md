## Method and independent paper-level checks
The simulator joins authored HumMod physiology to ACT-R rules. The experiment supplies a numerical offer rather than a physical object; an authored fairness comparison and accept/reject productions are modulated by an assigned need-to-utility association. The paper reports 1,000 simulation runs, not 1,000 independent human measurements. Original human study had 21 completers, 10 in the severe-thirst group and 11 in the comparison group, one choice per person. The 50% comparator is specifically 5/10 in the severe-thirst group, not the pooled 8/21. The original scale is 0–10, whereas the model paper's task description says 1–10 and its figure includes zero. No raw data, seeds, executable model or independent reproduction obtained.

1. Table 2 mixture check: using rounded simulation proportions and conditional means, 0.79*7.94 + 0.21*4.82 = 7.2848, consistent with the printed overall thirst mean 7.29. The two conditional means are nevertheless below the human counterparts by 0.96 and 0.78 points. The pooled mean is not independent evidence of conditional calibration.
2. With rounded within-group SDs 1.2, the mixture SD is sqrt(1.2^2 + 0.79*0.21*(7.94-4.82)^2) = 1.7478, approximately compatible with the published 1.71 given rounding. Human conditional means with equal weights give 7.25, compatible with rounded 7.30. Model osmolarity weighted mean 306.2629 matches its aggregate 306.27 within rounding; human aggregate 310, so physiological aggregate also differs.
3. For the human severe-thirst 5/10 choices, the approximate 95% Wilson interval is [0.237,0.763]. Under a hypothetical fixed, externally specified Bernoulli probability 0.79, P(X<=5 | n=10) = 0.0398624. This is a descriptive sensitivity check, not a valid preregistered model rejection test: probability rounded, model fitted after observing target, independence across human sessions not established and sample small. More simulation runs shrink simulation sampling uncertainty but do not resolve human sampling uncertainty.
4. Printed equations 1–2 on p.317 imply, assuming natural logarithm and positive sVal, utility offset = log(sVal)+reward_max. At fixed reward_max, derivative 1/sVal is positive and second derivative -1/sVal^2 is negative. Thus utility increases at a decreasing rate with sVal; a different plotting orientation or nonlinear state mapping can change apparent curvature. The p.319 prose claiming accelerating effect is not implied for utility versus sVal. At sVal=0 the printed log is undefined as a finite real value; Eq.3 also includes noise and does not explicitly specify clipping. Treat boundary handling and log convention as unresolved implementation details, not proof of a software defect. Figure 4 places subjective thirst on vertical axis and utility on horizontal axis, so an exponential inverse relationship is not evidence of accelerating utility as a function of normalized thirst.
5. Identifiability: a single binary decision at a fixed offer cannot identify a multi-parameter rule-utility model from aggregate acceptance and thirst summaries. A simplified threshold rule is a viable alternative explanation, not tested here. The paper acknowledges incomplete affective declarative memory and unresolved arbitration among competing needs. Original human paper reports subjective-state association but no statistically established treatment-group choice difference; its mechanistic interpretation is explicitly tentative. Neither study demonstrates acquired physiology-to-action mappings.

## Ora crosswalk (research questions, not execution authority)
Distinguish actual conserved resource state from an organism's internal information about it, and from any external observer's inferred valuation. A designer-selected state-dependent threshold may produce adaptive-looking decisions without acquired cognition. In the future, evidence for a newly acquired mechanism would require a matched authored/reactive comparator, independently varied current resource state and history, conditional rather than aggregate predictions, accounting for all information acquisition and execution costs, and separately measured lineage benefit.
