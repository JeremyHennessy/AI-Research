## Ora research crosswalk

A useful distinction is between a supplied knowledge representation, an automatically computed organization of that representation, the inference procedure, and the provenance of the resulting answer. The first two theorem-proving papers are a concrete example of efficiency gains from organizing already encoded knowledge; they are not evidence that the system independently constructed the semantic encoding or inference machinery. The 2003 paper's admitted loss of completeness under some restrictions is relevant to evaluating computationally cheap decisions: fewer steps alone do not imply equally reliable decisions. Inference Web shows the long-standing prior art of externally documented derivation/provenance. Ora's research-level evaluation should distinguish observer-generated logs from information actually accessible to the organism; a complete external trace does not establish internal awareness.
