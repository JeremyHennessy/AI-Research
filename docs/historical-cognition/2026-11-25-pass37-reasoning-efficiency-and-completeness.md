Independent checks against Figure 3, author PDF index 5:
1. PDO+SOS completes 9/9 displayed queries, MFS+SOS 6/9, unrestricted resolution 7/9. Missing entries are timeouts, not zero cost or negative answers. No population success probability follows from selected queries.
2. The text's universal step-ranking claim conflicts with its own table: p4 uses 382 PDO+SOS steps vs 381 MFS+SOS; p3 uses 594 vs 566; q1 ties plain PDO at 11. This qualifies the ranking, not the general practical improvement.
3. For p3, SOS requires 38,943 steps and 1,191 seconds, PDO+SOS 594 steps and 2.1 seconds, about 65.6-fold fewer steps and 567-fold lower runtime. For p5, vanilla MP requires 1,999 steps vs SOS 598 (3.34-fold more). Different cost metrics and queries can give different rankings.
4. Small analytical example of the admitted MP+SOS and PDO+SOS incompleteness: partition A contains p and (not p or q); B contains (not q or r); query r; shared vocabulary {q}. Running-intersection holds. Ordinary MP derives q in A and sends it to B, which entails r. Global support initially {not r} lies only in B, so A cannot derive q under MP+SOS; B derives not q but cannot send it back. For ordered resolution with p highest, q next and r lowest, SOS derives not q, but resolving with (not p or q) on q is disallowed because p is higher. No allowed inference brings p into support; refutation stalls although r is entailed. This illustrates the known restriction, not a new theorem claim.
5. Total resource cost is preparation cost K plus sum of query costs. Reduced resolution steps do not prove lower total resource expenditure, especially for short lifetimes. Preprocessing, communication and maintenance costs not fully established; runtime is not an energy measurement.
