## Independent analytical extension: when stopping is justified

Consider a finite deterministic prefix search n=0,...,N, with remaining execution cost f(n) and constant observation cost c>0. The paper's local stopping idea (Appendix A) motivates the following original analysis, not a claim that its stochastic ACT-R implementation is exactly this abstraction. Define g(n)=f(n)-f(n+1), T(n)=f(n)+nc. Then T(n+1)-T(n)=c-g(n). If g is nonincreasing, these increments are nondecreasing; the first strictly positive increment occurs at a global minimizing prefix (if none, choose N). Proof: all earlier increments are nonpositive and all later increments nonnegative, hence summing either side proves minimality. Ties are harmless. Without diminishing marginal returns the result fails, even with exact knowledge of the next increment.

Construct f=(20,19,6), c=3. Then T=(20,22,12). At n=0 the next saving is 1, below cost 3, so the local rule stops at cost 20. Two observations jointly save 14 against expense 6, and the global optimum costs 12. Regret is 8. In a second world f=(20,19,19), the same initial and next costs are observable, but T=(20,22,25) and stopping is optimal. A policy informed solely by this shared prefix cannot choose the globally optimal stopping action in both worlds. This is a finite information-identifiability counterexample, not proof that any particular organism will fail.

For Ora's paid spatial-information research, treat a fixed two-step exploration baseline, a local-reactive baseline and a history-sensitive baseline as distinct comparators in future research design. Account for all observation/maintenance costs and any downstream reproductive effects; do not credit unpaid future information. Vary hidden later resource opportunities while matching the immediate observable prefix and funding. This analytic comparison does not authorize implementation or runtime qualification, nor establish an evolved mechanism or world novelty. The deterministic theorem does not transfer without assumptions to stochastic, mortality-limited or replenishing ecologies.
