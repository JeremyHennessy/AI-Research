## Ora crosswalk and negative evidence

Row 226 provides a specifically conditional counterexample to treating smaller memory capacity as always beneficial: the authors report different capacity advantages before and after a change and a sex-specific result. Do not transfer the human finding to digital organisms without evidence. Row 233 explicitly proposes testing how necessary mechanisms are and how outcomes depend on parameter values and strategy choices; its two-model comparison is a useful antecedent to future causal controls, not evidence of autonomous mechanism formation. Row 239 preserves failure of a default binary credit model and authored alternatives; a better fit obtained by designer-supplied partial credit is not evolved valuation. Row 245 separates knowledge of a task from the availability of a general inference strategy; training both does not establish that either arose independently. Row 240 distinguishes resource allocation measured in time from physically conserved energy; do not collapse them.

Original analytical illustration, not a claim about these papers' exact equations: let two actions have equal cost and one-step binary success probability 1/2, but payoff conditional on success 2 versus 10 units. Their binary success observations have identical distributions even though expected gross returns are 1 versus 5. Conversely, if successful action A returns 2 with probability 1 and B returns 10 with probability 1/2, maximizing binary success favors A whereas maximizing expected resource return favors B. This establishes only that binary labels can discard economically relevant magnitude information. It does not establish that scalar feedback is needed, that a specific reward signal is appropriate, or that an organism has acquired any valuation mechanism. Compare matched resource costs, information access and downstream reproductive outcomes; distinguish supplied fitness/credit rules from learned or evolved associations. No runtime change authorized.
