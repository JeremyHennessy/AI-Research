## Method and independent audit

The paper compares two ACT-R controllers in a dynamic water-level task. Reitter learns preferences among authored estimation/calculation strategies using episodic retrieval; Halbrügge adjusts utilities of supplied strategies. Neither develops its own reasoning architecture. Researchers vary parameters externally across four development and five transfer conditions; fit uses 100-point curves of median absolute deviations from target across people or simulations. Initial sweeps use 15 runs per configuration and selected follow-ups 500. The reported approximately 40 million runs use external Cell/Tornado search and military HPC; not 40 million independent subjects or autonomous discoveries. Parameters for removed-component configurations are separately optimized (sections 3.1–3.2). Table 3 describes a delayed-control condition with 60 human subjects.

1. **Text-table exception to blanket necessity language.** Table 4 linear-increasing condition has RMSE 0.44 estimation-only versus 0.61 both, and 0.61 calculation-only. Correlation instead favors both (.95 versus .86 and .87). Hence neither metric alone justifies saying both are individually necessary in every development condition. Across all four displayed conditions, arithmetic means of condition RMSE are 1.1775 both, 1.3875 estimation, 1.6925 calculation. Equal-length concatenation of the four aggregate error curves would yield RMSE 1.38624, 1.68543, 1.96799 respectively (computed as sqrt(mean of squared condition RMSEs)). This supports a joint descriptive advantage under those weighting assumptions, not a significance test, individual-level fit, or a universal necessity theorem.
2. **Delayed feedback and scale.** Table 3 reports RMSE 15.00 both, 19.71 practical-only, 624739.20 analytical-only. The latter is 41649.28 times the both-strategy value; practical-only is 31.4% higher than both. These are discrepancies against human response curves, not measures of resource acquisition or even directly comparable individual control errors. A model matching human mistakes can fit better than a more successful controller.
3. **Parameter aggregation.** Table 1 Reitter transfer noise optima (.93,.07,.07,.07,.07) average .242; excluding Delay 3 leaves .07. The development mean .7475 (rounded .75) and this contrast are consistent with the reported direction of overfit. A mean of condition-wise optima is not necessarily an optimum of mean loss. Transfer data were unavailable to original challenge entrants but used during this subsequent search; optimized fits to these same conditions are not independent held-out generalization tests.
4. **Compute accounting.** 123000 processor hours / 240 wall-clock hours = 512.5 mean concurrent processor-equivalents, consistent with rounded reported 512. Dividing by the approximate 40 million runs gives 11.07 processor-seconds/run as an aggregate ratio, not measured per-run latency or organism cost.
5. **Finite search boundary.** An adaptive sampled surface and best observed settings do not mathematically establish global optimality or impossibility at all parameter settings. The paper's retuning design is a stronger comparative test than a fixed-setting ablation; necessity remains conditional on the model family, permitted resources, objective, sampled parameter domain and uncertainty. No confidence interval for the above arithmetic differences can be recovered from the aggregate table alone.

## Original analytical witness and Ora crosswalk

Consider an authored component indicator m in {0,1}, an adjustable compensator theta in [0,1], and a hypothetical nonnegative loss L(m,theta)=(m+theta-1)^2. The intact setting (1,0) gives zero loss. Removing m at fixed theta=0 produces loss 1, but the reduced model at theta=1 also has zero loss. Thus the fixed-setting ablation proves dependence of that specific configuration but cannot establish that the component is necessary across the permitted comparator class. If theta is physically unavailable or requires extra energy, the comparison changes. More generally, min_theta L(0,theta) <= L(0,theta_full); fixed-setting loss can overstate the disadvantage of the best restricted alternative. This is an original elementary illustration, not an assertion of literature novelty or a historical experiment.

For Ora, distinguish a local causal intervention (same history/setting, matched resources; does manipulating information change action?) from a class-level necessity comparison (could a restricted reactive, alternating or compact controller achieve equal outcome with its own fair configuration?). A negative result on the first does not prove absence of cognition, and a positive result does not establish superiority over all alternatives. Reoptimization must not quietly change the common budget, environment, observation rights, genome capacity or admission criteria. A future independently frozen evaluation should separate tuning worlds from evaluation worlds and record resource, reproduction and viability outcomes alongside decision behavior, without treating behavioral agreement as evidence of an independently acquired mechanism. The existing reported Ora lineage milestones do not themselves establish this comparison. No engineering work or experiment is proposed for execution here.
