## Ora crosswalk: external knowledge, correction and transfer

The 2015 ontology study is evidence that a designer-supplied knowledge resource can improve a cognitive model's task fit; it does not demonstrate that the model learned that resource by interacting with its environment. The 2016 eye-tracking study discloses a failed directional prediction and an authored corrective rule. Preserve this distinction between pre-correction and post-correction performance. The 2011 driving paper is a particularly useful negative comparator to Pass 45 row 274: quantitative comparisons are now reported, but discrepancies remain despite the absence of newly adjustable parameters. Parameter-free transfer is not equivalent to generalization success. These examples establish bounded historical precedent, not new Ora results.

Original analytical illustration: let a hidden binary state S be equiprobable. A supplied external cue C equals S with probability q, where 1/2 <= q <= 1. An agent that always copies C achieves accuracy q without retaining any information from past trials. If S is independently resampled and C removed, any history-only guess has accuracy 1/2 under the stated assumptions. Thus a perfect observed success rate at q=1 is compatible with zero acquired cross-trial knowledge. This toy model assumes no other cues, no feedback before choice and independent states; it is not a reproduction of the historical studies. In eventual Ora analysis, provenance of environmental cues, inherited state, and acquired representations matters alongside outcome scores. No new experimental authorization.
