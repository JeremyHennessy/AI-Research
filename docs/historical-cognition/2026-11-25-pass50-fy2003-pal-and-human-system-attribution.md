## Ora crosswalk and counterevidence

The RADAR trained/untrained contrast measures the benefit of previously acquired information within a designed system and a human-machine team. It is stronger than merely comparing the integrated system to an unrelated baseline, but neither that contrast nor the reported 32% score increase isolates autonomous machine task completion or the origin of the learning infrastructure. Citrine's corrected versus uncorrected outcomes show why observer assistance must be counted. Let A be the number of initially correct autonomous decisions among N tasks; let H be initially incorrect decisions corrected by a human, with no other changes. The joint score (A+H)/N can be 1 for any A from 0 to N if H=N-A, so even a perfect final joint score places no nonzero lower bound on A/N without intervention provenance. This is an analytical illustration, not a reconstruction of the historical experiment or Ora data. For Ora distinguish organism-generated state and action, externally supplied information, correction, and measured final outcome; keep equivalent infrastructure and costs in comparators.
