### Numerical crosswalk and counterevidence

Table 1 in the 2023 paper reports 91.6% and 93.4% online accuracy using one and five readout channels; corresponding offline accuracy 86.6% and 91.4%. Each uses 10,000 test samples averaged over five iterations. Online one pass over 50,000 training samples versus batch 100 passes; not equivalent compute, energy or algorithm controls. At fixed five-channel input, reported difference is 2.0 percentage points. At one channel, difference is 5.0 points. Authors state online performance becomes comparable to batch when active error correction is removed, and attribute much of the higher performance to the iterative algorithm. Thus adaptive material, readout algorithm and label/correction access must be separated. No biological equivalence, general intelligence, or energy advantage can be inferred from these classification comparisons.

For Ora, a useful research-only distinction is between a substrate whose state changes with experience and a system that autonomously extracts useful decisions from that state. A history-dependent state can encode information without a self-developed decision policy. A future evidentiary comparison would need equal accessible signals, externally supplied readout and correction accounted for, and measured functional advantage against a history-insensitive comparator; this is a conceptual requirement, not an experiment authorization. Unlike Ora's paid-reproduction research, the reviewed nanowire records do not demonstrate lineage replacement or resource-bounded heredity. No novelty claim or proposed runtime change.
