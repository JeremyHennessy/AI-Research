## Ora crosswalk: reward proxies and the limits of competitive adaptation

The 2009 NRL foraging paper (row 289, pp.271–275, selected sections only) is especially relevant to resource competition. Authors report 1000 model runs, 60 prior training trials per model, and three authored distance thresholds (1.5, 1.6, 1.7 m). Reported distribution: 21% settled on 1.5 m, 41% on the intended 1.6 m, 14% on 1.7 m and 24% no clear preference. Thus 59% did not clearly settle on the intended threshold, despite aggregate fit R²=0.96 and RMSE=7.2%. These are author-reported model results, not independent reproduction or raw-data verification. Authors explicitly describe conservative strategy use compensating for misclassification and simpler gaze-following being preferred over slower full perspective transformation. Reward/punishment was assigned probabilistically at target choice rather than waiting for actual consumption or conflict, with selected near/far punishment probabilities 0.1/0.9 after parameter exploration. Full trial validation was ongoing. Actual independent competitive survival, inherited mechanisms and self-developed learning rules were not established. For eventual Ora evidence, distinguish resource delivery from a proxy reward and report individual competence alongside population aggregates. This is a conceptual crosswalk, not experimental authorization.

Original analytical illustration (not a historical rerun): suppose a cheap immediate action has success probability 1-p and pays 4 on success or -8 on failure; an always-successful cautious action pays 4 but incurs additional cost c. Then immediate expected utility is 4-12p and cautious utility is 4-c. Cautious is strictly preferred exactly when p>c/12. At c=2 the threshold is 1/6; with p=0.1 versus 0.9 the preference reverses. The toy assumptions are not the ACT-R temporal-difference implementation, and the numerical example does not reproduce its reward model. It shows how a supplied payoff structure can determine the apparent value of a cognitive mechanism without proving that the organism independently developed it. Use actual paid outcomes, comparable cost and strong simple policies to distinguish mechanisms in future authorized research.
