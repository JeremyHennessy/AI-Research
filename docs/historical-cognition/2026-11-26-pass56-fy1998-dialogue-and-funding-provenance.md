## Ora crosswalk and negative evidence

Recognition of categories provided by a researcher is different from forming useful internal categories; an organism's causal use of a representation requires its own evidence. The 2000 article already proposes unsupervised formation of task-relevant categories, so this broad idea cannot be claimed novel for Ora. The 2001 evaluation illustrates outcome substitution: counting any finished task as success conflates achieving the original objective and adapting to system error. For future authorized ecological analyses, distinguish an intended functional outcome, alternate outcome, and failure; record actual resource costs and avoid inferring cognitive intent from an external label.
