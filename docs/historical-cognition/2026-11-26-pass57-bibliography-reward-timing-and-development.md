## Ora prior-art crosswalk
Row 301 directly addresses temporal credit assignment. Under fixed task/actor architecture, varying reward timing, objective and magnitude changes learned policy; timing was strongest in the reported comparison. Authors note their late-trial environmental constraints bias utilities toward actions near the reward and that individual differences were not investigated (sections 4.7, 5). This is evidence against interpreting a policy learned under a designer's critic as proof of intrinsic valuation. Acknowledgments explicitly name EPSRC EP/G043507/1 for Janssen and ONR N000140710033 and N000140910402 for Gray, project officer Ray Perez; author-reported funding links only, no administrative award amounts, exact recipient institution on award or acceptance verified.

Row 313 gives competing explanations for observed development, while row 312 demonstrates an interaction-time confound. Original analytic observation: if observed duration T=C+I (cognitive processing plus nonoverlapping interaction time), assigning all T to cognition overstates C by I, or a factor T/C when C>0. With I=T/2 this factor is two. This is a conditional accounting identity, not a rerun or a general biological law. For future authorized analyses, separate interaction latency, computation, actual paid resources, and decision quality; distinguish competing mechanisms using observations that separate them, not just an aggregate fit. No runtime or experimental change is proposed.
