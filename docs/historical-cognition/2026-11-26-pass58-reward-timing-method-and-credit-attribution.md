## Independent analytic checks (not historical re-execution)
A. Local versus total duration. Consider an authored task requiring eight successful placements, no errors, identical fixed action costs and deterministic completion. A places one item per round in 2 s; B places two per round in 3 s. Minimizing round duration prefers A (2 < 3), but total duration prefers B (8×2 = 16 s versus 4×3 = 12 s). For arbitrary positive durations d_A,d_B and batch sizes k_A,k_B that divide the task size, the reversal occurs when d_A < d_B and d_A/k_A > d_B/k_B. The result is arithmetic and conditional, not a new learning algorithm or historical model replication.
B. Temporal attribution. For a linear elapsed-time subtraction R = r - delta (as described in section 2.2), compare rewards in normalized units where the subtraction is dimensionally defined. If r_A > r_B, the critic nevertheless ranks B higher whenever delta_A-delta_B > r_A-r_B. E.g., r_A=6, delta_A=5 gives 1; r_B=4, delta_B=1 gives 3. A common terminal reward can therefore assign more credit to late rules than early ones without any evidence that the late rules caused the result. This is a counterexample to identifying causal contribution from the credit signal alone; not an assertion that all delayed-credit mechanisms fail.
C. State pooling. If one shared action utility is updated with fixed learning rate alpha in (0,1] from independent samples drawn from a stationary mixture of two contexts with probabilities p and 1-p, expected utility after n updates is (1-alpha)^n U_0 + [1-(1-alpha)^n][p m_1+(1-p)m_2], where m_j is the conditional mean credit. Derivation: take expectations in U_(n+1)=(1-alpha)U_n+alpha R_(n+1) and solve the scalar recurrence. Example: contextual credits +3 and -1 give pooled limit 4p-1. The sign changes at p=1/4 without any contextual change in underlying rewards. Actual historical visitation and selection are endogenous; this example does not estimate its utilities. It demonstrates why a pooled improvement is not enough to identify a learned mechanism.
