## Prior-art crosswalk
Row 326 (Role of Learning, Discussion, pp.125–126) explicitly separates creating an inhibitory strategy from learning its usefulness. The first was done by the researchers, and the latter was represented through supplied ACT-R utility updates; independent strategy construction was proposed, not demonstrated. The paper also states that empirical agreement of the combined fast/slow model was not yet shown. Its acknowledgments identify ONR N00014-08-1-0921 and N0001407WX20452/N0001408WX30007 (the latter two to J. Gregory Trafton at NRL) plus GMU Center for Social Complexity. These are three publication-acknowledged instrument identifiers, not verified new awards or expenditure. The latter two may overlap earlier NRL research; no claim of unique financial instruments. Row 329 provides a complementary warning about internal mechanism attribution: similar implicit-learning task outcomes can be obtained with an explicitly designed declarative-memory approach.

For future authorized Ora analysis, distinguish an authored fallback or inhibitory rule, acquired selection of that rule, and an acquired rule that actually changes behavior. A toy counterexample: two agents both suppress a default action under the same conditions; one executes an inherited lookup table, the other acquires an equivalent mapping from experience. Their current behavioral traces can be identical, so those traces alone cannot identify the origin of the mapping. Initialization and acquisition histories, and controls that separate the two explanations, are required. This is a conditional identifiability argument, not an Ora experiment, claim of consciousness, or new learning mechanism.
