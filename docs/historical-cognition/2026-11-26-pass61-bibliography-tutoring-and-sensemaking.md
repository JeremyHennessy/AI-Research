## Crosswalk: learning by the agent versus learning in its model of another
The 2006 tutor chapter distinguishes tracking a student's solution steps from estimating the student's skill mastery (printed p.62). It explicitly notes that ACT-R does not prescribe curriculum or instructional scaffolding. A tutor can revise its estimate of a learner without acquiring the learner's skill. The March 2002 CMU report's introduction separates architecture-wide constraints from choices about domain representations; its first page acknowledges James S. McDonnell Foundation grant 95-11. The 2013 sensemaking paper (section 3.3.4) uses memories of prior decisions and their payoffs to choose resource allocations; its acknowledgement names IARPA via US Department of the Interior contract D10PC20021. These are two publication-acknowledged funding links, one private foundation and one government, not authenticated award accounting, final acceptance or evidence of independent general cognition.

Original analytical illustration: let a controller estimate another agent's probability of success q from observations while its own fixed policy never changes. With a Beta(1,1) prior, observing 9 successes and 1 failure yields posterior mean 10/12; after 1 success and 9 failures instead it is 2/12. The controller's estimate is adaptive in both cases, but its own ability to perform the task need not change at all. Thus estimation improvement, task execution competence and an acquired mechanism for deciding must have separate evidence and provenance. For Ora, a changed external estimate of organism competence does not establish an internal acquired representation; an internally updated estimate likewise does not prove improved task execution or independent acquisition of the update machinery. This is a toy calculation and evaluation principle, not an Ora experiment or proposed runtime change.
