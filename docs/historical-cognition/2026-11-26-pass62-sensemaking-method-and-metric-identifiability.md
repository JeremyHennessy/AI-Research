## Mechanisms and controls
- Six AHA tasks within one designed domain; categorical observations and likelihood rules, memory chunks, architecture, task decomposition, retrieval laws and decision routines are supplied. Sections 3.3.1–3.3.3 show an authored centroid-of-centroids method that trades recency bias for inertia and a seeded table of prior/factor/posterior examples for approximate probability adjustment. The centroid representation discards frequency information despite event traces being available.
- Section 3.3.4: allocation decisions use remembered outcomes plus up to two supplied counterfactual alternatives after feedback. Allocation values between supplied boundary strategies can arise from blending; this is adaptation within an authored mechanism, not evidence that the agent invents counterfactual reasoning. Section 3.3.5 uses learned information preferences, but the reward for reducing entropy and the cost-sensitive search are designed.
- Section 4.1 compares 45 model runs with aggregate responses of 45 MITRE employees; section 5 reports new stimuli and 103 Penn State participants, with parameters and knowledge structures unchanged according to the authors. This is a meaningful within-framework transfer test, not independent replication or a test of open-world task discovery. Original task 3 struggles with centroid placement; supplying centroids in task 4 reduces that particular obstacle (section 4.1.2–3). Task 4 second-layer bias direction differs between humans and model. Table 6 task 5 S1 means both 84.5 but trial-level R² .000; task 3 S2 means 34.7 versus 73.8 despite R² .701. Thus good mean fit and correlation are not substitutes for matching individual trajectories or calibrated performance. No estimate of a new independently acquired cognitive mechanism follows.

## Original analytical checks and Ora crosswalk
1. Certainty is not correctness. The paper's section 4 negentropy is N=1-H(p)/log2(k). For two outcomes, reference p=(0.8,0.2) and model q=(0.2,0.8) have identical H≈0.721928 bits and N≈0.278072, but opposite preferred outcomes. D_KL(p||q)=0.8 log2(4)+0.2 log2(1/4)=1.2 bits. A certainty-only score cannot identify the target of a belief, much less causal confirmation bias. This does not negate the paper's separate distribution and behavioral analyses.
2. The relative success rate (eq. 9) is (S_model-S_uniform)/(1-S_uniform), similarities normalized to [0,1]. If reference is uniform, S_uniform=1, denominator zero. Even near uniform, the score can be sensitive: at S_uniform=.99, changing S_model from .995 to .994 shifts RSR .5 to .4. The authors acknowledge a near-uniform singularity in task 5. Report raw similarity, denominator and absolute errors rather than relying on normalized fit.
3. Mean fit and correlation are independent: reference outcomes (20,40,60), model (60,40,20) share mean 40 but reverse order; model (10,20,30) has perfect squared Pearson correlation with reference but half its mean. The table 6 examples above demonstrate why both discrepancies must remain visible. These are analytical illustrations, not a reproduction of the study.
For Ora, independently acquired representations would require identification of their content, causal effect on decisions under controlled novel conditions, and accounting for authored priors, counterfactuals, reward, available information and actual resource outcomes. Increased certainty or population-level performance alone is insufficient. An authored confidence criterion can favor confident error. These checks are future evaluation principles only; this batch does not supply evidence of current Ora cognition or alter experimental plans, baseline or authorization.
