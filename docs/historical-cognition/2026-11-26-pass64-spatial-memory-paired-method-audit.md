## Method and evidence boundaries
The 2004 study used four participants; the 2006 study used thirteen different participants according to the report's fixed-parameter evaluation description. Each participant had five sessions of 30 paths, each with 15 judgments: nominal 9,000 and 29,250 response opportunities respectively, not independent observations or recovered trial counts. The task supplies unit moves in a fixed coordinate frame on a 5×5×5 grid. Participants identify revisits without a displayed map. The later corpus includes equal numbers of planar and 3D paths. Both studies impose a near balance of revisits and non-revisits; no inference to arbitrary environments.
The supplied ACT-R architecture encodes positions and retrieves previous location chunks with similarity-based partial matching. The 2004 report varies a retrieval threshold (-0.65) and distance scaling (1.4); it reports RMSD .045 for the delay effect and .064 for the near-visit effect. The 2006 paper reports r=.95 and RMSD=.023 for segment-level accuracy, using earlier fitted parameters without adjustment to the new dataset. It reports worse fit without spatial interference even after retuning threshold; no numeric ablation effect or uncertainty estimate for that comparison is supplied. Rehearsal/recoding are omitted by design. No evidence of independently acquired coordinate semantics, similarity metric, memory machinery, or broad planning. This is bounded author-reported transfer; neither E3 nor validation of a unique human mechanism. The 2008 abstract supports continuity of the research programme, not an extra independent replication.

## Original analytical counterexample: more false alarms without worsening individual memories
Suppose a novel location is compared with k prior distractors, each independently producing a spurious match with fixed probability q, and a decision is positive if any match succeeds. Then false-alarm probability is F(k)=1-(1-q)^k. With q=.05, F(1)=.05, F(5)=.2262190625 and F(10)=.4012630608. Thus increasing distractor count can elevate false alarms even when the per-comparison error rate is unchanged. This is an illustrative independent-comparison model, not a reproduction of ACT-R retrieval or an Ora experiment. Dependence, retrieval competition, changing thresholds, or heterogeneous q invalidate the equality; under arbitrary dependence the union bound F(k)≤min(1,kq) holds for equal marginal probabilities. Aggregate load effects alone do not identify decay, interference, criterion shift, or capacity. The historical authors' own reported analyses and ablation support their specific account but do not uniquely establish it.
For Ora's research crosswalk, distinguish retention at matched delay, discrimination at matched distractor count, and access at matched current input; interpret any future evidence only with the cost and provenance of the representation recorded. A decrease in overall accuracy may reflect increased opportunities for confusion, not degradation of a memory mechanism. This is an evaluation consideration only, not permission or a specification for new execution.
