## Original crosswalk and limitations
Human self-correction under a designed tutor (397) is not equivalent to the tutor learning self-correction. A supplied error detector can achieve excellent correction without discovering the concept of error. Human choices in a social game (414) can likewise depend on own outcome history without representing another mind. Distributed resource competition (418) and temporal interference (404/425) suggest comparing distinct causal models rather than counting tasks as independent capabilities. As a conceptual counterexample, two fixed agents can complete separate tasks in identical time but differ when tasks overlap: if both require one exclusive unit of resource for T steps, completion of two simultaneous tasks needs at least 2T steps; if each uses disjoint resources, both may finish in T. The difference alone says nothing about learned scheduling, because resource topology can be supplied. A future authorized study would separate resource topology, authored scheduler, acquired schedule and observable interference. No execution or new mechanism proposed as proven.
