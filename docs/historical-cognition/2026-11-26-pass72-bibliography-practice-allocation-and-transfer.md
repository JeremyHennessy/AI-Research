A resource-allocation distinction: suppose two memory items yield expected gains g_A=0.4, g_B=0.6 per practice, with respective costs c_A=1, c_B=3. Under a one-practice choice, gain alone favors B; gain per cost favors A (0.4 versus 0.2). This is an illustrative arithmetic counterexample, not the paper's fitted objective or any Ora experiment. Whether a scheduler should optimize a ratio depends on horizon, budget and interdependence; greedy ratios need not solve a discrete global allocation problem. An externally provided scheduler can create apparent learning efficiency without the learner developing a scheduling capability. Distinguish endogenous action selection, acquisition of the scheduling mechanism, and environmental scheduling. For later authorized Ora assessment, equalize resource budgets and vary cues/novel conditions when interpreting transfer; distinguish faster responses to repeated instances from new-instance and new-operation transfer.
