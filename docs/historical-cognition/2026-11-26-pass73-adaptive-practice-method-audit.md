## E2 method and evidence
- pp.104–105: 60 randomized completers (20 per arm), 12 additional withdrawals (5/2/5); three one-hour sessions, one-week delayed test; 180 scheduled Japanese–English pairs and 28 independently scheduled context probes. Arms: ACT-R scheduler, flashcards, adapted Atkinson Markov scheduler. Equal time, not equal trial count.
- pp.106–108,115–117: supplied memory-decay model, fixed objective, fitted parameters and scheduling thresholds; Bayesian adjustment of difficulty estimates from responses. No autonomous invention of scheduler. Atkinson control spacing bug affected 1.2% of trials; procedural departures from 1972 study.
- p.108: first delayed-test recall, observed vs prior simulated: ACT-R 66% vs 56%; flashcards 35% vs 31%; Atkinson 40% vs 24%. Reported session-4 recall F(2,57)=5.4, p=.0073; successful latency F(2,57)=10.3, p=.00015. Failure latency not significant. Figure 3 aggregates assessment trials; do not confuse those bars with first-trial percentages.
- pp.108–111: prior item difficulty estimates improved training performance but not delayed performance; abandoned Atkinson items recalled at mean .57 (SD .26), contrary to permanent-state assumption. Context probes show only ACT-R/flashcard difference significant across all tests. Appendix reports omitted parameter-fitting details; p.117 threshold -0.66 differs from main-text -0.63 (preserve discrepancy, not resolved). Generalization to other tasks, populations and dependency structures not demonstrated.
## Provenance and interpretation

Do not treat the grant as proof of a general capability.
The random allocation and equal time support a comparison of externally supplied instructional policies for these human learners; attrition, differences in number of practice opportunities and the modified comparator bound inference. The intervention is a package: the design does not isolate the causal effect of each ACT-R equation, or show superiority to every possible scheduling strategy. A prediction can preserve the leading condition while misspecifying absolute recall and even the control ordering; compare the observed and simulated values above. The two delayed control outcomes reverse order relative to prediction. Early accuracy, long-delay retention and efficiency are distinct endpoints.
## Original Ora crosswalk (analytical only)
For a hypothetical equal-budget comparison, suppose policy A uses 2 resource units per exposure and improves a score by 0.03 per exposure; B uses 5 and improves by 0.06. Under a budget of 20, A can produce 0.30 improvement and B 0.24, even though B is twice as effective per exposure. Assumes additive unsaturated gains and deterministic cost; no historical data or Ora trial. Under equal exposure count B wins. Hence a ranking depends on denominator; both equal-cost and equal-exposure analyses are informative but not interchangeable. In Ora, accounting must include failed actions, sensing, computation, maintenance and environment-provided information; a useful change in inherited or acquired strategy is not established by greater practice opportunities alone. Distinguish an externally scheduled environment from an organism that causally selects its own experience, and distinguish parameter learning within a supplied rule from acquisition of the rule itself. Pass 2's scaffold-accounting principle and Pass 72's one-choice example are extended, not superseded. No claim that Ora already exhibits this capability or that the method is novel.
