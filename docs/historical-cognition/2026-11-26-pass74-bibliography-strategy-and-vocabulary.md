Ora relevance: distinguish (i) selecting among preprogrammed strategies, (ii) learning associations/parameters, (iii) constructing a new strategy from existing operators, and (iv) independently acquiring the machinery for strategy construction. The 2010 control model is relevant to (i)/(ii), the 2003 balance model to (iii) under explicitly supplied machinery; neither establishes (iv). An illustrative counterexample: a population of agents with two fixed strategies can shift all choices from the first to the second after an environmental reversal even though its strategy repertoire and learning rule never change. A different population may invent a third strategy while its aggregate success remains unchanged. Performance alone therefore does not distinguish adaptive selection from repertoire growth. Similarly, vocabulary convergence among agents with preloaded shared concepts does not establish independently developed semantics. Track provenance of representational primitives and distinguish within-pair agreement from novel-partner transfer; no Ora execution or novelty claim.
