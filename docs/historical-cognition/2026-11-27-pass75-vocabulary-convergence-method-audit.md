## Method and numerical evidence (E2; pp.176–184)

Authored ACT-UP associative retrieval, memory reinforcement/decay, preloaded concept network with corpus-derived weight distribution, fixed three-component messages and ambiguity verification; no procedural reinforcement learning. ## Original Ora crosswalk (conceptual, not an execution request)
A minimal counterexample: two isolated pairs learn different bijections over the same two meanings and two messages. Each achieves 100% within-pair identification, but when the pairs' mappings are reversed relative to one another, cross-pair accuracy is 0%. Neither needs to invent a new concept or cognitive operation. A third mechanism could use shared environmental cues and achieve perfect accuracy without any message causing the response. Thus neither pairwise success nor identical responses is adequate evidence of communication. For a future separately authorized study, distinguish (a) within-pair learning, (b) untrained-partner transfer without additional learning, (c) causal contribution of the message versus common input, (d) independent grounding of representational primitives, (e) cost of acquiring and using conventions and (f) persistence across descent versus direct copying. Matched message suppression or permutation with environmental information held fixed would help discriminate (c); compare pre-interaction and post-interaction mappings for (a)/(b). Report confusion matrices, not only mean accuracy; retain failures under unfamiliar partners and environments. These are prospective measurement concepts, not authorization, results or novelty claims. Current Ora evidence is not promoted by analogy.
