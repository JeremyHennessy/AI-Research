## Original Ora crosswalk (conceptual, not an execution)
Ranking cannot establish adequacy: hypothetical three predictors with losses 99, 100, 101 rank identically to losses 1, 2, 3, even though the winning absolute loss differs 99-fold. A predefined absolute criterion must accompany a comparative rank. Further, consider a deterministic delayed task in which action at step t affects the outcome at t+2, with rewards 1 for A and 0 for B. Alternating A,B yields observations 0,1 only if wrongly attributed to the immediately previous action (B,A versus the actual A,B two steps earlier); the immediate attribution can systematically reverse the ranking. More explicitly, at t=2 with A at t=0 and B at t=1, reward 1 belongs to A but is credited to B if the evaluator uses t-1. At t=3 the reverse occurs. This is a toy credit-assignment counterexample, not a claim about the historical implementation or current Ora. Future separately authorized research should distinguish predictive fit from functional success, define absolute criteria and matched baselines before seeing results, keep parameter selection separate from held-out worlds, and trace outcomes to the causally appropriate action/history under delays. Prior Ora lineage persistence alone does not establish any of these properties.
