Original research implication: sample efficiency and independently acquired representational machinery must be distinguished. For example, a learner supplied a binary feature that perfectly predicts a reward may learn its association after very few observations, whereas one given 100 irrelevant and one relevant feature must first identify the useful dimension. The difference may be representational assistance rather than a superior learning mechanism. Likewise, a fixed scheduler can interleave activities without learning, whereas a supplied learning rule can adjust their relative timing; neither proves independent creation of a scheduler. Future separately authorized evaluation should compare learning with matched feature access, measure total resource expenditure and held-out performance, separate feature invention from parameter acquisition, and avoid selecting best runs as representative. These are analytical distinctions, not Ora results or novelty claims.
