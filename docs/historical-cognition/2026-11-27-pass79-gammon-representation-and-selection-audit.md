## E2 review: what was and was not learned
- Sections 2.2.1–2.2.5: a standalone ACT-R-inspired algorithm, not an implementation in the actual ACT-R runtime. Three domain-specific feature schemas, similarity metric, memory allocation, move generator, stochastic policy, likelihood aggregation and feedback rules were designed externally. Acquired quantities were experience-dependent feature instances and their estimated outcome associations. The learner also received a designer-defined intermediate penalty for vulnerability followed by attack; it did not discover its own reward or representation. Reported memory footprint fewer than 50 stored feature instances, not a bound on total memory or compute.
- Sections 3.1–3.3 and Fig. 2: 20 stochastic training trials (except where specified), 1,000 games per trial versus pubeval, then the selected best trial evaluated for 5,000 games without learning. Table 1 reports 45.23% ± 0.71% for ACT-R-Gammon, 40.00% ± 3.46% for HC-Gammon and 59.25% ± 0.15% for the TD-trained network. The latter two are cross-paper comparisons, not matched experiments; different representation, feedback, compute and training exposure. The reported 45.23% does not lie on the 0.02 percentage-point grid of a single unweighted 5,000-game binary win tally (2261.5 implied wins); aggregation or reporting convention is unresolved, not evidence of fabrication. No run-level distribution or uncertainty definition established for Table 1; no significance inferred.
- Sections 3.4 and 4: the reported exposure-feature odds remain below neutral across the examined surface (Fig. 5); this is not proof that all such moves are harmful. Missing opponent distances are an acknowledged representational limitation. Feature plots have different run counts (attack 10, exposure 5, block table 1). No opponent-transfer, feature-discovery, human-learning comparison or matched reward/representation ablation establishes the authors' broader causal interpretation. Their own conclusion leaves representation formation open. ## Independent analytical check (not E3)
Equation 5 multiplies marginal likelihood ratios, which equals the joint likelihood ratio under conditional independence, not generally. Counterexample: let two observed binary features be perfect copies X=Y, with P(X=1|win)=2/3 and P(X=1|loss)=1/3. Observing both has true joint ratio 2, but a product of marginal ratios gives 4. A competing observation Z with conditional probabilities 3/4 and 1/4 has ratio 3. Thus the product ranks the duplicate evidence above Z, reversing the correct evidence ordering. This is an illustrative mathematical counterexample, not evidence that the historical feature dependencies actually caused a performance failure. Outcome association also does not identify the causal benefit of an action. The 5,000-game evaluation was selected at the trained-model level even though weights were frozen during evaluation; it estimates the selected policy, not a typical training run.
