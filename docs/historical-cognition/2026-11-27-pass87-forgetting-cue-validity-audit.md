## Methods and controls (printed pp. 614–627)
The authors supply production rules, retrieval equations and city identifiers; experience updates record strength. Simulated exposure to 83 German city names uses Chicago Tribune counts; activations averaged across 500 constructed environments. Recognition threshold and noise fitted to previously measured recognition rates (r=.91); held fixed across 101 decay settings. Recognition accuracy: 56% at zero decay, 63.3% at d=-.34, 61.3% at default d=-.50; frequency ranking achieved 76.5%. Excessive decay approaches chance. Fluency uses an assumed 100-ms discrimination threshold; clustered exposure and continuous retrieval variants retain the qualitative effect. These are author-reported simulations, not a new human experiment. The authors explicitly caution against a single-function evolutionary explanation.
## Independent analysis and evidence boundary
Let a be probability a binary recognition cue discriminates, v its conditional accuracy, and b accuracy of the fallback when it does not discriminate. Total accuracy is a*v+(1-a)*b. With random fallback b=1/2, gain above chance is a*(v-1/2). Thus increasing applicability hurts if cue validity is below chance: a=.8, v=.25 gives .30; a=.2 with the same v gives .45. If fallback uses retained factual knowledge, the benefit condition is v>b, not merely v>.5, at fixed v and b. For example v=.75, b=.9, a=.8 gives .78, versus .87 at a=.2. This is an original illustrative calculation, not a reanalysis of source observations or E3 reproduction. A policy able to ignore retained information need not lose information to implement the same decision; decay may instead offer an advantage in a restricted architecture or with explicit resource costs. No general necessity of forgetting or optimum across tasks is established. Fitted recognition and synthetic exposures do not establish independently learned cue validity, lifetime adaptation of decay, transfer to inverted cue relationships, or evolution of the learning machinery. Raw observations, executable model and independent reproduction not acquired.
## Ora prior-art crosswalk
Future separately authorized evaluation should distinguish loss of access from loss of stored information; fixed memory decay from acquired control of retention; and observed cue association from independently learning whether a cue is useful. Compare only with equal resource accounting and controls for fallback knowledge, reversed cue validity, rare consequential events and retention/retrieval costs. These are analytical evaluation distinctions, not a runtime design change, demonstrated capability or novelty claim.
