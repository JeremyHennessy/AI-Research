## Original counterexample: utility versus accuracy
Suppose a fallback rule A succeeds on 90% of cases and costs 3 units, while B succeeds on 10% and costs 1. If the sole objective is minimize production cost, B wins despite lower accuracy. If utility instead equals V times success probability minus cost, A wins exactly when 0.8V exceeds 2, i.e. V > 2.5. At equality the two tie. This is not a numerical reconstruction of ACT-R; it isolates why the provenance of the objective matters. Even a learned rule can reflect an authored cost preference rather than a discovered environmental regularity. In any future separately authorized Ora study, distinguish origin of costs, acquisition of particular rules, outcome utility, and transfer under changed cost schedules.
