Method (§§1–4): a hierarchy built from supplied fault priors, diagnostic tests, costs and action utilities; a greedy criterion ranks tests by expected gain in available action value divided by cost. Results (Figs.3–4): intermediate deadlines favor action utility, while the complete-diagnosis measure favors the decision tree around deadlines 4–7; both differences disappear at long deadlines. No patient-outcome validation, raw observations, run count, random seeds or uncertainty intervals found in this six-page paper. §3 optimality is conditional on strong additivity, uniformity and deadline assumptions; §6 acknowledges crude cost/value estimates and sequential rather than parallel testing.
Original analytical counterexample to unconditional greedy optimality, not a reproduction: suppose independent fair bits X,Y determine the correct binary action through parity, correct action utility 1 and incorrect 0. Observing either bit alone leaves expected utility 0.5; both together yield 1. A separate unit-cost noisy parity cue with 0.6 accuracy improves expected immediate utility to 0.6; after taking it and one bit, the remaining bit is unknown and expected utility stays 0.6. With two unit-cost observations available, selecting the best immediate gain first is inferior to observing X and Y. The example violates additivity and does not refute the authors' conditional result. It demonstrates why resource-bounded performance is not interchangeable with optimal planning.
