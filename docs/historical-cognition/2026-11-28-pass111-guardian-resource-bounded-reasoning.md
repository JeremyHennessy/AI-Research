## Original Ora crosswalk and causal boundaries
Resource sensitivity can appear at three distinct levels: an authored policy changes its behavior as resources vary; an individual acquires a new resource-allocation policy from experience; heritable changes alter the machinery that allocates resources. Guardian is a comparator for the first, not evidence of the latter two. Its separation of incoming information, processing cost and action also warns against interpreting a shorter execution trace as intrinsically better: the agent may simply omit observations. An eventual causal analysis of any Ora behavioral variant should distinguish opportunity exposure, resource consumption and subsequent survival or reproduction from a demonstrable acquired allocation mechanism; matched observations would need to account for lost information and environmental context. This is a research interpretation only, not an approved experiment or a novelty claim. The reported Ora functional inheritance does not itself establish learning, prediction or improved fitness.
