## Original analytical crosswalk
In a stylized two-target task, let a supplied directed action succeed with probability g and random selection with probability 1/2, and let q be the chance of selecting the directed action. Overall success is 1/2 + q(g-1/2). Thus an improvement can be produced solely by increasing q, without any improvement in perception or creation of a new action. For example g=1 and q=.6 gives .8 success. This is algebra, not reproduction of ACT-R/E or an Ora experiment. For Ora, a change in action-selection frequency is weaker evidence than the documented origin of a previously absent behavior; performance alone cannot establish the latter. This is a research-only crosswalk, not an experimental authorization.
